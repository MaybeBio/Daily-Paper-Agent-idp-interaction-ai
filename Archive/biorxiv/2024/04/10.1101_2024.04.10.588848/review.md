## Review setup

- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract, evaluated against the evidence described therein
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report biochemical reconstitution and cryo-electron microscopy of human MCM double hexamer (hDH) loading using purified proteins. They claim that hDH engages DNA differently from budding yeast, generates approximately 5 base pairs of unwound DNA at the hexamer interface, and that ORC6 is not essential for initial MCM recruitment or hDH loading, but contributes to an alternative assembly pathway involving an intrinsically disordered region in ORC1 and a novel MCM-ORC intermediate (hMO*). The work is positioned as the first step toward reconstitution of human DNA replication initiation.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, experimental methods, statistical analyses, cryo-EM data quality metrics, and any supporting biochemical data

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract describes a potentially significant advance in understanding eukaryotic DNA replication initiation, specifically the loading of the human MCM double hexamer. The claim of structural and mechanistic differences from the budding yeast system, particularly the non-essential role of ORC6 and the identification of a novel hMO* intermediate, is of considerable interest. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the technical soundness of the cryo-EM reconstructions, the robustness of the biochemical reconstitution, or the strength of the comparative claims. The conclusions are plausible but currently not verifiable from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in DNA replication, chromatin biology, and structural biology would be interested. The work addresses a fundamental question in eukaryotic replication initiation and provides a human-system perspective that may differ from the well-studied yeast paradigm. The identification of an alternative assembly pathway and the involvement of an intrinsically disordered region could also attract investigators studying protein disorder in macromolecular complex assembly.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a significant gap in the field by extending MCM double hexamer loading studies from yeast to human proteins. The combination of biochemical reconstitution and cryo-EM is appropriate for this question. The claim of generating unwound DNA at the hexamer interface, consistent with cell-derived hDH, suggests biological relevance. The identification of a non-essential role for ORC6 and an alternative pathway involving ORC1 IDR introduces mechanistic diversity that challenges the yeast-centric view.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that hDH engages DNA differently from yeast and generates approximately 5 base pairs of unwound DNA at the interface between hexamers.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract states that hDH generates about 5 base pairs of unwound DNA, but no details are provided on how this was measured, what the resolution of the cryo-EM map is, or how the unwound state was distinguished from alternative DNA conformations. Without these details, the claim cannot be assessed for technical validity.  
  - **Why it matters** The unwound DNA at the interface is a central mechanistic claim that distinguishes hDH from yDH. If the measurement is not robust, the comparative conclusion is weakened.  
  - **Resolution test** Provide cryo-EM map resolution, local resolution around the DNA interface, and a clear description of how the 5 base pair unwound region was modeled and validated. Show that the DNA conformation is not an artifact of reconstruction parameters.
