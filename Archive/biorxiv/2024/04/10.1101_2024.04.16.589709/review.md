- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report a synthetic biomolecular condensate platform based on consensus-designed tetratricopeptide repeat (CTPR) proteins fused to intrinsically disordered domains. They claim that CTPR structural attributes correlate with condensate propensity across experimental conditions and in silico modelling, and that incorporation of short binding motifs enables specific target-protein recruitment into condensates. The system is presented as rationally designable to tune condensate propensity and add new functions.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental details, quantitative results, or methodological descriptions provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, experimental protocols, modelling details, statistical analyses, and any supplementary information

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a conceptually appealing and potentially versatile synthetic biology approach for engineering biomolecular condensates with tunable properties. The combination of modular CTPR domains with disordered regions is a reasonable design strategy, and the claim of predictable modulation of condensate propensity is interesting. However, the abstract provides insufficient detail to evaluate the robustness of the experimental evidence, the quantitative nature of the trends, or the specificity and efficiency of the recruitment mechanism. The in silico modelling is mentioned but not described, and no numerical results or statistical measures are given. The work may be of interest to the synthetic biology and biomolecular condensate communities, but the current evidence base is too limited to assess whether the central claims are fully supported.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in synthetic biology, biomolecular condensate biology, protein engineering, and biophysics would be interested. The platform offers a modular approach to design condensates with predictable properties, which could be useful for studying phase separation mechanisms, building artificial cellular compartments, or developing biomaterials with tunable functions.
- **Major strengths** The design concept is clear and modular, combining structurally defined repeat proteins with disordered domains to achieve tunability. The claim that structural attributes of CTPR domains can systematically affect condensate propensity in a predictable manner is a potentially valuable advance. The incorporation of binding motifs to achieve specific recruitment adds functional versatility, which broadens the applicability of the system.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** "Trends between the CTPR structural attributes and condensate propensity were recapitulated across different experimental conditions and by in silico modelling, demonstrating that the CTPR domain can systematically affect the condensates in a predictable manner."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract claims a systematic and predictable effect of CTPR structural attributes on condensate propensity, but no quantitative data, statistical measures, or experimental conditions are described. The nature of the "trends" is unspecified, and the in silico modelling is mentioned without any description of the model, its parameters, or its validation against experiments.  
- **Why it matters** The central claim of predictability and systematic modulation is the core advance of the work. Without quantitative evidence and modelling details, the claim cannot be evaluated for robustness, reproducibility, or generalizability.  
- **Resolution test** Provide specific data showing the relationship between CTPR variants (e.g., number of repeats, surface properties) and condensate formation metrics (e.g., saturation concentration, droplet size, viscosity) across multiple conditions, with statistical analysis. Describe the in silico model, its inputs, and how well it predicts experimental outcomes.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** "Incorporating short binding motifs into the CTPR domain results in specific target-protein recruitment into the condensates."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The claim of specific recruitment is stated without any supporting data. No information is given on the binding motifs used, the target proteins, the degree of enrichment, or the specificity relative to non-target proteins.  
- **Why it matters** Specific recruitment is a key functional feature that would distinguish this platform from passive condensate formation. Without evidence of specificity and efficiency, the functional utility of the system is unsubstantiated.  
- **Resolution test** Show quantitative enrichment of target proteins in condensates compared to controls, demonstrate that non-target proteins are not recruited, and provide data on the binding affinity or motif requirements.
