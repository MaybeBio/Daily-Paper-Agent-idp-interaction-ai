## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially significant advance in understanding PTX3-mediated crosslinking of HC•HA matrices, with a novel pH-dependent regulatory mechanism. However, the evidence base available for review is limited to the abstract, which precludes rigorous evaluation of the structural models, binding stoichiometry, and functional claims. The work builds on prior published structures and extends them with new functional insights, but the strength of the conclusions cannot be fully assessed from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in extracellular matrix biology, reproductive biology, innate immunity, and inflammation would find these results relevant. The pH-dependent regulation of PTX3-HC1 interactions and the proposed crosslinking node model could inform studies on ovulation, fertilisation, and pathogenic matrix formation in viral lung infections. Structural biologists studying pentraxins or coiled-coil assemblies may also be interested.
- **Major strengths** The abstract integrates multiple structural techniques (X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, AlphaFold, SAXS) to validate the octameric architecture, which lends credibility to the model. The identification of a pH-dependent interaction between PTX3 and HC1 offers a novel regulatory mechanism that could explain context-specific matrix crosslinking. The stoichiometric claim of up to eight HC1 molecules per PTX3 octamer provides a clear and testable model for crosslinking node formation.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Technical soundness  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that PTX3 binds to HC1 at acidic pH and can be incorporated into pre-formed HC•HA complexes under these conditions, providing a novel mechanism for regulation of HA crosslinking.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
    **Concern** The abstract does not provide quantitative binding data, such as affinity constants, pH range tested, or controls for non-specific interactions. The claim of a pH-dependent conformational change in HC1 is stated as "likely" but no direct evidence is presented in the abstract.  
    **Why it matters** Without quantitative and controlled binding data, the physiological relevance of the acidic pH interaction is unclear, and the proposed regulatory mechanism cannot be distinguished from non-specific effects or artefacts.  
    **Resolution test** Provide binding curves or affinity measurements at multiple pH values, include negative controls (e.g., other HCs or unrelated proteins), and show direct evidence for the HC1 conformational change (e.g., structural or biophysical data).  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Technical soundness  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the PTX3 octamer associates simultaneously with up to eight HC1 molecules, forming a major crosslinking node within HC•HA matrices.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
    **Concern** The stoichiometry of eight HC1 molecules per PTX3 octamer is stated without presentation of the experimental method (e.g., mass photometry, analytical ultracentrifugation, or native mass spectrometry) or the data supporting this number. The abstract also does not clarify whether this stoichiometry is observed in solution, in the presence of HA, or under the acidic conditions where binding occurs.  
    **Why it matters** The crosslinking node model depends critically on the stoichiometry. If the binding is substoichiometric or influenced by HA, the proposed mechanism for matrix crosslinking would need revision.  
    **Resolution test** Show the raw data and fitting for the stoichiometry determination, specify the experimental conditions, and test whether the stoichiometry is maintained in the presence of HA or at different pH values.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** No  
    **Axis** Originality  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract states that the C-terminal pentraxin domains are arranged in a central cube with two long extensions, "essentially as described by" Noone et al. 2022.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
    **Concern** The abstract acknowledges that the overall architecture was previously described, which limits the novelty of the structural findings. The new contribution appears to be the inter-protomer salt bridges and the functional pH-dependent interaction, but the abstract does not clearly delineate which findings are novel versus confirmatory.  
    **Why it matters** For a high-impact venue, the incremental advance over prior work must be clearly articulated. If the structural model is largely confirmatory, the novelty rests on the functional data, which are currently under-supported.  
    **Resolution test** Clearly state in the abstract which structural features are new (e.g., the salt bridge network) and provide a direct comparison with the prior model, highlighting any differences or refinements.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** No  
    **Axis** Scientific importance  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the findings provide a novel mechanism for regulation of PTX3-mediated HA crosslinking during inflammation.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided

The abstract does not provide evidence that acidic pH occurs in the relevant physiological or pathological contexts, nor does it show that the pH-dependent interaction is functionally significant in a cellular or in vivo model.

    **Resolution test** Provide data showing that acidic pH is present in the proposed contexts (e.g., inflamed tissues) and demonstrate that the PTX3-HC1 interaction affects matrix properties in a cellular or in vivo assay.
