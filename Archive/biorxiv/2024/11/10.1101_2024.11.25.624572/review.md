## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract and significance statement
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that a catalytically inactive variant of the chaperone HtrA1 (HtrA1*) inhibits both α-Syn monomer aggregation and templated fibril seeding. They propose that inhibition arises from synergistic interactions between the PDZ and Protease domains of HtrA1* and α-Syn, with the PDZ domain engaging the C-terminal region of the monomer and the fibril C-terminal domain, and the Protease domain cleaving at T92/A93 in the NAC region of the monomer. The authors conclude that multi-pronged, multi-site recognition enables intervention at multiple stages of the aggregation pathway and may serve as a blueprint for therapeutic design.
- **Visible evidence base** The abstract references biomolecular NMR, AFM, Rosetta-based computational analyses, and aggregation kinetics, but no specific figures, tables, or quantitative results are provided in the supplied material.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript text, all figures and tables, experimental protocols, statistical details, NMR assignments, computational parameters, and raw or processed data. The abstract alone does not permit independent verification of any quantitative claim.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that HtrA1* effectively inhibits both α-Syn monomer aggregation and templated fibril seeding.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, no specific section or figure provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that inhibition occurs but provides no quantitative measures, such as extent of inhibition, concentration dependence, or comparison to controls. No kinetic parameters, lag times, or endpoint fibril mass data are mentioned.
- **Why it matters** The central claim of the paper is that HtrA1* is an effective inhibitor. Without quantitative data, the magnitude and significance of the effect cannot be assessed, and the claim remains unverifiable.
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative aggregation kinetics data, including dose response curves, inhibition percentages, and appropriate controls, in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Mechanistic support
- **Claim pointer** The authors propose that the PDZ domain interacts with the C-terminal end of the monomer and the intrinsically disordered C-terminal domain of the fibril, and that the Protease domain cleaves at T92/A93 in the NAC domain.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, NMR, AFM, Rosetta analyses mentioned but no data shown
- **Concern** The abstract describes the use of NMR, AFM, and Rosetta-based computations but does not present any of the resulting data. No binding affinities, chemical shift perturbations, AFM images, or computational models are described. The cleavage site T92/A93 is stated without supporting proteolysis data or sequence specificity calculations.
- **Why it matters** The mechanistic model is the core intellectual contribution. Without visible evidence for domain-specific interactions and cleavage specificity, the model remains speculative.
- **Resolution test** Include NMR titration data, AFM images with quantitation, Rosetta models with scores, and proteolysis assays with identified cleavage products in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Synergy claim
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that inhibition is mediated by synergistic interactions between the PDZ and Protease domains.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, no comparative data provided
- **Concern** The term "synergistic" implies that the combined effect of the two domains is greater than the sum of their individual effects. The abstract provides no data comparing the activity of the full HtrA1* construct to that of individual domain constructs or mutants.
- **Why it matters** Synergy is a strong and specific claim. Without direct comparative experiments, the claim is unsupported and could be misleading if the domains act additively rather than synergistically.
- **Resolution test** Provide experiments with isolated PDZ domain, isolated Protease domain, and the full construct, with quantitative comparison of inhibition activities.
