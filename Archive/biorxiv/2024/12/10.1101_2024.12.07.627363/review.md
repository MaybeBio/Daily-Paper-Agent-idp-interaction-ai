## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusions, supplementary information) as provided by the author.
- **Assessment boundary** The review is limited to the scientific content, methodology, and claims presented in the provided text. It does not include an assessment of the manuscript's adherence to specific journal formatting guidelines or the quality of the provided figures, which were not available for review.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that phosphorylation of the intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) of the myofilament protein ABLIM1, particularly at sites S216, S450, and S452, alters its conformational ensemble. This change is proposed to modulate the ability of ABLIM1's LIM domains to interact with the Z1Z2 segment of titin, thereby affecting passive tension and length-dependent activation (LDA) in cardiac muscle. The study uses a multiscale computational approach, including atomistic and coarse-grained molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, docking, and a state-based model of contraction, to support this hypothesis.
- **Visible evidence base** The evidence base consists of the manuscript text, including descriptions of methods and results, and references to figures and tables (e.g., Fig. 1-13, Fig. S1-S8, Table S1, S3-S10). The actual figures, tables, and supplementary data files were not provided for direct inspection.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** The review's confidence is significantly affected by the absence of the actual figures, tables, and supplementary data files. This prevents direct verification of the simulation data, structural models, and quantitative results. The GitHub repository link is provided but was not accessed for this review.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a compelling and timely computational investigation into the role of phosphorylation in modulating the structure and function of the intrinsically disordered protein ABLIM1 within the cardiac sarcomere. The study is well-motivated, integrating a multiscale modeling approach to connect molecular-level conformational changes to a physiological phenomenon (LDA). The central hypothesis is clearly stated, and the authors have made a commendable effort to validate their phosphomimetic approach with explicit phosphorylation simulations. However, the strength of the conclusions is currently limited by the lack of direct experimental validation and the inherent uncertainties in the computational models used. The link between the predicted conformational changes and the functional outcome in the contraction model is somewhat indirect and relies on several assumptions. While the work is of high interest to the cardiac biophysics community, the case would be significantly strengthened by addressing the technical concerns and providing a more direct connection to experimental data.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** This work will be of significant interest to researchers in the fields of cardiac biophysics, muscle physiology, and computational biology. Specifically, those studying the molecular mechanisms of length-dependent activation, the role of intrinsically disordered proteins in the sarcomere, and the structural and functional consequences of post-translational modifications on myofilament proteins will find this study highly relevant. The multiscale modeling approach, combining MD simulations with a systems-level model of contraction, will also appeal to computational biologists interested in bridging molecular and physiological scales.
- **Major strengths**
    1.  The study addresses a significant and challenging problem: understanding the functional role of IDRs in a myofilament protein. By focusing on ABLIM1, the authors tackle a protein that is poorly understood but potentially important in cardiac mechanosignaling.
    2.  The multiscale modeling strategy is a major strength. The combination of atomistic MD, coarse-grained Martini simulations, docking, and a state-based model of contraction is a sophisticated and appropriate approach to bridge molecular details with cellular function.
    3.  The authors are careful in their methodology, notably by validating their phosphomimetic (S→D) results with simulations using an explicitly phosphorylated serine (pSer). This is a critical control that strengthens the reliability of their findings.
    4.  The manuscript is generally well-written and logically structured, making a complex computational study accessible to a broader audience.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes)
        - **Axis** Validity of central claim
        - **Claim pointer** The central claim is that phosphorylation of ABLIM1's IDRs modulates its interaction with titin's Z1Z2 segment, and that this mechanism explains the reduced length-dependent activation observed in heart failure.
        - **Evidence pointer** Sections 4.4, 4.5, 5.1, and Figure 10, 11.
        - **Concern** The manuscript establishes a correlation between phosphorylation, conformational changes, and altered binding in silico. However, the direct causal link to the physiological phenotype of reduced LDA is not experimentally demonstrated. The authors use a state-based model (MATMyoSim) to show that a reduction in passive tension can lead to reduced LDA, but they do not directly demonstrate that the specific molecular mechanism they propose (ABLIM1 phosphorylation altering titin interaction) is the operative one in the context of heart failure. The connection from the molecular dynamics predictions to the whole-cell model is a logical inference, not a direct measurement.
        - **Why it matters** The title and abstract promise a mechanism for GSK3β-driven modulation of cardiac function. Without direct experimental evidence (e.g., a mutant ABLIM1 that cannot be phosphorylated, or a direct binding assay showing altered affinity), the claim remains a hypothesis, albeit a well-supported one. The current evidence is insufficient to establish the proposed mechanism as the definitive cause of the observed physiological changes.
        - **Resolution test** The central claim would be established if the authors could provide experimental data, such as (1) a direct binding assay (e.g., ITC, SPR) showing that phosphorylated ABLIM1 has a different affinity for the Z1Z2 segment than the unphosphorylated form, or (2) a functional experiment in a cellular or tissue model where a phospho-null or phospho-mimetic ABLIM1 mutant is expressed and the effect on LDA is measured.
    - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No)
        - **Axis** Technical soundness of computational models
        - **Claim pointer** The claim that the coarse-grained (CG) Martini model is a faithful representation of the atomistic system, particularly regarding the conformational ensembles of the IDRs.
        - **Evidence pointer** Sections 3.5, 4.3, and Figure 7.
        - **Concern** The manuscript states that the CG models show a weakening of secondary structure alignment and that Rg values are considerably smaller than in atomistic simulations. While the authors attribute this to differences in bead masses and positions, this discrepancy raises a question about the quantitative accuracy of the CG model for studying the subtle conformational changes in IDRs that are central to the paper's hypothesis. The validation of the CG model appears to be based on qualitative similarity (e.g., both show increased extension upon phosphorylation) rather than a rigorous quantitative match.
        - **Why it matters** The conclusions about the conformational ensembles and the resulting end-to-end distances, which are critical for the proposed mechanism, are largely derived from the CG simulations. If the CG model is not quantitatively accurate for these systems, the specific predictions about the distances and probabilities of interaction could be unreliable.
        - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a more detailed quantitative comparison between the CG and atomistic simulations for the same constructs, perhaps by comparing the distributions of specific inter-domain distances or the radius of gyration of the IDRs. If a direct comparison is not feasible, a sensitivity analysis showing that the conclusions are robust to the choice of model parameters would be helpful.
    - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No)
        - **Axis** Support for a key assumption
        - **Claim pointer** The assumption that the villin headpiece of ABLIM1 binds to actin at a location that allows the LIM domains to reach the Z1Z2 segment on an adjacent thin filament.
        - **Evidence pointer** Section 4.5 and Figure 1.
        - **Concern** The model's relevance depends on the geometric feasibility of the proposed tethering. The authors assume a specific binding location for the villin domain and a specific orientation of ABLIM1. While they calculate the probability of the IDR spanning the inter-filament distance, they do not provide evidence that this tethering geometry is correct or that the LIM domains would be sterically able to interact with the Z1Z2 segment in the context of the intact sarcomere.
        - **Why it matters** If the proposed geometry is incorrect, the entire model of how ABLIM1 modulates titin interactions and passive tension would be invalid. The calculation of the probability of spanning the distance is only meaningful if the starting point (the villin binding site) and the target (the Z1Z2 segment) are correctly identified.
        - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a more detailed structural model of the sarcomere Z-disk, showing the relative positions of actin, titin, and ABLIM1. They could also discuss or model the potential steric constraints of the system. A clearer justification for the chosen geometry, perhaps based on existing structural data, is needed.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity and Reproducibility, Affected Element: Methods Section 3.4, Evidence Pointer: Section 3.4)
        - **Issue** The methods section for atomistic simulations is somewhat brief. The text mentions the use of the CHARMM36m force field and GROMACS, but the description of the equilibration protocol is incomplete. It is unclear what the "final equilibration step" entails and how long the production runs were for the initial atomistic simulations of the isolated domains.
        - **Required correction** Please provide a more detailed step-by-step description of the simulation protocol, including the length of each step, the number of steps, and the specific parameters used for the final equilibration and production runs.
    - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Presentation of Results, Affected Element: Section 4.5, Evidence Pointer: Figure 11)
        - **Issue** The text in section 4.5 is difficult to follow. The values for ⟨r_e⟩ are presented in a somewhat confusing manner, with different values mentioned for the "full-length C-terminal cases" (340 and 230 Å) that do not seem to directly correspond to the earlier values of 311 and 276 Å from the extrapolated fits.
        - **Required correction** Please clarify the relationship between the different ⟨r_e⟩ values presented. A table summarizing the constructs, their lengths, the simulation-derived parameters, and the extrapolated values would greatly improve clarity.
    - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Interpretation of Results, Affected Element: Section 4.2, Evidence Pointer: Figure 6)
        - **Issue** The statement that a weak hydrogen bond (approximately 4.5 Å between residues D92 and K132) may explain the alignment of beta sheets is speculative. A distance of 4.5 Å is quite long for a hydrogen bond and may not be a significant stabilizing interaction.
        - **Required correction** Please either provide more evidence for this interaction (e.g., from a trajectory analysis showing the persistence of this interaction) or temper the claim, suggesting it as a possible but not definitive explanation.
    - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Literature Context, Affected Element: Section 5.2, Evidence Pointer: Section 5.2)
        - **Issue** The discussion of ABLIM1's binding partners is interesting but somewhat brief. The authors mention a potential association with ion channels but do not elaborate on the potential functional significance of this interaction in the context of the heart.
        - **Required correction** Consider expanding this section to discuss the potential implications of the ABLIM1-ion channel interaction for cardiac electrophysiology and mechano-electric coupling, even if it is speculative.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
    - The lack of direct experimental validation for the central claim (R1-M1) is the primary technical failing. The computational evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.
    - The quantitative accuracy of the CG model for studying the subtle conformational changes in IDRs is not fully established (R1-M2).
    - The geometric model of ABLIM1 tethering in the sarcomere is based on an assumption that is not rigorously tested (R1-M3).

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the proposed mechanism "provides a mechanism that elucidates previous findings of diminished length-dependent activation" is not fully supported by the evidence presented. The model shows a plausible link, but it does not rule out other mechanisms.
- The claim that the phosphomimetic (S→D) mutations are a reliable surrogate for phosphorylation is supported by the authors' own validation simulations, but the limitations of this approach are acknowledged. The claim that the pSer simulations "confirm" the phosphomimetic findings is an overstatement, as they are also computational predictions.
- The potential functional significance of the predicted ABLIM1-ion channel interaction is speculative and not supported by any data in this manuscript.
- The statement that the protein "likely spans the lattice spacing in the z-disk of the sarcomere in a phosphorylation-dependent manner" is a prediction based on extrapolated models and is not directly measured. The probability calculations are based on assumptions about the IDR behavior that may not hold in the complex sarcomere environment.

## Assessment against Nature-style criteria
- **Originality:** The work is original in its specific focus on ABLIM1 and its integration of multiscale modeling to address a specific question about IDR function in the sarcomere. While the general concept of phosphorylation modulating IDR conformation is not new, its application to this specific protein and its connection to LDA is novel.
- **Scientific importance:** The question of how IDRs and their post-translational modifications regulate muscle contraction is of high importance. The study addresses a fundamental gap in our understanding of sarcomere biology and could have implications for understanding heart failure. However, the importance is currently tempered by the lack of experimental validation.
- **Interdisciplinary readership:** The topic is inherently interdisciplinary, bridging structural biology, biophysics, and cardiac physiology. The multiscale approach will be of interest to a broad readership, but the heavy reliance on computational methods may limit its accessibility to purely experimental biologists.
- **Technical soundness:** The computational methodology is generally sound and state-of-the-art. The use of multiple simulation techniques and the validation of phosphomimetics are strengths. However, the concerns about the quantitative accuracy of the CG model and the indirect link to the physiological model are significant weaknesses.
- **Readability for nonspecialists:** The manuscript is well-written and does a good job of explaining complex concepts. The figures, if they are as described in the text, would likely aid in understanding. However, the level of detail in the methods and results sections may be challenging for a non-specialist in computational biology.

## Recommendation posture
**Supportive if technical concerns are resolved.** The manuscript presents a novel and potentially important hypothesis with a sophisticated computational approach. However, the central claim is not fully established from the provided evidence. The authors must address the major concerns, particularly the need for experimental validation or a more direct link to the physiological data, and clarify the technical details of their models before the case can be considered complete.
