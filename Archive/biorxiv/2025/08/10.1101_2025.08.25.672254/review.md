## Review setup

- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use graph neural networks to predict protein binding site residues in four eukaryotic model organisms. They report that an 8 Å cutoff for graph construction is optimal, that larger cutoffs introduce noise from non-binding site residues, that hybrid cutoffs may enhance performance, that sequence-based features from a pre-trained protein language model are informative, and that structure-based features from backbone geometry are inadequate due to overprediction. They further claim cross-organism consistency as evidence for evolutionary conservation of binding site residues.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no quantitative results, model architecture details, dataset descriptions, or statistical analyses are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, model specifications, hyperparameters, dataset composition and splits, baseline comparisons, statistical significance tests, and any supplementary information

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a plausible and potentially useful investigation into graph construction parameters and feature types for protein binding site prediction. The central claims are coherent and the cross-species design is a reasonable approach. However, the abstract provides no quantitative evidence, no baseline comparisons, and no methodological detail sufficient to evaluate the validity or generalizability of the findings. The claim of evolutionary conservation is particularly under-supported, as no comparative analysis or statistical test is described. The work may be of interest to the computational structural biology community, but the case is not established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists and bioinformaticians working on protein function prediction, graph neural network applications in structural biology, and feature engineering for binding site annotation. Researchers developing deep learning methods for molecular interaction prediction would also find the comparison of sequence-based and structure-based features relevant, as would those interested in parameter sensitivity in graph construction for protein graphs.
- **Major strengths** The investigation addresses a practical and under-explored question, namely the sensitivity of graph neural network performance to cutoff distance in protein residue graphs. The use of four eukaryotic model organisms adds a comparative dimension that is often missing in such studies. The distinction between sequence-based and structure-based features is a useful framing, and the finding that language model embeddings carry substantial binding information aligns with broader trends in the field.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** "sparse graphs generated by an 8 Å cutoff are most effective for binding site residue prediction"
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract states that an 8 Å cutoff is most effective but provides no quantitative performance metrics, no comparison against other cutoffs, and no statistical significance assessment. It is unclear what metric was optimized, how large the performance differences were, and whether the result is robust across the four organisms or only an average trend.
  - **Why it matters** The central methodological recommendation of the paper depends entirely on this claim. Without effect sizes, variance estimates, or significance tests, a reader cannot judge whether the 8 Å preference is meaningful or an artifact of a particular dataset or model configuration.
  - **Resolution test** Provide performance tables or figures showing metrics for each cutoff value across all four organisms, including standard deviations or confidence intervals, and a statistical test comparing the 8 Å cutoff against alternatives.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- The claim that an 8 Å cutoff is most effective is unsupported by any quantitative comparison.
- The claim that larger cutoffs introduce noise from non-binding site residues is presented as a mechanism but is not demonstrated.
- The claim that hybrid cutoffs have potential for enhancing performance is speculative and not defined.
- The claim that sequence-based features carry substantial binding information is unsupported by any ablation or feature importance analysis.
- The claim that structure-based features are inadequate due to overprediction is unsupported by any precision or false positive analysis.
- The claim of evolutionary conservation of binding site residues is an interpretive leap that is not supported by any conservation or comparative genomic analysis.
