## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods) as provided
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental logic, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the provided text; no external data or supplementary figures were available for assessment
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The leader (L) proteins of Theiler's virus and Boone cardiovirus contain two short linear motifs (SLiMs): a previously described DDVF motif that binds RSK kinases, and a newly identified "M-acidic" motif that targets the L-RSK complex to the nuclear pore complex (NPC) via interaction with RAE1. The combination of these two SLiMs enables the retargeting of RSK kinases to the NPC, leading to FG-nucleoporin hyperphosphorylation and disruption of nucleocytoplasmic trafficking.
- **Visible evidence base** Figures 1-6 (described in text), including split-GFP localization assays, alanine-scanning mutagenesis, co-immunoprecipitation experiments, competition assays, and AlphaFold2 multimer predictions; methods sections for cell culture, plasmid construction, virus production, and microscopy
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Actual figure images and data panels were not provided; supplementary tables (Table A1) and detailed statistical analyses were not available; raw data for quantification of microscopy experiments were not accessible; AlphaFold prediction parameters and confidence scores were not fully described

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a well-structured and conceptually interesting study identifying a second SLiM in cardiovirus L proteins that mediates NPC targeting through RAE1 interaction. The work builds logically on prior findings and uses complementary approaches (localization assays, mutagenesis, competition experiments, and structural predictions) to support the main conclusions. However, several technical concerns and gaps in evidence need to be addressed before the case is fully established, particularly regarding the strength of the co-immunoprecipitation data, the interpretation of competition experiments, and the generalizability of the AlphaFold predictions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Virologists studying virus-host interactions, particularly those focused on picornaviruses and nuclear trafficking; cell biologists interested in SLiM-mediated protein interactions and NPC function; researchers studying convergent evolution of viral immune evasion strategies; structural biologists using AlphaFold for protein interaction predictions.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clear and important question: how viral proteins retarget host kinases to the NPC. The identification of a second SLiM that acts combinatorially with the DDVF motif is conceptually novel and provides a mechanistic framework for understanding L protein function. The use of multiple experimental approaches (split-GFP, alanine scanning, co-IP, competition assays) strengthens the conclusions. The evolutionary perspective, linking the M-acidic SLiM to other viral proteins (ORF6, ORF10, M protein), adds broader significance. The AlphaFold predictions provide testable hypotheses for future work.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The co-immunoprecipitation data for TMEV L and RAE1 interaction are not detected, yet the authors conclude that TMEV L also uses the M-acidic SLiM. This is a significant gap in direct evidence.
  - R1-M2: The competition experiments between L and ORF6 for RAE1 binding are described but the quantitative data and controls are not fully presented, making it difficult to assess the strength of the competition.
  - R1-M3: The AlphaFold predictions are used to support the interaction model, but the confidence metrics (e.g., pLDDT, PAE scores) and the criteria for selecting the predicted models are not described, limiting the interpretability of these results.
  - R1-M4: The functional significance of the M-acidic SLiM in the context of viral infection is not directly tested. The authors show that mutations in the motif affect NPC localization and trafficking disruption, but the impact on viral replication or pathogenesis is not addressed.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The description of the split-GFP system and the selection of the HeLa S1-10 cell clone could be clarified; the text mentions "low amount of fluorescence in normal conditions" but does not specify how this was quantified.
  - R1-m2: The statement that "the leader protein of EMCV was shown to map around the nuclear envelope" in the discussion cites reference 39, but the relevance of this to the current findings could be more explicitly stated.
  - R1-m3: The authors mention that "transfected cells expressing higher protein fusion levels had fluorescence throughout the cell," but the implications of this observation for the interpretation of the localization data are not discussed.
  - R1-m4: The methods for the alanine-scanning mutagenesis are described, but the specific residues mutated and the rationale for choosing them (beyond the alanine scan) could be more clearly presented.
  - R1-m5: The authors state that "BCV and TMEV L proteins use a combination of two independent SLiMs," but the independence of the two motifs is not experimentally demonstrated; it is possible that the M-acidic motif function depends on prior RSK binding.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 
  - Lack of direct co-immunoprecipitation evidence for TMEV L-RAE1 interaction (R1-M1)
  - Incomplete presentation of competition experiment data and controls (R1-M2)
  - Insufficient description of AlphaFold prediction methodology and confidence metrics (R1-M3)
  - Absence of functional assays linking the M-acidic SLiM to viral replication or pathogenesis (R1-M4)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: High. The identification of a second SLiM in cardiovirus L proteins that targets the NPC via RAE1 is novel and extends the understanding of viral SLiM mimicry.
  - Scientific importance: Moderate to high. The findings have implications for understanding viral immune evasion and nuclear trafficking disruption, but the direct relevance to human disease is not fully established.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: Moderate. The work will appeal to virologists, cell biologists, and structural biologists, but may be too specialized for a broader audience.
  - Technical soundness: Moderate. The experimental approaches are generally appropriate, but the gaps in direct evidence (particularly for TMEV L) and the incomplete presentation of some data weaken the technical case.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: Good. The manuscript is clearly written and the concepts are explained well, though some sections (e.g., AlphaFold predictions) may be challenging for nonspecialists.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core hypothesis is interesting and the data are largely consistent with the conclusions, but the lack of direct evidence for TMEV L-RAE1 interaction and the incomplete presentation of some experiments need to be addressed before the case is fully established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that TMEV L interacts with RAE1 via the M-acidic SLiM is not directly supported by co-immunoprecipitation data; the authors rely on indirect evidence (competition experiments and AlphaFold predictions).
- The statement that "BCV and TMEV L proteins use a combination of two independent SLiMs" is not fully supported, as the independence of the two motifs is not experimentally tested.
- The prediction that the M-acidic SLiM occurs in other viral proteins (e.g., RVFV NSs) and cellular proteins (NXF1) is based solely on AlphaFold predictions and has not been experimentally validated.
- The claim that "L proteins thus use a combination of 2 SLiMs, both being shared by other pathogens, to retarget RSKs toward the NPC" is a reasonable synthesis, but the functional significance of this combinatorial mechanism in the context of viral infection is not directly demonstrated.
