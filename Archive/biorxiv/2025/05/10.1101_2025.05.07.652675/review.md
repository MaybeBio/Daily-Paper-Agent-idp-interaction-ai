## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods, supplemental figure/table legends)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, experimental design, data interpretation, and consistency between results and conclusions as presented in the provided text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that ZapC, a monomeric FtsZ-associated protein, crosslinks FtsZ filaments through a dual-binding mechanism: one site on the globular domain of FtsZ and a second site on the C-terminal peptide (CCTP). They further propose that the intrinsically disordered C-terminal linker (CTL) modulates this interaction, and that this modulatory role extends to other FtsZ-binding proteins.
- **Visible evidence base** Genetic screen for FtsZ mutants resistant to ZapC overexpression; localization assays (ZapC-GFP, ZapA-mCherry); bacterial two-hybrid (BTH) assays; in vitro co-sedimentation assays; negative stain electron microscopy; biolayer interferometry (BLI) binding measurements; AlphaFold 3 structural model
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full figures and supplemental figures/tables (only legends provided); numerical data for BLI binding curves and Kd values (only summary values in text); detailed strain/plasmid construction protocols; raw microscopy images; AlphaFold 3 model confidence metrics and coordinates

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a significant and unresolved question in bacterial cell division: how the monomeric protein ZapC crosslinks FtsZ filaments. The authors present a compelling combination of genetic, biochemical, and structural prediction data supporting a dual-binding model. The identification of mutations in three distinct regions of FtsZ (globular domain, CTL, CCTP) that confer resistance to ZapC toxicity is a strong starting point. The integration of in vivo localization, BTH, and in vitro binding data provides convergent evidence. However, several concerns limit the strength of the conclusions as currently presented. The claim that the CTL modulates binding is based on indirect evidence (mutations in the CTL affecting binding) and the proposed mechanism by which the CTL exerts this effect is speculative. The AlphaFold 3 model is used as a central piece of evidence, but no confidence metrics or validation beyond genetic correlation is provided. The in vitro BLI data, while supportive, are presented with limited quantitative detail. The finding that the CTL affects binding of the CCTP to multiple partners is intriguing but the mechanistic basis remains unclear. Overall, the work is potentially important and novel, but the case for the specific dual-binding mechanism and the role of the CTL would be strengthened by additional experimental validation and clearer presentation of quantitative data.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in bacterial cell division, cytoskeletal biology, and prokaryotic cell biology. The work is also relevant to those studying intrinsically disordered proteins and their role in modulating protein-protein interactions, as well as those interested in the application of AlphaFold-based structural prediction to biological problems. The findings may have broader implications for understanding how monomeric crosslinkers organize cytoskeletal filaments.
- **Major strengths** 1. The genetic screen is a powerful and unbiased approach to identify residues important for the FtsZ-ZapC interaction. 2. The combination of in vivo (localization, BTH) and in vitro (sedimentation, EM, BLI) methods provides strong convergent evidence for the involvement of the identified residues. 3. The proposed dual-binding mechanism is a novel and testable model that reconciles previously contradictory observations in the literature. 4. The observation that the CTL modulates binding to multiple partners is a potentially significant and general finding. 5. The use of AlphaFold 3 to generate a testable structural model is timely and appropriate.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence strength for the dual-binding mechanism
  - **Claim pointer** The central claim that ZapC crosslinks FtsZ filaments by simultaneously binding to the globular domain of one FtsZ molecule and the CCTP of another.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results sections "ZapC resistant FtsZ mutations are located in the globular domain, CTL and CCTP of FtsZ", "ZapC resistant FtsZ mutations reduce the interaction between FtsZ and ZapC in vivo", "ZapC resistant FtsZ mutations reduce the interaction between ZapC and FtsZ in vitro", "Structural model of the FtsZ-ZapC complex indicates that ZapC binds to the globular domain and the CCTP of FtsZ"
  - **Concern** The dual-binding model is inferred from the location of resistance mutations and the AlphaFold 3 model. While the data show that mutations in the globular domain and CCTP weaken the interaction, they do not directly demonstrate that a single ZapC monomer binds both sites simultaneously on two different FtsZ molecules. The AlphaFold 3 model is presented as supporting evidence, but no confidence metrics, model validation, or experimental testing of the predicted interface (beyond the point mutations) is provided. The in vitro crosslinking data (sedimentation, EM) show that ZapC promotes bundling of wild-type FtsZ, but the effect on the double mutant is only shown for one combination (F285S, A376T). It is not clear if the model is the only possible explanation for the data.
  - **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism is the central novel claim of the manuscript. If the dual-binding model is not directly supported, the work reduces to a collection of interaction-defective mutants without a clear mechanistic framework. The model has implications for how other monomeric crosslinkers might function.
  - **Resolution test** Provide direct evidence for simultaneous binding, for example, by demonstrating that a ZapC monomer can bridge two different FtsZ fragments (one containing the globular domain, one containing the CCTP) in a ternary complex (e.g., by size-exclusion chromatography, pull-down, or single-molecule imaging). Alternatively, provide a higher-resolution structure or crosslinking mass spectrometry data that unambiguously shows the two interfaces engaged simultaneously. Present AlphaFold 3 confidence metrics (ipTM, pTM, per-residue pLDDT) and ideally validate the model with additional mutations at the predicted interface.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Role of the CTL
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that the intrinsically disordered CTL modulates the interaction of the CCTP with ZapC and other binding partners.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "ZapC resistant FtsZ mutations reduce the interaction between FtsZ and ZapC in vivo", "ZapC resistant FtsZ mutations reduce the interaction between ZapC and FtsZ in vitro", "The globular domain and the CCTP of FtsZ bind to ZapC directly which are modulated by the CTL", Discussion
  - **Concern** The evidence that the CTL modulates binding is indirect. Mutations in the CTL (I323L, D360Y) confer resistance to ZapC toxicity and reduce binding in some assays, but the mechanism is not explored. The authors state that the CTL "affects the interaction indirectly" and suggest it may alter the "property or configuration" of the CCTP, but no direct evidence for this is provided. The BLI data with truncated constructs (FtsZ 1-370 vs FtsZ 1-330 vs FtsZ 1-316) show that removing the CTL and CCTP reduces affinity, but this does not distinguish between a direct binding role for the CTL and an indirect conformational effect. The claim that the CTL affects binding to "many other FtsZ binding proteins" is based on a single BTH experiment (Fig. 9) with three partners, and the physiological relevance of this effect is not established.
  - **Why it matters** The role of the CTL is presented as a major finding with broad implications for FtsZ biology. If the effect is indirect and non-specific, the significance is diminished. The claim that the CTL is a general modulator of CCTP interactions is a strong statement that requires more direct evidence.
  - **Resolution test** Provide direct evidence for the mechanism of CTL action. For example, use chimeric or deletion constructs to show that the CTL from a different species can functionally replace the E. coli CTL. Use NMR or other biophysical methods to show that the CTL affects the conformational ensemble of the CCTP. Test whether the effect of the CTL on binding is specific to ZapC or general by testing a wider panel of CCTP-binding proteins. Quantify the effect of CTL mutations on binding affinity in a more systematic manner.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Quantitative data presentation
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that wild-type FtsZ binds ZapC with a Kd of 0.39 μM and that mutants show reduced affinity.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "ZapC resistant FtsZ mutations reduce the interaction between ZapC and FtsZ in vitro", Figure 4, Table S4
  - **Concern** The BLI data are presented as Kd values in the text and a table, but the actual binding curves, the number of replicates, the error estimates, and the fitting method are not described in sufficient detail. The Kd values for the mutants are described as "1.5-2 times higher" or "16-fold reduced" but the absolute values and errors are not given in the main text. The statement that FtsZ 1-316 and FtsZ 1-330 show a "different binding profile" is vague. Without proper quantitative analysis, it is difficult to assess the robustness of the affinity differences.
  - **Why it matters** The quantitative binding data are a key piece of evidence for the differential roles of the globular domain and CCTP. If the affinity differences are not robust or are within experimental error, the conclusions are weakened.
  - **Resolution test** Provide representative BLI sensorgrams, the number of independent experiments, and the standard errors for all Kd values. Show the fitting curves and residuals. For the truncated constructs, provide a more detailed analysis of the binding kinetics (kon, koff) to support the claim of a "different binding profile."
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity of presentation
  - **Affected element** Figure 1 and Table S2
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Identification of FtsZ mutations providing resistance to ZapC overexpression toxicity"
  - **Issue** The text states that 13 mutant strains grew on plates with 60 μM IPTG, but then says "a total of 10 mutations in FtsZ exhibit varying degrees of resistance." It is unclear if some strains had multiple mutations and were excluded, or if some mutations did not confer resistance when tested individually.
  - **Required correction** Clarify the relationship between the 13 strains and the 10 mutations. State explicitly how many strains had single vs. multiple mutations and how the causative mutation was identified in each case.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Experimental detail
  - **Affected element** BTH assay
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "ZapC resistant FtsZ mutations reduce the interaction between FtsZ and ZapC in vivo", Figure 2B
  - **Issue** The BTH assay is described as using a specialized strain (LYA1) with the ftsA* mutation, but the rationale for this strain is only briefly mentioned. The reader is not told how the ftsA* mutation affects the assay or whether it could influence the interaction being measured.
  - **Required correction** Provide a brief explanation for why the ftsA* mutation was needed and whether control experiments were performed to ensure it does not artifactually affect the FtsZ-ZapC interaction.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Interpretation of structural model
  - **Affected element** AlphaFold 3 model
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Structural model of the FtsZ-ZapC complex indicates that ZapC binds to the globular domain and the CCTP of FtsZ"
  - **Issue** The description of the model is qualitative. The authors state that the CCTP binds to a hydrophobic pocket in the N-terminal domain of ZapC and that the loop region of ZapC binds to a pocket on FtsZ, but no details on the predicted interface size, the number of contacts, or the confidence of these predictions are given.
  - **Required correction** Provide the AlphaFold 3 confidence metrics (ipTM, pTM, pLDDT) and a more detailed description of the predicted interfaces. If possible, show a figure with the model and highlight the key residues.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Generalizability of CTL effect
  - **Affected element** Figure 9
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "The globular domain and the CCTP of FtsZ bind to ZapC directly which are modulated by the CTL"
  - **Issue** The claim that the CTL affects binding to "many other FtsZ binding proteins" is based on a single BTH experiment with FtsA, SlmA, and MinC. It is not clear if these are the only partners tested or if a broader screen was performed.
  - **Required correction** Clarify the scope of the experiment. If only these three partners were tested, soften the claim to "several other FtsZ binding proteins" and discuss the possibility that the effect may not be universal.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1. Lack of direct evidence for simultaneous binding of a single ZapC monomer to two FtsZ molecules (R1-M1). 2. Lack of mechanistic insight into how the CTL modulates CCTP binding (R1-M2). 3. Insufficient quantitative detail for BLI binding data (R1-M3). 4. No confidence metrics or validation for the AlphaFold 3 model. 5. The relationship between the 13 resistant strains and the 10 identified mutations is unclear.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** **Originality:** The proposed dual-binding mechanism for a monomeric crosslinker is novel and challenges the prevailing view that crosslinking requires multimeric adaptors. The finding that the CTL modulates CCTP interactions is also original and has implications beyond ZapC. **Scientific importance:** The work addresses a fundamental question in bacterial cell division and provides a mechanistic framework that could be applicable to other systems. The potential general role of the CTL in regulating FtsZ interactions is of broad significance. **Interdisciplinary readership:** The topic is of primary interest to microbiologists and cell biologists. The use of AlphaFold 3 and biophysical methods may attract readers from structural biology and biophysics, but the presentation is likely to be most accessible to specialists in bacterial cell division. **Technical soundness:** The genetic screen and the combination of in vivo and in vitro assays are technically sound. However, the lack of direct evidence for the dual-binding mechanism and the limited quantitative analysis of binding data are weaknesses. The reliance on an unvalidated AlphaFold 3 model is a concern. **Readability for nonspecialists:** The manuscript is generally well-written and the logic is clear. However, the results section is dense and would benefit from a summary figure that illustrates the proposed model and the location of the mutations. The discussion is somewhat speculative in places, particularly regarding the role of the CTL.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core finding, that mutations in the globular domain, CTL, and CCTP of FtsZ affect ZapC binding, is well-supported. However, the central mechanistic claim of dual-binding by a monomeric ZapC requires direct experimental validation. The role of the CTL, while intriguing, needs a clearer mechanistic basis. If the authors can provide direct evidence for the dual-binding model and strengthen the quantitative analysis, this would be a significant contribution. Currently, the case is not fully established from the provided evidence.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that ZapC crosslinks FtsZ filaments by binding to the globular domain of one filament and the CCTP of another is not directly supported by the data. It is a plausible model based on the AlphaFold 3 prediction and the location of resistance mutations, but no experiment directly demonstrates this bridging function.
- The claim that the CTL modulates the interaction of the CCTP with ZapC and other partners is supported by indirect evidence (mutations in the CTL affecting binding), but the mechanism is unknown. The statement that the CTL "affects the property or configuration of the CCTP" is speculative.
- The claim that the CTL affects binding to "many other FtsZ binding proteins" is an overstatement based on the data presented, which only tests three partners.
- The AlphaFold 3 model is presented as a key piece of evidence, but its accuracy is not assessed. No confidence metrics are provided, and the model is not validated by independent methods.
- The statement that FtsZ 1-316 and FtsZ 1-330 show a "different binding profile" in the BLI assay is vague and not supported by a detailed kinetic analysis.
