## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a fundamental question in developmental and evolutionary biology, namely how regulatory networks maintain both stability and adaptability. The authors present a compelling conceptual framework linking IDP biophysics to developmental transitions and evolutionary divergence. However, the provided material is incomplete, with key results, figures, and statistical details not fully visible. The conceptual argument is intriguing but the evidentiary basis for the central claims cannot be fully assessed from the supplied text. The link between mechanosensitivity, binding plasticity, and evolutionary divergence is asserted rather than demonstrated with direct experimental evidence in the visible portions.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Evolutionary developmental biologists studying the molecular basis of phenotypic plasticity and evolvability would find this work relevant. Researchers working on intrinsically disordered proteins and their functional roles in gene regulation would also be interested. Additionally, those studying avian beak development as a model system for understanding morphogenesis and diversification would benefit from this framework. The proposed mechanism connecting physical cell states to regulatory network evolution has broad implications for understanding how developmental systems accommodate both precision and change.

- **Major strengths** The manuscript tackles a significant conceptual problem in biology, namely the reconciliation of developmental precision with evolutionary flexibility. The authors propose a mechanistically grounded hypothesis linking IDP biophysics to regulatory network behavior. The use of avian beak development as a model system is appropriate given its well-documented role in ecological diversification. The integration of cell-level physical measurements with protein structural analyses represents a multidisciplinary approach. The comparative evolutionary analyses across avian proteomes provide a broad phylogenetic perspective.

- **Major Concerns**

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "mechanosensitive IDPs mediate shifts between physical cell states via dosage-dependent binding plasticity, converting stochastic protein variation into discreet regulatory networks"
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, Figures 2 and 3, location not provided
- **Concern** The central mechanistic claim that IDPs mediate cell state transitions through dosage-dependent binding plasticity is not directly demonstrated. The visible results describe correlations between protein expression and cell morphology/jamming state, but no direct experimental manipulation of IDP dosage or binding is presented. The causal link between IDP biophysical properties and the observed network changes remains inferred rather than established.
- **Why it matters** The entire conceptual framework rests on this causal relationship. Without direct evidence that altering IDP dosage or binding plasticity produces the predicted changes in cell state transitions and network topology, the claims remain correlational.
- **Resolution test** Provide experimental evidence such as knockdown or overexpression of specific IDPs in the beak mesenchyme system, demonstrating that altering IDP levels or binding partners produces the predicted changes in cell jamming transitions and network architecture.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Statistical rigor
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "the connectivity of intrinsically disordered transcriptional regulators is tuned to cell jamming transitions" and that "patterns of connectivity in the cell state divergence matrix and population divergence matrix are remarkably congruent"
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, Figure 3, Supplementary Tables S5 and S6, location not provided
- **Concern** The statistical methods for comparing network divergence matrices are not described in sufficient detail. The claim of congruence between cell state divergence and population divergence matrices requires appropriate statistical tests (e.g., Mantel tests, Procrustes analysis) with proper null distributions. The visible methods section does not describe how matrix congruence was assessed or what statistical thresholds were applied.
- **Why it matters** The claim that the same axes define both developmental transitions and evolutionary divergence is central to the paper's thesis. Without rigorous statistical demonstration of matrix congruence, this claim cannot be evaluated.
- **Resolution test** Provide detailed description of matrix comparison methods, including null model construction, test statistics, and effect sizes. Show that the observed congruence exceeds what would be expected by chance given the phylogenetic and developmental constraints.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Interpretive validity
- **Claim pointer** The authors conclude that "disorder-enabled connectivity in these proteins resets their regulatory specialization and promotes population divergence"
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, Figures 3 and 4, Discussion, location not provided
- **Concern** The interpretation that IDP-enabled connectivity "resets" regulatory specialization and promotes divergence is not directly supported by the visible evidence. The data show correlations between IDP properties and network patterns, but the directional claim that IDPs cause population divergence requires longitudinal or experimental data. Alternative explanations, such as population divergence driving IDP evolution, are not addressed.
- **Why it matters** The causal direction is critical for the paper's evolutionary claims. If IDP evolution follows rather than precedes population divergence, the proposed mechanism would not explain evolvability.
- **Resolution test** Provide evidence for the temporal or causal ordering of IDP changes relative to population divergence, such as ancestral state reconstruction or experimental evolution approaches.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Generalizability
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that IDPs "flexibly regulate developmental pathways and reconcile precision with evolvability" as a general principle
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Discussion, location not provided
- **Concern** The study examines a single regulatory network in a single tissue type across related bird species. The generalization to a broad principle of developmental and evolutionary biology requires demonstration across multiple systems. The authors do not discuss potential limitations of their system or alternative mechanisms that might operate in other developmental contexts.
- **Why it matters** The paper's significance depends on the generality of the proposed mechanism. If the findings are specific to avian beak development, the broader claims are overstated.
- **Resolution test** Discuss the scope and limitations of the proposed mechanism, and identify testable predictions that would distinguish this mechanism from alternatives in other developmental systems.
