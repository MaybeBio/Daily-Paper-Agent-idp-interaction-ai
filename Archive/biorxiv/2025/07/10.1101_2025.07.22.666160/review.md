## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, declarations) as provided
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, and support of claims based solely on the supplied text; no supplementary figures, tables, or data files were available for inspection
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that alphaherpesvirus pUL21 homologues bind protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) via a non-canonical motif (TROPPO) that occupies the same hydrophobic groove as cellular RVxF motifs, that this interaction is of modest affinity, and that the suboptimal sequence is evolutionarily maintained to fine-tune phosphatase recruitment during infection
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract; Introduction; Methods sections for plasmids, peptides, protein purification, ITC, fluorescence polarisation, cell culture, immunoprecipitation, and structure prediction; Results sections describing peptide binding, deletion analysis, structural models, and mutagenesis; Discussion; funding and data availability statements
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures and figure legends; all supplementary tables (including Table S1 referenced for ITC conditions); supplementary figures (Fig. S1, Fig. S2); raw biophysical data; AlphaFold model files and validation metrics beyond pLDDT/ipTM values; experimental details for ITC fitting; statistical analysis details for immunoprecipitation quantification

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a timely and interesting question in viral hijacking of host phosphatase regulation. The authors combine structural prediction, targeted mutagenesis, and biophysical competition assays to argue that the TROPPO motif of pUL21 and pORF38 binds the canonical RVxF groove on PP1 despite lacking sequence similarity to known motifs. The central claim is plausible and the mutagenesis data appear internally consistent with the predicted model. However, the evidence base as presented is incomplete. Key quantitative data (ITC thermograms, competition curves, binding affinities for the TROPPO peptides) are referenced but not shown in the supplied text. The inability of TROPPO peptides alone to bind PP1, while full-length pORF38-NLT binds with approximately 500 nM affinity, raises questions about the minimal binding unit and the role of the N-terminal domain that are not fully resolved. The evolutionary argument for suboptimal motif maintenance is speculative and would benefit from direct functional testing. Overall, the work is potentially significant for the virology and phosphatase biology communities, but the case is not fully established from the provided material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Virologists studying herpesvirus replication and host-pathogen interactions, particularly those focused on viral modulation of phosphorylation signalling. Biochemists and structural biologists interested in short linear motif recognition and protein phosphatase regulation. Researchers studying PP1 biology and the diversity of PP1-binding motifs. The finding that viruses use non-canonical motifs to compete with cellular adaptors has broader implications for understanding how pathogens rewire host signalling networks.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clear gap in understanding of a biologically important viral-host interaction. The combination of AlphaFold2 predictions with targeted mutagenesis provides a testable structural hypothesis. The competition experiments directly demonstrate that pORF38-NLT competes with a canonical RVxF-containing peptide for PP1 binding, which is a key mechanistic point. The observation that TROPPO peptides alone do not bind PP1, while the NLT construct does, suggests an interesting cooperativity between the N-terminal domain and the motif that is worth pursuing. The evolutionary discussion is thoughtful and raises testable hypotheses about the selective pressures on viral phosphatase recruitment.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence completeness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that TROPPO-containing peptides do not bind PP1 based on ITC experiments showing no heat above background, and that pORF38-NLT binds PP1 with 497 ± 143 nM affinity based on ITC.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "The TROPPO motif alone is insufficient to bind PP1"; Figure 1C,D; Table S1
  - **Concern** The ITC data supporting the key claim that TROPPO peptides do not bind PP1 are not shown in the supplied text. No thermograms, binding isotherms, or fitted parameters are presented. The statement that "titrating high concentrations (500–1000 µM) of pUL21 or pORF38 TROPPO-containing peptides against the PP1 catalytic domain did not evolve heats above background levels" is a negative result that requires careful demonstration of assay sensitivity and positive controls. Similarly, the ITC data for pORF38-NLT binding PP1 with approximately 500 nM affinity are referenced but not displayed. Without seeing the raw data, the quality of the fits, or the stoichiometry, the reliability of this affinity measurement cannot be assessed.
  - **Why it matters** The inability of TROPPO peptides alone to bind PP1 is a central observation that motivates the structural model involving the N-terminal domain. If this negative result is not robustly demonstrated, the interpretation that the N-terminal domain contributes to binding could be undermined. The affinity measurement for pORF38-NLT is also critical for the competition experiments and for the argument that the interaction is of modest affinity.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the ITC thermograms and fitted binding isotherms for all experiments, including the peptide titrations and the pORF38-NLT titration. Include a positive control demonstrating that the ITC setup can detect binding of a known PP1 ligand. Report stoichiometry (N values) and the full set of thermodynamic parameters for the pORF38-NLT interaction.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Claim support
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "competition fluorescence polarisation confirms that pUL21 competes directly with cellular PP1 RvXF motifs for PP1 binding, albeit with lower affinity" and that pORF38-NLT competes with an IC50 of 417 ± 11 nM.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Alphaherpesvirus proteins compete directly with cellular regulatory proteins for PP1 binding"; Figure 5D,F
  - **Concern** The competition fluorescence polarisation data are described but not shown. The claim that pUL21 is "unable to out-compete GADD34 for binding to PP1" is a negative result that requires demonstration that the assay has sufficient dynamic range and that the full-length pUL21 protein is properly folded and functional. The buffer change for pUL21 experiments (Tris pH 8.5 versus HEPES pH 8) introduces a variable that could affect the comparison. The IC50 value for pORF38-NLT is reported, but without the competition curves, the quality of the fit and the validity of the comparison to the ITC-derived Kd cannot be evaluated.
  - **Why it matters** The competition experiments are the direct evidence that the viral proteins bind the same site as cellular RVxF motifs. If these data are not robust, the central mechanistic claim is weakened. The inability of full-length pUL21 to compete, while pORF38-NLT does, is an important difference that is not mechanistically explained.
  - **Resolution test** Show the competition curves for pORF38-NLT and full-length pUL21, including the fitted IC50 values and confidence intervals. Demonstrate that full-length pUL21 is folded and functional (e.g., by showing it can bind a known interaction partner or by SEC-MALS analysis). Address the potential effect of the buffer change on the competition results.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Model validation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "AlphaFold2-Multimer structural models suggest that the TROPPO motifs of pUL21 and of pORF38 bind the same hydrophobic groove on PP1 as RVxF and ϕϕ[xF] motifs, forming an extended β-sheet that bridges PP1 and the N-terminal domain of pUL21 or pORF38."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Structural model of the pUL21:PP1 and pORF38:PP1 complexes"; Figure 3A-D
  - **Concern** The AlphaFold2-Multimer models are described in text, but the figures showing the predicted structures are not available in the supplied material. The pLDDT and ipTM scores are reported, but these global metrics do not convey the local confidence of the interface residues. The claim that the TROPO motif forms an extended beta-sheet bridging the two proteins is a specific structural hypothesis that requires visual inspection of the model and ideally validation by additional methods (e.g., cross-linking, NMR, or co-crystallisation). The authors note that co-crystallisation attempts were unsuccessful, which is acknowledged, but the structural model is a cornerstone of the paper.
  - **Why it matters** The structural model underpins the mutagenesis strategy and the interpretation of the results. If the model is incorrect in key details, the mutagenesis data could be misinterpreted. The field would benefit from a clearer presentation of the model quality at the interface.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the predicted structure figures with interface residues highlighted. Report per-residue pLDDT values for the interface residues. Consider validating the model with an orthogonal approach, such as hydrogen-deuterium exchange or limited proteolysis, or at minimum discuss the limitations of the model more explicitly.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Evolutionary claim support
  - **Claim pointer** The authors conclude that "the conservation of suboptimal binding residues within the region structurally equivalent to the cellular RVxF implies that the viruses have deliberately regulated PP1 binding affinity to support optimal virus replication."
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section
  - **Concern** The evolutionary argument that the suboptimal motif is maintained by selection is plausible but not directly tested. The authors cite the existence of a second PP1-binding protein in HSV-1 (ICP34.5) with a canonical RVxF motif, and the observation that enhancing the pUL21 motif increases binding, but they do not test whether this enhanced binding is detrimental to viral fitness. The hypothesis that the low affinity is important for temporal or spatial regulation of PP1 recruitment is speculative and would require functional experiments, such as engineering a high-affinity pUL21 variant into the virus and measuring replication, spread, and phosphorylation of viral substrates.
  - **Why it matters** The evolutionary interpretation is a major conclusion of the paper and is presented as a key insight. Without direct functional testing, the claim that the suboptimal motif is "deliberately" maintained remains a hypothesis. The paper would be strengthened by acknowledging this limitation more explicitly or by including preliminary functional data.
  - **Resolution test** Perform a viral fitness experiment with a pUL21 variant carrying an optimised RVxF motif, measuring replication kinetics, spread, and phosphorylation of known pUL21/pUS3 substrates. Alternatively, temper the conclusion to state that the data are consistent with, but do not prove, selection for suboptimal binding.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity
  - **Affected element** Methods, ITC section
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Isothermal Titration Calorimetry (ITC)"
  - **Issue** The ITC methods state that "titrations were conducted at 25°C using 12 x 3 µL injections" but do not specify the concentrations of the syringe and cell contents, the stirring speed, the reference power, or the spacing between injections. The fitting model is stated as "one-site binding model" but no details on the fitting procedure or the number of replicates beyond "three independent experiments" are given.
  - **Required correction** Provide full ITC experimental parameters, including cell and syringe concentrations, injection volume and duration, spacing, stirring speed, and reference power. Specify the fitting software and model parameters. State whether the reported affinity is the mean of independent fits or a global fit.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Data presentation
  - **Affected element** Results, fluorescence polarisation
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "The TROPO motif alone is insufficient to bind PP1"; Figure 1A
  - **Issue** The Kd for the GADD34 peptide binding PP1 is reported as 144 ± 84 nM with a large standard error. The text notes this is "similar to previous ITC affinity measurements" but does not discuss the precision of this measurement or the potential sources of variability.
  - **Required correction** Discuss the variability in the Kd measurement and consider reporting the median and range or providing a confidence interval. If the variability is due to peptide solubility or aggregation, address this in the methods.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Completeness
  - **Affected element** Results, deletion mutagenesis
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "The C-terminal region of the pUL21 linker is dispensable for PP1 binding"; Figure 2B
  - **Issue** The deletion mutants pUL21ΔL, pUL21ΔR, and pUL21ΔLR are described, but the exact residues deleted are only partially specified in the text. The text states that ΔL removes residues 216-237 and ΔR removes residues 257-276, but the boundaries of the linker region and the rationale for these specific deletions are not fully explained.
  - **Required correction** Provide a schematic of the pUL21 domain structure with the deleted regions clearly marked. State the boundaries of the linker region and justify the choice of deletion endpoints.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Statistical reporting
  - **Affected element** Results, immunoprecipitation
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Site-directed mutagenesis confirms the predictive power of the pUL21:PP1 model"; Figure 4B,C,E
  - **Issue** The immunoprecipitation results are described qualitatively (e.g., "profoundly less efficient", "dramatically reduced") without quantitative analysis or statistical tests. The densitometry methods are described, but the results of the statistical tests are not reported in the text.
  - **Required correction** Report the quantitative densitometry results with statistical analysis (e.g., mean ± SEM, p-values from appropriate tests) for the key comparisons. State the number of independent biological replicates.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m5
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Terminology
  - **Affected element** Abstract and Introduction
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Introduction
  - **Issue** The term "non-canonical motifs" is used to describe the TROPO motif, but the manuscript also shows that the motif binds the same site as canonical RVxF motifs. The distinction between "non-canonical" in sequence and "canonical" in binding site could be clarified to avoid confusion.
  - **Required correction** Define what is meant by "non-canonical" in the context of this work, specifically that the sequence does not match the consensus but the binding site is shared. Consider using "sequence-divergent" or "non-consensus" for clarity.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (ITC data not shown) and R1-M2 (competition data not shown) are the primary technical issues that prevent full evaluation of the central claims. The negative result that TROPO peptides do not bind PP1 requires demonstration of assay sensitivity. The competition experiments require visual inspection of the curves and demonstration that full-length pUL21 is functional in the assay. The structural model (R1-M3) would benefit from additional validation or more detailed presentation of model confidence at the interface.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The finding that a viral protein uses a sequence-divergent motif to compete with cellular PP1 adaptors at a shared binding site is conceptually interesting and adds to the growing literature on viral mimicry of host motifs. The specific structural arrangement involving an extended beta-sheet bridging two proteins is novel. Scientific importance: The work has implications for understanding herpesvirus replication and the evolution of viral phosphatase recruitment. However, the importance is somewhat limited by the lack of functional data linking the biochemical findings to viral replication or pathogenesis. Interdisciplinary readership: The topic bridges virology, structural biology, and cell signalling, which could attract readers from multiple fields. The writing is generally accessible, though some sections assume familiarity with PP1 biology. Technical soundness: The combination of structural prediction, mutagenesis, and biophysics is appropriate, but the incomplete presentation of key data prevents full assessment. The AlphaFold2-based model, while useful for hypothesis generation, is not a substitute for experimental structure determination. Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract and introduction are clear, but the results section is dense and would benefit from more context for readers outside the immediate field. Overall, the manuscript has potential but does not currently meet the bar for a high-impact general journal due to incomplete evidence presentation and the lack of functional validation of the evolutionary claims.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core hypothesis is interesting and the preliminary data are suggestive, but the manuscript requires the full presentation of biophysical data, additional validation of the structural model, and ideally functional experiments linking the suboptimal motif to viral fitness before the claims can be considered established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that TROPO peptides alone do not bind PP1 is unsupported without showing the ITC data and demonstrating assay sensitivity with positive controls.
- The claim that pUL21 competes with cellular RVxF motifs for PP1 binding is unsupported because the competition data are not shown and full-length pUL21 did not compete in the assay.
- The claim that the suboptimal motif is evolutionarily maintained to fine-tune PP1 recruitment is speculative and not directly tested.
- The structural model of the pUL21:PP1 complex is based solely on AlphaFold2 predictions and is not experimentally validated; the claim that the extended beta-sheet bridges the two proteins is a prediction that requires confirmation.
- The statement that "many other PP1 regulators may possess non-canonical binding motifs and thus remain undiscovered" is a reasonable speculation but is not supported by data presented in this manuscript.
