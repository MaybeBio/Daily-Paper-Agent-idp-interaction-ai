## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, figure legends not provided)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, experimental logic, and technical soundness based on the provided text; figure panels referenced but not visually inspected
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that centriole biogenesis is not solely driven by self-assembly of constituent proteins but is mediated by an external, non-incorporating factor, the intrinsically disordered protein ALMS1. They identify a "cartwheel seed" (CS) composed of CEP152/CEP63/PCNT that forms independently of centrioles, cycles through assembly and disassembly in a cell-cycle-dependent manner, and is required for cartwheel formation. Disease-associated ALMS1 mutations disrupt this cycle, leading to centriole amplification and heritable structural diversity.
- **Visible evidence base** Results sections describing UExM, Ex-dSTORM, iU-ExM, immunoprecipitation, proximity ligation assay, sucrose gradient fractionation, live-cell imaging, and CRISPR/RNAi perturbations; figures 1-5 referenced but not provided as images
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figure panels and supplementary figures (S1-S4) not available for direct inspection; quantification details, statistical methods, and sample sizes not fully described; movie files referenced but not accessible; no information on antibody validation for ALMS1 isoforms

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a conceptually provocative model that challenges the prevailing view of centriole biogenesis as a purely self-organizing process. The identification of ALMS1 as an external, non-incorporating mediator of cartwheel dynamics is novel and potentially significant. However, the evidence as presented in the text has several gaps that prevent full confidence in the central claims. The reliance on descriptive imaging without robust quantitative frameworks, the lack of direct biochemical evidence for the proposed "memory" mechanism, and the incomplete characterization of the ALMS1-CS interaction leave important questions unresolved. The work is likely to interest the centriole and cell biology communities, but the case for a paradigm shift is not yet fully established from the provided material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Cell biologists studying organelle biogenesis, centriole duplication, and centrosome biology; researchers interested in intrinsically disordered proteins and their roles in cellular organization; clinical researchers studying Alström syndrome and ciliopathies; biophysicists interested in self-assembly versus templated assembly mechanisms
- **Major strengths** 1) The conceptual framing of centriole biogenesis as potentially template-dependent rather than purely self-organizing is intellectually important and testable. 2) The identification of a specific disease-linked IDP as a non-incorporating mediator provides a concrete molecular entry point. 3) The use of multiple advanced imaging modalities (UExM, Ex-dSTORM, iU-ExM) to characterize nanoscale structures is technically impressive. 4) The observation of heritable, selectable centriole architectural diversity following ALMS1 add-back is striking and, if robust, would be a major finding.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The central claim that ALMS1 is "not incorporated" into the CS or cartwheel rests on immunofluorescence and co-IP data, but the text does not provide direct evidence (e.g., super-resolution localization of ALMS1 relative to CS components at the molecular level) that ALMS1 is truly absent from these structures. The possibility of transient or low-stoichiometry incorporation is not excluded.
  - R1-M2: The proposed "memory" mechanism is purely conjectural and not supported by any direct experimental evidence. The authors themselves label it a "conjecture," but the Discussion frames it as a plausible interpretation of the add-back experiments. Without a mechanistic hypothesis that can be tested, this claim risks overinterpretation.
  - R1-M3: The quantification of cartwheel amplification phenotypes relies on categorical classifications (e.g., "2-2-0" to "6-x-x") without clear presentation of statistical rigor, inter-experiment variability, or blinding procedures. The text states percentages but does not provide confidence intervals or replicate numbers for many key comparisons.
  - R1-M4: The ALMS1 depletion-addback experiments are described as producing "diverse yet heritable architectures," but the text does not clearly demonstrate that the observed diversity is specifically due to ALMS1 loss and restoration rather than to off-target effects of the inducible shRNA system or to the prolonged culture period (4 weeks) used for clonal selection.
  - R1-M5: The relationship between the CS described here and previously characterized centriole precursor structures (e.g., centriolar satellites, deuterosomes, or the CEP152-CEP63 complex at the mother centriole) is not adequately discussed. The claim that CSs are a novel entity requires clearer distinction from known structures.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states that ALMS1 is "undetectable at S-phase procentrioles" but the text later shows ALMS1 is required for their formation. This temporal paradox (if ALMS1 is absent during S, how does it act in S-phase formation?) is not explicitly resolved.
  - R1-m2: The use of "9.5 μM RO-3306" for full CDK1 inhibition and "6 μM" for partial inhibition is described, but the text does not explain how these concentrations were validated or whether they produce consistent effects across cell lines.
  - R1-m3: The statement that "similar centriole triplets were identified in Drosophila tissues" is presented as supporting evidence, but the relevance of Drosophila CDK1 phenotypes to human cell culture conditions is not discussed.
  - R1-m4: The Methods section for Ex-dSTORM mentions "15,000-30,000 frames" per image, but the text does not specify how many cells or structures were analyzed for the radius measurements in Fig. 4E.
  - R1-m5: The author contributions list "T-J. B. C., W-Q. Y., and T. T. Y." for imaging studies, but the corresponding author list in the header includes different initials; this inconsistency should be checked.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (ALMS1 non-incorporation evidence), R1-M3 (quantification rigor), R1-M4 (specificity of ALMS1 depletion effects), R1-M5 (distinction from known structures)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: High. The concept of an external, non-incorporating IDP mediating centriole biogenesis is novel and challenges a long-held assumption of self-assembly.
  - Scientific importance: Potentially high. If confirmed, this would reshape understanding of organelle biogenesis and provide a new framework for ciliopathy mechanisms. However, the importance is contingent on the robustness of the central claims.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: Moderate. The work is primarily of interest to cell biologists and centriole specialists; the broader relevance to IDP biology and disease mechanisms may attract wider attention but is not fully developed in the text.
  - Technical soundness: Mixed. The imaging approaches are state-of-the-art, but the quantitative framework and controls for key perturbations are not fully described in the provided text.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract and introduction are accessible, but the results sections assume significant background in centriole biology and advanced microscopy. The Discussion introduces speculative concepts (memory) without sufficient context for a general audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The conceptual advance is significant, but the evidence as presented does not yet fully establish the central claims. The authors should strengthen the case for ALMS1 non-incorporation, provide more rigorous quantification, and temper the speculative "memory" framing until direct evidence is available.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that ALMS1 is "not incorporated" into the CS or cartwheel is not fully supported by the evidence described; the text does not rule out transient or low-stoichiometry association.
- The "memory" mechanism proposed in the Discussion is explicitly conjectural and not supported by direct experimental evidence; it should be clearly labeled as speculation rather than a conclusion.
- The statement that ALMS1 is "required for cartwheel formation" is supported by knockdown data, but the text does not exclude the possibility that ALMS1 acts indirectly (e.g., by regulating transcription or protein stability of core components).
- The claim that centriole architectural diversity following ALMS1 add-back is "heritable" and "evolves through selection" is based on clonal isolation experiments, but the text does not demonstrate that the observed phenotypes are stable across multiple cell divisions or that selection is the operative mechanism.
- The identification of the CS as a "dense, ring-like CEP152-CEP63 composite of ∼60 nm in diameter" is based on imaging data, but the text does not provide independent biochemical validation (e.g., purified CS composition analysis) to confirm this structure is a discrete entity.
