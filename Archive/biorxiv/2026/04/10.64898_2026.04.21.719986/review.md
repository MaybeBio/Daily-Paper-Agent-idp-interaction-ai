## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses an important and technically challenging problem: generating atomistic conformational ensembles of a dynamic IDP:folded protein complex restrained by multiple experimental data types. The combination of smFRET, NMR, and computational sampling is timely and the biological system (4E-BP2:eIF4E) is of broad interest in translation regulation. However, the manuscript as provided has several critical gaps. The figures and supplementary data, which are essential for evaluating the quality of the ensembles and the validity of the claims, were not available for review. The methodological description of X-EISDv2 is incomplete, and the validation of the ensembles against experimental data is not quantitatively presented in the text. The biological conclusions, while plausible and consistent with prior work, rest on ensemble features that cannot be independently assessed from the provided material. The claim of "one of the first conformational ensembles of an IDP in complex with its folded target" is not contextualized against recent literature. The manuscript has potential but the case is not fully established from the provided evidence.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists studying intrinsically disordered proteins and their complexes; researchers in the smFRET and integrative modeling communities; biochemists and cell biologists interested in translation initiation regulation and mTOR signaling; computational biologists developing ensemble refinement methods. The methodological advance (X-EISDv2 applied to a protein complex) and the biological insights into dynamic complex architecture would appeal to these groups.
- **Major strengths** 1) The study tackles a difficult and under-addressed problem: atomistic ensemble determination of a fuzzy IDP:folded complex, not just a free IDP. 2) The use of multiple complementary experimental restraints (intramolecular and intermolecular smFRET, NMR chemical shifts, R_h) is a rigorous approach. 3) The design of eight intramolecular FRET constructs spanning the full 4E-BP2 sequence is systematic. 4) The comparison between apo and bound ensembles provides functional insight into binding-induced conformational changes. 5) The identification of new contact regions (eIF4E N-IDR with 4E-BP2 N-terminus; 4E-BP2 C-terminus with eIF4E) is biologically interesting and testable.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence availability
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that the optimized ensembles are "consistent with experiment" and that specific contact regions (R1-R5, RN1, RN2) are present in the refined ensemble.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figures 2-6, Figures S1-S11, Tables S1-S3; location not provided
  - **Concern** The core evidence for the ensemble quality and the biological conclusions is contained in figures and supplementary materials that were not provided for review. Without the smFRET histograms, contact maps, distance distributions, and validation plots, it is impossible to assess whether the ensembles genuinely agree with the experimental data, whether the identified contacts are statistically robust, or whether the clustering analysis is meaningful.
  - **Why it matters** The central claims of the paper are ensemble features (contacts, compaction, expansion) that are only visible in the figures. The text alone cannot support these claims. A reviewer cannot verify the quality of the fit to experimental data, the convergence of the optimization, or the significance of the identified contact regions.
  - **Resolution test** Provide all figures and supplementary data for review. The authors should show quantitative agreement between back-calculated and experimental observables for each restraint type, and demonstrate that the identified contact regions are robust to the choice of analysis parameters.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodological transparency
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that X-EISDv2 is a "new" version of the Bayesian X-EISD method and that it was used to select ensembles consistent with all experimental restraints.
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section, "IDP conformational ensembles: generation, refinement and analysis"; location not provided
  - **Concern** The description of X-EISDv2 is insufficient. The text mentions that it "uses the same core logic" as the original X-EISD and that it has been "re-written to be generalized," but does not specify what changes were made, how the weighting scheme (Ω = 50) was chosen, how the number of conformers (100) was determined, or how convergence was assessed. The equation for the Bayesian likelihood is shown but not explained in the context of the new version. The relationship between the prior (initial pool) and the posterior (selected ensemble) is not quantified.
  - **Why it matters** The methodological advance is a key part of the paper's contribution. Without a clear description of the algorithm, its parameters, and its validation, the results cannot be reproduced or compared to other methods. The choice of Ω = 50 and the selection of 100 conformers appear arbitrary without sensitivity analysis.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed description of X-EISDv2, including pseudocode or a clear algorithmic outline, the likelihood function, the proposal and acceptance criteria, and a sensitivity analysis showing how the results depend on Ω, the number of conformers, and the initial pool size.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Claim support
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract and discussion state that the ensemble shows "delocalization of contacts around canonical regions" and that two new contact regions emerged (eIF4E N-IDR with 4E-BP2 N-terminus; 4E-BP2 C-terminus with eIF4E).
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, "Intermolecular Contacts in the 4E-BP2:eIF4E Ensemble"; Figures 6b-c, 6h-i; location not provided
  - **Concern** The text describes contact regions R1-R5 in the initial pool and RN1/RN2 in the optimized ensemble, but the quantitative basis for these assignments is not given. How were the contact regions defined? What is the contact frequency threshold? How do these contacts compare to the crystal structure contacts? The claim that RN1 and RN2 "emerged" after optimization is not supported by a statistical comparison between initial and optimized ensembles.
  - **Why it matters** The biological interpretation depends on the identification of specific contact regions. If the contact definitions are arbitrary or the differences between initial and optimized ensembles are not statistically significant, the conclusions about new binding interfaces and allosteric effects are not justified.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a clear definition of contact criteria, show the statistical significance of the differences in contact frequencies between initial and optimized ensembles, and compare the identified contacts to the crystal structure contacts and to prior NMR data.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No

  - **Claim pointer** The discussion states "we report one of the first conformational ensembles of an IDP in complex with its folded target."

  - **Why it matters** The novelty claim is important for the paper's impact. Without a proper literature comparison, the reader cannot judge whether the methodological approach or the biological findings are genuinely new.
  - **Resolution test** Revise the claim to be more specific and cite relevant prior work on IDP:folded complex ensembles, clearly stating what is new about this study.

- The identification of new contact regions (RN1, RN2) and the claim that they "emerged" after optimization are not supported by quantitative data in the text.
- The statement that the crystal structure is "not representative of the ensemble present in solution" is a strong claim that requires the ensemble validation data to support.
- The claim of being "one of the first conformational ensembles of an IDP in complex with its folded target" is not contextualized against prior literature.
- The biological interpretation regarding phosphorylation site accessibility and allosteric effects is speculative and not directly supported by the data presented in the text.
