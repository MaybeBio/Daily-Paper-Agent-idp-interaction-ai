## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods) for a preprint deposited on bioRxiv.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, experimental logic, computational methodology, and interpretation as presented. No assessment of editorial fit or journal policy.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors investigate how intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) flanking the homeodomain (HD) influence the usage of conserved short linear motifs (HX and UA) in the Drosophila Hox proteins Ubx and AbdA. Using in vivo functional assays in embryos and coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulations, they report that (i) HX and UA motifs are required for Ubx activity but largely dispensable for AbdA activity across four shared developmental functions; (ii) AbdA is more compact than Ubx in simulations, with lower accessibility of HX and UA motifs; (iii) swapping N- and C-terminal IDR sequences between Ubx and AbdA alters motif accessibility in silico and, in some cases, changes motif usage in vivo; and (iv) the relationship between motif accessibility and usage is context-dependent, as exemplified by the Exd-dependent repression of Distalless.
- **Visible evidence base** Full text including four in vivo functional assays (beard suppression, cerebral branch repression, somatic muscle specification, cardiac lineage specification), one Exd-dependent assay (Dll repression), transgenic constructs for wild-type and mutant proteins, chimeric proteins (uAu, aUa), CRISPR-tagged endogenous lines for expression calibration, and coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulations with contact maps, radius of gyration, interaction energies, and accessibility scores.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures and supplementary tables (Figures 1–8, S1–S13; Tables S1–S4) are referenced but not provided. Statistical details (exact n, effect sizes, dispersion) for several assays are not fully described in the text. Simulation parameters (force field values, equilibration protocol, convergence checks) are partially described but not fully specified. No raw data or analysis scripts are provided.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a significant and timely question in the field of transcription factor biology: how intrinsically disordered regions modulate the functional usage of conserved protein motifs. The combination of quantitative in vivo assays with coarse-grained simulations is conceptually appealing and the observation that motif requirements differ between two closely related Hox proteins is potentially important. However, the current presentation has several limitations that prevent full evaluation. The in vivo data are described qualitatively in places, with key quantitative details relegated to inaccessible supplementary materials. The simulation methodology, while reasonable, is not described with sufficient detail to assess robustness, and the link between simulated accessibility and in vivo function remains correlative. The chimeric protein experiments are informative but incomplete, with several constructs inactive and the interpretation of these losses not fully explored. The manuscript would be strengthened by a more rigorous presentation of the quantitative data, additional controls for the chimeric experiments, and a more cautious interpretation of the simulation results.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying Hox protein function and specificity, the role of intrinsically disordered regions in transcription factor activity, the evolution of protein functional divergence, and the development of predictive models linking protein sequence to function. The work may also interest those using coarse-grained simulations to study IDP behavior, and developmental biologists working on Drosophila segmental patterning.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a fundamental question about how protein sequence divergence in disordered regions translates into functional differences. The use of a closely related Hox pair with shared developmental functions is a well-chosen system. The combination of in vivo genetics with in silico modeling is a forward-looking approach. The quantitative framework for defining motif contribution is a useful conceptual tool. The inclusion of an Exd-dependent function provides an important contrast to the Exd-independent assays.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence quality and reproducibility
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that HX and UA motifs are required for Ubx but largely dispensable for AbdA across four shared functions, and that motif contributions can be quantified as differences in activity between wild-type and mutant proteins.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, Figures 2, S1–S3, Table S2; location not provided for specific data points
  - **Concern** The quantitative basis for the central claim is not fully presented in the text. The authors report mean activity scores and motif contributions, but do not provide the underlying distributions, sample sizes, or statistical comparisons for most assays. The reader cannot assess the variability between embryos, the consistency of the scoring, or whether the differences between Ubx and AbdA are statistically robust. The statement that expression levels were matched within +/-15% is described, but the actual measurements and their precision are not shown. Without these data, the central conclusion that motif usage differs between Ubx and AbdA cannot be fully evaluated.
  - **Why it matters** The claim of differential motif usage is the foundation of the entire manuscript. If the quantitative differences are small, variable, or confounded by expression differences, the conclusion would be substantially weakened. The current presentation does not allow the reader to distinguish a robust biological difference from experimental noise.
  - **Resolution test** Provide full distributions of activity scores for each genotype and assay, with sample sizes and appropriate statistical tests. Show the expression level measurements for each construct and demonstrate that the +/-15% criterion was met for all experiments. Present the data in a way that allows the reader to assess the magnitude and consistency of the motif contribution differences.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Computational methodology and interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulations predict that AbdA is more compact than Ubx, that HX and UA motifs are less accessible in AbdA, and that these differences correlate with the observed in vivo motif usage.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, Figures 3–4; location not provided for simulation details
  - **Concern** The simulation methodology is described at a level that is insufficient for reproducibility. Key parameters are missing, including the specific values for the Debye-Hückel potential, the criteria for defining native contacts, the equilibration protocol, and the convergence assessment. The authors state that three independent simulations were performed, but do not report the variability between runs or whether the observed differences between Ubx and AbdA are statistically significant. The accessibility score is defined, but its relationship to the in vivo functional readouts is not validated. The authors acknowledge that the simulations cannot predict all aspects of function, but the correlative link between accessibility and usage is presented as a central finding without a clear framework for testing it.
  - **Why it matters** The simulation results are used to provide a mechanistic explanation for the observed in vivo differences. If the simulations are not robust, reproducible, or statistically grounded, the proposed mechanism is speculative. The field of IDP simulations is sensitive to force field choices and sampling protocols, and the current description does not allow the reader to assess whether the results are an artifact of the model or a genuine property of the proteins.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a complete description of the simulation parameters, including all force field constants, the protocol for generating initial structures, the equilibration and production run lengths, and the criteria for assessing convergence. Report the variability between independent simulations and perform statistical comparisons between Ubx and AbdA for the key observables (Rg, accessibility scores, interaction energies). Validate the accessibility score against an independent measure or a known functional readout.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Experimental design and interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that grafting Ubx N- and C-terminal sequences onto the AbdA central core does not convert motif usage to the Ubx mode, while grafting AbdA sequences onto the Ubx core does convert usage to the AbdA mode, suggesting an asymmetric influence of IDRs.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, Figures 5–6, S5–S8; location not provided for specific data
  - **Concern** The chimeric protein experiments are a key part of the study, but the interpretation is complicated by the fact that several chimeric constructs are inactive in some assays. The authors interpret this as functional interdependence between the central core and flanking IDRs, but do not explore alternative explanations, such as protein misfolding, instability, or improper subcellular localization. The asymmetric influence is based on a small number of comparisons, and the authors do not provide a mechanistic explanation for why the influence is asymmetric. The choice of which functions to test for each chimera appears to be dictated by which constructs retain activity, which introduces a selection bias.
  - **Why it matters** The chimeric experiments are intended to demonstrate that IDRs influence motif usage, but the interpretation is confounded by the loss of activity in several constructs. Without controls for protein stability and localization, the observed effects could be due to non-specific disruption of protein structure rather than a specific influence on motif usage. The asymmetry claim is interesting but would be more convincing with a larger set of constructs and a mechanistic explanation.
  - **Resolution test** Include controls for protein expression, stability, and nuclear localization for all chimeric constructs. Test additional chimeric constructs to determine whether the asymmetry is a general property or specific to the particular junctions chosen. Provide a mechanistic hypothesis for the observed asymmetry and design experiments to test it.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scope of conclusions
  - **Claim pointer** The authors conclude that IDRs influence protein motif usage in a context-dependent manner, and suggest that this may be a general principle for Hox protein function.
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section; location not provided
  - **Concern** The study examines two closely related proteins in a limited set of developmental contexts. The conclusion that IDRs influence motif usage in a context-dependent manner is supported by the data, but the generalization to other Hox proteins or other contexts is speculative. The authors do not discuss how their findings might apply to Hox proteins with different domain architectures or to functions that require different cofactors. The context-dependence is demonstrated for one Exd-dependent function, but the mechanistic basis for this context-dependence is not explored.
  - **Why it matters** The broader significance of the work depends on whether the observed phenomena are general or specific to the Ubx/AbdA pair. The current data do not distinguish between these possibilities, and the authors should be more cautious in their generalizations.
  - **Resolution test** Discuss the limitations of the study in terms of the number of proteins and contexts examined. Suggest specific predictions that could be tested in other Hox proteins or in different developmental contexts. Consider whether the framework of motif accessibility could be applied to other IDP-containing transcription factors.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The quantitative in vivo data must be presented in full, with sample sizes and statistical analyses, to support the central claim of differential motif usage. The simulation methodology must be described in sufficient detail to allow reproduction and assessment of robustness. The chimeric protein experiments require controls for protein stability and localization to rule out non-specific effects. The relationship between simulated accessibility and in vivo function needs to be validated or presented with appropriate caveats.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The question of how IDRs influence motif usage in Hox proteins is relatively underexplored, and the combination of in vivo assays with simulations is a novel approach. However, the conceptual framework of IDRs modulating protein function is well established, and the specific findings, while interesting, do not represent a fundamental paradigm shift. Scientific importance: The work has the potential to inform our understanding of how protein sequence divergence in disordered regions contributes to functional specificity, which is a topic of broad interest. The specific findings for Ubx and AbdA are likely to be of interest to the Hox and developmental biology communities. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges developmental biology, protein biophysics, and computational biology, and could appeal to readers across these fields. However, the presentation is likely to be most accessible to specialists in Drosophila development and IDP biology. Technical soundness: The in vivo assays are well-established, but the quantitative presentation is incomplete. The simulation methodology is reasonable but not described in sufficient detail to assess its robustness. The interpretation of the chimeric experiments is complicated by the loss of activity in several constructs. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and the logic is clear, but the heavy reliance on supplementary figures and the incomplete quantitative presentation make it difficult for a non-specialist to fully evaluate the claims.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The central question is important and the experimental approach is promising, but the current presentation does not provide sufficient evidence to fully establish the claims. The authors should be encouraged to revise the manuscript to address the concerns raised, particularly the quantitative presentation of the in vivo data and the methodological detail of the simulations.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that HX and UA motifs are required for Ubx but dispensable for AbdA across four shared functions is not fully supported by the data presented, as the quantitative results are not shown in the text.
- The claim that AbdA is more compact than Ubx and that this leads to lower motif accessibility is based on simulations that are not described in sufficient detail to assess their validity.
- The claim that IDRs influence motif usage in a context-dependent manner is supported by the data, but the generalization to other Hox proteins or contexts is not supported.
- The claim that the UA motif in AbdA is not generally non-functional, based on the Ubx UA(AbdA) rescue experiments, is only partially supported, as the rescue is incomplete in some assays and the interpretation is complicated by the loss of activity in some chimeric constructs.
- The relationship between simulated motif accessibility and in vivo motif usage is correlative and not validated by any direct experimental test.
