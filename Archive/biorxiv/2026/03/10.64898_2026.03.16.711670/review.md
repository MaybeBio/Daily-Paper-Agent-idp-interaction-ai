## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** The assessment is limited to the claims, evidence, and logic presented in the abstract. No full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that the C9ORF72-derived polyGR polypeptide disrupts passive nucleocytoplasmic transport by modulating the affinity of client proteins for the FG-rich nuclear pore barrier. They report a biphasic effect: low-affinity proteins are unaffected, intermediate-affinity proteins show enhanced transport, and high-affinity (hydrophobic) proteins experience suppressed transport, cytoplasmic accumulation, and aggregation. This mechanism is proposed to explain selective protein vulnerability in C9ORF72-associated ALS/FTD.
- **Visible evidence base** The abstract references coarse-grained simulations, in vitro FG-phase reconstitution, human cell lines, and neurons. No specific data, figures, or statistical details are provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures, tables, methods, statistical analyses, and supplementary information. The abstract alone is insufficient to evaluate the robustness of the simulations, the fidelity of the in vitro system, or the quantitative support for the biphasic model.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that polyGR produces a non-linear, biphasic modulation of nuclear pore passage, with low-affinity proteins unaffected, intermediate-affinity proteins enhanced, and high-affinity proteins suppressed.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The biphasic model is the central claim of the paper, but the abstract provides no quantitative data, no thresholds, and no statistical support. It is unclear how "low," "intermediate," and "high" affinity are defined, and whether the effect is robust across different protein clients.  
- **Why it matters** Without quantitative evidence, the biphasic model cannot be evaluated or reproduced. The claim is central to the paper's significance, and its unsupported nature undermines the entire conclusion.  
- **Resolution test** Provide data from simulations and experiments showing the relationship between client protein affinity (e.g., measured or computed binding constants) and transport rates, with clear thresholds and statistical significance.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Model fidelity  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that findings are based on coarse-grained simulations, in vitro FG-phase reconstitution, and human cell lines and neurons.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not describe the parameters, validation, or limitations of the coarse-grained simulations. The in vitro FG-phase system is not characterized, and its relevance to the native nuclear pore environment is not established. The cell and neuron experiments are not described in terms of controls or specificity.  
- **Why it matters** The conclusions depend on the fidelity of these models. If the simulations or in vitro systems do not accurately recapitulate the nuclear pore barrier, the biphasic model may be an artifact.  
- **Resolution test** Describe the simulation parameters and validation against known transport properties. Show that the in vitro FG-phase system reproduces key features of the native pore. Provide details on cell and neuron experiments, including controls for polyGR toxicity and specificity.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic link  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that polyGR retunes passive nucleocytoplasmic transport according to client surface chemistry, leading to selective protein vulnerability.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not demonstrate a direct mechanistic link between polyGR's interaction with the FG-phase and the observed changes in transport. It is unclear whether polyGR acts by binding to FG repeats, altering the phase properties, or affecting the pore structure. The link to TDP-43 mislocalisation is asserted but not shown.  
- **Why it matters** The paper's relevance to ALS/FTD depends on this link. Without evidence that polyGR's effect on transport is mediated by the proposed mechanism and that it applies to TDP-43, the clinical significance is speculative.  
- **Resolution test** Provide direct evidence (e.g., binding assays, mutagenesis, or imaging) that polyGR interacts with the FG-phase and that this interaction is necessary for the transport changes. Show that TDP-43 mislocalisation is dependent on the same mechanism.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Generalizability  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract implies that the biphasic model explains selective protein vulnerability in C9ORF72-associated ALS/FTD.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not address whether the model applies to other C9ORF72-associated polypeptides (e.g., polyPR, polyGA) or to other diseases with transport dysfunction. The specificity of the effect to polyGR is not discussed.  
- **Why it matters** If the model is specific to polyGR, its relevance to the broader C9ORF72 pathology is limited. The abstract's framing suggests a general mechanism, but this is not supported.  
- **Resolution test** Discuss or provide data on whether other C9ORF72-derived polypeptides show similar effects, and whether the biphasic model is generalizable across different transport disruptors.
