### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that sequence composition of RG/RGG motifs is a key determinant of RNA-mediated condensate formation, stress granule recruitment, and transportin-1-mediated chaperoning and nuclear import.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract asserts a causal relationship between sequence composition and multiple functional outcomes, but no quantitative data, experimental conditions, or statistical analyses are presented. It is unclear how many sequences were tested, what controls were used, or how the effects were measured.  
- **Why it matters** Without these details, the claim that sequence composition is a "key determinant" cannot be evaluated. The multifunctionality claim requires evidence that the same sequence features drive all three processes, rather than independent or context-dependent effects.  
- **Resolution test** Provide specific data showing that systematic variation of RG/RGG sequence composition leads to predictable changes in each functional readout, with appropriate statistical support and controls.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Generalizability  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract implies that the findings apply broadly to RG/RGG proteins across the proteome, based on studies of synthetic and natural peptides and FUS.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The use of FUS as a single model system and a limited set of peptides may not be representative of the diversity of RG/RGG motifs in the proteome. The abstract does not indicate how many natural sequences were examined or whether the conclusions hold across different protein contexts.  
- **Why it matters** The claim of generalizability is central to the paper's significance. If the effects are specific to FUS or a narrow set of sequences, the broader conclusions would be overstated.  
- **Resolution test** Show that the observed sequence-function relationships hold across a diverse panel of RG/RGG-containing proteins or that the bioinformatics analysis supports a generalizable grammar.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic clarity  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that sequence composition determines transportin-1-mediated chaperoning and nuclear import.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The mechanistic link between sequence composition and transportin-1 function is not explained. It is unclear whether the effects are due to direct binding affinity, conformational changes, or competition with RNA, and whether these are separable from condensate formation.

- **Resolution test** Provide binding assays, mutagenesis data, or structural information that directly links specific sequence features to transportin-1 interaction and function.
