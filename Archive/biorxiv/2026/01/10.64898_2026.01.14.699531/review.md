## Review setup

- **Input scope** Abstract and significance statement only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the supplied abstract and significance statement
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors introduce a dimensionless descriptor, susceptibility, to quantify how solutes shift the phase equilibria of biomolecular condensates. They report measurements across three model condensates and over a dozen solutes, with susceptibilities spanning four orders of magnitude, and propose a thermodynamic framework based on Flory-Huggins and polyphasic linkage theory. They further claim predictive power by using enzymatic activity to induce condensation and modulate material properties.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text and significance statement only. No figures, tables, methods, or numerical data are provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, experimental methods, model system descriptions, solute identities and concentrations, thermodynamic derivations, statistical analyses, and the enzymatic demonstration. None of these are available for assessment.

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a conceptually appealing and potentially broadly useful framework for quantifying solute effects on biomolecular condensates. The introduction of a dimensionless susceptibility parameter is a reasonable and timely idea, and the claim of four orders of magnitude variation across diverse systems is striking. However, the supplied material is insufficient to evaluate the technical validity of the measurements, the robustness of the thermodynamic framework, or the strength of the enzymatic demonstration. The core claims are plausible but currently not verifiable from the provided evidence.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in phase separation and biomolecular condensates, biophysicists studying intracellular organization, cell biologists interested in metabolic regulation and stress responses, and scientists in drug discovery targeting condensate-modulating small molecules. The framework could also interest theorists working on polymer physics and multicomponent thermodynamics.
- **Major strengths** The concept of susceptibility as a compact descriptor of condensate responsiveness is elegant and could unify disparate observations. The choice of three condensates with distinct cohesive mechanisms and a diverse solute panel is a sensible design. The connection to established thermodynamic formalisms, Flory-Huggins and polyphasic linkage, provides a theoretical anchor. The potential translational relevance to therapeutic design is well framed.
- **Major Concerns** The concerns below are based solely on the abstract and significance statement. The absence of methodological detail and quantitative results prevents verification of the central claims.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract is well written and accessible. The significance statement effectively frames the problem. However, several terms and claims require clarification or additional support.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3, R1-M4. The case for susceptibility as a robust and predictive descriptor is not established from the supplied material.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality is high, as the susceptibility framework appears to be a novel synthesis. Scientific importance is potentially high, given the broad relevance of solute effects on condensates. Interdisciplinary readership is plausible, spanning biophysics, cell biology, and chemistry. Technical soundness cannot be assessed from the abstract alone. Readability for nonspecialists is good, with clear motivation and framing, though the thermodynamic content may be dense for some readers.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The concept is promising and the authors should be encouraged to submit the full manuscript for a complete review. A supportive posture is possible if the technical concerns are resolved in the full paper.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that susceptibilities span over four orders of magnitude across three model condensates and diverse solutes.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract provides no numerical data, no definition of how susceptibility is calculated from experimental measurements, and no information on the precision or reproducibility of the reported values. The claim of four orders of magnitude variation is striking but cannot be evaluated without the underlying measurements, error bars, and statistical treatment.
- **Why it matters** The magnitude and range of susceptibility values are central to the paper's thesis. If the measurement protocol is not robust or the values are not reproducible, the entire framework loses quantitative credibility.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript must provide a clear operational definition of susceptibility, a description of the experimental protocol, and a quantitative presentation of results including replicates, error estimates, and statistical comparisons across systems and solutes.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that their results are consistent with theoretical descriptions including Flory-Huggins and polyphasic linkages.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** No theoretical derivation, fitting procedure, or comparison between model and experiment is presented. It is unclear whether the theory is used to predict susceptibility values, to fit them, or merely to rationalize trends post hoc. The abstract does not specify which parameters are measured, which are fitted, and how the models are validated.
- **Why it matters** The claim of consistency with established theory is a key part of the paper's contribution. Without a transparent account of the theoretical framework and its quantitative comparison to data, the claim is unfalsifiable from the supplied material.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript must present the thermodynamic model, specify all parameters and assumptions, and show a quantitative comparison between theoretical predictions and experimental susceptibility values, including goodness-of-fit metrics or equivalent statistical measures.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Support for claims
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that susceptibility has predictive power, demonstrated by using enzymatic activity to induce condensation and modulate material properties.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The enzymatic demonstration is described in a single sentence with no details on the enzyme, the reaction, the condensate system, the measured outcomes, or the controls. It is impossible to assess whether the observed effect is due to susceptibility-based prediction, a trivial consequence of substrate depletion, or an unrelated artifact.
- **Why it matters** The claim of predictive power is a major selling point of the paper. If the demonstration is not rigorous or the connection to susceptibility is not explicit, the claim is overstated.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript must provide a detailed description of the enzymatic experiment, including the enzyme and substrate identities, the predicted and observed susceptibility values, appropriate negative and positive controls, and a clear demonstration that the outcome is quantitatively consistent with the susceptibility framework.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Generalizability
- **Claim pointer** The authors imply that susceptibility is a general descriptor applicable to biomolecular condensates in complex cellular environments.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract and significance statement, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract reports measurements on three model condensates and over a dozen solutes. It is unclear how representative these systems are of the diversity of cellular condensates and solute compositions. The significance statement acknowledges that mapping full phase diagrams is infeasible, but the abstract does not address how susceptibility measured in dilute in vitro conditions translates to the crowded, multicomponent cellular environment.
- **Why it matters** The stated implications for cell physiology and therapeutic design depend on the generalizability of the susceptibility framework. If the measured values are system-specific and do not transfer to cellular contexts, the broader significance is diminished.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript must discuss the limitations of the in vitro measurements, provide evidence or arguments for their relevance to cellular conditions, and ideally include at least one demonstration in a more complex or cellularly relevant milieu.
