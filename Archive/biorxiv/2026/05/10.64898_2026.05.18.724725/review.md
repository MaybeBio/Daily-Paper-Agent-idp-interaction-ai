### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the NTD engages in interdomain contact with other FXR domains and interacts directly with coregulator proteins, and that a novel SXXLF motif mediates these interactions.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract provides no quantitative data, experimental conditions, or statistical analyses to support the claimed interactions. No binding affinities, mutation effects sizes, or reproducibility metrics are reported. The methods cited (mutagenesis, mammalian two-hybrid, molecular dynamics) are named but not described in sufficient detail to evaluate their appropriateness or rigor.
- **Why it matters** Without quantitative evidence and methodological detail, the core claims of interaction and motif-mediated function cannot be independently evaluated. The reader cannot determine whether the observed effects are robust, specific, or biologically meaningful.
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative results from the mammalian two-hybrid assays (e.g., fold changes, statistical significance), binding data if available, and detailed methods including constructs, cell lines, controls, and simulation parameters. Show that the SXXLF motif mutations specifically disrupt the claimed interactions without global structural disruption.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Claim support
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that mutation of the motif induces large changes in conformational and allosteric coupling in FXR.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The claim of "large changes in conformational and allosteric coupling" is presented without any supporting data. No metrics of conformational change, allosteric coupling strength, or comparison to wild-type are provided. The molecular dynamics simulations are mentioned but no simulation outcomes, convergence criteria, or validation are described.
- **Why it matters** This is a central mechanistic claim that underpins the proposed functional significance of the motif. Without evidence, the claim is unsupported and cannot be distinguished from speculative interpretation.
- **Resolution test** Present quantitative measures of conformational ensembles (e.g., RMSD, contact maps, principal component analysis) and allosteric coupling (e.g., correlation analyses, perturbation response) for wild-type versus mutant FXR, with appropriate statistical comparisons and simulation validation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Novelty verification
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims the SXXLF motif is "novel" and "the first defined functional element within the FXR-NTD."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not provide any comparison to previously identified NTD motifs in FXR or other nuclear receptors. The claim of novelty requires a demonstration that this motif has not been previously described or functionally characterized.
- **Why it matters** Novelty is a central criterion for significance in this field. If the motif or its function has been partially described, the claim of novelty would be overstated and the contribution diminished.
- **Resolution test** Include a literature search summary demonstrating that the SXXLF motif in FXR NTD has not been previously identified or functionally characterized, and discuss any related motifs in other nuclear receptors to establish distinctiveness.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Clarity
- **Affected element** Abstract background
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Issue** The abstract states that "few NTD functions are conserved between nuclear receptors" and lists AR, ER, and MR as examples, but does not specify what functions are conserved or how FXR relates to these examples.
- **Required correction** Briefly specify the conserved functions in AR, ER, and MR, and clarify how FXR NTD function relates to or diverges from these known examples.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Terminology
- **Affected element** Motif nomenclature
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Issue** The abstract uses both "SXXLF motif" and "SENLF motif" without explaining the relationship. The graphical abstract description uses "SENLF" while the title and main text use "SXXLF."
- **Required correction** Define the SXXLF consensus and explicitly state that SENLF is the specific sequence in FXR, and use consistent terminology throughout.

- **Concern ID** R1-m3
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Functional significance
- **Affected element** Transcriptional activity claim
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Issue** The abstract claims the motif "modulates the transcriptional activity of FXR" and "drives NTD-mediated transcriptional activity," but no functional assay (e.g., reporter gene, endogenous target gene expression) is described.
- **Required correction** Specify the functional assays used to demonstrate transcriptional effects, and report the magnitude and direction of effects on transcriptional activity.
