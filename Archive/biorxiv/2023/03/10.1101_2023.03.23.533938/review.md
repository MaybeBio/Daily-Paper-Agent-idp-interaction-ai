### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that phosphorylation of five residues within the intrinsically disordered domain of Sfr1 regulates the interaction with Rad51.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that five residues are phosphorylated, but no evidence is presented to show that these sites are phosphorylated in vivo or in vitro. The identification of these residues is not described, and it is unclear whether they were predicted, found by mass spectrometry, or mutated based on prior data.
- **Why it matters** Without evidence that the five residues are actual phosphorylation sites, the central premise of the study is unverified. The claim that phosphorylation regulates the interaction depends on the biological relevance of these sites.
- **Resolution test** Provide data showing phosphorylation of these residues, such as mass spectrometry from yeast or in vitro kinase assays, and demonstrate that mutation of these sites alters phosphorylation status.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Functional evidence
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that a phosphomimetic mutant version of Swi5-Sfr1 is defective in both the physical and functional interaction with Rad51, and that this translates to a defect in DNA repair.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract mentions biochemical reconstitutions and yeast strains, but no quantitative results are provided. It is unclear how the physical interaction was measured, what functional assays were used, and what the magnitude of the defects was. The phenocopy claim requires direct comparison with the interaction mutant, but no data are shown.
- **Why it matters** The core conclusion that phosphorylation controls Rad51 interaction and repair relies on these functional data. Without quantitative and comparative evidence, the claim is not substantiated.
- **Resolution test** Include representative data from binding assays, recombination or repair assays, and survival curves, with statistical analysis and direct comparison to the interaction mutant.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Mechanistic insight
- **Claim pointer** The authors propose that controlled phosphorylation of Sfr1 is important for the role of Swi5-Sfr1 in promoting Rad51-dependent DNA repair.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided

- **Resolution test** Provide experiments that dissect the mechanism, such as binding kinetics, structural analysis, or in vivo localization studies, and discuss how phosphorylation modulates the interaction.
