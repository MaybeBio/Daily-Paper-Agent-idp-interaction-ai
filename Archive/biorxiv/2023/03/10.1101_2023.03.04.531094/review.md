## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use molecular dynamics simulations and previously published experimental data to examine the temperature-dependent behaviour of Histatin 5, a 24-residue disordered polypeptide. They test the hypothesis that increasing temperature causes a loss of polyproline II structure and more compact conformations. Simulations agree with small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) data but show discrepancies with pulsed-field gradient NMR (hydrodynamic radius) and circular dichroism (secondary structure). Reweighting against scattering and NMR data partially captures the temperature-dependent behaviour and links decreased hydrodynamic radius to loss of polyproline II structure, but agreement with both data types within experimental errors is not achieved. The authors discuss possible causes including force field inaccuracies, experimental condition differences, and hydrodynamic radius calculation issues.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or numerical results provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, simulation details, force field parameters, experimental data sources, reweighting methodology, quantitative agreement metrics, and all figures and tables

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a scientifically interesting and timely investigation of temperature-dependent conformational behaviour in a disordered protein, combining simulations with multiple experimental probes. The study is honest about limitations and highlights an important methodological point about integrating multiple data types. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the technical soundness of the simulations, the reweighting procedure, or the quantitative basis for the stated agreements and discrepancies. The core claim that the decrease in hydrodynamic radius is linked to loss of polyproline II structure is plausible but not fully established from the provided material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of intrinsically disordered proteins, molecular dynamics simulation, and biophysical characterization of protein conformational ensembles. The work is also relevant to those developing and benchmarking force fields for disordered proteins and to methodologists interested in integrative modelling approaches that combine multiple experimental data types.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a specific and mechanistically meaningful hypothesis about temperature-dependent conformational changes in a disordered protein. The use of multiple complementary experimental probes (SAXS, NMR, CD) is appropriate and strengthens the integrative approach. The authors are transparent about the limitations and unresolved discrepancies, which is scientifically honest and useful for the community. The discussion of potential causes for disagreement is valuable for future work.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the conformational ensembles generated by simulations generally agree with SAXS data but show discrepancies with NMR and CD data.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract provides no quantitative metrics for the level of agreement or discrepancy with each experimental probe. Terms such as "generally agree" and "some discrepancies" are vague and cannot be assessed without numerical values, such as chi-squared statistics, reduced chi-squared values, or root-mean-square deviations between calculated and experimental observables.
  - **Why it matters** Without quantitative measures of agreement, the reader cannot judge whether the discrepancies are within acceptable limits, whether the reweighting procedure meaningfully improved agreement, or whether the conclusions about force field accuracy are justified. This is central to the technical soundness of the study.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative agreement metrics for each experimental observable before and after reweighting, including statistical uncertainties, and state explicitly which discrepancies remain outside experimental errors.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that reweighting the conformational ensembles against scattering and NMR data partially captures the temperature-dependent behaviour and links the observed decrease in hydrodynamic radius to a loss of polyproline II structure.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
  - **Concern** The reweighting procedure is described only in general terms. No details are given about the method used, the weights applied, the number of iterations, the convergence criteria, or the uncertainty in the reweighted ensembles. Without this information, the reliability of the reweighted results and the strength of the causal link between hydrodynamic radius decrease and polyproline II loss cannot be evaluated.
  - **Why it matters** Reweighting methods can introduce biases or overfitting, and the validity of the resulting ensembles depends critically on the implementation. The claim of a mechanistic link is only as strong as the reweighting procedure that produced it.
  - **Resolution test** Describe the reweighting method in detail, including validation steps, and show that the reweighted ensembles are robust to methodological choices and that the polyproline II loss is statistically significant across the temperature range.
