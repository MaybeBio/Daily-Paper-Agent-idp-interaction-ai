### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that TF binding to empty 70S ribosomes is enthalpy-driven with micromolar affinity, while binding to RNCs achieves nanomolar affinity through a favorable entropic contribution.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract provides no quantitative values, such as enthalpy and entropy changes, dissociation constants, or confidence intervals. Without these data, the thermodynamic profiles cannot be evaluated or compared.
- **Why it matters** The core conclusion rests on the distinction between enthalpy-driven and entropy-driven binding. If the thermodynamic parameters are not robustly measured and reported, the proposed mechanism is unsupported.
- **Resolution test** Provide the full thermodynamic data, including ΔH, ΔS, ΔG, and Kd values with errors, for both empty ribosomes and RNCs, and demonstrate that the differences are statistically significant.

- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Mechanistic claim support
- **Claim pointer** The authors propose that co-translational TF function relies on occupation of the exposed substrate-binding groove rather than specific complementarity between chaperone and RNC.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not present any experiments that directly test the groove-occupation mechanism, such as mutagenesis of the binding groove, competition assays, or structural data. The claim appears to be an interpretation rather than a demonstrated finding.

- **Resolution test** Include experiments that perturb the substrate-binding groove and show that TF function is affected in a manner consistent with groove occupation, or provide structural or kinetic evidence that rules out specific complementarity.
