## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, author contributions, conflict of interest, acknowledgment)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content and technical soundness of the claims as presented in the provided manuscript text. No external validation of experimental data or computational reproducibility was performed.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors claim that the C-terminal region (CTR, residues 38-61) of SARS-CoV-2 ORF6 is intrinsically disordered, gains α-helical structure in organic solvents but not in lipid or molecular crowding environments, and that specific residues (Met58, di-acidic motif 53DE54, and the 58MEID61 segment) are critical for maintaining its disordered conformation and structural flexibility, which may underpin its multifunctional interactions with host proteins.
- **Visible evidence base** Far-UV CD spectroscopy data (Figures 1-3), disorder predictions (Figure 1a), AlphaFold structural models (Figure 4), and MD simulation results (described in text, referenced to Figure 4). Methods section is incomplete for several key techniques.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full methods for CD spectroscopy (buffer conditions, peptide concentration, temperature protocols), MD simulation setup (box size, water model, ion concentration, equilibration protocol, simulation length per replicate, number of replicates), AlphaFold model selection criteria, and raw data or statistical analyses for CD spectra and simulation trajectories. Figure legends are not provided.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a relevant and timely topic, namely the structural behavior of the SARS-CoV-2 ORF6 C-terminal region and the role of specific residues in modulating its conformational landscape. The combination of biophysical experiments and computational modeling is appropriate in principle. However, the current presentation has substantial technical gaps that prevent the claims from being fully established. The CD experiments are described without sufficient methodological detail, the MD simulations appear to lack rigorous statistical treatment, and the link between the observed in vitro and in silico behaviors and the proposed functional implications is largely speculative. The manuscript would benefit from a more complete methods section, quantitative analyses, and a more cautious interpretation of the results.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists studying intrinsically disordered proteins and their conformational plasticity; virologists interested in SARS-CoV-2 accessory proteins and host-pathogen interactions, particularly the ORF6-Rae1/Nup98 axis; and computational biologists using MD simulations to study disordered peptides. The potential therapeutic angle regarding targeting ORF6 interactions may also interest drug discovery researchers.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a relatively under-explored region of a functionally important viral protein. The combination of experimental (CD) and computational (AlphaFold, MD) approaches is commendable. The identification of specific residues (Met58, di-acidic motif) that appear to influence structural order is a potentially valuable observation that could guide future functional studies.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness of experimental methods
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that ORF6-CTR is disordered in isolation and gains structure in organic solvents but not in lipids or molecular crowders is based on CD spectroscopy.
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section, "CD spectroscopy was performed using 1 mm quartz cuvette on JASCO J1500 spectrometer using previously reported protocol 9." Results section, Figures 1-3.
  - **Concern** The methods for CD spectroscopy are critically incomplete. The authors reference a "previously reported protocol" but do not provide essential details such as peptide concentration, buffer composition and pH, temperature, scan speed, number of accumulations, and how the data were processed (e.g., baseline subtraction, smoothing parameters). The concentration of the peptide is not stated anywhere, which is essential for calculating mean residue ellipticity. The conditions for the "minimal physiological conditions" mentioned in the abstract are not defined. The temperature-dependent experiments (Figure 3i, 3j) lack a description of the temperature range and step size.
  - **Why it matters** Without these details, the CD data cannot be evaluated for quality, reproducibility, or correct interpretation. The secondary structure estimates and the conclusions drawn about disorder-to-order transitions are not verifiable. This is a fundamental issue that undermines the experimental foundation of the manuscript.
  - **Resolution test** The authors must provide a complete description of the CD spectroscopy methods, including all buffer conditions, peptide concentrations, temperature protocols, and data processing steps. Ideally, raw CD data (or at least representative spectra with error bars from replicates) should be provided as supplementary material.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness of computational methods
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that MD simulations show the wild-type CTR is disordered while mutants (M58A, DE53/54AA, 58MEID61-AAAA) are more ordered is based on simulations described in the text.
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section, "All parameters were calculated using Charmm36 forcefield using previously used protocols 9." Results section, "Met58 and di-acidic motif control the structural conformations of ORF6-CTR."
  - **Concern** The MD simulation methodology is severely under-reported. The authors state that they used Charmm36 and "previously used protocols," but do not provide any details on the simulation system setup. Critical information is missing, including: the length of the simulations (the text mentions "500 ns long MD simulations" for one mutant, but the length for the wild-type and other mutants is not stated), the number of independent replicates, the water model, the box size and shape, the salt concentration, the temperature and pressure coupling schemes, the equilibration protocol, and the integration time step. The analysis of the trajectories is also not described (e.g., how secondary structure was assigned, how "structural integrity" was quantified, what metrics were used to assess disorder). The statement "which is evident from trajectory analysis" is not a substitute for quantitative analysis.
  - **Why it matters** MD simulations of short disordered peptides are highly sensitive to simulation setup and analysis choices. Without a complete description, the results cannot be reproduced or assessed for convergence and statistical significance. The claim that the mutants are more ordered than the wild-type is a central conclusion of the paper and must be supported by rigorous, well-documented simulations with appropriate error analysis.
  - **Resolution test** The authors must provide a full description of the MD simulation protocol, including system setup, simulation length, number of replicates, and all analysis methods. They should also provide quantitative metrics (e.g., fraction of helix per residue, RMSD, radius of gyration) with error bars from multiple independent simulations to support their claims.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Logical coherence and support for functional claims
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that the disordered nature of ORF6-CTR and the specific residues (Met58, di-acidic motif) are responsible for its interaction with ORF9b and Rae1-Nup98 is made in the Discussion.
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section, "we can speculate that ORF6-CTR’s disordered nature might be responsible for its interaction with ORF9b, and the residues such as 53DE54 and M58 might be responsible for such interactions."
  - **Concern** The leap from the observed in vitro and in silico behavior of an isolated peptide to the functional interactions of the full-length protein within the cellular context is not well-supported. The authors do not provide any direct experimental evidence (e.g., binding assays, mutagenesis in the context of full-length ORF6, cellular assays) linking the conformational properties of the CTR to its interactions with host factors. The discussion is largely speculative and does not adequately address alternative explanations or the limitations of studying an isolated peptide.
  - **Why it matters** The functional significance of the structural observations is a key part of the manuscript's narrative and its potential impact. Overstating the implications without direct evidence weakens the overall argument and may mislead readers.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either provide direct experimental evidence for the functional role of these residues in the context of full-length ORF6, or substantially temper the claims in the Discussion, clearly stating that the functional implications are speculative and require further investigation.

  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity and completeness of figure presentation
  - **Affected element** Figures 1-4
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, Figures 1-4
  - **Issue** The manuscript does not include figure legends. It is impossible to fully interpret the figures without knowing what is plotted, what the axes represent, and what the error bars (if any) indicate.   - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Precision of language
  - **Affected element** Abstract and Discussion
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, "gain of structure properties in minimal physiological conditions"; Discussion, "structural flexibility and possible capabilities to perform multifunctionality"
  - **Issue** The phrase "minimal physiological conditions" is vague and not defined anywhere in the manuscript. The term "gain of structure properties" is awkward and could be more precisely stated as "gain of secondary structure" or "structural stabilization." The phrase "possible capabilities to perform multifunctionality" is speculative and imprecise.
  - **Required correction** Define "minimal physiological conditions" in the Methods section. Use more precise and standard terminology for structural transitions. Rephrase speculative statements to clearly indicate they are hypotheses.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Completeness of references
  - **Affected element** Introduction and Discussion
  - **Evidence pointer** Introduction, references 1-6; Discussion, references 12-24
  - **Issue** Several statements in the Introduction and Discussion are supported by references, but the relevance of some references is not clear from the text. For example, the statement about CTRs being "multifunctional in all life forms" is very broad and the cited references may not fully support it. The authors should ensure that all references are directly relevant to the specific claims they support.
  - **Required correction** Review the reference list and ensure each reference is appropriate for the specific claim it supports. Add references where needed, particularly for the functional claims about ORF6 and its interactions.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The lack of detailed methods for both CD spectroscopy and MD simulations (R1-M1, R1-M2) is a critical technical failing that prevents the evaluation and reproduction of the core results. The absence of figure legends (R1-m1) further hampers interpretation. These issues must be resolved before the claims can be considered established.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** The study is moderately original in its focus on the isolated CTR of ORF6 and the specific residues that may modulate its disorder. However, the general concept of disordered CTRs gaining structure in specific environments is well-established. The specific findings on ORF6 are novel but incremental.
  - **Scientific importance** The importance is moderate. Understanding the structural basis of ORF6 function is relevant to SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis, but the study does not provide direct functional data. The importance would be significantly enhanced by linking the structural observations to functional outcomes.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The topic has potential appeal to structural biologists, virologists, and computational biologists. However, the current presentation is likely too specialized and lacks the broader context or implications that would attract a wider interdisciplinary audience.
  - **Technical soundness** The technical soundness is currently not established due to the incomplete methods and lack of quantitative analyses. The CD data are presented without sufficient detail, and the MD simulations lack the rigor expected for a study making claims about conformational ensembles.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is written in a reasonably clear style, but the lack of figure legends and the use of some vague terminology (e.g., "minimal physiological conditions") make it difficult for nonspecialists to fully grasp the findings. The Discussion is somewhat accessible but could be improved by more clearly separating established findings from speculation.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript addresses a relevant question and has a reasonable conceptual framework, but the technical details are insufficient to support the claims. The authors must provide a complete methods section, quantitative data analysis, and figure legends. They should also temper the functional speculations or provide direct evidence. If these issues are addressed, the manuscript could become a solid contribution to the field.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that ORF6-CTR is intrinsically disordered is supported by prediction and CD data, but the CD data lack methodological detail and quantitative analysis, making the claim only weakly supported.
- The claim that ORF6-CTR gains structure in organic solvents but not in lipids or molecular crowders is based on CD data that are not fully described and lack statistical treatment.
- The claim that Met58 and the di-acidic motif control the structural conformations of ORF6-CTR is based on AlphaFold models and MD simulations that are not adequately described and lack quantitative analysis.
- The claim that the disordered nature of ORF6-CTR and specific residues are responsible for interactions with ORF9b and Rae1-Nup98 is entirely speculative and not supported by any direct experimental evidence in this manuscript.
- The statement that "the structure contains 15 residues long α-helix and then short disorder region" for the di-acidic motif mutant after MD simulations is not supported by any quantitative data or figure showing the final structure.
