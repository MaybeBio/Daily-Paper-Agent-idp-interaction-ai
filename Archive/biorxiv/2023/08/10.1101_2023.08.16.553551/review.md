- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract and significance statement
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that the C-terminal domains (CTDs) of mGluR2 and mGluR3 bind to membranes in a lipid composition-dependent manner, identify conserved residues and cancer-associated mutations that modulate this binding, and provide evidence that CTD-membrane interactions regulate mGluR3 transducer coupling in live cells, proposing this as a general regulatory mechanism across the GPCR superfamily.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract and significance statement only. No figures, tables, methods, or full-text data are available for assessment.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript text, all figures and tables, NMR spectroscopy data and assignments, molecular dynamics simulation parameters and trajectories, mutagenesis constructs and validation, live-cell functional assay details, statistical analyses, and any supplementary information.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting and novel mechanism for GPCR regulation via CTD-membrane interactions. The combination of biophysical, computational, and cellular approaches is appropriate in principle. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the rigor of the experimental design, the quality of the data, or the strength of the conclusions. The claim of generality across the GPCR superfamily is speculative given that only two closely related receptors are studied. The work may be of interest to the GPCR and membrane biophysics communities, but the case is not fully established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in GPCR signaling and pharmacology, particularly those studying metabotropic glutamate receptors and receptor-transducer interactions. Membrane biophysicists interested in intrinsically disordered protein-membrane interactions and lipid sensing. Structural biologists studying GPCR C-terminal domains and their post-translational modifications. Those interested in cancer-associated mutations in GPCRs and their functional consequences.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses an underexplored aspect of GPCR biology, namely the potential lipid-sensing role of CTDs. The combination of NMR, molecular dynamics simulations, mutagenesis, and live-cell functional assays provides a multi-pronged approach. The identification of cancer-associated mutations that modulate CTD-membrane binding could have translational relevance. The proposal of a general regulatory mechanism, if supported, would be conceptually significant.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "mGluR3 transducer coupling is controlled by CTD-membrane interactions in live cells."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that evidence for this claim was obtained in live cells, but no details are provided regarding the assay type, the magnitude of the effect, the specificity of the manipulation, or the controls used. It is unclear whether the observed effects on transducer coupling are directly attributable to CTD-membrane interactions or could be due to other consequences of the mutations or treatments.  
- **Why it matters** This is the central functional claim of the study. Without adequate experimental detail, the reader cannot judge whether the effect is robust, specific, and physiologically relevant.  
- **Resolution test** In the full manuscript, the authors should provide a detailed description of the live-cell assay, including the specific transducer measured, the concentration-response relationships, the effects of CTD mutations on receptor expression and plasma membrane localization, and appropriate controls such as mutations that disrupt membrane binding without affecting other CTD functions.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Generalizability  
- **Claim pointer** The authors propose that "CTD-membrane binding may be a general regulatory mode throughout the broad GPCR superfamily."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The study examines only two closely related receptors, mGluR2 and mGluR3, which share high sequence similarity in their CTDs. The leap from these two receptors to the entire GPCR superfamily is not supported by the data presented in the abstract. No evidence is provided for other GPCR families or even for other members of family C.  
- **Why it matters** The generality claim is a key part of the significance of the work. If the mechanism is specific to these receptors or to family C, the broader impact is substantially reduced.  
- **Resolution test** The authors should either provide additional data on other GPCRs, or temper the claim to reflect the limited scope of the current study. At minimum, a discussion of the sequence features that might predict CTD-membrane binding across the superfamily would be needed.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic detail  
- **Claim pointer** The authors report "lipid composition-dependent modes of membrane binding" characterized by NMR spectroscopy.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not specify which lipids were tested, what differences in binding modes were observed, or how these relate to physiologically relevant membrane compositions. The functional relevance of the lipid dependence is also not clarified.  
- **Why it matters** The lipid dependence is a central aspect of the proposed mechanism and is necessary to understand how membrane composition might regulate GPCR function in different cellular contexts.  
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should provide a clear description of the lipid systems used, the NMR observables that report on binding, and a quantitative comparison of binding affinities or modes across lipid compositions. The physiological relevance of the tested lipids should be discussed.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mutational interpretation  
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "key conserved residues and cancer-associated mutations that modulate CTD-membrane binding" were identified.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not indicate which residues were identified, how the cancer-associated mutations were selected, or whether the effects of these mutations on membrane binding were validated in functional assays. The relationship between the cancer-associated mutations and the proposed regulatory mechanism is unclear.  
- **Why it matters** The identification of specific residues and disease-associated mutations is important for the translational relevance of the work and for understanding the molecular determinants of CTD-membrane interactions.  
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should list the specific residues and mutations, provide structural or simulation-based rationale for their roles, and show functional data linking these mutations to changes in receptor signaling or transducer coupling.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1  
- **Severity** Minor  
- **Axis** Clarity  
- **Affected element** Abstract wording  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Issue** The phrase "membrane-interacting intrinsically disordered C-terminal domains" in the title is somewhat redundant, as intrinsically disordered regions are often defined by their lack of stable structure, and the membrane interaction is the key novel feature.  
- **Required correction** Consider simplifying the title to emphasize the novel finding, such as "Membrane interactions of GPCR C-terminal domains regulate receptor function."

- **Concern ID** R1-m2  
- **Severity** Minor  
- **Axis** Contextualization  
- **Affected element** Significance statement  
- **Evidence pointer** Significance statement, location not provided  
- **Issue** The significance statement does not mention prior work on GPCR CTD interactions with membranes or other lipids, which would help position the novelty of the findings.  
- **Required correction** Add a sentence in the significance statement or introduction acknowledging any prior related observations and clearly stating what is new in this work.

- **Concern ID** R1-m3  
- **Severity** Minor  
- **Axis** Terminology  
- **Affected element** Abstract  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
- **Issue** The term "transducer coupling" is used without specifying which transducers (G proteins, GRKs, arrestins) are affected. This is important because different transducers may be regulated differently by CTD-membrane interactions.  
- **Required correction** Specify in the abstract which transducer or transducers were measured in the live-cell assays.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that CTD-membrane binding is a "general regulatory mode throughout the broad GPCR superfamily" is unsupported by the data presented, as only two closely related receptors were studied.
- The claim that "mGluR3 transducer coupling is controlled by CTD-membrane interactions in live cells" is not adequately supported by the abstract alone, as no experimental details are provided.
- The functional relevance of the lipid composition-dependent binding modes is not established from the abstract.
- The relationship between the identified cancer-associated mutations and the proposed regulatory mechanism is not explained in the abstract.
- The physiological relevance of the CTD-membrane interactions under native membrane conditions is not assessable from the supplied material.
