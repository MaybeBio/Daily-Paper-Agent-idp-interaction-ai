## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting model linking SOSS1, RNAPII, and LLPS at DSBs, but the evidence base is entirely inaccessible. The claims are broad and mechanistically detailed, yet no data are shown to support them. The work could be of interest to the DNA repair and transcription fields, but the current submission cannot be evaluated for technical soundness or scientific robustness from the abstract alone.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying DNA double-strand break repair, RNA polymerase II function, R-loop biology, and biomolecular condensates would be interested. The proposed connection between transcription-coupled repair and phase separation could appeal to those investigating nuclear organization and DNA damage responses.
- **Major strengths** The abstract identifies a novel mechanistic link between SOSS1, Y1P RNAPII, and LLPS at DSBs, which is conceptually interesting. The inclusion of both in vitro and in vivo LLPS evidence suggests an attempt at multi-level validation, and the focus on a trimeric complex with a specific phosphorylation event provides a testable model.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the SOSS1 complex and RNAPII form dynamic repair compartments at DSBs via LLPS, demonstrated both in vitro and in vivo.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no figures or methods provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract asserts LLPS formation without presenting any experimental data, such as microscopy images, fluorescence recovery after photobleaching (FRAP) data, or in vitro droplet assays. No controls or quantitative metrics are described.  
  - **Why it matters** LLPS is a mechanistically specific claim that requires rigorous demonstration, including concentration dependence, reversibility, and physiological relevance. Without these data, the central conclusion is unsupported.  
  - **Resolution test** Provide representative images and quantitative analyses of phase separation in vitro and in vivo, including FRAP or similar dynamic measurements, and demonstrate that the observed compartments are not artifacts of fixation or overexpression.

  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no experimental support provided  
  - **Concern** The phosphorylation event is presented as a causal step, but no data are shown to confirm the kinase-substrate relationship, the specific residue(s) modified, or the functional consequence of phosphorylation on binding and recruitment.
