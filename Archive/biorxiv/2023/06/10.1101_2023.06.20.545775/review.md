## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods) from bioRxiv preprint
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, validity of claims, and presentation quality as presented in the provided material
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present integrative structural models of the intrinsically disordered protein 4E-BP2 in two phosphorylation states (non-phosphorylated and five-fold phosphorylated), generated by combining Rosetta-based sampling (FastFloppyTail) with Bayesian Maximum Entropy refinement against NMR, SAXS, and smFRET data. They report distinct conformational features for each state, including charge- and pi-driven long-range contacts in the NP state and phosphorylation-dependent interactions between the N-IDR and folded domain in the 5P state. Clustering analysis reveals sub-populations with distinct structural characteristics, one of which resembles the eIF4E-bound state.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (Figs. 1-7), tables (Tables 1-2), supplementary figure references (Figs. S1-S11), supplementary tables (Tables S1-S8), and methods sections 4.1-4.6
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary information (detailed methods, additional figures and tables) not provided; experimental data files not available; specific parameter values for BME optimization not fully detailed in main text; validation metrics for clustering quality not shown

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a technically sophisticated integrative modelling study of an important intrinsically disordered protein, 4E-BP2, in two phosphorylation states. The combination of multiple experimental restraints (NMR, SAXS, smFRET) with a Rosetta-based sampling approach and Bayesian refinement is appropriate and current. The authors provide a detailed analysis of conformational features, including charge segregation effects and phosphorylation-dependent interactions, and use clustering to identify structurally distinct sub-populations. The work addresses a significant gap in understanding how phosphorylation regulates 4E-BP2 function through conformational changes. However, several technical concerns regarding the validation of the ensembles, the interpretation of clustering results, and the robustness of the conclusions need to be addressed. The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, though some methodological details require clarification.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying intrinsically disordered proteins, particularly those interested in phosphorylation-dependent regulation of protein function; structural biologists using integrative modelling approaches; biophysicists working on protein conformational ensembles; and investigators studying translational regulation and the eIF4E/4E-BP axis in cell biology and disease contexts.
- **Major strengths** 1) The study addresses a biologically important question about how phosphorylation regulates the structure and function of an IDP. 2) The integrative approach combining multiple complementary experimental techniques is methodologically rigorous and appropriate for the system. 3) The use of withheld validation data (PRE) to tune hyperparameters is a good practice that strengthens confidence in the results. 4) The clustering analysis provides a useful framework for interpreting heterogeneous ensembles. 5) The authors provide specific, testable predictions about intramolecular interactions that could guide future experiments.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Validation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the BME-optimized ensembles "agree with experimental data" from NMR, SAXS, and smFRET, and that the PRE data were used for validation.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2, Fig. 1, Tables 1-2, Section 4.2
  - **Concern** The manuscript does not provide sufficient quantitative assessment of how well the optimized ensembles reproduce the experimental data. While χ² values are mentioned, the individual contributions from each restraint type (SAXS, CS, FRET) are not clearly presented. More critically, the validation using PRE data is described qualitatively ("PRE RMSD follows a similar downward trend"), but the actual agreement between back-calculated and experimental PRE data for the final optimized ensembles is not shown. The choice of the "knee" point for N_eff selection appears somewhat subjective, and the manuscript does not demonstrate that the selected N_eff values provide statistically robust agreement with all restraint types simultaneously.
  - **Why it matters** The central claim of the paper is that the ensembles are experimentally validated. Without clear quantitative evidence of agreement with all restraint types, including the validation data, the reliability of the structural conclusions cannot be assessed. The selection of the refinement endpoint (N_eff) is critical, as over-refinement could lead to overfitting while under-refinement would not adequately capture the experimental constraints.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a table or figure showing the final χ² values for each restraint type separately for both NP and 5P ensembles. Show the agreement between back-calculated and experimental PRE data for the final optimized ensembles, including a quantitative metric (e.g., RMSD values). Demonstrate that the choice of N_eff is robust by showing the sensitivity of the results to reasonable variations in this parameter.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Clustering analysis
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that clustering partitions the ensembles into four distinct structural states with "different global dimensions and contact maps" and that these clusters represent meaningful conformational sub-populations.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2, Fig. 5, Section 4.3, Figs. S6-S8
  - **Concern** The clustering methodology and its validation are not adequately described. The manuscript does not provide information on how the number of clusters was determined beyond a brief mention of L-curve analysis, and no metrics of clustering quality (e.g., silhouette scores, cophenetic correlation) are presented. The dendrogram in Fig. 5A is mentioned but not shown in the provided material. The biological significance of the clusters is inferred from contact maps, but the statistical significance of the differences between clusters is not established. The manuscript also does not address whether the clustering results are stable across different random seeds or subsampling of the ensemble.
  - **Why it matters** The clustering analysis is a key component of the paper's conclusions about distinct structural states. If the clustering is not robust or the number of clusters is not well-justified, the interpretation of distinct conformational sub-populations and their functional implications becomes questionable.
  - **Resolution test** Provide details on the clustering algorithm parameters, the method for determining the optimal number of clusters, and quantitative metrics of clustering quality. Show that the identified clusters are stable (e.g., through bootstrap analysis or repeated clustering with different initial conditions). Provide statistical comparisons of the contact maps and global properties between clusters to demonstrate that they are significantly different.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance / Interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the NP cluster resembling the eIF4E-bound state provides evidence for conformational selection as a binding mechanism, and that the 5P clustering reveals a mechanism for stabilization of the folded domain by the C-IDR involving the N-IDR as both a chaperone and an inhibitor.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 2 (Clustering results), Section 3 (Discussion)
  - **Concern** The functional interpretations go beyond what the data can directly support. The identification of a "bound-state-like" cluster in the apo ensemble is suggestive but does not establish a conformational selection mechanism without direct evidence of binding kinetics or population shifts upon eIF4E addition. Similarly, the proposed mechanism for C-IDR stabilization of the folded domain, while plausible, is presented as a narrative that is not directly tested by the data. The authors acknowledge this is a hypothesis, but the discussion presents it with more certainty than the evidence warrants.
  - **Why it matters** Overinterpretation of structural data can mislead the field and lead to incorrect models of protein function. The distinction between data-supported conclusions and speculative hypotheses is important for the scientific community.
  - **Resolution test** Clearly label the proposed binding mechanism and the C-IDR stabilization mechanism as hypotheses that are consistent with but not directly proven by the data. Consider adding a paragraph explicitly discussing alternative interpretations and what additional experiments would distinguish between them.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Ensemble generation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that the 5P initial ensemble was generated by fixing the folded domain structures from NMR and sampling only the N- and C-terminal IDRs with FFT.
  - **Evidence pointer** Section 4.1
  - **Concern** The manuscript does not address potential limitations of fixing the folded domain during sampling. The NMR structures represent an ensemble of conformations, and fixing them may bias the results. Additionally, the junction regions between the folded domain and the IDRs may have specific conformational preferences that are not captured by simply concatenating the structures. The authors do not discuss how sensitive their results are to the choice of the 20 NMR structures or to the treatment of the folded domain.
  - **Why it matters** The treatment of the folded domain could significantly affect the resulting ensemble and the conclusions about IDR-folded domain interactions, which are central to the paper's findings.
  - **Resolution test** Discuss the potential impact of fixing the folded domain and justify this choice. If feasible, perform a sensitivity analysis using different subsets of NMR structures or allowing some flexibility in the folded domain. Alternatively, provide evidence that the results are robust to these choices.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (validation of ensemble agreement with experimental data) and R1-M2 (clustering robustness and validation) are the most critical technical issues. Without addressing these, the central claims of the paper cannot be fully evaluated.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** The work is original in its application of integrative modelling to this specific system and in the combination of techniques used. The focus on phosphorylation-dependent conformational changes in 4E-BP2 is timely and relevant. However, the methodological approach itself is not novel, building on established techniques.
  - **Scientific importance** The 4E-BP2/eIF4E system is biologically important, and understanding how phosphorylation regulates its function has implications for translational control and disease. The structural insights provided could be valuable for the field. However, the importance is somewhat limited by the lack of direct functional validation of the proposed mechanisms.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The work bridges structural biology, biophysics, and cell biology, and would be of interest to researchers in these areas. The integrative approach is relevant to a broad community studying protein structure and dynamics.
  - **Technical soundness** The methodology is generally sound, but the validation concerns raised above (R1-M1, R1-M2) need to be addressed. The use of multiple experimental restraints and withheld validation data is a strength, but the quantitative assessment of agreement is insufficient.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is generally well-written and the main concepts are accessible. However, some specialized terminology and methodological details could be explained more clearly for a broader audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses an important question with a sophisticated approach, and the findings have potential value. However, the validation of the ensembles and the robustness of the clustering analysis need to be demonstrated more rigorously before the conclusions can be fully accepted.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the NP cluster is "compatible with the eIF4E-bound state" is based on comparison of FRET efficiencies but does not constitute direct structural evidence of binding competence.
- The proposed mechanism for C-IDR stabilization of the folded domain via N-IDR chaperone/inhibitor activity is speculative and not directly tested.
- The statement that "charge segregation and pi interactions" drive contacts is inferred from correlation with NCPR analysis but not directly demonstrated experimentally.
- The claim that the 5P ensemble has "fewer and deeper structural wells" than the NP ensemble is based on clustering characteristics but lacks statistical validation.
- The manuscript states that "smFRET experiments are currently under way" to probe different segments, implying that the current data may be insufficient to fully support the binding mechanism claims.
