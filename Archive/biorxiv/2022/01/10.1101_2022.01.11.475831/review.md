## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and conclusion sections. Figures and supplementary materials referenced but not provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, methodological soundness, clarity of claims, and adequacy of evidence as presented in the supplied text. No assessment of code, data availability, or server functionality beyond stated claims.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose RocketX, a de novo protein structure prediction method that integrates a geometric constraint prediction network (GeomNet), a structure simulation module, and a model quality assessment network (EmaNet) in a closed-loop feedback mechanism. They claim that this feedback improves prediction accuracy iteratively, outperforming trRosetta (without templates) and RaptorX on benchmark proteins and CASP14 FM targets, and achieving comparable performance to template-based methods on CAMEO blind tests.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, full methods section, results sections 3.1–3.5, tables referenced (Tables 1–4), figures referenced (Figures 1–7), and supplementary materials referenced (Figures S1–S6, Tables S1–S5). Actual figures, tables, and supplementary files not provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures 1–7, Tables 1–4, all supplementary figures and tables, detailed network architecture diagrams, benchmark protein lists, MSA generation details, and CAMEO target identifiers. Without these, quantitative claims cannot be independently verified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a plausible and potentially valuable approach to integrating model quality assessment into de novo protein structure prediction via a feedback loop. The core idea is timely and relevant given the rapid advances in deep learning for structural biology. However, the evidence as presented is incomplete. Key quantitative results are only referenced in tables and figures that are not provided, making it impossible to assess the magnitude of improvements, statistical significance, or robustness of the claims. The methodological description is detailed but lacks critical specifics on training data, hyperparameters, and validation procedures. The comparison with state-of-the-art methods is limited to two baselines and a short CAMEO window, which weakens the generalizability of the conclusions. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of the feedback mechanism's contribution and a more rigorous benchmarking strategy.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists and bioinformaticians working on protein structure prediction, particularly those interested in deep learning approaches, model quality assessment, and iterative refinement strategies. The closed-loop feedback concept may also appeal to researchers studying protein folding mechanisms and the interpretability of deep learning models in structural biology.
- **Major strengths** 1) The closed-loop feedback mechanism is a novel and conceptually interesting integration of model quality assessment into structure prediction, addressing a recognized gap in the field. 2) The use of both 1D and 2D features in EmaNet, including residue-level USR features, is thoughtful and potentially more informative than simpler quality metrics. 3) The authors evaluate on multiple benchmarks (483 proteins, CASP14 FM targets, CAMEO blind test), which demonstrates an effort to validate across different settings. 4) The manuscript is generally well-structured and the writing is clear, making the approach accessible to a broad audience.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes) The core quantitative claims are not verifiable from the provided text. Tables 1–4 and Figures 1–7 are referenced but not included, and no numerical results are reported in the text. Without these data, the claimed improvements over trRosetta and RaptorX, the contribution of the feedback mechanism, and the CAMEO performance cannot be assessed.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes) The description of the training and validation procedures is incomplete. Key details such as the exact split of training/validation/test sets, hyperparameter selection, early stopping criteria, and the handling of potential data leakage between the GeomNet and EmaNet training are not specified. This limits the reproducibility of the method.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No) The comparison with state-of-the-art methods is limited. Only trRosetta and RaptorX are used as baselines on the benchmark set, and the CAMEO comparison is restricted to a single month. Given the rapid progress in the field, a broader comparison including more recent methods (e.g., AlphaFold2, RoseTTAFold) on the same benchmarks would strengthen the claims of superiority.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No) The manuscript does not clearly explain how the feedback from EmaNet is incorporated into GeomNet. Specifically, the mechanism by which the predicted inter-residue distance deviation and per-residue lDDT are converted into "dynamic features" for GeomNet is not described in sufficient detail. This is a critical component of the proposed closed-loop system.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity, Affected element: Section 2.2, Evidence pointer: "location not provided") The description of the binning for distance and orientation predictions is incomplete. The text states "36 equidistant bins in step of 0.5Å" but does not specify the range or the handling of distances beyond 20Å. Clarification is needed.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Reproducibility, Affected element: Section 2.2, Evidence pointer: "location not provided") The MSA generation procedure is described as using HHblits with "gradually relaxed e-value cutoffs," but the specific databases, number of iterations, and alignment depth are not stated. This information is essential for reproducibility.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Statistical rigor, Affected element: Section 3.2, Evidence pointer: "location not provided") The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is mentioned, but the exact p-values or effect sizes are not reported. Providing these would strengthen the statistical claims.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity, Affected element: Section 3.5, Evidence pointer: "location not provided") The CAMEO results are described only in terms of rankings. It would be helpful to report the actual TM-score and lDDT values for each method to allow quantitative comparison.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Completeness, Affected element: Section 2.4, Evidence pointer: "location not provided") The definition of the per-residue lDDT formula is incomplete. The equation is referenced but not fully written out, and the variables are not defined. This should be corrected.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 and R1-M2 are blocking. The absence of quantitative results and incomplete methodological details prevent the core claims from being evaluated. Additionally, the lack of clarity on the feedback mechanism (R1-M4) undermines the central novelty of the approach.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The closed-loop feedback concept is original and distinguishes this work from standard one-pass prediction methods. However, the novelty is somewhat incremental given the existing use of quality assessment in refinement pipelines.
  - Scientific importance: If the claims are substantiated, the work could have significant impact on de novo structure prediction, particularly for template-free modeling. The potential to improve accuracy iteratively is of broad interest.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The topic is of interest to computational biologists, structural biologists, and machine learning researchers. The writing is accessible, though some domain-specific terms may require familiarity with protein structure prediction.
  - Technical soundness: The methodological description is plausible but incomplete. The lack of training details and the absence of quantitative results prevent a full assessment of technical soundness.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and the high-level concept is clear. However, the methods section is dense and may be challenging for readers without a background in deep learning or structural biology.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript presents a promising idea, but the absence of quantitative results and incomplete methodological details are blocking issues. The authors should provide the missing data and clarify the feedback mechanism before the claims can be evaluated.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that RocketX outperforms trRosetta and RaptorX on the benchmark set is unsupported without the actual numerical results from Tables 2 and 3.
- The claim that the closed-loop feedback mechanism "significantly contributes" to performance is unsupported without the ablation results from Table 3.
- The claim that RocketX achieves comparable performance to template-based methods on CAMEO is unsupported without the actual scores from Figure 6 and Supplementary Figures S5–S6.
- The claim that EmaNet's feedback improves GeomNet's predictions is not verifiable without a detailed description of the feedback mechanism and supporting data.
- The claim that the branch network architecture in GeomNet is superior to a simpler trunk-only architecture is unsupported without the results from Table 4.
