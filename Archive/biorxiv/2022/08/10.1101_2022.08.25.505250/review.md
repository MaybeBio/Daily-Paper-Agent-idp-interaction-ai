## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no access to figures, tables, methods, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that glutamate (Glu) and aspartate (Asp) are differentially enriched in intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs), with Glu more enriched than Asp. Using the disordered protein Dss1 and three Glu/Asp variants, they combine interaction studies, NMR spectroscopy, SAXS, and MD simulations to show that Glu and Asp support similar global dimensions and function, but differ in local transient structural elements and binding affinities. They further propose that these local differences may underlie functional diversity, with Glu promoting helicity and Asp favoring extended structures and helical caps.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no quantitative data, statistical details, or methodological specifics are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, methods section, statistical analyses, sequence variant details, and any supplementary information

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting and timely question about the differential roles of two chemically similar amino acids in IDPs. The combination of bioinformatics, biophysical methods, and functional assays is appropriate in principle. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient quantitative evidence to evaluate the robustness of the claims. The central conclusion that Glu and Asp differ in local but not global properties is plausible but not established from the supplied material. The speculative functional interpretation, while reasonable, extends beyond the data presented in the abstract.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of intrinsically disordered proteins, protein biophysics, and molecular recognition would find these results relevant. The work addresses a fundamental question about sequence-ensemble-function relationships in IDPs, which is of broad interest to those studying protein folding, binding, and cellular signaling. The potential link to transactivation domains and ion binding may also attract investigators in transcriptional regulation and metallobiology.
- **Major strengths** The question is well framed and addresses a gap in understanding of IDP sequence determinants. The use of multiple complementary techniques (NMR, SAXS, MD, interaction assays, cell growth) is a strong approach. The inclusion of DisProt database analysis to rule out taxonomy bias is a thoughtful control. The study leverages a natural model system (Dss1) with designed variants, which is a sound experimental strategy.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that Glu and Asp variants "differ in their binding affinities and population of local transient structural elements" while supporting "similar function and global dimensions."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that differences exist in binding affinities and local structural populations, but no quantitative data, statistical measures, or effect magnitudes are reported. It is impossible to assess whether these differences are robust, reproducible, or biologically meaningful.  
- **Why it matters** The central conclusion of the paper rests on the existence of local differences despite global similarity. Without quantitative support, the claim is unverifiable and the distinction between local and global effects cannot be established.  
- **Resolution test** Provide numerical values with confidence intervals or p-values for binding affinities and for populations of transient secondary structure elements, along with a clear description of the number of independent replicates and the statistical tests used.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Generalizability  
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "Glu/Asp support similar function and global dimensions" based on a single model protein (Dss1) and three variants.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The study uses one acidic IDP from yeast and three Glu/Asp variants. The abstract does not specify the positions of substitutions, the sequence context, or whether the variants represent conservative or extreme changes. The generalizability of the findings to other IDPs is therefore unclear.  
- **Why it matters** The title and framing suggest a general "alphabet of disorder," but the evidence base is narrow. If the observed effects are specific to Dss1 or to the particular substitution sites, the broader conclusion would be overstated.  
- **Resolution test** Clarify the design of the variants, including sequence positions and rationale. Discuss whether the observed local effects are consistent across multiple IDP systems or provide evidence that Dss1 is a representative model.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Interpretive overreach  
- **Claim pointer** The authors speculate that "glutamates can support increased helicity important for folding and binding, while aspartates support extended structures and form helical caps, as well as playing more relevant roles in e.g., transactivation domains and ion-binding."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** This speculation extends beyond the data presented in the abstract. While the authors appropriately use the word "speculate," the functional implications are broad and not directly tested. The link between local structural differences and roles in transactivation or ion binding is not supported by any evidence in the abstract.  
- **Why it matters** Overinterpretation can mislead readers into treating hypotheses as established findings. The functional claims may attract attention but could be premature without direct experimental tests.  
- **Resolution test** Either provide direct experimental evidence linking the observed local structural differences to functional outcomes, or clearly frame these as untested hypotheses requiring future work.
