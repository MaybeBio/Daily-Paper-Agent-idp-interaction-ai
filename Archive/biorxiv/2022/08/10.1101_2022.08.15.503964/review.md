### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that FUS condensates exhibit coexisting liquid and gel phases within the same condensate, resulting in highly inhomogeneous structures.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no figures or data provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that the authors measured changes in structure, dynamics, and mechanics in time and space, but no data, quantification, or representative results are shown. The claim of coexisting liquid and gel phases is central to the paper, yet the evidence for this coexistence cannot be evaluated.  
- **Why it matters** The coexistence of liquid and gel phases is the foundational observation of the study. Without visible data demonstrating this phenomenon, the claim is unsupported.  
- **Resolution test** Provide representative microscopy images, rheological or mechanical measurements, and quantitative analyses showing distinct liquid and gel regions within the same condensate, with appropriate controls.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the liquid-to-solid transition is initiated at the interface between the dense phase within condensates and the dilute phase.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no figures or data provided  
- **Concern** The identification of the interface as the initiation site is a specific and strong claim. The abstract does not describe how this was determined, what criteria were used to define initiation, or how the two new optical techniques were applied to reach this conclusion.  
- **Why it matters** This is the central mechanistic claim of the paper. If the initiation site is incorrectly identified, the conclusions about the spatiotemporal dimension of the transition would be invalid.  
- **Resolution test** Provide time-resolved imaging data with clear spatial markers showing that solidification begins at the interface and propagates inward, with statistical support and appropriate controls for potential artifacts.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
