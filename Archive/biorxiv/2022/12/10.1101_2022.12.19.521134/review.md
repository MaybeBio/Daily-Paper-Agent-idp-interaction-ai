## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, results and discussion, conclusion, materials and methods, and supplementary figure descriptions.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, methodological soundness, interpretation of results, and clarity of the presented integrated SANS/MD/DL approach for characterizing IDP ensembles.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present an integrated workflow combining residue-specific deuterium-labeled SANS, microsecond all-atom MD simulations with four force fields, and a convolutional variational autoencoder to characterize the structural ensemble of the NCBD/ACTR IDP complex. They claim that Rg-based classification of MD structures yields an ensemble that better recapitulates experimental SANS and NMR data than any single-force-field ensemble, and that DL analysis reveals three distinct conformational clusters.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, full introduction, full results and discussion, conclusion, materials and methods, and references to supplementary figures and tables (Figures S1-S6, Table S1).
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables (S1-S6, Table S1) are referenced but not provided. Raw SANS data, MD trajectory details, and CVAE training/validation curves are not available for independent verification.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a potentially valuable methodological advance in combining selective deuteration SANS with MD simulations and deep learning to study IDP complexes. The concept is timely and the integration of experimental constraints with computational ensemble generation is a promising direction. However, the current manuscript has several technical and interpretive gaps that need to be addressed before the central claims are fully established. The lack of supplementary materials for verification, limited discussion of potential biases in the Rg-based selection, and insufficient detail on the DL analysis weaken the current case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of intrinsically disordered proteins, structural biology, biophysics, and computational chemistry would be interested. The work is also relevant to those developing integrated experimental-computational workflows and applying machine learning to biomolecular ensembles. The methodological framework could be adapted to other IDP systems and scattering techniques.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a challenging problem (IDP complex ensemble characterization) with a multi-pronged approach. The use of residue-specific deuteration in SANS is a sophisticated experimental design that provides more localized information than traditional contrast matching. The systematic comparison of four force fields is thorough. The application of a CVAE for unsupervised structural classification is innovative and could be broadly useful. The validation against both SANS and NMR data strengthens the conclusions.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the Rg-based classification produces an ensemble that "captures the experimental SANS and, in addition, NMR data better than MD ensembles generated by one single force field."
  - **Evidence pointer** Figures 2 and 3, Section "Results and Discussion", location not provided
  - **Concern** The selection of structures based on Rg constraints introduces a potential circularity. The Rg values are derived from the same SANS data used to validate the final ensemble. Selecting structures that match Rg and then showing that the selected ensemble matches the SANS data is not an independent validation. The authors need to clarify whether the comparison in Figure 3A-C is truly independent or if it is a self-consistency check. A more rigorous validation would involve withholding a portion of the scattering data (e.g., high-Q region) from the selection process and testing the ensemble against it.
  - **Why it matters** If the validation is circular, the central claim that the selected ensemble is superior is weakened. The scientific value of the approach depends on demonstrating that the Rg-based selection genuinely improves ensemble quality beyond what a single force field provides, not just that it reproduces the constraints used for selection.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should perform a cross-validation where the Rg constraints are derived from a subset of the SANS data (e.g., low-Q only) and the resulting ensemble is tested against the full scattering profile. Alternatively, they could use a different experimental observable (e.g., NMR chemical shifts) as the sole selection criterion and test against SANS, or vice versa.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "DL reveals three clusters of distinct conformations in the ensemble" and use these clusters to characterize the structural heterogeneity.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figures 5 and 6, Section "Results and Discussion", location not provided
  - **Concern** The CVAE analysis is applied only to the 12,270 Rg-satisfying structures, which represent only 11.4% of the total sampled structures. The authors do not discuss whether the clustering results are sensitive to this pre-filtering. It is possible that the three clusters are an artifact of the Rg-based selection rather than a genuine feature of the underlying conformational landscape. Additionally, the choice of a 3-D latent space is stated to be for visualization purposes, but the authors do not justify that this dimensionality is sufficient to capture the relevant structural diversity.
  - **Why it matters** The interpretation of the three clusters as distinct conformational states is a key result. If the clustering is an artifact of the selection process or the latent space dimensionality, the biological conclusions about the ensemble composition could be incorrect.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should apply the CVAE to the full set of 108,020 structures (or a representative subset) without Rg filtering and compare the clustering results. They should also test different latent space dimensions (e.g., 2-D, 4-D, 5-D) to assess the robustness of the three-cluster finding.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that their integrated method is a "new integrated approach for characterizing structural ensembles of IDPs" and that it provides insights beyond what single techniques offer.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Introduction, Conclusion, location not provided
  - **Concern** While the integration is novel, the authors do not clearly articulate what new biological or biophysical insight is gained about the NCBD/ACTR complex specifically. The three clusters are described in terms of RMSD ranges and flexible regions, but the functional significance of these clusters is not discussed. How do these conformations relate to the known function of NCBD/ACTR in transcriptional regulation? What is the biological relevance of the 37%/49%/14% composition?
  - **Why it matters** For a Nature-level audience, the methodological advance alone may not be sufficient. The work needs to demonstrate that the approach can uncover biologically meaningful information about IDP function that is not accessible by other means.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should expand the discussion to connect the identified conformational clusters to functional hypotheses. For example, do the clusters differ in their ability to bind other partners? Are the flexible regions known to be important for post-translational modifications or protein-protein interactions?

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** The combination of residue-specific deuteration SANS with MD and DL is novel and represents a creative integration of techniques. The application of CVAE to IDP ensembles is innovative.
  - **Scientific importance** The methodological framework has potential broad applicability to IDP research, but the current manuscript does not demonstrate a major biological discovery about NCBD/ACTR. The importance is more methodological than biological at this stage.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The work bridges neutron scattering, molecular dynamics, and machine learning, which could appeal to a broad readership. However, the presentation is somewhat technical and may not be accessible to all.
  - **Technical soundness** The experimental and computational methods are generally sound, but the circularity concern (R1-M1) and the potential artifact in clustering (R1-M2) need to be addressed. The lack of supplementary data for verification is a significant issue.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is clearly written but assumes significant background knowledge. The introduction and figure captions could be improved for a broader audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The methodological framework is promising and the work addresses an important problem. However, the circularity in validation and the potential artifact in clustering must be addressed, and the biological significance of the findings needs to be more clearly articulated.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the Rg-based ensemble "captures the experimental SANS and, in addition, NMR data better than MD ensembles generated by one single force field" is potentially circular and not fully supported without independent validation.
- The claim that "DL reveals three clusters of distinct conformations" is not robustly supported because the analysis was performed only on the pre-filtered Rg-satisfying subset, and the sensitivity to this filtering was not tested.
- The statement that "the flexibility of labeling choice in combination with varying contrast of D2O in the solvent offers an auspicious option" is speculative and not directly demonstrated by the data presented.
- The biological relevance of the three identified clusters is not established; the connection to NCBD/ACTR function is not discussed.
