## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that polyacrylic acid-polyethylene glycol coacervate droplets enhance the equilibrium concentration of a fluorescent imine product of a model condensation reaction by approximately 140-fold relative to bulk solution. They attribute this enhancement to preferential partitioning of reactants and products into the droplets and to a roughly threefold increase in the reaction equilibrium constant within the droplets. A reaction-diffusion model is claimed to quantitatively describe the spatial distribution of the imine product. The authors conclude that compartmentalization stabilizes kinetically labile products and propose a general mechanism for biomolecular condensates to promote multistep reactions and molecular complexity.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental methods, numerical data, model equations, or figure/table references are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, experimental protocols, raw data, model derivation and parameter values, statistical analyses, and all figures and tables

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting finding that coacervate droplets can shift the equilibrium of a reversible small molecule reaction and stabilize a labile product. The quantitative claims, particularly the 140-fold enhancement and the threefold change in equilibrium constant, are specific and testable. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the rigor of the experiments, the validity of the model, or the generality of the conclusions. The mechanistic interpretation is plausible but not fully supported by the evidence presented in the abstract. The broader claim about promoting molecular complexity is speculative and not directly demonstrated.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of membraneless organelles, liquid-liquid phase separation, synthetic protocells, and prebiotic chemistry would find these results relevant. The work addresses how compartmentalization can alter reaction equilibria and stabilize reactive intermediates, which is of interest to those studying biochemical reaction networks in condensates and to those exploring abiotic pathways for the origin of life. The quantitative modeling approach may also appeal to physical chemists and biophysicists working on reaction-diffusion systems.
- **Major strengths** The study combines experimental measurements with quantitative modeling, which is a rigorous approach for understanding complex phenomena. The use of a well-defined model reaction and a specific coacervate system allows for clear interpretation. The reported fold-enhancement values are concrete and provide a quantitative benchmark for future studies. The focus on reversible reactions and unstable intermediates addresses an important gap in the coacervate literature, which often emphasizes irreversible enzymatic reactions.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Technical soundness  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the imine product concentration in coacervate droplets is approximately 140-fold larger than in bulk solution at equilibrium.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no figure or table reference provided  
    **Concern** The abstract does not describe how the 140-fold enhancement was measured. It is unclear whether this value represents a concentration ratio at a single condition or an average across multiple conditions. The abstract also does not specify how equilibrium was verified, what the time scale of equilibration was, or whether the imine product is stable enough to allow accurate quantification. Without these details, the reliability of the fold-enhancement value cannot be assessed.  
    **Why it matters** The 140-fold enhancement is a central quantitative claim that underpins the entire conclusion. If the measurement is flawed or the value is not reproducible, the main finding would be undermined.  
    **Resolution test** Provide experimental details on the measurement method, including calibration, detection limits, and controls for imine degradation. Report the number of replicates and the variability across conditions. Show that equilibrium was reached and that the enhancement is consistent across a range of reactant concentrations.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Technical soundness  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the reaction equilibrium constant is roughly threefold larger in coacervate droplets than in solution.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no figure or table reference provided  
    **Concern** The abstract does not explain how the equilibrium constant was determined in each phase. It is not clear whether the value was derived from direct concentration measurements in both phases or inferred from the model.     **Resolution test** Describe the method for measuring or calculating the equilibrium constant in both phases. Provide error estimates and discuss the role of activity coefficients. Show that the threefold value is statistically significant and not an artifact of the model assumptions.
