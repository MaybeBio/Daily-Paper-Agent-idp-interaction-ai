## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that Hsp70 inhibits IAPP aggregation at substoichiometric concentrations under diverse solution conditions, including ATP-free conditions, with the strongest effect when added early, suggesting nucleation prevention. Ensemble binding measurements indicate weak Hsp70-IAPP interactions, leading the authors to hypothesize involvement of rare oligomeric species. Size exclusion chromatography and field flow fractionation, combined with multiangle light scattering and fluorescence correlation spectroscopy, reveal a dominant fraction containing nanomolar Hsp70-IAPP complexes amid micromolar free Hsp70. Single-particle two-color coincidence detection identifies a minor fraction with fluorescence bursts from heterogeneous oligomeric complexes. The authors conclude that Hsp70 binds poorly to monomers but strongly to oligomers, proposing this as a generic feature of Hsp70 interactions with amyloidogenic IDPs.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental details, numerical data, or methodological specifics provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, experimental protocols, raw data, statistical analyses, and supplementary information

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a plausible and mechanistically interesting hypothesis that Hsp70 inhibits IAPP aggregation through preferential binding to oligomeric intermediates rather than monomers. The experimental strategy combining bulk measurements with single-particle techniques is thoughtful and potentially powerful. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient quantitative detail to evaluate the robustness of the claims. Key issues include the absence of specific binding affinities, the lack of stoichiometry information for the detected complexes, and the unquantified relationship between the minor oligomeric fraction and the observed inhibition. The claim of generality to other amyloidogenic IDPs is speculative given the single-system evidence presented.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in protein misfolding and amyloid diseases, molecular chaperone biology, and biophysical chemistry would find these results relevant. The work addresses a fundamental question about how chaperones selectively target toxic oligomers while sparing functional monomers, which has implications for understanding disease mechanisms and designing therapeutic strategies for type 2 diabetes and other amyloidoses.
- **Major strengths** The study combines ensemble and single-molecule approaches to address a mechanistic question, which is methodologically rigorous. The observation of inhibition at substoichiometric concentrations and in ATP-free conditions is notable and suggests a direct binding mechanism rather than ATP-dependent remodeling. The hypothesis that weak monomer binding and strong oligomer binding represents a generic chaperone strategy is conceptually appealing and testable.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that Hsp70 interacts poorly with monomers but strongly with oligomers of IAPP, based on ensemble binding measurements and detection of oligomeric complexes.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, sections on ensemble binding affinity and single-particle detection; specific figures or tables not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that ensemble measurement of binding affinity suggests poor interactions between Hsp70 and IAPP, but no quantitative values are given. The detection of a minor fraction with fluorescence bursts is presented as evidence of strong oligomer binding, yet the stoichiometry, binding affinity, and abundance of these complexes relative to the total IAPP population are not reported. Without these numbers, the claim of strong oligomer binding cannot be evaluated.
- **Why it matters** The central mechanistic conclusion rests on the contrast between weak monomer binding and strong oligomer binding. If the oligomer binding affinity is not quantified or if the detected complexes represent a negligible fraction, the conclusion may be overstated.
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative binding affinities for Hsp70 with IAPP monomers and oligomers, including error estimates. Report the fraction of IAPP present as oligomers and the fraction of those oligomers that form complexes with Hsp70. Demonstrate that the detected complexes are stoichiometrically significant relative to the inhibition observed.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Causal link
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that Hsp70 prevents nucleation of IAPP based on the observation that inhibition is strongest when Hsp70 is added at the beginning of aggregation.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, first paragraph; specific kinetic data not provided
- **Concern** The temporal dependence of inhibition is interpreted as evidence for nucleation prevention, but alternative explanations are possible. For example, Hsp70 could bind to early oligomers and redirect them to off-pathway species, or it could slow elongation without affecting nucleation. The abstract does not provide kinetic traces, lag time measurements, or quantitative comparisons of inhibition at different addition times.
- **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism of nucleation prevention is a key conclusion. If the data only show that earlier addition leads to stronger inhibition, this is consistent with several mechanisms and does not uniquely support nucleation prevention.
- **Resolution test** Provide kinetic aggregation curves with and without Hsp70 added at different time points. Quantify lag time, growth rate, and final fibril mass. Show that Hsp70 specifically extends the lag phase without affecting the growth phase, or provide direct evidence of reduced nucleation rates.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Generality claim
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the mechanism is likely a generic feature of Hsp70 interactions with amyloidogenic IDPs.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, final sentence; no comparative data provided
- **Concern** The study only examines IAPP. The claim of generality to other amyloidogenic IDPs is presented without any supporting data from other systems. While the hypothesis is plausible, it is not tested here.
- **Why it matters** The abstract presents this as a broad conclusion, which could mislead readers into assuming broader applicability than is demonstrated. For a Nature-style claim, such generality would need experimental support.
- **Resolution test** Either remove the generality claim or provide data from at least one additional amyloidogenic IDP system showing similar preferential oligomer binding and inhibition.
