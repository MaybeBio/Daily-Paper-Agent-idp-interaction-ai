## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, technical soundness, and claims as presented in the provided manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors characterize the interaction between the Sema3A C-terminal polybasic region (SCT, residues 725–771) and glycosaminoglycans (GAGs), identify specific residues involved in binding, and propose that a novel peptoid (CSIC02) can disrupt this interaction, with potential therapeutic applications for axonal regeneration.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Results, Discussion, Conclusions, Materials and Methods, figure legends referenced in text
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures, tables, and supplementary materials referenced but not provided; raw NMR, SPR, and MD data not accessible; no experimental details for biological assays

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a relevant and timely question in the field of CNS regeneration, specifically the role of Sema3A-GAG interactions. The authors combine multiple biophysical techniques to characterize the SCT domain and its interaction with GAGs, and they propose a novel peptoid inhibitor. However, the evidence presented has significant limitations. The NMR assignment and CSP data are described but not shown, the SPR data are presented only as qualitative statements, and the molecular dynamics simulations are not sufficiently validated against experimental data. The claim that CSIC02 disrupts the SCT-heparin interaction is supported by only modest NMR CSP changes, and the functional relevance of this disruption is not demonstrated. The manuscript is technically sound in its approach but falls short of establishing the therapeutic potential claimed in the title and abstract.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of axon guidance, CNS injury and regeneration, extracellular matrix biology, and glycosaminoglycan chemistry would find this work of interest. The structural characterization of the Sema3A C-terminal domain and its GAG-binding properties could inform future drug design efforts targeting Sema3A signaling. The peptoid-based approach may also appeal to medicinal chemists interested in protein-GAG interaction inhibitors.
- **Major strengths** The combination of multiple biophysical techniques (NMR, SPR, CD, heparin affinity chromatography) provides a comprehensive approach to studying the SCT-GAG interaction. The identification of specific residues involved in GAG binding through NMR CSP is a valuable contribution. The use of molecular dynamics simulations to complement experimental data is appropriate. The study addresses a clinically relevant target with potential applications in CNS injury and glaucoma.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The NMR data supporting the key claims are not shown in the provided text. The CSP experiments are described but the actual spectra, assignments, and quantitative CSP values are not presented. This makes it impossible to evaluate the quality of the data or the validity of the conclusions drawn from them.
  - R1-M2: The SPR data are presented only as qualitative statements. The sensorgrams are referenced but not shown, and the kinetic parameters (kon, koff) and Kd(app) values are not reported in the text. This limits the ability to assess the affinity and specificity of the SCT-GAG interaction.
  - R1-M3: The claim that CSIC02 disrupts the SCT-heparin interaction is based on NMR CSP experiments, but the magnitude of the effect is not quantified. The text states a "modest decrease" in CSP, which is insufficient to establish a clear disruption. The functional relevance of this disruption is not demonstrated in any biological assay.
  - R1-M4: The molecular dynamics simulations are described but the results are not presented in sufficient detail. The text mentions that simulations support the NMR findings, but no quantitative data (e.g., binding energies, contact frequencies) are provided. The simulations are not validated against experimental binding data.
  - R1-M5: The therapeutic potential claimed in the title and abstract is not supported by the data presented. No in vitro or in vivo functional assays are shown that demonstrate axonal regeneration or neuroprotection. The manuscript is purely a structural/biophysical study, and the therapeutic claims are speculative.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states that "CSIC02 may disrupt the interaction between SCT and heparin," but the conclusion section states this as a definitive finding. The language should be consistent and appropriately cautious.
  - R1-m2: The Materials and Methods section describes the use of fluorescence techniques, but no fluorescence data are presented in the Results. This discrepancy should be clarified.
  - R1-m3: The manuscript mentions that Sema3A binds preferentially to heparin and CS-E, but the experiments presented only test heparin and CS-A. The relevance of CS-E binding is not addressed experimentally.
  - R1-m4: The authors state that the SCTRR/QH mutant shows abolished binding to CS-A, but this is based on a single experiment (heparin affinity chromatography) and the data are not shown. Additional validation would strengthen this claim.
  - R1-m5: The discussion of the therapeutic potential of targeting Sema3A-GAG interactions is speculative and not grounded in the experimental data presented. The authors should either provide functional data or temper their claims.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3, R1-M4
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: Moderate. The study provides new structural information on the Sema3A C-terminal domain and its GAG-binding properties, but the concept of targeting protein-GAG interactions for therapeutic purposes is not novel.
  - Scientific importance: Moderate. The findings could inform future drug design, but the lack of functional data limits the immediate impact.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: Limited. The manuscript is primarily of interest to structural biologists and glycosaminoglycan chemists; the therapeutic claims are not supported by functional evidence that would attract a broader audience.
  - Technical soundness: The experimental approach is sound, but the presentation of data is incomplete. Key figures and quantitative results are missing, which undermines the ability to assess the validity of the conclusions.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is written in a technical style that assumes familiarity with NMR, SPR, and MD techniques. The therapeutic context is introduced but not developed in a way that would engage a general scientific audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript has potential, but the key claims are not supported by the data presented. Major revisions are required to provide the missing experimental data and to temper the therapeutic claims.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that CSIC02 disrupts the SCT-heparin interaction is not quantitatively supported by the data presented.
- The therapeutic potential for axonal regeneration is not supported by any functional data.
- The identification of specific residues involved in GAG binding is based on NMR CSP data that are not shown.
- The molecular dynamics simulations are described as supporting the NMR findings, but no quantitative results are provided.
- The claim that SCT is an intrinsically disordered region is supported by bioinformatics predictions and limited experimental data, but the evidence is not fully presented.
