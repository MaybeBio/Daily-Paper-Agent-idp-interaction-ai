## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, logical coherence, evidence quality, and alignment with the stated aims of a retrospective review article
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present a 30-year retrospective of their work on cAMP-dependent protein kinase (PKA), focusing on how structural biology, beginning with the first PKA catalytic subunit structure in 1991, has revealed the dynamic and allosteric nature of protein kinases as molecular switches. They emphasize the discovery of the N3A motif in the RIα regulatory subunit, initially dismissed as a crystal-packing artifact, and argue that such interfaces should be reconsidered as biologically relevant protein:protein interaction sites, validated by disease mutations and biophysical methods.
- **Visible evidence base** The manuscript includes descriptions of multiple published structures (PKA C-subunit, RIα fragments, full-length RIα dimer, RIIβ holoenzyme), computational analyses (Local Spatial Pattern alignment, Markov State Models), biophysical data (SAXS, NMR, optical tweezers), and references to disease-associated mutations (Carney Complex, Acrodysostosis). Figures 1–9 are cited but not provided in the submitted text.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures and their legends are not included; supporting information (video) is not accessible; several key claims reference unpublished results or manuscripts in preparation, which cannot be independently assessed.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a well-written, historically grounded review that will be of genuine interest to the signaling and structural biology communities. The narrative arc from static structure to dynamic allostery is compelling, and the emphasis on the N3A motif as a case study in reinterpreting crystal-packing interfaces is a valuable conceptual contribution. However, the manuscript is more of a personal retrospective than a comprehensive review, and several claims rest on unpublished data or are presented without sufficient quantitative detail. The central conceptual claim, that crystal-packing interfaces should be viewed as a "treasure chest" of potential biological interfaces, is thought-provoking but requires more rigorous framing and supporting evidence to be fully persuasive.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists, protein kinase researchers, and those interested in allostery and dynamic protein conformations will find this review valuable. The discussion of the N3A motif and its disease relevance will appeal to those studying Carney Complex and Acrodysostosis. The broader message about the value of the PDB and the importance of interdisciplinary approaches will resonate with the wider biochemistry and molecular biology community.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript provides a unique, first-person perspective on three decades of PKA structural biology, which is both informative and engaging. The focus on the N3A motif as a case study in reinterpreting crystallographic data is a novel and useful contribution. The figures, as described, appear to effectively summarize the structural evolution and key motifs. The writing is clear and accessible to a broad audience.
- **Major Concerns** The manuscript's central conceptual claim about crystal-packing interfaces is not developed with sufficient rigor. The evidence for the biological relevance of the N3A dimer interface, while suggestive, relies heavily on unpublished data and qualitative descriptions. The review is also somewhat narrow in scope, focusing almost exclusively on the authors' own work without adequately contextualizing it within the broader field of kinase structural biology. The lack of quantitative detail in several key claims (e.g., Hill coefficients, SAXS data) weakens the scientific argument.
- **Minor Comments** The manuscript would benefit from a more explicit discussion of the limitations of the approaches used. The transition from discussing the C-subunit to the regulatory subunits could be smoother. The use of "we" throughout is appropriate for a retrospective but could be tempered in places to avoid seeming overly personal.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The claim that the N3A motif is a biologically relevant dimer interface is not fully established from the provided evidence. The authors state that the interface was seen in all monomeric structures but attributed to crystal packing, and only with the full-length dimer structure was its importance appreciated. However, the evidence that this interface is not merely a crystallization artifact is not presented in sufficient detail. The unpublished results cited for the ACRDYS mutants and the ATP-coupling studies are critical to the argument but cannot be evaluated.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  - **Originality**: The manuscript offers a personal and historical perspective that is not entirely original in its scientific content but is original in its framing, particularly the emphasis on the N3A motif and the reinterpretation of crystal-packing interfaces.  
  - **Scientific importance**: The topic is of high importance to the signaling community, and the conceptual message about crystal-packing interfaces is potentially significant, but the evidence presented is not yet strong enough to fully support this claim.  
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The manuscript is likely to appeal to a broad readership across structural biology, biochemistry, and cell signaling.  
  - **Technical soundness**: The technical quality of the described work is high, but the presentation of key evidence is incomplete, relying on unpublished data and qualitative descriptions.  
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The manuscript is well-written and accessible, with clear explanations of complex concepts.  
  Overall, the manuscript is a strong candidate for publication in a journal like *JBC* but would not meet the higher bar for a general-interest journal like *Nature* due to its narrow scope and the incomplete evidence for its central claim.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved, particularly regarding the presentation of unpublished data and the quantitative evidence for the N3A motif's biological relevance.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence quality
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the N3A motif, originally attributed to crystal packing, is a biologically relevant dimer interface in the RIα subunit, and that this is supported by disease mutations and biophysical data.
- **Evidence pointer** Figures 5 and 6; text sections "Discovering the N3A motif as a dimer interface" and "Crystal packing"
- **Concern** The central claim of the manuscript rests on the reinterpretation of the N3A motif as a biologically relevant interface. However, the evidence presented is largely qualitative. The authors state that the interface was observed in all monomeric structures but dismissed as crystal packing, and that the full-length dimer structure revealed its importance. The supporting evidence from mutagenesis, SAXS, and disease mutations is described but not presented in sufficient detail. The Hill coefficient values are mentioned but not shown in a table or figure. The unpublished results on ACRDYS mutants and ATP coupling are critical to the argument but cannot be assessed.
- **Why it matters** The conceptual message of the review, that crystal-packing interfaces should be reconsidered as potential biological interfaces, is only as strong as the evidence for the N3A motif being a genuine example. Without quantitative data and a clear demonstration that the interface is not a crystallization artifact, the claim remains speculative.
- **Resolution test** The authors should provide a more detailed presentation of the evidence, including quantitative data (e.g., binding affinities, Hill coefficients, SAXS profiles) and a clearer discussion of how they ruled out crystal-packing artifacts. The unpublished results should either be included or clearly flagged as preliminary.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Scope and framing
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript is framed as a review of "the dynamic regulation of a molecular switch" over three decades, but it focuses almost exclusively on the authors' own contributions.
- **Evidence pointer** Entire manuscript
- **Concern** While a personal retrospective is a valid format, the title and abstract suggest a broader review of the field. The manuscript does not adequately contextualize the authors' work within the wider landscape of kinase structural biology, missing opportunities to discuss alternative perspectives or competing models. This limits the manuscript's value as a review for readers seeking a comprehensive overview.
- **Why it matters** The manuscript's impact as a review is diminished if it is perceived as a narrow account of one laboratory's work. A more balanced perspective would strengthen its credibility and broaden its appeal.
- **Resolution test** The authors should either adjust the framing to clearly indicate this is a personal retrospective or add a section that places their work in the context of the broader field, discussing key contributions from other groups.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "the second metal ion is essential for the synergistic high-affinity binding of ATP in the protein kinase inhibitor complex and for phosphotransfer" and that "most other protein kinase structures still do not properly capture or grasp the importance of that second metal ion."
- **Evidence pointer** Figure 2B; text section "PKA catalytic subunit"
- **Concern** This is a strong claim that is presented without a detailed comparison to other kinase structures. The authors do not provide a systematic analysis of how many other kinase structures lack the second metal ion or how this affects their function. The statement is presented as a general truth but is not supported by a comprehensive survey of the literature.
- **Why it matters** The claim is central to the authors' argument about the uniqueness of their structural insights. Without a more rigorous comparison, the statement appears overstated and could be challenged by experts in the field.
- **Resolution test** The authors should provide a more detailed comparison with other kinase structures, either through a literature survey or a systematic analysis, to support their claim about the second metal ion.
