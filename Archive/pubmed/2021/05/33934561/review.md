## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a well-written, thought-provoking perspective/review that synthesizes existing literature to argue for a broader role of PPII helices in biomolecular condensates. The authors present a plausible hypothesis, supported by selected examples, but the manuscript is primarily speculative and lacks new experimental evidence. The central claim that glycine-rich PPII helices may mediate condensate formation is intriguing but remains untested, as the authors acknowledge. The review is accessible and likely to stimulate discussion, but its impact is limited by the absence of quantitative analysis or novel data.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of phase separation, intrinsically disordered proteins, structural biology, and cell biology would find this review useful. It offers a new perspective on condensate formation mechanisms, potentially guiding experimental design for those studying PPII-rich proteins or PPII-binding domains. The speculative hypothesis about glycine-rich PPII helices may also interest protein engineers and biophysicists.
- **Major strengths** 1) Clear and engaging writing style, making complex concepts accessible. 2) Comprehensive coverage of known PPII-binding domains and their roles in condensates. 3) Novel hypothesis regarding glycine-rich PPII helices, supported by prior structural work on sfAFP. 4) Honest acknowledgment of speculative elements, with explicit calls for testing.
- **Major Concerns** 1) The central hypothesis is not experimentally tested in this manuscript, limiting its scientific impact. 2) The evidence for PPII helices in condensates is largely circumstantial, with few direct demonstrations. 3) The claim that glycine-rich PPII helices may self-associate is highly speculative, with no direct evidence presented. 4) The review lacks a critical evaluation of alternative explanations for the cited observations.
- **Minor Comments** 1) The "Cinema Paradiso" analogy, while charming, may be distracting for an international audience. 2) The manuscript would benefit from a summary table of all PPII-mediated condensate examples with key features. 3) The authors should clarify whether the "prediction" mentioned in the abstract has been published or is ongoing work.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None identified from the supplied material, as this is a review without new experimental claims requiring technical validation.

Could influence research directions but lacks direct evidence. Interdisciplinary readership: Good. Relevant to cell biology, biophysics, and structural biology. Technical soundness: Acceptable for a review, but the sfAFP NMR claims rely on prior work not fully detailed here. Readability for nonspecialists: Excellent. The writing is clear and engaging.
