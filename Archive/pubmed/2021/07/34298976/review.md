## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (Abstract, Introduction, Sections 2–5, Acknowledgments)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, logical coherence, and evidentiary support of the claims presented in the manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript argues that VDAC regulation by tubulin and α-synuclein occurs via a common mechanism: membrane binding of the inhibitor, followed by voltage-driven capture of its disordered polyanionic C-terminal domain into the VDAC pore. This blockade mimics voltage-gated states, reducing metabolite flux and increasing calcium flux, and is proposed as a general model for VDAC inhibition with therapeutic implications.
- **Visible evidence base** Descriptions of prior experimental results (electrophysiology, neutron reflectometry, molecular dynamics simulations, cell-based assays) referenced from the authors' own and others' published work; no new experimental data presented in this manuscript
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** No original data, figures, or methods are included; the manuscript is a review that relies entirely on previously published results. No supplementary information or detailed experimental protocols are provided.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a well-written and conceptually coherent review that synthesizes a decade of the authors' work on VDAC regulation by tubulin and α-synuclein. The central proposal—that disordered polyanionic C-terminal domains, anchored to the membrane, block VDAC in a voltage-dependent manner—is mechanistically plausible and supported by a range of complementary techniques. The review is likely to be of interest to researchers in mitochondrial biology, biophysics, and neurodegeneration. However, as a review, it does not present new data, and its conclusions are only as strong as the underlying primary literature. The speculative sections on post-translational modifications and physiological relevance are clearly flagged as such but would benefit from more explicit caveats. The manuscript is suitable for publication in a specialist journal, though its broad claims about a "general model" of VDAC inhibition require more comparative evidence across diverse VDAC-interacting proteins to be fully convincing.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Mitochondrial physiologists studying metabolite and calcium flux regulation; biophysicists interested in protein-pore interactions and disordered protein domains; researchers in Parkinson's disease and other synucleinopathies, given the established link between α-synuclein and mitochondrial dysfunction; cancer biologists investigating the role of tubulin isotypes and VDAC in metabolic reprogramming (Warburg effect). The review provides a unified framework that could inform drug discovery efforts targeting VDAC.
- **Major strengths** 1) Clear and compelling synthesis of a large body of work into a single mechanistic model. 2) Strong use of quantitative biophysical data (energy landscape modeling, voltage-dependent kinetics) to support the proposed mechanism. 3) Integration of structural, computational, and functional evidence. 4) Thoughtful discussion of physiological implications, including the role of membrane potential and post-translational modifications. 5) Well-organized with helpful figures referenced throughout.
- **Major Concerns** 1) The review is heavily based on the authors' own work, which may introduce bias; independent validation is not discussed. 2) The claim of a "general model" of VDAC inhibition is extrapolated from only two proteins (tubulin and α-synuclein); the generality is asserted rather than demonstrated. 3) The physiological relevance of the in vitro findings, particularly regarding the magnitude of the membrane potential and the concentrations of inhibitors used, is discussed but not critically evaluated. 4) The section on post-translational modifications is speculative and lacks direct experimental support. 5) The review does not address potential alternative mechanisms of VDAC regulation by these proteins (e.g., direct protein-protein interactions outside the pore).
- **Minor Comments** 1) The abstract could more clearly state that this is a review article. 2) Some figures are referenced but not described in sufficient detail for readers unfamiliar with the original papers. 3) The discussion of α-synuclein translocation into mitochondria would benefit from a more balanced view, acknowledging ongoing debates in the field. 4) The authors use "docking protein" and "inhibitor" interchangeably; this should be clarified. 5) The conclusion that "biochemical compatibility plays a lesser role" is intriguing but may be overstated given the limited number of proteins studied.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None identified from the supplied material, as this is a review and does not present new experimental data. However, the authors should ensure that all referenced data are accurately represented and that the limitations of the in vitro systems are clearly stated.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  *Originality*: The review presents a unified mechanistic model that is a synthesis of existing work; the model itself is not entirely novel but is presented in a new integrative framework. This is moderate originality, appropriate for a review.  
  *Scientific importance*: The topic is of high importance given the central role of VDAC in mitochondrial function and its involvement in disease. The review consolidates evidence that could guide future research and therapeutic development.  
  *Interdisciplinary readership*: The work bridges biophysics, cell biology, and neurobiology, and would appeal to a broad audience. However, the heavy reliance on electrophysiology and modeling may be challenging for some readers.  
  *Technical soundness*: The underlying data are from peer-reviewed publications and appear technically sound. The energy landscape modeling is a strength, but the review does not critically assess potential artifacts in the in vitro systems.  
  *Readability for nonspecialists*: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, but some sections (e.g., energy landscape modeling) assume familiarity with biophysical concepts. A glossary or more explanatory text would improve accessibility.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that tubulin and α-synuclein represent a "general model" of VDAC inhibition is not supported by comparative data from other proteins.
- The prediction that phosphorylated tubulin will respond more strongly to voltage is speculative and not experimentally tested.
- The suggestion that polyglutamylation of tubulin could regulate VDAC interaction during development is a hypothesis without direct evidence.
- The statement that "biochemical compatibility plays a lesser role" in VDAC-inhibitor interactions is an interpretation that may not hold for other potential inhibitors.
- The physiological relevance of the in vitro findings, particularly the required membrane potentials and inhibitor concentrations, is not critically evaluated against in vivo conditions.
