## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no figures, tables, or methods details were available for evaluation
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use grand canonical Monte Carlo simulations of a coarse-grained model for disordered proteins to show that a normalized sequence charge decoration (SCD) parameter provides a predictive criterion for distinguishing macroscopic liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) from finite-size aggregation. They further report a strong correlation between SCD and critical density, and argue that at sufficiently long chain lengths most sequences will phase separate when short-ranged attractive interactions dominate.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no simulation details, parameter definitions, quantitative results, or figures were provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, methods section, simulation protocol, definition and calculation of SCD, quantitative correlation coefficients, phase diagrams, and any comparison with experimental data

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially useful computational framework for predicting phase behavior of disordered proteins based on sequence features. The central claim, that a normalized SCD parameter can distinguish LLPS from finite aggregation, is plausible and of interest to the biophysics community. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to assess the robustness of the simulations, the generality of the criterion, or the strength of the reported correlations. The work appears to be a solid contribution to the field, but the evidence base available here is too limited to fully evaluate the technical soundness of the claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying phase separation of intrinsically disordered proteins, computational biophysicists working on coarse-grained models of biomolecular condensates, and experimentalists seeking sequence-based predictors for LLPS behavior. The proposed SCD criterion could be useful for designing mutations or predicting which proteins are likely to form condensates in cells.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a timely and important question about the relationship between sequence properties and phase transition type. The use of a simple coarse-grained model with grand canonical Monte Carlo simulations is appropriate for systematically varying sequence parameters. The introduction of a normalized SCD parameter as a predictive criterion is conceptually appealing and could have practical utility.
- **Major Concerns** None identified from the supplied material, as the abstract does not provide enough detail to raise specific technical concerns. However, the lack of quantitative results and methodological detail prevents a thorough assessment.
- **Minor Comments** None identified from the supplied material.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The abstract does not provide quantitative evidence for the claimed correlation between SCD and critical density, nor does it define the SCD parameter or describe how it is normalized. Without these details, the predictive criterion cannot be evaluated or reproduced.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The idea of using a sequence charge decoration parameter to predict phase transition type is novel and could be impactful. Scientific importance: The question of when proteins phase separate versus aggregate is of broad relevance to cell biology and biophysics. Interdisciplinary readership: The work would appeal to biophysicists, computational chemists, and cell biologists, though the abstract is written in a way that is accessible to specialists. Technical soundness: Cannot be fully assessed from the abstract alone; the simulation methodology and analysis need to be scrutinized in the full manuscript. Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is clear and concise, though some terms such as SCD and grand canonical Monte Carlo may require background knowledge.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The abstract presents a promising framework, but the full manuscript must provide sufficient methodological detail and quantitative results to substantiate the claims.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that SCD establishes a "soft" predictive criterion for distinguishing LLPS from finite aggregation is not quantitatively supported in the abstract.
- The reported strong correlation between SCD and critical density is stated without any numerical values or statistical measures.
- The assertion that at sufficiently long chain lengths most sequences will phase separate is presented without supporting data or a defined threshold for "sufficiently long."
- The suggestion that classical LLPS should be the primary phase transition when short-ranged attractive interactions dominate is a reasonable hypothesis but is not backed by direct evidence in the abstract.
