### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The claim that the intrinsically disordered C-terminal tail shifts the conformational ensemble towards a pseudo-bound state through weak interactions with the ligand-binding pocket.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that the C-terminal tail shifts the ensemble towards a pseudo-bound state, but no experimental data are presented to support this specific interaction or the nature of the pseudo-bound state. It is unclear how this state was characterized and whether the interaction is direct or inferred from indirect observations.
- **Why it matters** This claim is central to the proposed mechanism of ensemble redistribution. Without direct evidence, the interpretation that flanking disorder tunes the folded domain via weak interactions remains speculative, which undermines the core conclusion.
- **Resolution test** Provide NMR or other biophysical data showing direct interaction between the C-terminal tail and the ligand-binding pocket, including quantitative measures of the interaction strength and the resulting conformational changes.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Quantitative support
- **Claim pointer** The claim that an unfolded excited state is accessible on the ms timescale and its population is lowered by 50% in the presence of flanking disorder.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract reports a 50% reduction in the population of an excited state, but no details are given on how this population was quantified, what experimental conditions were used, or what the uncertainty in this measurement is. The ms timescale is mentioned but not contextualized with respect to the methods used.
- **Why it matters** This quantitative claim is a key part of the ensemble redistribution model. Without proper quantification and error analysis, the reliability of the 50% figure is uncertain, and the conclusion that flanking disorder modulates the excited state population is not robustly supported.
- **Resolution test** Include detailed NMR relaxation dispersion or other kinetic data showing the excited state population and its change, with appropriate statistical analysis and error bars.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Mechanistic interpretation
- **Claim pointer** The claim that flanking disorder lowers transcription factor binding-affinity without affecting the dissociation rate constant, in accordance with similar bound-states assessed by NMR.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that binding affinity is lowered but the dissociation rate is unchanged, which implies a change in the association rate. However, no data are provided to support this kinetic distinction, and the phrase "in accordance with similar bound-states" is vague. It is unclear how the bound-state assessments were performed and whether they directly support the kinetic interpretation.
- **Why it matters** This claim is critical for the proposed mechanism that flanking disorder tunes binding through ensemble redistribution rather than direct steric effects. Without clear kinetic data, alternative explanations, such as changes in the bound-state conformation or non-specific effects, cannot be excluded.
- **Resolution test** Provide binding kinetics data (e.g., from surface plasmon resonance or stopped-flow) showing association and dissociation rates separately, and demonstrate that the affinity change is due to a change in association rate while the dissociation rate remains constant.
