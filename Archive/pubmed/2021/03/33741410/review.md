## Review setup

- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions presented in the abstract, with reference to the stated scope of the manuscript as a review article
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors argue that current high-resolution structures of TRP ion channels are incomplete because they omit intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs), which constitute 25 to 50 percent of the protein sequence in some cases. They propose that these IDRs serve as recruitment hubs for lipids and regulatory proteins, and that their functions, including intra- and intermolecular crosstalk, splicing effects, and post-translational modifications, are essential for a full understanding of TRP channel biology. The manuscript is presented as a review that complements existing structural studies with an emphasis on "unstructural" biology.

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a conceptually interesting and timely perspective on a recognized gap in TRP channel structural biology. The emphasis on intrinsically disordered regions as functional elements is well aligned with broader trends in the field. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the rigor of the analysis, the accuracy of the quantitative claims, or the depth of the literature synthesis. The manuscript may be of value to the community, but the current evidence base does not allow verification of the central assertions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists studying membrane proteins, ion channel researchers, and investigators focused on intrinsically disordered proteins would find this review relevant. It addresses a methodological blind spot in cryo-EM structure determination and offers a framework for integrating dynamic regions into functional models, which is of interest to those working on TRP channel physiology, pharmacology, and regulation.
- **Major strengths** The topic addresses a genuine and underappreciated limitation of current structural approaches. The framing of IDRs as recruitment hubs is a useful conceptual contribution. The abstract clearly articulates a complementary perspective to conventional structural biology, which could stimulate further research and discussion.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Quantitative claim accuracy
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that intrinsically disordered regions "can represent between a quarter to almost half of the entire protein sequence" in TRP channels.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The percentage range is presented as a general fact, but no source, calculation method, or specific channel examples are given. It is unclear whether this range refers to all TRP channels, specific subfamilies, or particular isoforms. The basis for the upper bound of "almost half" is not explained.
- **Why it matters** This quantitative claim is central to the manuscript's premise that IDRs are a major and underappreciated component of TRP channels. If the range is inaccurate or based on a narrow subset, the overall argument is weakened.
- **Resolution test** Provide a clear definition of IDR prediction criteria, specify the dataset of TRP channel sequences analyzed, and show the distribution of disorder content across subfamilies, ideally in a supplementary figure or table.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence for functional claims
- **Claim pointer** The abstract asserts that IDRs "act as important recruitment hubs for lipids and regulatory proteins" and that they mediate "intra- and intermolecular crosstalk."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not specify which experimental or structural evidence supports these functional roles. It is not clear whether the examples discussed are based on direct biochemical data, mutagenesis studies, or inferred from structural models. The strength of the evidence for each example is not indicated.
- **Why it matters** The functional significance of IDRs is the core message of the review. Without a transparent assessment of the evidence quality, readers cannot judge whether the claims are well supported or speculative.
- **Resolution test** For each example discussed, state the type of evidence (e.g., crosslinking, NMR, mutagenesis, functional assays) and explicitly note the level of confidence, such as direct observation versus inference from structural absence.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Scope and completeness of literature synthesis
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that the authors "analyze the currently available TRP channel structures" and "discuss select examples" of IDR function.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not indicate how many structures were analyzed, which subfamilies are covered, or how the "select examples" were chosen. There is no mention of potential biases in the selection or of conflicting findings in the literature.
- **Why it matters** A review should provide a balanced and comprehensive synthesis. If the selection of examples is arbitrary or incomplete, the conclusions may not be representative of the field.
- **Resolution test** Include a table listing all TRP channel structures analyzed, with their PDB identifiers, resolution, and the extent of missing regions. Justify the choice of examples in the text and acknowledge any contradictory studies.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Terminology clarity
- **Affected element** The term "unstructural biology"
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract title and text
- **Issue** The term is used without definition. It may be interpreted as a play on "unstructured" but could confuse readers unfamiliar with the concept.
- **Required correction** Define the term explicitly at first use, for example, as the study of regions that are not resolved in structural models but are functionally relevant.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Historical context accuracy
- **Affected element** The claim that the first high-resolution single particle cryo-EM structure of a membrane protein was TRPV1 in 2013
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text
- **Issue** This claim is likely accurate for TRP channels, but the abstract states it as a general fact for membrane proteins. Earlier cryo-EM structures of other membrane proteins, such as aquaporin or rhodopsin, may have been reported at lower resolution, and the phrasing could be misleading.
- **Required correction** Clarify that TRPV1 was the first high-resolution single particle cryo-EM structure of a TRP channel, or specify the context to avoid overgeneralization.

- **Concern ID** R1-m3
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Readability for nonspecialists
- **Affected element** The phrase "unstructural biology" and the use of "unstructural" in the title
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract title and text
- **Issue** The term may be seen as jargon and could reduce accessibility for readers outside the structural biology community.
- **Required correction** Consider adding a brief explanatory phrase in the abstract, such as "the biology of structurally unresolved regions," to enhance clarity.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- The claim that IDRs constitute 25 to 50 percent of TRP channel sequences is unsupported in the abstract, as no data or methodology are provided.
- The assertion that IDRs act as recruitment hubs for lipids and regulatory proteins is plausible but not verifiable from the abstract alone.
- The statement that the manuscript "analyzes" all currently available TRP channel structures is not assessable, as the scope and criteria for inclusion are not described.
- The historical claim about the first high-resolution cryo-EM membrane protein structure is potentially overgeneralized and requires qualification.
