## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodology, data interpretation, and claims as presented in the manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors investigate how folded domains (RRMs and a SUMO solubility tag) modulate the phase separation behavior of the intrinsically disordered low-complexity domain (LCD) of hnRNPA1. They report that the presence of folded domains reverses the salt dependence of phase separation relative to the LCD alone, and propose that transient electrostatic interactions between the LCD and folded domains contribute to phase separation at low ionic strength while folded domains solubilize the protein at higher ionic strength. These conclusions are supported by phase diagram measurements, SAXS, AUC, and coarse-grained MD simulations.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (Figures 1–6), methods section, and referenced supplementary figures (S1–S6). Supplementary data files were not provided for direct inspection.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary Figures S1–S6, raw SAXS data, simulation trajectories, and the GitHub repository containing simulation outputs were not accessible for verification. This limits independent assessment of data quality, fitting procedures, and reproducibility of the computational results.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses an important and timely question in the field of biomolecular phase separation: how folded domains modulate the phase behavior of disordered low-complexity domains. The work is carefully executed, combining experimental phase diagrams, SAXS, AUC, and coarse-grained simulations to build a mechanistic picture. The finding that folded domains can reverse the salt dependence of phase separation is novel and has broad implications for interpreting phase behavior of multi-domain proteins. The conclusions are largely supported by the data presented, though some aspects of the simulation analysis and the generalizability of the claims require further scrutiny. The manuscript is well-written and accessible to a broad readership.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying liquid–liquid phase separation, biomolecular condensates, intrinsically disordered proteins, and RNA-binding proteins. The work is also relevant to those interested in the physicochemical principles governing protein solubility and the design of fusion proteins for biophysical studies. The finding that folded domains can obscure the intrinsic interaction signatures of LCDs is a cautionary and practically useful insight for the field.
- **Major strengths** 1. The study directly addresses a gap in the field by systematically comparing the phase behavior of the LCD alone versus in the context of folded domains. 2. The combination of experimental phase diagrams, SAXS, AUC, and simulations provides complementary and converging evidence. 3. The finding that folded domains reverse the salt dependence of phase separation is unexpected and mechanistically well-explained. 4. The use of a hexapeptide deletion to prevent fibrillization is a thoughtful experimental design choice that enables equilibrium measurements. 5. The manuscript is clearly written and the figures are informative.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Major, Blocking: No): The simulation model validation and the interpretation of the λ parameter require more detailed justification. The authors tune λ to match SAXS data at a single salt concentration and then apply it across all conditions. The transferability of this parameter across salt concentrations and constructs is not fully established.
  - **R1-M2** (Major, Blocking: No): The claim that electrostatic interactions between the LCD and folded domains contribute to phase separation at low ionic strength is inferred primarily from simulations and indirect measurements. Direct experimental evidence for these specific interdomain interactions is lacking.
  - **R1-M3** (Major, Blocking: No): The generalizability of the conclusions to other multi-domain proteins is asserted but not demonstrated. The authors discuss implications for other systems but do not provide comparative data or a broader framework.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - **R1-m1** (Minor, Axis: Clarity): The description of the phase diagram fitting procedure could be more explicit regarding the number of data points and the uncertainty in the fitted parameters.
  - **R1-m2** (Minor, Axis: Reproducibility): The GitHub repository is mentioned, but the specific contents and how to access the analysis scripts are not described in sufficient detail.
  - **R1-m3** (Minor, Axis: Presentation): The note added in proof introduces additional findings that are not integrated into the main text. This is acceptable but slightly disrupts the flow of the manuscript.
  - **R1-m4** (Minor, Axis: Interpretation): The authors state that the effect of folded domains on phase behavior "can obscure signatures of physicochemical interactions." This is an important point that could be emphasized more strongly in the discussion.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None identified that would invalidate the core conclusions. The concerns raised above relate to the strength of evidence for specific mechanistic claims and the generalizability of the findings, but the main observations are well-supported.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality**: High. The finding that folded domains reverse the salt dependence of LCD phase separation is novel and challenges simple assumptions about the dominance of LCD-driven interactions in multi-domain proteins.
  - **Scientific importance**: High. The work provides a mechanistic framework for understanding how domain architecture modulates phase behavior, which is relevant to a wide range of biological processes and disease mechanisms.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: Moderate to high. The topic is of interest to biophysicists, cell biologists, and biochemists. The methods span experimental biophysics and computational simulation, broadening the potential audience.
  - **Technical soundness**: Generally strong. The experimental design is careful, and the combination of techniques is appropriate. The main technical weakness is the reliance on a single simulation model with a tuned parameter, which requires further validation.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: Good. The manuscript is clearly written, and the concepts are explained in an accessible manner. The figures are well-designed and support the narrative.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core findings are novel and well-supported, but the mechanistic interpretation would be strengthened by additional validation of the simulation model and, ideally, direct experimental evidence for the proposed interdomain interactions.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the λ parameter tuned at 150 mM NaCl is transferable to other salt concentrations and constructs is not fully supported by the data presented.
- The assertion that electrostatic interactions between the LCD and folded domains contribute to phase separation at low ionic strength is inferred from simulations and indirect measurements; direct experimental evidence is not provided.
- The generalizability of the findings to other multi-domain proteins is discussed but not experimentally demonstrated.
- The statement that the effect of folded domains "can obscure signatures of physicochemical interactions underlying phase separation" is a reasonable interpretation but is presented as a general principle without comparative data from other systems.
