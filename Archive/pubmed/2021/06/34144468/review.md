### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Clarity and support of claims  
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "solution NMR studies directly observing the liquid condensed phases have found no support for assertions of increased β-sheet ordering" and that "the LC domains of FUS, DDX4, hnRNPA2, and elastin-like peptides all retain predominant disorder within liquid condensed phases."  
- **Evidence pointer** Section "NMR spectroscopy", location not provided  
- **Concern** The claim is presented as a general conclusion, but the supporting evidence is not described in detail. The authors cite multiple studies (references 12–14, 21) but do not specify which experimental observations (e.g., chemical shift perturbations, relaxation measurements) lead to this conclusion. A more explicit description of the evidence would strengthen the claim.  
- **Why it matters** Readers who are not NMR specialists may not understand how the data support the conclusion of retained disorder. Without this context, the claim may appear under-supported.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a brief description of the key NMR observables (e.g., secondary chemical shifts, relaxation rates) that support the conclusion of disorder in condensed phases.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Completeness of discussion  
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "coarse-grained (CG) models to access longer timescales including the process of phase separation as well as examine partitioning and surface properties" are used, and that "these recent investigations suggest that these simulations can provide meaningful information that is difficult to access by experiment."  
- **Evidence pointer** Section "Molecular simulations", location not provided  
- **Concern** The discussion of CG models is brief and does not address potential limitations, such as the sensitivity of results to model parameters or the challenges in mapping CG predictions back to atomistic detail. A more critical evaluation would improve the balance of the review.  
- **Why it matters** The review aims to provide a comprehensive overview, and a one-sided presentation of simulation capabilities may mislead readers about the current state of the field.  
- **Resolution test** Add a paragraph discussing the limitations of CG models, including parameterization challenges and the need for experimental validation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Support for future directions  
- **Claim pointer** The conclusion states that "cooperation between these techniques becomes even more important" for studying condensates in living cells.  
- **Evidence pointer** Section "Conclusion", location not provided  
- **Concern** The claim about the importance of cooperation is reasonable, but the manuscript does not provide specific examples of how such integration would work in practice for in-cell studies. A brief discussion of emerging approaches (e.g., in-cell NMR combined with simulations) would strengthen this point.  
- **Why it matters** The conclusion is forward-looking, and readers would benefit from concrete examples of how the proposed integration could be achieved.  
- **Resolution test** Add a sentence or two describing a specific example of how NMR and simulation could be combined to study condensates in cells.
