## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods) as provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, technical soundness, internal consistency, and support of claims by the presented evidence. Editorial or stylistic issues are noted only where they affect comprehension.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that electrostatic interactions, particularly ion-mediated bridging and the high valency of ATP-Mg, can largely rationalize salt- and ATP-dependent reentrant phase behaviors of the Caprin1 IDR and its phosphorylated variant pY-Caprin1, and that these effects represent a general mechanism for ATP's roles in biomolecular condensates.
- **Visible evidence base** Experimental phase diagrams (Figures 1, 3), analytical theory (rG-RPA, Figures 1, 2), coarse-grained molecular dynamics (Figures 4-7), field-theoretic simulations (Figures 8, 9), and supporting analyses in Appendix 1 (figures referenced but not fully visible).
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Appendix 1 text and figures are referenced but not provided in full. Source data files, simulation parameter tables, and detailed force field parameters are not available. Experimental methods for turbidity assays and NMR data (referenced from prior work) are only summarized.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a comprehensive multi-method study addressing a significant question in the field of biomolecular condensates: the extent to which electrostatics can explain salt- and ATP-dependent reentrant phase behavior. The combination of analytical theory, coarse-grained MD, and field-theoretic simulations applied to a biologically relevant system (Caprin1) with experimental validation is commendable. The central claims, however, are partially supported by the evidence as presented. The consistency across methods is encouraging, but quantitative agreement with experiment is limited, and the authors themselves acknowledge several simplifying assumptions. The claim that electrostatics "highlights diverse roles of ATP" is somewhat overstated given that nonelectrostatic interactions are acknowledged but not systematically incorporated. The manuscript would benefit from clearer articulation of the predictive power and limitations of the models, and from a more direct comparison between theory and experiment where possible.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying phase separation of intrinsically disordered proteins, particularly those interested in the roles of ATP, salt, and phosphorylation in condensate formation. The methodological combination (rG-RPA + MD + FTS) will be of interest to computational biophysicists. The experimental data on Caprin1 and pY-Caprin1 provide useful benchmarks for future studies. The discussion of salt partitioning and counterion valency effects has broader relevance to polymer physics and colloid science.
- **Major strengths**
  - Multi-method approach (analytical theory, MD, FTS) applied to the same system, allowing cross-validation of trends.
  - Direct comparison with experimental phase diagrams for both NaCl and ATP-Mg dependence.
  - Clear articulation of the physical mechanisms (ion bridging, counterion valency, salt partitioning) underlying the observed behaviors.
  - The study addresses a timely and debated topic (ATP's role in condensates) with a focused electrostatic perspective.
  - The FTS model for ATP-Mg as a short charged polymer is a useful methodological contribution.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Blocking: Yes): The quantitative agreement between theory and experiment is limited, and the manuscript does not provide a systematic error analysis or sensitivity study. The authors state that rG-RPA "overestimates" LLPS propensity (Shen and Wang, 2017) but do not quantify this for their specific system. Without a clear statement of expected quantitative accuracy, the claim that the models "rationalize" experimental data is weakened.
  - **R1-M2** (Blocking: No): The role of nonelectrostatic interactions is acknowledged but not systematically addressed. The authors state that "the impact of excluded volume and solvent/cosolute-mediated temperature-dependent effective interactions should be incorporated" (Discussion), but this is presented as future work. Given that the title emphasizes "electrostatics," the manuscript should more clearly delineate what fraction of the observed behavior is attributable to electrostatics versus other factors.
  - **R1-M3** (Blocking: No): The experimental data for ATP-Mg-dependent phase behavior (Figure 3) are presented as turbidity measurements, but the relationship between turbidity and phase separation is not fully established. Turbidity can be affected by droplet size, refractive index, and kinetics. The authors should clarify whether the turbidity data were calibrated against direct observation of phase separation (e.g., microscopy).
  - **R1-M4** (Blocking: No): The FTS model for ATP-Mg assumes no dissociation of the ATP-Mg complex. This is a strong assumption given that the manuscript elsewhere discusses the importance of ATP4- and Mg2+ as separate species (Figure 2i-l). The authors should justify this assumption or discuss its potential impact on the results.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Axis: Clarity): The definition of "counterions" and "salt ions" in the Results section is clear, but the notation in Equation 1 could be more explicit. The relationship between Q, zs, and zc is stated, but the derivation of the equation is not shown.
  - **R1-m2** (Axis: Reproducibility): The manuscript states that "source data for figures" are included, but the specific simulation parameters (e.g., box size, number of chains, equilibration time, force field parameters) are not provided in the main text. These should be available in the Appendix or as supplementary material.
  - **R1-m3** (Axis: Presentation): Figure 8f compares FTS predictions with experimental NMR data from a prior study (Kim et al., 2021). The comparison is qualitative, and the authors should clarify whether the experimental data were obtained under the same conditions (e.g., pH, temperature, ATP-Mg concentration) as the simulations.
  - **R1-m4** (Axis: Interpretation): The discussion of "true bridging" versus "neutralizing" configurations in the MD analysis (Figure 6) is interesting but somewhat speculative. The authors should clarify whether this distinction is based on a rigorous energetic criterion or a heuristic one.
  - **R1-m5** (Axis: Completeness): The manuscript mentions "other IDRs" in the abstract and discussion but does not provide data for these systems. If the authors intend to claim generality, they should either include such data or temper the claim.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - R1-M1 (quantitative agreement and error analysis)
  - R1-M3 (turbidity calibration)
  - R1-M4 (ATP-Mg dissociation assumption)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality**: The study is original in its systematic combination of three theoretical methods applied to a specific biological system, and in its focus on electrostatic mechanisms for ATP-dependent reentrant behavior. However, the individual methods are established, and the conceptual framework (electrostatics in LLPS) is not new.
  - **Scientific importance**: The question of how ATP modulates condensate behavior is of high importance, and the study provides a useful electrostatic perspective. However, the limited quantitative agreement with experiment and the acknowledged role of nonelectrostatic interactions temper the significance of the conclusions.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The topic is relevant to biophysics, cell biology, and polymer physics. The methods are described at a level accessible to a broad audience, though some familiarity with field-theoretic approaches is assumed.
  - **Technical soundness**: The methods are appropriate and generally well-executed, but the lack of quantitative error analysis and the simplifying assumptions (e.g., ATP-Mg as a single species) reduce confidence in the quantitative claims.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The manuscript is generally well-written and the main concepts are explained clearly. The Results section is structured logically, and the figures are informative. However, the Discussion is somewhat lengthy and could be more focused.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses an important question with a multi-method approach, and the qualitative trends are consistent across methods and with experiment. However, the quantitative limitations and simplifying assumptions need to be addressed more explicitly, and the claims should be tempered accordingly.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "electrostatics alone can, in large measure, rationalize hydrolysis-independent ATP-modulated biomolecular phase reentrance" (Introduction) is not fully supported, as the authors acknowledge the role of nonelectrostatic interactions but do not quantify their contribution.
- The statement that "the high valency of ATP-magnesium is a significant factor for its colocalization with the condensed phases" (Abstract) is supported by the FTS and rG-RPA results, but the experimental evidence for colocalization is only referenced from prior work and not directly shown here.
- The prediction that "monovalent positive salt ions such as Na+ can be attracted into biomolecular condensates scaffolded by positively charged polyelectrolytic IDRs in the presence of divalent counterions" (Results) is based on rG-RPA and FTS but is not directly tested experimentally.
- The claim that "an overwhelming majority (≈ 87%) of Cl− counterions in the coarse-grained MD system considered are engaged in condensation-driving true bridging interactions" (Results) is based on a specific MD model and may not be generalizable to other systems or conditions.
