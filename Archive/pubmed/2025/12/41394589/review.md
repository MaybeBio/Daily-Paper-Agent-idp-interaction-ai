## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (preprint, bioRxiv)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodology, experimental validation, and reproducibility as presented in the provided text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors introduce BoltzGen, a unified all-atom generative model for designing protein and peptide binders across diverse target modalities. The model integrates structure prediction and design, is controllable via a specification language, and is validated across eight wetlab campaigns covering 26 targets, including novel targets with no known bound structures.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, Introduction, Wetlab Results Summary, Method, Detailed Wetlab Results, Computational Results, Limitations, Conclusion
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures and tables referenced but not provided; detailed experimental protocols (Appendix E); training and evaluation details (Appendices B, C); affinity data tables (Tables 2–7); computational comparison details (Figure 21); code and model weights (external repository)

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a substantial and ambitious contribution to the field of computational protein design. The unification of structure prediction and generative design in a single all-atom model is a conceptually appealing and technically challenging goal. The breadth of experimental validation across multiple modalities and targets is commendable and represents a significant effort. However, the current manuscript, as provided, has several critical gaps that prevent a full assessment of the claims. The absence of key figures, tables, and methodological appendices makes it impossible to verify the technical soundness of the model, the rigor of the experimental validations, and the strength of the comparative analyses. The claims of "universal" binder design and state-of-the-art performance are not fully supported by the evidence presented in this text alone. The manuscript would benefit from a clearer presentation of the model's novelty relative to existing methods, a more detailed description of the experimental methods and statistical analyses, and a more cautious interpretation of the results, particularly where success rates are modest or where alternative explanations (e.g., training data leakage) are possible.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in computational protein design, structural biology, and synthetic biology would be the primary audience. The work is also relevant to those interested in generative models for biomolecules, drug discovery, and the development of novel therapeutic modalities such as nanobodies and peptide binders. The open-source release of the model and pipeline would be of significant value to the community.
- **Major strengths**
  - The conceptual advance of unifying structure prediction and design in a single all-atom generative model is significant and timely.
  - The breadth of experimental validation, spanning multiple binder modalities (nanobodies, proteins, peptides, cyclic peptides) and target types (proteins, small molecules, disordered regions), is impressive and demonstrates a commitment to real-world applicability.
  - The focus on testing against novel targets with low sequence identity to known bound structures is a strong methodological choice that directly addresses the generalization question.
  - The open-source release of code, weights, and data is a major positive for reproducibility and community adoption.
  - The design specification language is a practical and powerful feature that enhances the model's utility.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes): The manuscript's central claim of "universal" binder design is not supported by the evidence presented. The experimental validations, while broad, show variable success rates across targets and modalities. For example, the nanobody and protein designs against novel targets achieve a 66% success rate, but this is based on a small number of tested designs per target (≤15). The results for small molecules and cyclic peptides show weak or moderate affinities (μM range). The claim of universality is an overstatement given the current data.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes): The manuscript lacks critical methodological details and data to assess the technical soundness of the model. The provided text references figures (e.g., Figure 20 for folding performance, Figure 21 for diversity comparison) and tables (e.g., Tables 2–7 for affinity data) that are not included. Without these, it is impossible to evaluate the model's performance relative to state-of-the-art methods (e.g., RFdiffusion, Boltz-2) or to assess the quality of the experimental data. The description of the training data, model architecture, and hyperparameters is insufficient for replication.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes): The experimental validation methodology is not described in sufficient detail. The manuscript mentions "SPR and BLI affinity assays" but does not provide details on the experimental setup, replicates, controls, or statistical analysis. The criteria for defining a "binder" or a "hit" are not clearly stated. The yeast display assay for nanobodies is described, but the lack of a calculated EC50 due to the limited concentration range is a significant weakness. The manuscript would benefit from a more rigorous and transparent presentation of the experimental methods and results.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No): The manuscript does not adequately address the potential for training data leakage or memorization. While the authors acknowledge a memorization issue for a specific length range (ubiquitin), the broader question of whether the model is generalizing or simply retrieving near-neighbor solutions from the training set is not systematically addressed. The comparison with RFdiffusion (Figure 21) is a step in the right direction, but the methodology and results are not presented in the text.
  - **R1-M5** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No): The computational comparison with existing methods is incomplete. The manuscript mentions a comparison with RFdiffusion and RFdiffusionAA but does not provide details on the experimental setup, metrics, or results. A more comprehensive comparison, including runtime, memory usage, and success rates on a common benchmark, would strengthen the claims of superiority.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity): The abstract states "eight diverse wetlab design campaigns with functional and affinity readouts across 26 targets." However, the text describes experiments against 9 novel targets, 3 bioactive peptides, 1 disordered region, 1 RagC, 1 RagA:RagC, 2 small molecules, and 5 benchmark targets. The total number of unique targets and the categorization of "campaigns" should be clarified.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Reproducibility): The manuscript mentions "some data are temporarily confidential at collaborators' request." While this is understandable, it limits the ability of the community to fully assess the claims. The authors should consider providing anonymized or aggregated data where possible.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity): The description of the "geometric encoding of residue type" is interesting but brief. A more detailed explanation, perhaps with a schematic figure, would help readers understand this key innovation.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Completeness): The manuscript mentions "BoltzGen's rich design specification language provides a large degree of control" but does not provide a formal specification or examples of the language's syntax and capabilities. A supplementary section with examples would be valuable.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Interpretation): The statement "we test 15 nanobody and protein binder designs against each of nine novel targets... yields nanomolar binders for 66% of targets" is a key result. However, the number of binders per target that were actually successful is not reported. A more detailed breakdown of the results per target would be informative.
  - **R1-m6** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Context): The introduction would benefit from a more thorough discussion of the limitations of existing methods, particularly regarding their ability to generalize to novel targets and handle diverse modalities. This would better contextualize the contribution of BoltzGen.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3 (as detailed above)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality:** The concept of a single all-atom model unifying structure prediction and design is a novel and potentially impactful contribution. The geometric encoding of residue identity is an innovative approach. However, the originality of the overall framework relative to existing diffusion-based methods (e.g., RFdiffusion, Chroma) needs to be more clearly articulated.
  - **Scientific importance:** The potential to design binders against a wide range of targets, including those without known structures, is of high scientific and therapeutic importance. The experimental validation, if rigorously supported, would be a significant step forward. However, the current evidence is insufficient to fully establish this importance.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership:** The work is likely to appeal to a broad audience in computational biology, structural biology, and drug discovery. The writing is generally accessible, but the technical details of the model may be challenging for non-specialists.
  - **Technical soundness:** The technical soundness cannot be fully assessed from the provided text. The lack of figures, tables, and appendices prevents a thorough evaluation of the model's architecture, training, and performance. The experimental methods are not described in sufficient detail to assess their rigor.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists:** The abstract and introduction are well-written and accessible. However, the methods section is dense and assumes a high level of familiarity with diffusion models and protein design. A more accessible summary of the key innovations and results would be beneficial.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript presents a promising and ambitious framework with a substantial experimental effort. However, the absence of key data and methodological details prevents a full assessment of the claims. The authors should be encouraged to resubmit a revised version that addresses the major concerns, particularly by providing the missing figures, tables, and appendices, and by tempering the claims of universality.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "universal" binder design is not supported by the current evidence.
- The claim that BoltzGen "matches the performance of state-of-the-art folding models" (Figure 20) cannot be verified without the figure and details of the evaluation setup.
- The claim that BoltzGen "pays more attention" to the target than RFdiffusion (Figure 21) cannot be verified without the figure and details of the comparison.
- The experimental success rates, while promising, are based on small numbers of tested designs and lack detailed statistical analysis.
- The generalizability of the model to targets beyond those tested is not established.
