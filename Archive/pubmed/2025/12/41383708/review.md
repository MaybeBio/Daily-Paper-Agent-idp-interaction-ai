## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a well-structured and readable review that synthesizes current knowledge on m1A and m6A in the context of biomolecular condensation. The authors present a balanced view, including conflicting findings (e.g., Khong et al. vs. Ries et al.), and propose mechanistic models and future directions. The manuscript is likely to be of interest to researchers in RNA biology, phase separation, and neurodegeneration. However, several claims are made with insufficient nuance or without clear distinction between established findings and speculative interpretations. The review would benefit from more explicit caveats regarding the correlative nature of some studies and the technical limitations of detection methods. Overall, the manuscript is informative but requires revisions to strengthen its critical rigor and clarity.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying RNA modifications, epitranscriptomics, liquid-liquid phase separation, stress granule biology, and protein aggregation disorders such as ALS and Huntington's disease. The review provides a useful synthesis of two rapidly evolving fields and highlights open questions that could guide future experimental work.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript offers a clear and organized overview of m1A and m6A biology, including detection methods, computational tools, and functional roles in condensation. It explicitly addresses conflicting evidence, which is a strength in a review. The discussion of therapeutic implications is forward-looking and relevant. The writing is accessible to nonspecialists.
- **Major Concerns** Several claims are presented with insufficient critical evaluation. The distinction between direct experimental evidence and inferred mechanisms is not always clear. The review does not adequately address the technical limitations of the cited studies, particularly regarding detection methods and the potential for artifacts. The discussion of m1A's protective versus toxic roles is somewhat speculative and would benefit from a more explicit framework for how these opposing outcomes might be reconciled. The figures are referenced but not available for evaluation, which limits the ability to assess the clarity of the visual summaries.
- **Minor Comments** Some terminology is used inconsistently (e.g., "methyladenine" vs. "methyladenosine"). The abbreviation list is helpful but could be expanded. The section on computational tools is dense and could be streamlined for readability. The discussion of therapeutic strategies is brief and could be expanded with more concrete examples or challenges.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The manuscript is a review, so the "case" is the synthesis of existing evidence. The main technical failing is the lack of critical depth in evaluating the cited studies. For example, the claim that m1A "protects transcripts from entanglement with misfolded proteins" is presented as a model but is not clearly distinguished from a hypothesis. Similarly, the statement that m6A "accelerates stress granule assembly" is supported by some studies but contradicted by others, and the review does not fully explore the reasons for these discrepancies.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence quality and interpretation
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that m1A "acts as a protective mark in stress granules, facilitating mRNA triage and translational recovery" and that on CAG repeat RNAs it "aberrantly recruits TDP-43 into neurotoxic inclusions."
- **Evidence pointer** Section "m1A in stress granules and protein aggregates", location not provided
- **Concern** The review presents these opposing roles of m1A as established findings, but the evidence for both is largely correlative and derived from a limited number of studies. The mechanistic basis for how m1A can be protective in one context and toxic in another is not adequately explained. The authors should more clearly distinguish between direct experimental evidence and proposed models, and discuss the limitations of the studies that support these claims.
- **Why it matters** Without this distinction, readers may overinterpret the strength of the evidence, leading to potentially misleading conclusions about the role of m1A in disease and physiology.
- **Resolution test** The authors should revise the text to explicitly state which findings are directly supported by experimental data and which are speculative or inferred. They should also discuss alternative interpretations and the need for further validation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Critical evaluation of conflicting evidence
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that m6A "enhances the capacity for phase separation" and "accelerates stress granule assembly," citing studies by Ries et al. and others, but also acknowledges conflicting findings from Khong et al.
- **Evidence pointer** Section "m6A as a phase separation enhancer", location not provided
- **Concern** The review presents the pro-phase-separation role of m6A as a dominant narrative but does not sufficiently explore the reasons for the discrepancies between studies. The authors should critically evaluate the methodological differences, experimental systems, and potential confounding factors that may explain why some studies find a strong effect of m6A on stress granule assembly while others do not.
- **Why it matters** A balanced and critical discussion of conflicting evidence is essential for a review, as it helps readers understand the current state of the field and the limitations of existing data.
- **Resolution test** The authors should add a dedicated subsection or paragraph that systematically compares the conflicting studies, discusses potential reasons for the discrepancies, and outlines what additional experiments would be needed to resolve them.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Technical limitations of detection methods
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript describes various methods for detecting m1A and m6A, including antibody-based approaches, chemical conversion, and nanopore sequencing, but does not critically evaluate their limitations.
- **Evidence pointer** Section "Methods for m1A and m6A detection", location not provided
- **Concern** The review mentions the issue of antibody cross-reactivity and the Dimroth rearrangement but does not provide a comprehensive assessment of the strengths and weaknesses of each method. For example, the low resolution of antibody-based approaches and the potential for false positives in m6A mapping due to m1A conversion are not fully discussed.
- **Why it matters** The reliability of detection methods is fundamental to interpreting the functional studies described later in the review. A more critical evaluation would help readers assess the robustness of the evidence.
- **Resolution test** The authors should expand the methods section to include a more detailed comparison of detection techniques, including their sensitivity, specificity, and potential artifacts, and discuss how these limitations affect the interpretation of the cited studies.
