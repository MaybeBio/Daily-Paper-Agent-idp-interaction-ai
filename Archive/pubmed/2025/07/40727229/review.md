## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and supplementary figure/table references.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological rigor, validity of claims, and alignment with stated conclusions. Editorial and stylistic issues are noted only where they affect comprehension.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that charge-based transient interactions within intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) can be quantitatively predicted using a novel sequence metric, the effective charge patch length (N_eff), derived from coarse-grained simulations. They further claim that approximately 20% of human disordered regions exhibit such interactions, that these interactions promote network formation in condensates, and that they are functionally enriched in specific biological processes.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures (referenced as a through f across sections), supplementary figures S1-S10, supplementary tables S1-S6, methods descriptions, and a code availability link.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Actual figure images, supplementary tables (S1-S6), supplementary figures (S1-S10), and the code repository contents were not provided. Simulation parameter details (e.g., force field parameters, salt concentration ranges) are only partially described in the methods text.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a systematic computational study of charge-based transient interactions in IDPs, introducing a new sequence metric (N_eff) that collapses two sequence parameters (N and f_c) into one predictive variable. The work is timely and addresses an important gap in IDP research, namely the quantitative prediction of transient, specific interactions from sequence alone. The authors demonstrate a clear progression from model peptide simulations to proteome-wide analysis and functional enrichment, which is commendable. However, several technical concerns must be addressed before the central claims can be fully accepted. The most significant issues relate to the transferability of the fitted parameters (β, b1, b2) beyond the training set, the treatment of asymmetric charge patches in the prediction algorithm, and the statistical robustness of the GO enrichment analysis given the small number of sequences in some f_t bins.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of IDP biophysics, computational biology, and protein phase separation would find this work directly relevant. The proposed N_eff metric offers a simple, sequence-based predictor of transient interaction propensity that could be widely adopted. The proteome-wide analysis identifying ~20% of disordered regions with charge-driven transient interactions will be of interest to those studying IDP function and dysfunction. The connection between transient interactions and condensate network formation is also relevant to the growing community investigating biomolecular condensates.
- **Major strengths** The study is well-designed, moving logically from controlled model systems to complex biological sequences. The introduction of N_eff as a unifying metric is conceptually elegant and practically useful. The authors are transparent about limitations, such as the inability to determine f_t at very small sequence separations. The GO enrichment analysis, while correlational, provides a useful framework for generating hypotheses about IDP function. The computational methods are described in sufficient detail to be reproducible.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Generalizability
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the empirical relationship between f_t and N_eff (with β = 0.76) can be used to predict transient interactions in arbitrary IDP sequences.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Empirical Estimation of Transient Interactions Driven by Sequence Factors", Figures 2a-2f, Supplementary Figures S2-S5
  - **Concern** The scaling parameter β = 0.76 was optimized globally across all simulated conditions and sequence variants. However, the authors then apply this fixed β to predict f_t for biological IDPs with potentially very different sequence compositions, including those with asymmetric charge patches. The validation of this transferability is limited to a small set of new model peptides (Table S6), and the authors themselves note that the prediction consistently overestimates f_t for asymmetric patches, requiring an additional correction factor. This raises the question of whether β is truly a universal constant or an artifact of the training set. The manuscript does not provide a clear physical justification for why β should be independent of sequence context or environmental conditions.
  - **Why it matters** The central practical contribution of this work is the ability to predict transient interaction propensity from sequence alone. If the prediction is not robust across diverse sequence types, the utility of N_eff as a general metric is significantly diminished. The correction for asymmetry appears ad hoc and may not capture the full complexity of real IDP sequences.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should validate the predictive power of their model on a larger, diverse set of IDP sequences with experimentally characterized transient interactions (e.g., from PRE or FRET studies). They should also test whether β varies systematically with sequence composition or environmental conditions, and if so, provide a more nuanced model that accounts for this variation. Alternatively, they should clearly state the limitations of their prediction for highly asymmetric charge distributions and provide guidance on when the model is expected to fail.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / Statistical rigor
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that GO enrichment analysis reveals functional categories associated with different levels of transient interactions, including a "turning" class where enrichment peaks at medium f_t.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Exploration of the Functional Relevance of Transient Interactions", Figure 3e-3f, Supplementary Figure S10
  - **Concern** The GO enrichment analysis classifies terms into four categories (ascending, descending, turning, none) based on Spearman correlation coefficients and visual inspection of fold enrichment patterns. The threshold of 0.8 for the correlation coefficient is arbitrary, and the classification of "turning" terms relies on a two-sided test that may be sensitive to noise, especially for terms with low representation. The manuscript does not report confidence intervals or statistical significance for the fold enrichment values, nor does it account for multiple hypothesis testing across the large number of GO terms examined. The statement that "low-level terms showed a significant preference for the turning class" is not supported by any statistical test.
  - **Why it matters** The functional relevance of transient interactions is a key claim of the manuscript. If the GO enrichment analysis is not statistically robust, the conclusions about which biological processes depend on transient interactions may be unreliable. The distinction between "turning" and "none" classes is particularly important, as it suggests a non-monotonic relationship that could have interesting biological implications, but this needs to be demonstrated rigorously.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide statistical measures of uncertainty for the fold enrichment values (e.g., bootstrap confidence intervals) and apply multiple testing corrections (e.g., Benjamini-Hochberg). They should also justify the choice of correlation coefficient threshold and demonstrate that the classification is stable with respect to this choice. For the "turning" class, they should show that the peak in enrichment is statistically significant and not an artifact of sparse data in certain f_t bins.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance / Conceptual clarity
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "transient interactions may shape IDP condensate microstructures, fine-tuning membraneless organelle formation" and that their simulations show network formation in condensates.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Influence of Transient Interactions on Conformational Behaviors", Figures 3a-3d, Supplementary Figures S6-S8
  - **Concern** The connection between the single-chain f_t parameter and the multichain network formation in condensates is not fully developed. The authors show that higher N_eff correlates with larger clusters and increased R_g in condensates, but the mechanistic link is not clearly articulated. Specifically, it is unclear whether the network formation is driven by the same transient interactions that govern single-chain behavior, or whether multivalent interactions in the condensed phase introduce new physics not captured by the single-chain model. The authors also do not discuss how their findings relate to experimental observations of condensate properties (e.g., viscosity, dynamics) in systems with varying charge blockiness.
  - **Why it matters** The claim that transient interactions are functionally relevant in condensates is a significant extension of the single-chain analysis. If the connection is not rigorously established, the functional implications for phase separation remain speculative. The manuscript would benefit from a more explicit discussion of how the single-chain f_t relates to multichain properties, and what experimental predictions arise from their model.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the relationship between f_t and network properties, such as the number of interchain contacts per chain, the cluster size distribution, and the dynamics of chain exchange. They should also discuss how their findings compare to experimental studies of condensate dynamics in systems with varying charge distributions, and propose specific experimental tests of their predictions.

  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Affected element** Methods, Coarse-Grained (CG) Model
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods, "Coarse-Grained (CG) Model" section
  - **Issue** The methods state that simulations were run at "a temperature of 298 K" but earlier in the results section, simulations are described at "300 K" (e.g., "we conducted additional simulations at 300 K and 0.1 M ionic strength"). This inconsistency is confusing and should be clarified.
  - **Required correction** The authors should verify the correct simulation temperature and ensure consistency throughout the manuscript.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Affected element** Results, "Empirical Estimation of Transient Interactions Driven by Sequence Factors"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, equation 2
  - **Issue** The sigmoid function in equation 2 is written as "ft=1−exp[b1(Neff2−b2)]−1". This appears to be a logistic function, but the notation is unusual. The standard form would be ft = 1 / (1 + exp[-b1(N_eff^2 - b2)]). The current notation is ambiguous and should be rewritten for clarity.
  - **Required correction** The authors should rewrite equation 2 in a standard mathematical form and ensure that the parameters b1 and b2 are clearly defined.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Affected element** Results, "Exploration of the Functional Relevance of Transient Interactions"
  - **Evidence pointer** Results, Figure 3e-3f
  - **Issue** The manuscript states that "approximately 20.3% of IDPs show f_t values larger than 0.1" but does not specify the total number of sequences analyzed or the distribution of f_t values across the five bins. This makes it difficult to assess the robustness of the enrichment analysis.
  - **Required correction** The authors should provide the number of sequences in each f_t bin and the total number of sequences analyzed, either in the main text or in a supplementary table.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The transferability of the N_eff-based prediction model to diverse biological sequences (R1-M1) and the statistical rigor of the GO enrichment analysis (R1-M2) are the two most critical technical issues. Both must be addressed before the central claims of the manuscript can be considered fully supported.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** The concept of an effective charge patch length as a predictive metric for transient interactions is novel and represents a useful conceptual advance. The application of this metric to proteome-wide analysis is also original.
  - **Scientific importance** The work addresses a fundamental question in IDP biology, namely how sequence encodes transient, specific interactions. The finding that ~20% of human disordered regions may exhibit such interactions has broad implications. However, the functional significance of these interactions remains correlational, and the mechanistic link to condensate properties is not fully established.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The work bridges biophysics, computational biology, and functional genomics. The N_eff metric is simple enough to be adopted by experimentalists, and the proteome-wide analysis will interest a broad audience. However, the technical details of the simulation and fitting procedures may be challenging for non-specialists.
  - **Technical soundness** The simulation methodology is sound, and the fitting procedure is clearly described. However, the transferability of the model and the statistical robustness of the enrichment analysis are not fully demonstrated, as detailed in the major concerns.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is generally well-written, but the introduction contains formatting errors that impede readability. The methods are detailed but may be dense for non-computational readers. The discussion does a good job of summarizing the key findings in accessible language.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a valuable conceptual framework and a useful predictive tool, but the transferability of the model and the statistical rigor of the functional analysis must be strengthened before the claims can be fully accepted.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that N_eff can predict f_t for arbitrary biological IDP sequences is not fully supported, as validation is limited to a small set of model peptides with asymmetric charge patches, and the correction factor appears ad hoc.
- The claim that "low-level terms showed a significant preference for the turning class" is not supported by any statistical test.
- The claim that transient interactions "may play roles in their biological functions" is based on correlational GO enrichment analysis and is not mechanistically demonstrated.
- The relationship between single-chain f_t and multichain network formation in condensates is not rigorously established, and the claim that transient interactions "shape IDP condensate microstructures" is speculative.
- The statement that "over 10% of IDPs exhibit charge-driven transient interactions" in the discussion is inconsistent with the ~20% figure reported in the results; this discrepancy should be clarified.
