The misfolding of intrinsically disordered alpha-synuclein protein, which can form beta-sheet-rich fibrillar amyloid structures, is closely associated with Parkinson's disease (PD). The peptide alpha-synuclein 47-56 has been identified as the toxic core of alpha-synuclein and plays a pivotal role in the aggregation and misfolding processes of this protein. Investigating the template induction behavior of this peptide is crucial for elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying alpha-synuclein misfolding and aggregation. To explore the molecular mechanism of the peptide alpha-synuclein 47-56, guided by the alpha-synuclein pentamer template, we conducted a 400 ns molecular dynamics simulation. In this simulation, the peptide alpha-synuclein 47-56 was positioned on both sides of the alpha-synuclein pentamers. Our results demonstrate distinct elongation characteristics of the peptide alpha-synuclein 47-56 on the two sides of the pentamer. The beta-sheet structure readily formed on the left side of the alpha-synuclein pentamer, facilitating template induction. In contrast, the formation of beta-sheet secondary structures was hindered on the right side of the alpha-synuclein pentamer. Furthermore, our analysis reveals that hydrogen bonding, electrostatic interactions, and van der Waals forces between the alpha-synuclein pentamer and monomer are crucial for beta-sheet extension. Notably, we identified the alpha-synuclein 49-53 region as a key peptide segment in this process.
