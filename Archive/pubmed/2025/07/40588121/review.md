## Review setup

- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that a prion-like disordered region in the C-terminal half of the human RNA-binding protein SMAUG1 is required but not sufficient for liquid-like condensate formation in cells. They report that condensation is independent of RNA binding and of other large disordered regions. They report that 14-3-3 proteins negatively regulate SMAUG1 condensation via at least four phospho-regulated short linear motifs, acting synergistically, and that dimeric but not monomeric 14-3-3 induces condensate dissolution, suggesting conformational bridging is important.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental details, numerical data, or methodological descriptions provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, supplementary information, detailed methods, statistical analyses, and raw data

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting and mechanistically focused study on the regulation of SMAUG1 phase separation by 14-3-3 proteins. The central claims are coherent and the proposed regulatory model is plausible. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the rigor of the experimental support. Several claims, particularly those regarding RNA independence, the sufficiency of the prion-like region, and the mechanistic role of dimeric 14-3-3, require careful scrutiny of the underlying data. The work is likely to be of interest to the biomolecular condensate and RNA-binding protein communities, but the strength of the conclusions cannot be fully assessed from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying biomolecular condensates and liquid-liquid phase separation, particularly those focused on RNA-binding proteins and their regulation. The 14-3-3 field will also find this relevant given the emerging role of 14-3-3 proteins in modulating condensation. Additionally, investigators interested in intrinsically disordered regions and phospho-regulated short linear motifs as drivers of phase behavior will find the work pertinent.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a timely and important question regarding the regulatory mechanisms of RBP condensation. The combination of computational prediction, structural modeling, in vitro biochemistry, and cell-based assays is a strong multi-pronged approach. The specific focus on the role of dimeric versus monomeric 14-3-3 provides a mechanistically interesting angle that goes beyond simple binding inhibition. The identification of multiple synergistic phospho-regulated motifs adds depth to the proposed regulatory model.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that SMAUG1 liquid-liquid phase separation appears to be independent of RNA binding and does not depend on other large, disordered regions of the protein.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** The abstract states that LLPS is "independent of RNA binding" and "does not depend on other large, disordered regions." These are strong negative claims. Without seeing the specific deletion constructs, the RNA-binding mutants used, and the quantitative assays (e.g., turbidity, microscopy, FRAP) that support these conclusions, it is impossible to verify that the experiments were sufficiently comprehensive to rule out contributions from these elements. For example, the claim of RNA independence requires demonstration across a range of RNA concentrations and with multiple RNA species.
  - **Why it matters** If the negative claims are not rigorously supported, the central model of what drives SMAUG1 condensation could be incomplete or incorrect. Overstated independence could mislead the field about the minimal determinants of SMAUG1 phase separation.
  - **Resolution test** The full manuscript must show that multiple RNA-binding-deficient mutants or RNA-depleted conditions do not alter condensation properties in quantitative assays, and that deletion of each large disordered region individually and in combination does not reduce condensate formation or alter their material properties.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Mechanistic interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that dimeric 14-3-3 proteins drive condensate dissolution and that the conformational constraints imposed by dimeric 14-3-3, potentially bridging distant binding sites, are important for the regulatory mechanism.
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** The interpretation that dimeric 14-3-3 acts via conformational bridging is one possible explanation, but alternative mechanisms exist. For example, dimeric 14-3-3 could simply have higher avidity for the four motifs, leading to more effective sequestration of SMAUG1 molecules, without requiring a specific bridging or conformational constraint model. The abstract does not indicate whether the authors tested avidity-matched controls or whether the monomeric variant was truly equivalent in binding affinity to individual motifs.
  - **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism is a key novel contribution of the study. If the alternative avidity explanation is not excluded, the mechanistic claim is overstated and the conceptual advance is diminished.
  - **Resolution test** The manuscript must provide evidence that the monomeric 14-3-3 variant binds the SMAUG1 motifs with comparable affinity to the dimeric form, and that the inability of the monomer to dissolve condensates is not simply due to weaker binding. Ideally, experiments with engineered dimeric variants with altered spacing or orientation of binding sites would strengthen the conformational constraint model.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Generalizability and completeness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that interactions between SMAUG1 and 14-3-3 are "likely intertwined with other unknown regulatory mechanisms."
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
  - **Concern** This statement is appropriately cautious, but it also highlights that the presented model may be incomplete. The abstract does not indicate whether the authors tested the effect of phosphorylation state of the four motifs in cells, whether they examined the dynamics of 14-3-3 binding in live cells, or whether they considered other post-translational modifications. The claim of synergy among the four motifs is made, but the abstract does not describe how synergy was quantified or whether all four motifs are required simultaneously.
  - **Why it matters** For a Nature-style audience, the completeness of the regulatory model is important. If the synergy claim is not quantitatively supported, the model may be oversimplified. The acknowledgment of unknown mechanisms is honest but leaves the story feeling incomplete.
  - **Resolution test** The full manuscript should include quantitative binding assays (e.g., ITC, MST) for each motif and combinations thereof, and cell-based experiments with phospho-mimetic or phospho-dead mutants to validate the synergy and the role of phosphorylation in the cellular context.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 and R1-M2 are the primary technical concerns. The negative claims of RNA independence and independence from other disordered regions require robust experimental evidence. The mechanistic interpretation of dimeric 14-3-3 action requires exclusion of simpler avidity-based explanations. Without these, the central model is not fully established.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The application of 14-3-3-mediated regulation to SMAUG1 condensation appears novel and extends recent work on 14-3-3 in phase separation. The focus on dimeric versus monomeric 14-3-3 is a distinctive angle. However, the abstract does not clearly differentiate this from prior 14-3-3 condensation studies.
  - Scientific importance: The work addresses a fundamental question in RBP biology and condensation regulation. If the mechanistic claims hold, it would be a valuable contribution. The importance is moderate to high, but the abstract alone does not demonstrate broad significance beyond the specific protein.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The topic bridges cell biology, biophysics, and biochemistry. The abstract is written in a way that is accessible to these communities, but the mechanistic detail may limit appeal to a broader readership.
  - Technical soundness: Cannot be fully assessed from the abstract. The multi-method approach is promising, but the strength of the negative claims and the mechanistic interpretation are unverified.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is reasonably clear but uses field-specific terms (e.g., prion-like, short linear motifs, LLPS) without full explanation. It is readable for specialists but not for a general scientific audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The topic is relevant and the proposed model is interesting, but the abstract-level evidence is insufficient to establish the central claims. The authors must provide rigorous data addressing R1-M1 and R1-M2 in the full manuscript.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- The claim that LLPS is "independent of RNA binding" is unsupported at the abstract level; no experimental detail is provided.
- The claim that condensation "does not depend on other large, disordered regions" is unsupported at the abstract level.
- The claim that dimeric 14-3-3 acts via "conformational constraints" and "bridging distant binding sites" is a plausible interpretation but is not supported over alternative avidity-based explanations from the abstract alone.
- The claim of "synergistic" action of the four motifs is stated but not quantitatively supported in the abstract.
- The statement that the prion-like region is "required, but not sufficient" is a clear claim, but the evidence for "not sufficient" is not detailed.
- The overall regulatory model is presented as coherent, but the abstract does not provide enough detail to assess whether all components of the model are experimentally validated.
