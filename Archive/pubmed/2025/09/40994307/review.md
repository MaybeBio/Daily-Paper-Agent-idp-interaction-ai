## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, experimental section, results, discussion, conclusion, and supplementary material references.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological rigor, internal consistency, and support of claims by presented evidence. No assessment of editorial fit or publication policy.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that Zn2+ binding to N-terminally acetylated α-synuclein monomers induces conformational compaction, as measured by native MS and ion mobility MS, and that this compaction correlates with a ca. 5-fold acceleration of amyloid formation kinetics measured by ThT fluorescence. NMR and MD simulations are used to propose a mechanism involving transient, multivalent electrostatic interactions bridging N- and C-terminal regions.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract, full text of introduction, experimental methods, results, discussion, conclusion, and acknowledgements. Figures are referenced but not provided. Supplementary figures are referenced but not provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures and supplementary figures are referenced but not included in the provided material. This limits the ability to assess data quality, statistical treatment, and the validity of quantitative claims. Raw data, fitting parameters, and validation metrics for the Kd fitting are not available.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a question of significant interest in the field of protein misfolding and neurodegeneration: how a physiologically relevant metal ion modulates the conformational ensemble of an intrinsically disordered protein and its amyloid propensity. The combination of native MS, IM-MS, ThT kinetics, NMR, and MD is ambitious and appropriate. The central claim, that Zn2+-induced compaction correlates with accelerated aggregation, is plausible and potentially important. However, the provided text alone does not allow verification of the key quantitative relationships, and several methodological details require clarification. The mechanistic model, while attractive, is inferred from correlative data and simulations, and the causal link between compaction and accelerated fibril formation is not directly established.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying α-synuclein aggregation, metal ion interactions with intrinsically disordered proteins, amyloid assembly mechanisms, and the application of native mass spectrometry and ion mobility to protein conformational analysis. The work may also interest those investigating the molecular basis of Parkinson's disease and other synucleinopathies, particularly regarding environmental factors that modulate aggregation.
- **Major strengths** The study integrates multiple orthogonal biophysical techniques under matched solution conditions, which is a methodological strength. The use of N-terminally acetylated αS, the physiologically relevant form, is appropriate. The observation that binding affinity is similar across charge states and that binding is non-cooperative is a useful contribution. The MD simulations provide a plausible mechanistic framework for how weak, transient interactions could drive conformational change.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The central correlative claim between IM-MS compaction and ThT t50 is presented as "mirror precisely," but the quantitative analysis supporting this correlation is not visible in the provided text. No statistical measures, correlation coefficients, or error analysis are presented.
  - R1-M2: The Kd values derived from native MS are described as "apparent" and "semiquantitative," but the fitting procedure and its limitations are not fully described. The claim that binding is non-cooperative relies on this analysis, which is not fully visible.
  - R1-M3: The mechanistic model proposing that Zn2+ bridges N- and C-terminal regions to expose the NAC domain is speculative. The NMR data identify H50 and D121 as key residues, but the text does not demonstrate that these interactions are causally linked to the observed compaction or accelerated aggregation.
  - R1-M4: The relationship between the concentration ranges used in different techniques (ThT, MS, NMR) and the claim that "all emerge in the overlapping submillimolar regime" requires more explicit presentation of the actual concentration ranges and how cross-technique comparisons were made.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The term "t50" is used throughout but not explicitly defined in the text. It should be defined as the time to reach half-maximal ThT fluorescence.
  - R1-m2: The manuscript states that Zn2+ binding was monitored at concentrations up to 800 μM, but the text does not clarify whether all techniques used the same maximum concentration or how saturation was defined.
  - R1-m3: The discussion of Ca2+ binding as a comparison is interesting, but the text does not provide quantitative comparison of Ca2+ effects on compaction or kinetics under the same conditions.
  - R1-m4: The statement that "the most amyloid-prone conformational family in the presence of Zn2+ is the most compact" is presented as a conclusion, but the evidence for identifying this specific family as "amyloid-prone" is not clear from the text.
  - R1-m5: The manuscript would benefit from a more explicit statement of the limitations of IM-MS for IDPs, particularly regarding gas-phase compaction effects and their relevance to solution conformations.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 
  - R1-M1: Quantitative correlation between compaction and t50 is not demonstrated with statistical rigor.
  - R1-M2: Kd fitting procedure and validation are not fully described.
  - R1-M4: Cross-technique concentration comparisons are not clearly presented.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The study is original in its combination of techniques and its focus on N-terminally acetylated αS with Zn2+. The finding that weak, multivalent interactions can drive conformational change is not entirely new but is applied here in a novel context.
  - Scientific importance: The question of how metal ions modulate αS aggregation is important for understanding Parkinson's disease pathogenesis. The study provides a plausible mechanism, but the causal link is not fully established.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work will appeal to biophysicists, mass spectrometrists, structural biologists, and those in the neurodegeneration field. The methods are described in sufficient detail for specialists, but the broader significance could be more clearly articulated for non-specialists.
  - Technical soundness: The techniques are appropriate and well-established. However, the lack of visible quantitative analysis and figures limits the assessment of technical soundness. The MD simulations are described but their convergence and validation are not discussed.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, but some sections, particularly the MD and IM-MS descriptions, assume specialist knowledge. The abstract is clear and conveys the main findings effectively.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The study addresses an important question with an appropriate multi-technique approach. However, the central correlative claim and the mechanistic model require stronger quantitative support and clearer presentation of the data. The manuscript would be strengthened by explicit statistical analysis of the correlation, a more detailed description of the Kd fitting, and a more cautious interpretation of the causal relationship between compaction and aggregation.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that IM-MS compaction "mirrors precisely" the accelerated aggregation kinetics is not supported by visible quantitative analysis.
- The claim that Zn2+ binding is non-cooperative is based on a fitting procedure that is not fully described.
- The mechanistic model proposing that Zn2+ bridges N- and C-terminal regions to expose the NAC domain is speculative and not directly tested.
- The statement that "the most amyloid-prone conformational family in the presence of Zn2+ is the most compact" is not directly supported by the data presented.
- The claim that the observed effects occur at "biologically relevant concentrations" is based on a comparison with literature values, but the actual concentration dependence of the effects is not fully presented.
