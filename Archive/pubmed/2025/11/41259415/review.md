## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion) without figures, tables, or supplementary information
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodological soundness, and evidence consistency based solely on the provided text; no assessment of figures, tables, or supplementary files
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulations with the CALVADOS2 model to compare phase separation behavior of M- and Z-α1-antitrypsin variants, propose that the Z variant's disordered C-terminus drives liquid-liquid phase separation, and show that inter-protein β-sheet formation increases condensate viscosity over time, with surface-induced heterogeneous nucleation as a potential mechanism for ER membrane-associated aggregate formation
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of simulation methods, qualitative results (coexistence lines, contact maps, viscosity values, density profiles), and references to figures (Fig 1-4) and supplementary sections (S1 File) that are not directly visible
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures, tables, supplementary information, simulation parameter details, and quantitative error estimates; the manuscript text alone is insufficient to verify numerical claims or statistical significance

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a plausible computational model for α1-antitrypsin condensate formation and maturation, with a clear mechanistic narrative linking the Z variant's disordered C-terminus to enhanced phase separation and subsequent β-sheet-mediated hardening. The work is relevant to the field of protein aggregation diseases and condensate biology. However, the evidence base visible in the text alone is insufficient to fully evaluate the robustness of the claims, particularly regarding the quantitative viscosity changes, the specificity of the proposed mechanism, and the biological relevance of the surface model. The authors should be commended for addressing a clinically important problem with a tractable computational approach, but several technical and interpretive concerns need resolution.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying protein phase separation, condensate biology, and aggregation disorders (including α1-antitrypsin deficiency, neurodegeneration, and related protein misfolding diseases) would find this work relevant. Computational biophysicists interested in coarse-grained models of protein behavior and the development of aging algorithms for β-sheet formation would also be interested. Clinicians and translational researchers focused on AATD pathogenesis and therapeutic development may find the mechanistic insights valuable, though the computational nature of the study limits direct clinical applicability.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a clinically significant problem (AATD) with a mechanistic computational framework that connects molecular-level behavior to macroscopic condensate properties. The use of a sequence-dependent coarse-grained model allows for direct comparison of M and Z variants, and the adaptation of an aging algorithm to model β-sheet transitions is a novel methodological contribution. The inclusion of surface effects (both attractive and repulsive) to model heterogeneous nucleation is a thoughtful extension that aligns with experimental observations of ER membrane-associated aggregates. The authors also validate their approach against experimental findings, such as the lack of phase separation in C-terminally truncated variants, which strengthens the credibility of the model.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: The authors claim that the Z variant has a greater propensity to undergo LLPS compared to the M variant, as shown by coexistence lines in Fig 1c, but the text does not provide quantitative details on the critical temperatures, coexistence densities, or statistical uncertainties. Evidence pointer: Section 3.1, Fig 1c (location not provided). Concern: The claim of differential phase separation propensity is central to the paper, but the visible text lacks numerical values for Tc, coexistence densities, or error bars. Without these, it is impossible to assess whether the difference between M and Z is statistically significant or biologically meaningful. The authors state that Z has a higher critical solution temperature, but no specific values are given. This is a critical gap because the entire mechanistic narrative depends on this difference. Why it matters: The central hypothesis is that the Z variant's disordered C-terminus drives phase separation, but if the difference in Tc is marginal or within simulation error, the conclusion would be weakened. Resolution test: Provide quantitative values for Tc and coexistence densities for both variants, including error estimates from multiple independent simulations. Show that the difference is statistically significant (e.g., via bootstrapping or replicate analysis).)
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: The authors claim that inter-protein β-sheet formation increases condensate viscosity from 0.336 mPa·s to 0.850 mPa·s after 200 ns incubation, but the text does not provide details on the statistical significance, convergence, or systematic errors in these calculations. Evidence pointer: Section 3.3, Fig 3c (location not provided). Concern: The viscosity values are presented as single numbers without error bars or replicate information. The Green-Kubo integration and Maxwell mode fitting are sensitive to simulation length and system size, and the authors acknowledge that the coarse-grained model underestimates relaxation timescales. However, they do not discuss how this affects the absolute viscosity values or the relative change. The claim that the viscosity increase corresponds to "maturation" is plausible but not quantitatively supported. Why it matters: The viscosity change is a key output of the study, as it links β-sheet formation to material property changes. Without uncertainty quantification, the reader cannot judge whether the 2.5-fold increase is robust or an artifact of the fitting procedure. Resolution test: Provide error bars on viscosity values from multiple independent simulations, discuss convergence of the Green-Kubo integral, and show that the increase is statistically significant. Also, discuss how the known timescale underestimation affects the interpretation of the 200 ns incubation period.)
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Scientific importance, Claim pointer: The authors claim that the presence of either attractive or repulsive surfaces enhances Z-α1-antitrypsin condensate formation, suggesting heterogeneous nucleation as a mechanism for ER membrane-associated aggregation, but the surface model is highly simplified and may not capture the biochemical complexity of the ER membrane. Evidence pointer: Section 3.4, Fig 4 (location not provided). Concern: The surface is modeled as a flat, structureless wall with either a weak attractive or purely repulsive potential. While the authors acknowledge this simplification, the biological relevance of the findings is limited. The ER membrane is a complex, dynamic environment with specific lipid compositions, proteins, and curvature, none of which are captured. The claim that "both types of external surfaces induce local high-density fluctuations" is interesting but does not directly inform on the in vivo mechanism. Why it matters: The study aims to explain AATD pathogenesis, and the surface model is presented as a step toward understanding ER membrane effects. However, the oversimplified model may lead to overinterpretation of the results. Resolution test: Discuss the limitations of the surface model more explicitly and temper the claims about biological relevance. Alternatively, perform simulations with more realistic membrane models (e.g., lipid bilayers) to test whether the observed effects hold.)
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Technical soundness, Claim pointer: The authors claim that the aging algorithm accurately models inter-protein β-sheet transitions, but the text does not provide sufficient detail on the algorithm's parameters, validation, or sensitivity to choices. Evidence pointer: Section 2, Section 3.3, S1 File Sect II (location not provided). Concern: The aging algorithm is central to the maturation mechanism, but the visible text only briefly describes it as "dynamically evaluating high-density local fluctuations of protein domains." The specific criteria for β-sheet formation, the scaling of interaction strengths, and the time dependence are not described in the main text. The authors reference their previous work, but the adaptation to α1-antitrypsin (e.g., identifying relevant LARKS sequences) is not detailed. Why it matters: The algorithm's parameters could significantly affect the results, and without a clear description, the reproducibility of the study is compromised. Resolution test: Provide a detailed description of the algorithm parameters, including the criteria for β-sheet formation, the scaling factors, and the time constants. Show sensitivity analyses to demonstrate that the conclusions are robust to reasonable parameter variations.)
- **Minor Comments**

Required correction: Add a brief explanation of why condensate formation is relevant to AATD pathology, and define key terms (e.g., LLPS, condensate) for readers outside the immediate field.)

Required correction: Add a brief discussion of the model's known limitations and how they might affect the conclusions, particularly for the structured domains of α1-antitrypsin.)
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Scientific importance, Affected element: Discussion, Evidence pointer: Section 4 (location not provided). Issue: The discussion of therapeutic implications is vague, stating that findings "can contribute to the development of therapeutic strategies" without specifying how. Required correction: Provide more concrete suggestions for how the molecular insights could inform therapeutic approaches, such as targeting the C-terminus or inhibiting β-sheet formation.)
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Readability for nonspecialists, Affected element: Results, Evidence pointer: Section 3.2, Fig 2 (location not provided). Required correction: Add a brief explanation of the contact map visualization and highlight the key differences between the variants in the text.)
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (quantitative phase diagram comparison), R1-M2 (viscosity uncertainty quantification), R1-M4 (aging algorithm parameter transparency)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The application of a coarse-grained model with an aging algorithm to α1-antitrypsin is novel, and the focus on the C-terminus as a driver of phase separation is a fresh perspective. However, the general concept of condensate maturation via β-sheet formation is not new, and the originality lies mainly in the specific system and methodological adaptation. Scientific importance: The work addresses a clinically relevant disease and provides a mechanistic framework that could inform therapeutic development. However, the computational nature of the study and the simplified surface model limit the direct biological impact. Interdisciplinary readership: The study bridges computational biophysics, protein biochemistry, and clinical relevance, making it potentially interesting to a broad audience. However, the technical details may be challenging for nonspecialists. Technical soundness: The methods are appropriate for the questions asked, but the lack of quantitative details and uncertainty quantification in the visible text undermines confidence in the results. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written but assumes familiarity with phase separation concepts and coarse-grained modeling, which may limit accessibility.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a plausible and potentially important mechanistic model, but the current evidence base is insufficient to fully establish the claims. The authors must provide quantitative data with error estimates, clarify the aging algorithm parameters, and temper claims about biological relevance given the simplified surface model. If these issues are addressed, the work could make a valuable contribution to the field.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that Z-α1-antitrypsin has a greater propensity to undergo LLPS compared to M-α1-antitrypsin is not quantitatively supported in the visible text (no Tc values or error estimates).
- The claim that inter-protein β-sheet formation increases condensate viscosity from 0.336 to 0.850 mPa·s is presented without uncertainty quantification or convergence analysis.
- The claim that both attractive and repulsive surfaces enhance condensate formation is based on a highly simplified surface model and may not be generalizable to the ER membrane.
- The claim that the aging algorithm accurately models β-sheet transitions is not verifiable from the text alone, as the algorithm parameters and validation are not described in sufficient detail.
- The claim that the proposed mechanism explains the onset of AATD is speculative, as the study does not directly test in vivo relevance.
