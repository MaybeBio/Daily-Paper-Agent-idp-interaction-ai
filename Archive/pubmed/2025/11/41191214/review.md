- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript is a comprehensive review arguing that biomolecular condensates are dynamic, membraneless organelles formed via phase separation, which play central roles in gene regulation, stress responses, and disease pathogenesis. It claims that dysregulation of condensates is a unifying feature in neurodegeneration, cancer, and viral infection, and that condensates represent promising therapeutic targets. The review further asserts that emerging technologies, including CRISPR/Cas9 tagging, optogenetics, and AI-based prediction tools, enable real-time monitoring and manipulation of condensates, paving the way for condensate-based precision medicine.

No figures, tables, or experimental data are available for direct inspection.

The lack of figure legends and table data prevents independent verification of the described condensate properties, disease associations, and therapeutic outcomes. The review's reliance on secondary sources, without access to primary data, reduces confidence in the strength of some causal claims.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a broad and ambitious review that attempts to synthesize a large and rapidly evolving field. The manuscript is well-structured, moving logically from molecular mechanisms to biological functions, disease relevance, therapeutic strategies, and technological advances. The writing is generally clear and accessible to a nonspecialist. However, the review suffers from a lack of critical depth. It often presents a descriptive catalog of condensate types, functions, and disease associations without sufficiently interrogating the strength of the evidence or the controversies within the field. The therapeutic section, while timely, overstates the clinical readiness of condensate-directed approaches, and the inclusion of failed clinical trials (e.g., arimoclomol) without adequate contextualization weakens the overall argument. The technological section is informative but reads more as a list of tools than a critical evaluation of their capabilities and limitations. The review's main value lies in its comprehensive scope, but it does not yet provide the critical, mechanistic insight expected of a leading review.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers new to the field of biomolecular condensates, including graduate students and postdocs, would find this review a useful introductory overview. Scientists working in adjacent areas such as RNA biology, cell signaling, or neurobiology might use it to gain a broad perspective on how condensates relate to their own work. Clinicians or translational researchers interested in novel therapeutic targets in neurodegeneration or cancer might find the disease and therapeutic sections of interest. However, experts in the condensate field are unlikely to find new insights or critical synthesis, as the review largely reiterates established concepts without deep engagement with ongoing debates.
- **Major strengths** 1. Comprehensive scope covering molecular mechanisms, functions, diseases, and therapies. 2. Logical organization and clear writing style suitable for a broad audience. 3. Up-to-date inclusion of emerging technologies such as CRISPR/Cas9 imaging, optogenetics, and AI-based prediction tools. 4. Good use of specific examples (e.g., G3BP1 in stress granules, NEAT1 in paraspeckles, SMN in Cajal bodies) to illustrate general principles. 5. The final section on technological challenges and future directions identifies important open questions.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Scientific evidence / support for claims
    - **Claim pointer** The review repeatedly asserts that dysregulation of biomolecular condensates is a "central pathogenic mechanism" and a "unifying feature" across diverse diseases, and that targeting condensates is a "promising frontier" for therapy.
    - **Evidence pointer** Sections "Biomolecular condensates in disease", "Biomolecular condensates as therapeutic targets", "Conclusion and perspective". Location not provided.
    - **Concern** The review presents a strong causal narrative linking condensate dysregulation to disease pathogenesis and therapeutic response. However, the text largely relies on correlative observations and descriptive statements without critically evaluating the evidence for causality. For example, the claim that stress granules contribute to chemoresistance is supported by studies showing that disrupting stress granules sensitizes cells to drugs, but the review does not discuss potential off-target effects of the perturbations used or alternative explanations for the observed sensitization. Similarly, the assertion that condensate modulation is a viable therapeutic strategy is presented as a near-certainty, despite the acknowledged failure of DNL343 in Phase 2/3 trials. The review does not adequately address the significant gap between preclinical promise and clinical reality, nor does it discuss the challenges of specifically targeting condensates without disrupting their normal functions.
    - **Why it matters** A review in a high-impact journal must provide a balanced and critical assessment of the evidence. Overstating the causal role of condensates in disease and the readiness of condensate-based therapies could mislead researchers and clinicians, potentially directing resources toward approaches with limited translational potential. The failure of DNL343, which the review itself notes, directly contradicts the optimistic tone and should be analyzed more deeply to understand what it means for the field.
    - **Resolution test** The authors should revise the disease and therapeutic sections to clearly distinguish between well-established causal mechanisms, strong correlations, and speculative associations. For each major disease area, they should explicitly state the strength of the evidence linking condensate dysfunction to pathology. The therapeutic section should include a critical discussion of the DNL343 failure, analyzing why the preclinical promise did not translate and what implications this has for other condensate-targeting strategies. The conclusion should be tempered to reflect the current state of evidence.

    - **Severity** Major

    - **Axis** Technical soundness / methodological rigor
    - **Claim pointer** The review states that "CRISPR/Cas9-mediated endogenous fluorescent protein knock-in systems" enable "real-time monitoring of condensate dynamics" and that these systems "faithfully recapitulated endogenous GBS3P1 behavior."
    - **Evidence pointer** Section "Technological approaches for monitoring biomolecular condensates", subsection "CRISPR/Cas-based system". Location not provided.
    - **Concern** The review presents CRISPR/Cas9 tagging and optogenetic tools as relatively straightforward and reliable methods. However, the text itself acknowledges that "protein tags can, in some cases, perturb condensation" and that "data from tagged constructs should be interpreted cautiously." This is a significant caveat that is not adequately integrated into the overall positive framing of these technologies. The review does not discuss the specific challenges of these methods, such as the potential for tags to alter protein dynamics, the difficulty of achieving endogenous expression levels, or the artifacts that can arise from overexpression in optogenetic systems. The claim that these tools "faithfully recapitulate" endogenous behavior is too strong given the acknowledged limitations.
    - **Why it matters** The technological section is intended to inform readers about the state-of-the-art tools available for studying condensates. If the limitations of these tools are not clearly and prominently presented, readers may overinterpret data generated with them, leading to flawed conclusions. A critical evaluation of the strengths and weaknesses of each method is essential for a review that aims to guide future research.
    - **Resolution test** The authors should expand the discussion of the limitations of CRISPR/Cas9 tagging and optogenetic methods. They should provide specific examples of how tags or overexpression can alter condensate properties and discuss strategies to mitigate these issues. The claim that these tools "faithfully recapitulate" endogenous behavior should be softened to acknowledge the potential for artifacts and the need for validation with orthogonal methods.
