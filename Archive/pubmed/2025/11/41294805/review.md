## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript provides a broad, readable overview of low-complexity domains, covering sequence composition, structural properties, biological functions, disease relevance, and methodological approaches. The topic is timely and of interest to a wide audience in cell biology and protein science. However, the review is largely descriptive and lacks critical depth. Several sections contain redundancy, and some claims are presented without sufficient supporting detail or critical evaluation. The absence of figures, tables, and the reference list in the provided material prevents full verification of the evidence base. The manuscript would benefit from a more focused thesis, deeper mechanistic discussion, and clearer differentiation between established findings and open questions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying protein phase separation, intrinsically disordered proteins, and biomolecular condensates would find this review useful as an entry point. Cell biologists interested in transcriptional regulation, nuclear transport, and cytoskeletal organization may also benefit. Clinicians and translational researchers working on neurodegenerative diseases and cancer could find the disease sections relevant. The methodological summary may serve as a practical reference for newcomers to the field.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript covers a broad range of topics relevant to LCD biology, from basic structural features to disease implications. The organization is logical, progressing from structure to function to pathology to methods. The inclusion of diverse methodological approaches, from biophysical to computational, is valuable. The text is generally accessible to nonspecialists, with clear explanations of key concepts such as LLPS.
- **Major Concerns** The review lacks critical synthesis; it often lists findings rather than evaluating evidence quality or comparing conflicting results. The distinction between well-established mechanisms and speculative or emerging ideas is not always clear. Some sections repeat content (e.g., vimentin IF structure is discussed in both Section 2.3 and Section 3.5). The disease section, while broad, does not deeply explore mechanistic links between LCD dysfunction and specific pathologies. The methods section is descriptive but does not critically assess the limitations and complementary strengths of different techniques in a comparative manner.
- **Minor Comments** Several claims are made without specific citations visible in the text (e.g., the definition of LCDs as regions of at least 20 amino acids with 40% single amino acid composition). The classification of LCDs into over 400 types is mentioned but not explained in detail. The discussion of therapeutic targeting is brief and could benefit from more concrete examples of ongoing clinical or preclinical efforts. The future directions section is somewhat generic and could be more specific about testable hypotheses or technological needs.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The manuscript does not provide a clear framework for evaluating the strength of evidence linking LCD phase separation to disease causality. The review would be strengthened by a critical discussion of the challenges in distinguishing physiological from pathological condensates, and by acknowledging controversies in the field (e.g., whether LLPS is the primary driver of aggregation in all cases). The absence of a comparative analysis of different LCD prediction tools and their limitations is a notable gap.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  *Originality*: The topic is not new, and the review does not offer a novel conceptual framework or unique synthesis. It covers ground that has been reviewed elsewhere.  
  *Scientific importance*: LCDs are increasingly recognized as important, and a comprehensive review has value, but this manuscript does not advance the field conceptually.  
  *Interdisciplinary readership*: The topic bridges cell biology, biophysics, and clinical research, and the text is accessible across these disciplines.  
  *Technical soundness*: The technical content appears generally accurate, but the lack of figures, tables, and references prevents full verification. Some statements are overly general.  
  *Readability for nonspecialists*: The text is clearly written and explains concepts well, making it suitable for a broad audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The manuscript is a competent but largely descriptive review. It does not provide sufficient critical analysis or novel synthesis to meet the standards of a high-impact venue. Major revisions would be needed to sharpen the thesis, deepen mechanistic discussion, and provide a more evaluative framework. The absence of key materials (figures, tables, references) further limits the ability to assess the manuscript fully.
