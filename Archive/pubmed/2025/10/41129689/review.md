### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that antibody-induced phase separation is specific and orthogonal for four different antigen-conjugated nanostars (biotin, DIG, DNP, MUC1), each with their corresponding antibody.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that specificity and orthogonality were demonstrated, but no experimental data are shown. It is unclear whether cross-reactivity tests were performed (e.g., each nanostar with non-cognate antibodies), whether negative controls without antibody or with non-specific antibodies were included, and how specificity was quantified.  
- **Why it matters** Orthogonality is a central claim that underpins the programmability and versatility of the system. Without evidence of cross-reactivity controls and quantitative specificity metrics, the claim cannot be verified.  
- **Resolution test** Provide data showing that each antigen-conjugated nanostar phase-separates only in the presence of its cognate antibody and not with mismatched antibodies, including quantitative measures (e.g., turbidity, microscopy counts) and appropriate negative controls.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that antibody-DNA hybrid condensates are reversible and can be cyclically formed and dissolved by cyclic degradation and addition of the specific antibody.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract mentions reversibility and cyclability but provides no details on the number of cycles tested, the efficiency of dissolution and reformation, or whether degradation of the antibody is complete and specific. No data on kinetics or yield per cycle are presented.  
- **Why it matters** Reversibility is a key functional property that distinguishes this system from static aggregates and is essential for applications in sensing and drug delivery. Without quantitative cycling data, the claim is unsupported.  
- **Resolution test** Show time-resolved data for multiple formation-dissolution cycles, including measurements of condensate size, number, or turbidity, and demonstrate that the system returns to its initial state after each cycle.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the experimental results are qualitatively and in some cases quantitatively reproduced by a theory and simulation approach using a phase-field model.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not specify which experimental observables were compared with simulations, the parameter values used, the fitting procedure, or the criteria for qualitative versus quantitative agreement. No error bars or statistical measures are mentioned.  
- **Why it matters** The claim of quantitative agreement is strong and requires rigorous validation. Without details on model parameters, boundary conditions, and comparison metrics, the reliability of the simulation results cannot be assessed.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a detailed comparison of experimental and simulated phase diagrams or condensate properties, including parameter values, sensitivity analysis, and statistical measures of agreement (e.g., R-squared, residuals).

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that phase separation starts at low nanomolar levels of the antibody and is concentration-dependent for a fixed nanostar concentration of 300 nM.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract reports a threshold antibody concentration in the low nanomolar range but does not provide the exact value, the range tested, or the method used to determine the onset of phase separation. No dose-response curve or statistical analysis is shown.  
- **Why it matters** The concentration dependence is a fundamental characterization of the phase behavior and is necessary to support the proposed mechanism. Without quantitative titration data, the claim is incomplete.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a full titration curve showing condensate formation as a function of antibody concentration, including the threshold value, error bars, and the method of detection (e.g., turbidity, confocal microscopy, or dynamic light scattering).
