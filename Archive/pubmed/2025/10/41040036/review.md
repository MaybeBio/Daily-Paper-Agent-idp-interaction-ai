## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and methods sections) as provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific and technical evaluation of the proposed framework, its demonstration on FAT10, and the validity of the claims made. No assessment of literature completeness beyond what is cited in the text.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose a modular, graph-based clustering framework that uses residue closeness centrality fingerprints from MD simulations as input for dimensionality reduction (PCA, EncoderMap, UMAP) and density-based clustering (HDBSCAN) to identify and characterize residue interaction states in conformationally heterogeneous proteins. The framework is demonstrated on a 30 μs MD ensemble of FAT10, revealing state-specific domain interfaces and local conformational changes.
- **Visible evidence base** Figures referenced (Figures 1–5, S1–S4), tables referenced (Tables 1–2), and methodological descriptions in the Methods section. No raw data, code, or supplementary files were provided for independent verification.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** The manuscript text was provided without figures, tables, or supplementary information. This limits the ability to assess the quality of the visualizations, the quantitative results, and the specific parameter choices. The MD simulation data and analysis code are not available for reproducibility checks.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a conceptually interesting and timely framework that addresses a real gap in the application of network methods to heterogeneous MD ensembles. The use of closeness centrality as a compact, interpretable feature for state identification is sensible and well-motivated. The demonstration on FAT10, a challenging multi-domain protein with intrinsic disorder, is appropriate. However, the current manuscript has several technical and presentational weaknesses that need to be addressed before the framework's utility and the validity of the biological insights can be fully assessed. The lack of quantitative validation of the clustering results, the limited comparison to existing methods, and the absence of a clear statistical framework for assessing cluster robustness are the most significant concerns.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biophysicists and chemists working on MD simulations of flexible proteins, intrinsically disordered proteins, and multi-domain systems. Researchers developing and applying network-based methods for protein structure-function analysis. Method developers in the field of unsupervised learning for molecular simulations.
- **Major strengths**
  - The proposed framework is modular and uses a physically interpretable feature (closeness centrality) that captures global network topology in a compact form.
  - The workflow is well-structured and the authors demonstrate its application at multiple representation levels (embedding, fingerprints, consensus graphs, structures), which is a valuable feature.
  - The choice of FAT10 as a test case is appropriate, given its conformational heterogeneity and biological relevance.
  - The discussion of the limitations of aggregated network descriptions and the potential for state-specific artifacts is well-articulated.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Blocking: Yes): The manuscript lacks a quantitative validation of the clustering results. The authors do not provide any metrics (e.g., silhouette score, Davies-Bouldin index, or comparison to known structural states) to assess the quality or robustness of the clusters identified by HDBSCAN. Without such validation, it is unclear whether the identified "states" are statistically meaningful or an artifact of the chosen algorithms and parameters.
  - **R1-M2** (Blocking: Yes): The authors do not compare their framework to existing methods for state identification from MD simulations (e.g., time-structure based independent component analysis (tICA) + k-means, or Markov state model construction). A direct comparison would be necessary to demonstrate the added value of the network-based featurization over more standard approaches.
  - **R1-M3** (Blocking: No): The choice of the closeness centrality as the sole node feature is not critically examined. The authors mention that other centralities or graph representation learning could be used, but they do not provide any evidence that closeness centrality is optimal or even sufficient for capturing the relevant conformational differences. A sensitivity analysis or comparison to other features would strengthen the claim of generalizability.
  - **R1-M4** (Blocking: No): The manuscript does not provide a clear statistical framework for assessing the significance of differences between clusters (e.g., in consensus graphs or mean fingerprints). The biological interpretations, such as the role of the Trp17-Lys152 interaction, are presented as qualitative observations without statistical support.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Axis: Clarity): The description of the "difference graph" in the UMAP section is brief. It would be helpful to explicitly state how the subtraction of consensus graphs is performed and how to interpret the resulting edge weights.
  - **R1-m2** (Axis: Reproducibility): The manuscript states that parameters for EncoderMap and HDBSCAN are in Tables 1 and 2, but these tables were not provided. Please ensure all parameters are fully described in the text or tables.
  - **R1-m3** (Axis: Presentation): The use of "states" to describe clusters is potentially misleading, as it implies a kinetic or thermodynamic definition. Consider using "clusters" or "network states" to avoid confusion with Markov states.
  - **R1-m4** (Axis: Technical Soundness): The authors state that the backbone is included in the contact criterion to ensure graph connectivity. This is a reasonable choice, but it may introduce a bias towards sequential neighbors. A brief discussion of the potential impact of this choice on the centrality values would be useful.
  - **R1-m5** (Axis: Readability): The introduction could be slightly streamlined to more directly state the gap in the current methodology and the specific contribution of this work.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
  - Lack of quantitative cluster validation (R1-M1).
  - Lack of comparison to existing state-identification methods (R1-M2).
  - Insufficient statistical support for biological interpretations (R1-M4).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality**: The framework is a novel combination of existing techniques (centrality measures, dimensionality reduction, clustering) applied to a specific problem. The originality is moderate; the core idea of using network features for state identification is not entirely new, but the specific implementation and the focus on interpretability are valuable.
  - **Scientific importance**: The problem of analyzing heterogeneous MD ensembles is of high importance. The framework has the potential to be useful to a broad community, but its impact will depend on demonstrating clear advantages over existing methods.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The work is primarily of interest to computational chemists and biophysicists. The use of network theory and machine learning may attract readers from those fields, but the biological insights are specific to FAT10 and may not have broad appeal.
  - **Technical soundness**: The methodological approach is sound in principle, but the lack of validation and comparison limits the current assessment of its technical robustness.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The manuscript is generally well-written and the concepts are explained clearly. However, the Methods section is dense and assumes familiarity with the specific algorithms and packages used.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The framework is promising and the demonstration on FAT10 is interesting, but the manuscript requires significant revisions to provide quantitative validation, comparative analysis, and statistical support for the claims before it can be considered for publication.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the framework "provides state-specific insight" is not fully supported without quantitative validation of the clusters.
- The claim that the Trp17-Lys152 interaction is a "unique" feature of the red state is not statistically tested.
- The claim that the framework is "easily adapted" is plausible but not demonstrated beyond the three dimensionality reduction methods shown.
- The biological significance of the identified states for FAT10 function is speculative and not experimentally validated.
