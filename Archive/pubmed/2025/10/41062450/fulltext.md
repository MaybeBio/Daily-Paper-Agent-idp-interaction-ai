# PhaSepDB 3.0: a comprehensive knowledgebase of phase separation-related proteins from AI-assisted curation


## Abstract
  Phase separation (PS) is a fundamental principle driving the formation of membraneless organelles (MLOs), which are critical for various cellular functions and pathological conditions. We present PhaSepDB 3.0 (https://db.phasep.pro/), a significantly updated knowledgebase of proteins related to PS. To address the challenges of curating a vast body of literature, we have implemented a novel human-AI collaborative workflow that integrates a large language model (LLM)-based agentic system with expert verification, enabling a major expansion and enrichment of the database. PhaSepDB 3.0 now contains 3,484 expert-curated entries for 1849 PS-related proteins, more than doubling the content of the previous version. The annotation framework has been restructured to capture deeper insights, including functional relevance, experimental evidence, and the intrinsic and extrinsic regulations of PS. A key new feature is the protein-wise summary page, which synthesizes data from multiple publications to provide a comprehensive overview of each protein’s PS behaviour and functional relevance. With redesigned, user-friendly web interfaces, PhaSepDB 3.0 serves as a critical resource for the community, supporting researchers to explore the intricate basis of PS and its biological implications in greater detail.


## Introduction
  The phase separation (PS) of proteins and nucleic acids has emerged as a key organizing principle underlying the formation of membraneless organelles (MLOs) or biomolecular condensates [1], supporting the spatiotemporal regulation of cellular processes such as transcription [2], genome organization ([3, 4]) and signaling [5]. Aberrant PS of proteins has been increasingly linked to various diseases, including cancer [6] and developmental disorders [7]. The rapid expansion of the field has been accompanied by the emergence of valuable databases, including our previous versions of PhaSepDB [8] and other key resources [9–12]. These fundamental platforms were instrumental in cataloging PS-related proteins and providing essential data for computational prediction tools [13–15].

  However, the focus of research in this field has significantly evolved. The emphasis has shifted from merely identifying proteins that undergo PS to understanding their specific biological functions, investigating their connections to disease, and defining the precise molecular basis that governs their assembly and regulation. Moreover, the therapeutic relevance of PS is a rapidly expanding frontier, as researchers now explore how condensates impact drug efficacy and how PS itself can be directly targeted for new therapies [16–18]. This shift in research focus presents two major challenges for database curation: the exponentially increasing volume of literature makes traditional manual curation by experts unsustainable, and the increasing depth of the research demands a more sophisticated annotation framework to capture these complex biological and therapeutic insights. Therefore, a scalable and in-depth curation solution is needed to keep pace with the field's trajectory.

  Recent advancements in generative artificial intelligence (AI), particularly large language models (LLMs) and agentic systems, have shown remarkable potential for transforming scientific research and literature mining [19]. These technologies offer a powerful solution to speed up the extraction and structuring of complex information from scientific texts, a capability that has been successfully explored in fields like materials science [20] and genomics [21]. Inspired by these advances, we recognized that an AI-assisted workflow offered transformative solutions to the challenges of literature volume and data depth.

  Here, we present PhaSepDB 3.0, a comprehensive knowledgebase developed through a novel AI-assisted curation workflow. By developing an LLM-based agentic system combined with expert verification, we have massively scaled our annotation efforts, more than doubling the database's content. We organized annotations for each entry to reveal the protein's PS properties, intrinsic determinants, PS partners, extrinsic regulation and functional relevance. Furthermore, PhaSepDB 3.0 introduces protein-wise summary pages that synthesize findings from multiple publications to offer a rapid and comprehensive overview. Coupled with redesigned modern website for enhanced user experience, PhaSepDB 3.0 represents a major update that provides the research community with accessible, comprehensive and contextualized knowledge on PS.


## Material and methods

### Literature collection
  We performed a systematic literature search in PubMed using the same keywords as in previous versions: ‘(phase transition[Title/Abstract] OR phase separation[Title/Abstract] OR membraneless organelles[Title/Abstract] OR biomolecular condensates[Title/Abstract]) AND protein AND cell’. This search covered publications from April 1, 2022, to April 20, 2025, yielding 2,386 candidate publications. The primary criterion for inclusion was that a publication must contain experimental validation of PS association for at least one protein. Publications in PhaSepDB 2.1 were also included for consistent annotations. The high-throughput MLO entries are extracted from relevant literature and mapped to UniProt accession. All collected data in PhaSepDB were at least double checked by different expert curators to ensure accurate annotations.


### The AI-assisted expert curation workflow
  We developed a human-AI collaborative pipeline composed of an LLM-based agentic system and a two-round human verification process.


#### LLM-based Agentic System
  We designed a system with three specialized agents to automate data extraction and structuring. (i) Data Extraction Agent: For each full-text publication, this agent identifies all proteins with experimental PS evidence and extracts relevant information into a structured markdown format. (ii) Data Formatting Agent: This agent receives the output from the Extraction Agent and processes the information into a formatted JSON object. (iii) Protein-wise Summary Agent: After expert verification, this agent synthesizes all approved annotations for a given protein (identified by its UniProt accession ID) from multiple publications to generate a consolidated summary. The inputs for this agent include both cases where a protein is curated as an independent entry (i.e. directly shown to undergo PS), and where it appears as a partner within another protein's entry. For system development and validation, data from PhaSepDB 2.1 served as a labeled dataset. We randomly selected 50 publications as a validation set for prompt engineering and designated the remaining 382 publications as a test set for final performance evaluation. We employed the DeepSeek-V2 LLM via its official API (https://api-docs.deepseek.com/) and utilized CrewAI framework (https://docs.crewai.com/) for agentic interactions.


#### Human Verification
  All AI-generated data undergoes a two-round verification process by expert curators. Each extracted entry is independently reviewed by two different curators to ensure accuracy, completeness, and fidelity to the source publication. To facilitate this, we developed a custom web-based curation tool that displays the entry annotation and the AI-generated summary side-by-side, allowing for efficient comparison, editing, and approval (Supplementary Fig. S1). All revisions are logged in the database for quality control and traceability. The consistency between the agentic system and expert curators was defined as a metric for performance assessment.


### PS entry classification and annotation
  We have retained the PS entry as the fundamental unit of the database. A Protein refers to a unique biological entity (defined by species and gene), and its summary page integrates all related entries. In contrast, an Entry is a record from a single publication, capturing the specific experimental annotations of a protein as reported in that study, and each entry was mapped to a UniProt accession ID [22]. Consistent with previous versions, PS entries are classified into two categories: PS-self and PS-other. Proteins that have been experimentally demonstrated to undergo PS in vitro without the need for partners are categorized as PS-self, while others that require partners (e.g. other proteins, RNA) to PS in vitro or have only been shown to form condensates in vivo. The ‘PS-other’ entry does not rule out the possibility that the protein can undergo PS by itself; it only reflects the evidence presented in that specific entry. ## Results

### Performance of the AI-assisted curation workflow
  To exploit the capabilities of LLMs to facilitate the curation process, we designed an AI-assisted curation workflow that integrates an agentic system with expert verification (Fig. 1A). To ensure comprehensive capture, the full text of each publication serves as the input for the Data Extraction Agent, which generates a thorough summary of all experimentally validated PS-related proteins and their PS properties. Subsequently, the Data Formatting Agent processes the summary and converts it into a structured format that aligns with the predefined data fields and schema, making it ready for database import. Finally, the Protein-wise Summary Agent consolidates annotations from both direct entries and partner roles, providing an integrated summary for each protein, followed by manual human review to ensure accuracy and reliability. To validate this system, we first refined the agent prompts using the validation set (Supplementary Table). After prompt refinement, this agent workflow successfully extracted 91.5% of the entries in the validation set (Fig. 1B). Furthermore, the accuracy for key annotations—such as organism, subcellular location, MLOs, PS-class, and material state—exceeded an average of 70% (Fig. 1C). The system demonstrated comparable performance on the test set, confirming the generalizability of our approach.

  Every AI-generated entry then underwent a two-round verification by expert curators on a web-based curation tool (Supplementary Fig. S1). The experts approved over 80.0% of the entries, confirming the high consistency between AI output and expert assessment (Fig. 1D). In terms of efficiency, the automated extraction by agents required less than five minutes per publication, resulting in a significant increase in overall curation throughput. Collectively, these results demonstrate that our AI-assisted workflow is a reliable, efficient, and effective solution for curation.


### Database content summary and website
  Benefiting from our AI-assisted workflow, PhaSepDB 3.0 now contains 3484 curated entries for 1849 unique PS-related proteins, representing more than a twofold increase in both entries and distinct proteins compared to the previous version (Table 1). The database maintains a strong focus on human proteins, which constitute the majority of entries, followed by significant collections for key model organisms (Fig. 2A). Reflecting the current landscape of PS research, proteins that are frequently studied and central to the field are extensively annotated. This includes well-known scaffold proteins for stress granules like FUS and G3BP1, components of transcriptional condensates like MED1, and proteins implicated in neurodegenerative diseases such as TDP-43 (Fig. 2B). These proteins are associated with a diverse range of MLOs (Fig. 2C). To facilitate user understanding of these data, the About section of the website now provides a glossary of PS-related terminology (e.g. PS Class, Intrinsic Determinants and Regulations).

  To further empower researchers to investigate the mechanisms and functions of these proteins, we have enriched the annotations in two critical ways. First, each entry now includes details on the functional relevance of PS, linking to its biological significance or pathological role. We have documented the intrinsic determinants and regulations of PS, including key domains or regions, PTMs, mutations, repeat and oligomerization. The influence of extrinsic partners and regulations is also comprehensively cataloged, with 2,304 entries identifying protein partners and more than 1,000 entries noting the effects of DNA, RNA and chemicals. We have curated 2,231 entries that specify the material state (e.g. liquid, hydrogel, solid) and, where available, document the conditions that trigger material state transitions, which are often linked to pathological aggregation [23]. Second, to enhance transparency and synthesize the growing body of literature, we have implemented a dual approach to data presentation. To aid in the validation of individual findings, the database now provides granular details for 9,257 experimental evidence records, capturing the specific protein constructs, cell lines, and conditions (Fig. 2D). Complementing this detailed view, the new protein-wise summary pages consolidate data from all relevant publications for a given protein (Fig. 2E). This offers users a comprehensive, at-a-glance overview of a protein's PS behaviour, its regulators, and functional consequences, with all information traced back to its source to accelerate discovery and hypothesis generation.

  As an example, there are 26 entries about the PS properties of human alpha-synuclein, summarized in the protein-wise page (Fig. 2D and E). Alpha-synuclein facilitates synaptic vesicle clustering and mRNA metabolism regulation via P-body associations. Under pathological conditions, liquid-to-solid transitions form cytotoxic amyloid aggregates in Lewy bodies, driving neurodegeneration. The transition is accelerated by RNA G-quadruplexes, Ca2+/Mn2+ dysregulation, and disease mutations [24–26]. Therapeutic strategies targeting alpha-synuclein phase transitions reduce aggregation and improve neuronal viability ([27, 28]). This example illustrates how the database's integrated views empower researchers to dissect PS mechanisms and accelerate therapeutic discovery for PS-associated diseases.


## Discussion
  In this study, we demonstrate the application of an AI-assisted workflow to significantly expand and deepen the content of PhaSepDB. Our LLM-based agentic system, operating within a ‘human-in-the-loop’ framework, is proved highly effective, increasing curation throughput significantly while ensuring data accuracy through expert verification. This synergistic approach represents a scalable and sustainable solution for developing scientific knowledgebases in rapidly advancing fields ([20, 22, 29, 30]). The addition of automatically generated protein-wise summary pages further addresses the challenge of knowledge fragmentation, offering users an up-to-date overview.

  The primary limitation of PS database remains the heterogeneity of data reported in the literature. The lack of standardized experimental reporting in information such as material states and functional domain poses a significant challenge to data uniformity and hinders the development of robust predictive models. Inspired by our approach, a promising future direction would be to leverage AI’s capabilities to facilitate the creation of standardized benchmark datasets [19], which are essential for training and validating the next generation of algorithms that aim to predict important PS properties.

  In conclusion, by offering significantly more data and new features, PhaSepDB 3.0, as a comprehensive knowledgebase, serves as a valuable asset for scientists investigating the mechanisms, regulation, and pathological implications of PS, directly facilitating their research and accelerating discoveries.
