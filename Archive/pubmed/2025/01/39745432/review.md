## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a well-written, ambitious review that synthesizes a substantial body of work from the authors' laboratories and others to argue for a mechanosensing paradigm in αβTCR activation. The central thesis is coherent and the evidence marshalled is substantial. However, the review is heavily reliant on the authors' own published findings, and some claims extend beyond what the cited evidence can support. The translational section, while interesting, is speculative and would benefit from clearer framing as such. The manuscript would be strengthened by a more critical discussion of alternative models and unresolved questions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Immunologists studying T-cell activation and antigen recognition; biophysicists interested in mechanotransduction; structural biologists working on immune receptors; researchers in immuno-oncology and adoptive T-cell therapy. The review provides a comprehensive framework that could influence experimental design and therapeutic development.
- **Major strengths** 1) The review provides a coherent and compelling synthesis of biophysical, structural, and cellular data into a unified mechanosensing model. 2) The authors clearly explain sophisticated single-molecule techniques (OT, SMSC, SCAR) and their relevance, making the work accessible to a broad immunology audience. 3) The inclusion of the preTCR and γδTCR comparisons provides valuable evolutionary and functional context. 4) The translational implications section, while speculative, raises important considerations for CAR-T and TCR-based therapy design.
- **Major Concerns** See detailed items below.
- **Minor Comments** See detailed items below.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (overgeneralization of catch-bond model), R1-M2 (unsubstantiated energetic claims), R1-M3 (speculative translational claims presented as conclusions)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** **Originality**: High. The mechanosensing paradigm is presented as a transformative shift, and the synthesis of structural and biophysical data is novel. **Scientific importance**: High. - **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. ### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Overgeneralization of model
- **Claim summary** The authors state that "αβT cells... use mechanical force to distinguish between pathophysiologic cellular alterations and normal self-constituents" and that the mechanosensing paradigm provides a general explanation for TCR activation across all αβT cells.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Introduction; Section "Mechanosensing and Biological Roles of T Cells"
- **Concern** The review presents the mechanosensing model as a universal paradigm for αβTCR activation, but the evidence cited is derived from a limited number of TCR-pMHC systems (primarily N15, A6, and a few IAV-specific TCRs). The authors do not adequately address whether the catch-bond behavior and structural transitions are general properties of all αβTCRs or specific to certain TCR-pMHC combinations. The claim that this is a "paradigmatic shift" implies universality, which is not established.
- **Why it matters** If the model is not general, then the translational recommendations (e.g., selecting "digital" TCRs for therapy) would only apply to a subset of TCRs, and the review's central thesis would be overstated.
- **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) provide evidence or arguments that the mechanosensing properties are conserved across diverse TCRs, or (b) explicitly limit the scope of their claims to the systems studied and discuss how the model might be tested in other systems.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Unsubstantiated energetic claims
- **Claim summary** The authors state that "the free energy of several hundred transitions can be generated" during volleying and that this energy is sufficient to "agitate and energize protein–protein or protein–lipid interfaces, inducing enhanced fluctuations in local membrane curvature, mechanical disruption of TCRαβ-CD3 contacts and the lipids proximal to the αβTCR leading to reorganization of the TM region and unbinding of ITAM motifs from the inner leaflet of the membrane."
- **Evidence pointer** Section "Mechanosensing and Biological Roles of T Cells"; Figure 11
- **Concern** The quantitative claim about the free energy generated by volleying is not supported by any calculation or citation. The subsequent claim that this energy is sufficient to drive the described downstream effects (membrane curvature, TM reorganization, ITAM unbinding) is speculative and presented without mechanistic detail or experimental support. The authors do not provide a quantitative framework linking the mechanical energy to the proposed biochemical outcomes.
- **Why it matters** This is a central mechanistic claim of the review. If the energy argument is incorrect or overstated, the proposed signaling mechanism is weakened. The review should either provide a rigorous energetic analysis or clearly label this as a hypothesis.
- **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) provide a quantitative estimate of the energy involved in volleying and compare it to the energy required for the proposed downstream effects, or (b) reframe these statements as speculative hypotheses requiring direct testing.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Speculative translational claims
- **Claim summary** The authors state that "adoptive T cell transfer will require digital performance" and that "provision of high pMHC expression on APCs may yield unprotective analog responses" in vaccine design.
- **Evidence pointer** Section "Translational Implications"
- **Concern** These are strong prescriptive claims based on the mechanosensing model, but they are not supported by direct experimental evidence in patients or in vivo models. The authors extrapolate from in vitro single-molecule and cell-based assays to clinical recommendations without acknowledging the substantial gap. For example, the claim that "digital" TCRs are required for effective adoptive therapy is not tested in any clinical or even preclinical in vivo model.
- **Why it matters** If these claims are taken as evidence-based recommendations, they could mislead therapeutic development. The review should clearly distinguish between model-derived hypotheses and evidence-based conclusions.
- **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) provide evidence from in vivo or clinical studies supporting these claims, or (b) explicitly frame them as hypotheses that need to be tested in appropriate models.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Clarity of figure descriptions
- **Affected element** Figures 3, 4, 5, 10, 11
- **Evidence pointer** Figure legends and in-text references
- **Issue** The figures are described in the text but were not provided for review. Some descriptions are dense and difficult to follow without seeing the actual figure. For example, the description of Figure 3C,D (volleying) and Figure 11 (model) would benefit from clearer step-by-step explanations.
- **Required correction** Ensure figure legends are self-contained and provide sufficient detail for readers to understand the key points without needing to refer back to the text. Consider adding schematic diagrams that summarize the proposed model in a simplified form.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Terminology consistency
- **Affected element** Terms "digital" and "analog" TCRs
- **Evidence pointer** Sections "Mechanosensing and Biological Roles of T Cells" and "Translational Implications"
- **Issue** The terms "digital" and "analog" are introduced to describe TCR response types, but the definitions are somewhat implicit. The authors should provide a clear, explicit definition early in the text to avoid confusion.
- **Required correction** Add a brief definition of "digital" (all-or-nothing response at low ligand number) and "analog" (graded response proportional to ligand number) when these terms are first introduced.

- **Concern ID** R1-m3
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Discussion of alternative models
- **Affected element** Section "Introduction"; Section "Mechanosensing and Biological Roles of T Cells"
- **Evidence pointer** Location not provided
- **Issue** The review presents the mechanosensing model as the primary explanation for TCR activation but does not thoroughly discuss alternative models (e.g., kinetic proofreading, kinetic segregation). A more balanced discussion would strengthen the review by acknowledging competing hypotheses and explaining why the mechanosensing model is superior or complementary.
- **Required correction** Add a paragraph in the Introduction or Discussion that briefly summarizes alternative models and explicitly states how the mechanosensing model addresses their limitations.

- **Concern ID** R1-m4
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Citation of conflicting evidence
- **Affected element** Section "Mechanosensing and Biological Roles of T Cells"
- **Evidence pointer** Location not provided
- **Issue** The review cites evidence supporting the mechanosensing model but does not discuss studies that may not support it or that show conflicting results. For example, some studies have questioned the role of catch bonds in TCR activation.
- **Required correction** Include a brief discussion of any conflicting or contradictory evidence and explain how the authors reconcile these with their model.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that volleying generates sufficient free energy to drive downstream signaling events (Section "Mechanosensing and Biological Roles of T Cells") is not quantitatively supported.
- The claim that "adoptive T cell transfer will require digital performance" (Section "Translational Implications") is presented as a conclusion without direct clinical or in vivo evidence.
- The claim that "provision of high pMHC expression on APCs may yield unprotective analog responses" (Section "Translational Implications") is speculative and not directly tested.
- The generalizability of the catch-bond model to all αβTCRs is asserted but not established from the cited evidence.
- The statement that "the same extended conformation can be replicated by isolating the full receptor in digitonin or digitonin-like micelles" (Section "Biophysical and Structural Features") implies equivalence between micelle and native membrane environments that is not fully justified.
