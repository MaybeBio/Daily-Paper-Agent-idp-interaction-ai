### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Mechanistic claim
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that complex coacervates "lower the nucleation barrier" and promote peptide assembly.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The claim of nucleation barrier lowering is presented without any direct or indirect evidence, such as kinetic measurements, free energy calculations, or comparison with control systems lacking coacervates.
- **Why it matters** The catalytic mechanism hinges on this barrier reduction. Without evidence, the distinction between catalysis and simple templating or concentration effects is unclear.
- **Resolution test** Provide kinetic data (e.g., lag time, growth rate) for assembly with and without coacervates, and show that the rate enhancement is not due to increased local peptide concentration alone. Include a quantitative analysis of nucleation barriers if possible.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Claim of non-monotonic behavior
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that as binding affinity and SA dosage decrease, the system shifts from template-induced to catalytic assembly and then to slow spontaneous assembly, implying an optimal regime.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The non-monotonic dependence is stated but not supported by any data. The abstract does not specify how binding affinity was varied, what range of SA dosages was tested, or how assembly rate was quantified.
- **Why it matters** The central conclusion that "more is not better" depends on this trend. Without experimental points and error analysis, the claim is speculative.
- **Resolution test** Present a systematic titration of SA dosage and binding affinity (e.g., via peptide mutations or salt conditions) with measured assembly rates, including replicates and statistical significance.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Definition of catalytic versus template-induced regimes
- **Claim pointer** The abstract distinguishes "template-induced assembly" from "catalytic assembly" but does not define the operational criteria for each.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The terms are used without clear definitions. It is unclear whether the distinction is based on stoichiometry, turnover, or kinetic signatures. This ambiguity undermines the interpretability of the claimed regime shift.
- **Why it matters** A catalytic process implies that the coacervate is not consumed and can mediate multiple assembly events. Without turnover evidence, the "catalytic" label may be misleading.
- **Resolution test** Define the criteria for catalytic behavior (e.g., coacervate recovery, multiple rounds of assembly) and provide experimental evidence that the coacervate retains activity after assembly.
