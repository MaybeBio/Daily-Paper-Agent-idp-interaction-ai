## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Conceptual claims and proposed therapeutic strategy as presented in the abstract
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors argue that intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) constitute a large fraction of the human proteome, are implicated in several severe diseases, and are difficult to drug because of their conformational plasticity. They propose that short synthetic peptides mimicking the ligand-induced structured domains of IDPs, termed "adaptive peptides," can selectively antagonize harmful IDP effects by targeting their ligands rather than the IDPs themselves, thereby preserving physiological functions. They further assert that AI-based approaches such as AlphaFold are unsuitable for this challenge.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or experimental data provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, experimental data, peptide design details, in vitro or in vivo validation, specificity and toxicity data, and any comparative analysis with existing approaches

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents an intriguing conceptual framework for targeting IDP-associated pathologies through ligand-directed adaptive peptides. The central idea, that short peptides can recapitulate the conformational adaptability of IDP interaction domains, is creative and potentially significant. However, the abstract provides no experimental evidence, no mechanistic detail, and no comparison with existing strategies. The claim that AI methods are "clearly unsuitable" is stated without supporting analysis. As presented, the case is conceptually appealing but not established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in drug discovery, structural biology, and neurodegeneration would find the concept relevant. Those working on undruggable proteins, peptide therapeutics, and IDP biology may be particularly interested. The proposed paradigm shift from targeting the IDP to targeting its ligand could appeal to medicinal chemists and translational scientists seeking new avenues for challenging targets.
- **Major strengths** The conceptual framing is clear and addresses a genuine unmet need in drug development. The idea of using conformational mimicry in short peptides is mechanistically plausible and distinguishes this approach from conventional docking-based design. The emphasis on preserving physiological functions of IDPs is an important consideration often overlooked.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that adaptive peptides can selectively antagonize harmful effects of IDPs without affecting their physiological properties.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** No experimental data, case studies, or mechanistic demonstrations are presented to support the central therapeutic claim. The abstract states the strategy "is promised to open a new route" but provides no proof of concept.  
- **Why it matters** The core value of the proposed paradigm rests on its feasibility and selectivity. Without evidence that adaptive peptides can indeed discriminate between harmful and physiological IDP interactions, the claim remains speculative.  
- **Resolution test** Provide experimental data showing that an adaptive peptide selectively blocks a pathogenic IDP interaction while preserving a physiological function in a relevant model system.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Claim support  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract asserts that AI solutions such as AlphaFold are "clearly unsuitable" for targeting IDPs.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** This statement is made without any comparative analysis or citation of specific limitations. While IDP conformational dynamics pose challenges for structure prediction, recent advances in AI-based methods may partially address these issues. The blanket dismissal is not justified.  
- **Why it matters** The claim is used to position the proposed approach as superior, but without evidence, it may mislead readers about the current state of the field.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a reasoned comparison or data showing that AlphaFold or similar tools fail specifically for the IDP domains targeted by the adaptive peptides.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic clarity  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that adaptive peptides are derived from domains of IDPs that become structured after ligand interaction and that they target selected ligands rather than the IDPs themselves.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The mechanism by which a peptide mimicking a structured domain of an IDP would selectively bind a ligand and antagonize the IDP effect is not explained. It is unclear how the peptide would avoid competing with the IDP for the ligand in a way that disrupts physiological functions.  
- **Why it matters** Without a clear mechanistic rationale, the feasibility of the approach cannot be evaluated, and the risk of unintended consequences remains undefined.  
- **Resolution test** Describe the proposed binding mode and selectivity mechanism, ideally with structural or biophysical data demonstrating that the adaptive peptide engages the ligand without interfering with essential IDP functions.
