This study systematically investigated the synergistic effects of l-glutamic acid, l-proline, and l-lysine on the self-assembly behavior of myofibrillar proteins (MPs) in aqueous systems when combined with non-enzymatic glycation reactions. Results revealed that glycation with amino acid supplementation effectively inhibited MP self-assembly, with l-glutamic acid and l-lysine showing the strongest impact, increasing MP water solubility to 50.83 % and 42.03 %, respectively, while ensuring stable dispersion (P < 0.05). Structural and micromorphological analyses showed that l-glutamic acid and l-lysine boosted electrostatic repulsion in myosin, significantly lowering alpha-helix content and particle size (P < 0.05), causing a loose, disordered protein structure. This facilitated dextran-myosin interactions, further inhibiting assembly via steric hindrance. Conversely, l-proline limited dextran's inhibitory effect by forming unique protein aggregates. Self-assembly simulations confirmed these findings. Overall, combining amino acid supplementation with glycation offers a promising approach for controlling MP assembly, with potential for innovative meat protein-based products.
