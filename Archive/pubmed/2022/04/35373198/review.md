## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (main text, methods, results, discussion, conclusions) without supplementary figures or tables
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodological soundness, and evidence quality as presented in the provided text; no assessment of supplementary material beyond what is referenced
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present a computational method, reweighted hierarchical chain growth (RHCG), to build atomistic ensembles of the intrinsically disordered protein tau K18 by assembling fragments from MD simulations, biased by NMR chemical shifts and corrected via Bayesian ensemble refinement. They claim the resulting ensembles capture both local and global structural properties validated against NMR, SAXS, FRET, and DLS data, and that pathogenic P301 mutations shift the conformational balance from microtubule-binding-competent turns toward aggregation-prone extended structures.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures referenced (Figures 1, 2, 3, 4, 5), equations (1-13), methods descriptions, and experimental protocols; supplementary figures and tables are referenced but not provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures (S1-S14), supplementary tables (S1, S2), supplementary text, and the full experimental details for FRET and SAXS measurements; these are essential for full evaluation of the statistical analyses and parameter choices

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a technically sophisticated and potentially valuable methodological advance for modeling intrinsically disordered proteins. The RHCG approach addresses a real and important problem, namely the poor overlap between initial and refined ensembles in Bayesian reweighting of long polymer chains. The validation against multiple experimental observables is commendable. However, the central conceptual claim, that global structure "emerges" from local structure, is not fully established by the evidence presented. The manuscript would benefit from clearer articulation of what is meant by "emergence" and from a more direct test of this claim, for example by comparing RHCG ensembles to predictions from models that explicitly include long-range interactions. The mutation analysis is interesting but the quantitative differences between WT and mutants are small and the biological interpretation may be overstated relative to the magnitude of the effects.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of intrinsically disordered proteins, computational biophysics, and integrative structural biology would be the primary audience. The methodological advance in RHCG will be of interest to those developing ensemble refinement methods. The tau-specific findings will interest the neurodegeneration community, particularly those studying the structural basis of tau aggregation and the effects of pathogenic mutations. The work also speaks to the broader question of how local sequence-encoded conformational preferences shape the global properties of disordered proteins.
- **Major strengths** The RHCG method is a genuine methodological contribution that addresses a known limitation of ensemble refinement for long polymers. The integration of multiple experimental datasets (NMR chemical shifts, J-couplings, RDCs, SAXS, FRET, DLS) provides a multi-angle validation strategy. The importance sampling framework is formally rigorous, with a clear connection to Bayesian inference. The finding that short fragments can recapitulate global structural properties is conceptually interesting and potentially impactful. The mutation analysis, while limited in magnitude, offers a plausible mechanistic link between local conformational shifts and disease-associated aggregation propensity.
- **Major Concerns** (1) The central claim that global structure "emerges" from local structure is not precisely defined or directly tested. (2) The validation against FRET data reveals a systematic discrepancy that is only partially addressed. (3) The mutation effects, while directionally consistent, are small and the statistical significance is not fully established. (4) The comparison to alternative models, such as those including explicit long-range electrostatic interactions, is missing. (5) The manuscript does not fully address the potential limitations of the fragment library approach, particularly regarding the transferability of local conformational preferences from short peptides to the full-length protein context.
- **Minor Comments** (1) The abstract claims "well-defined ensembles" but the definition of "well-defined" is not operationalized. (2) The description of the SAXS data analysis is brief and would benefit from more detail on the fitting procedure. (3) The manuscript would benefit from a more explicit discussion of the computational cost and scalability of RHCG. (4) The relationship between the P301 mutation effects observed here and previously published experimental data could be discussed more thoroughly. (5) The figures are referenced but not shown; the text descriptions are generally clear but some quantitative details are missing.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** (1) The FRET comparison shows a systematic offset that is corrected by reweighting, but the physical origin of this offset is not explained. (2) The statistical significance of the mutation-induced shifts in conformational populations is not quantified with error bars or confidence intervals. (3) The convergence of the RHCG sampling is not demonstrated; the manuscript states ensemble sizes but does not show convergence tests. (4) The sensitivity of the results to the choice of the confidence parameter θf is shown only in supplementary material, which is not available for review.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The RHCG method is original and addresses a recognized problem in a novel way. The application to tau is not entirely new, but the specific insights regarding local structure determining global properties are a fresh perspective. Scientific importance: The work has the potential to influence how IDP ensembles are constructed and validated, and the tau findings could inform understanding of disease mechanisms. However, the importance would be enhanced by a more definitive test of the emergence claim. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges computational biophysics, NMR spectroscopy, and neurodegeneration research, and should be accessible to a broad readership with appropriate background. Technical soundness: The methodology is generally sound, but the missing convergence tests and incomplete statistical analysis of mutation effects are concerns. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is well-written and the concepts are explained clearly, though the methods section assumes familiarity with ensemble refinement and Bayesian inference. The abstract and introduction are accessible to a broad audience.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that global structure "emerges" from local structure is not directly tested; the manuscript demonstrates correlation, not causation, between local conformational preferences and global chain properties.
- The statement that RHCG ensembles "capture" the global structure of tau K18 is supported for RG and RDCs, but the FRET comparison required reweighting to achieve agreement, suggesting the initial RHCG ensemble was not fully consistent with this experimental observable.
- The claim that P301 mutations "shift the balance" from functional to disease-associated conformations is based on small population shifts (approximately 2-fold reduction in turn fraction) that are not quantified with statistical uncertainty.
- The assertion that the RHCG ensemble is "well-defined" is not supported by convergence tests or an analysis of the uniqueness of the ensemble.
- The statement that "the global structure of tau K18 thus emerged from its local structure" is presented as a conclusion but is more accurately a hypothesis consistent with the data, not a proven mechanism.
