## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a computationally intensive study of a biologically important transmembrane protein, APP-C99, and proposes a novel mechanism whereby extramembrane domain secondary structure modulates transmembrane dimerization. The central claim, that transient extramembrane β-strands control TM helix dimerization, is potentially significant and would be of interest to the membrane protein and neurodegeneration communities. However, the evidence presented in the text alone is insufficient to fully evaluate the robustness of this claim. The reliance on a single simulation method (gREST), the lack of quantitative convergence data in the main text, and the absence of experimental validation for the dimerization mechanism leave key aspects of the case not fully established. The manuscript is well-written and the conceptual framework is clear, but the technical evidence base must be examined in detail before the conclusions can be accepted.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying membrane protein structure and dynamics, particularly those focused on single-pass transmembrane receptors and their dimerization mechanisms. The amyloid research community, specifically those investigating APP processing, Aβ production, and the role of the C99 fragment in Alzheimer's disease pathogenesis, would find the proposed mechanism relevant. Additionally, computational biophysicists developing and applying enhanced sampling methods to intrinsically disordered proteins and membrane systems would be interested in the methodological approach.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a challenging and important problem: the role of intrinsically disordered extramembrane domains in modulating TM helix interactions. The use of full-length protein in a realistic lipid bilayer environment is a strength over truncated or detergent-based systems. The authors connect their simulation results to prior NMR paramagnetic probe data, providing some experimental anchoring. The observation that glycine zipper hydrogen bonds are not dominant in the full-length context challenges a widely held assumption and is a potentially important conceptual contribution. The writing is clear and the logical flow from monomer to dimer to mechanistic implications is well-structured.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness / simulation convergence
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that their gREST simulations have converged and that the sampled ensembles represent the equilibrium distribution of C99 monomer and dimer conformations.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, "gREST simulations of C99 monomer were initiated from 16 conformational states identified in 30 Å hydrophobic thickness implicit bilayer REMD simulations"; "The convergence of these gREST simulations was evaluated using an ergodic measure-like parameter... to select a subset of sampled conformations to treat as the equilibrium ensemble (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 to S4)."
  - **Concern** The convergence assessment is only described qualitatively in the main text, with all quantitative details relegated to the SI. The text states that an "ergodic measure-like parameter" was used, but no values, thresholds, or statistical tests are provided. For a study whose conclusions depend entirely on the quality of the sampled ensembles, this is a critical gap. Furthermore, the dimer simulations were initiated from only two starting structures (Gly-in and Gly-side), which may not adequately sample the conformational space available to the dimer. The 200 ns equilibration for the extramembrane domains may be insufficient for these intrinsically disordered regions to reach equilibrium, given their expected slow relaxation times.
  - **Why it matters** If the simulations are not converged, all downstream analyses of secondary structure propensities, cluster populations, and dimer interface geometries are potentially biased by the initial conditions and insufficient sampling. The central claim of the paper, that extramembrane β-strands control dimerization, would be undermined if the observed β-strands are artifacts of incomplete sampling.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative convergence metrics in the main text or a clear summary in the SI that demonstrates: (1) the ergodic measure values for each replica and how they were used to select the equilibrium subset; (2) block-averaged or time-series analyses showing that secondary structure propensities and cluster populations are stable over the production period; (3) evidence that the dimer simulations, initiated from two distinct TM interfaces, converge to similar ensembles or that the differences are statistically characterized; (4) a demonstration that the extramembrane domain conformational distributions are independent of the starting conditions.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence for causal claim
  - **Claim pointer** The title and abstract state that "formation of extramembrane β-strands controls dimerization of transmembrane helices," implying a causal, directional relationship.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, "C99 Changes Upon Homodimerization" and "Intramembrane Homodimer Motifs Depend on Extramembrane Domain States"; Discussion section, "extramembrane domain structure influences TM domain conformational states."
  - **Concern** The manuscript demonstrates correlations between extramembrane domain secondary structure and TM dimer interface geometry, but the causal direction is not established. The authors show that certain extramembrane conformations are associated with certain TM interfaces, but they do not perform any perturbation analysis (e.g., mutating key extramembrane residues, restraining extramembrane secondary structure, or systematically varying the TM interface) to show that altering the extramembrane β-strands changes the dimerization outcome. The claim of "control" is therefore stronger than the evidence supports. It is equally plausible that the TM interface geometry dictates which extramembrane conformations are sterically accessible, or that both are determined by a common factor such as membrane thickness or lipid composition.
  - **Why it matters** The title and abstract make a strong mechanistic claim. If the relationship is merely correlative, the significance of the work is substantially reduced. The authors' own data, as described, cannot distinguish between "extramembrane β-strands control TM dimerization" and "TM dimerization stabilizes certain extramembrane conformations."
  - **Resolution test** Perform and report at least one of the following: (1) mutations in the extramembrane domain that disrupt or enhance β-strand propensity and show a corresponding change in TM dimer interface distribution; (2) simulations where extramembrane β-strands are artificially stabilized or destabilized (e.g., via restraints or D-amino acid substitutions) and show a resulting change in TM dimerization; (3) a free energy analysis that decomposes the contributions of extramembrane and TM domain interactions to the overall dimerization free energy, demonstrating that extramembrane interactions are the driving force.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Comparison with experimental data
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that their simulation results "match well with the likelihood of protein backbone hydrogen bonds with water" and that the simulations "independently recapitulate the same conclusions as NMR characterizations."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, "Accessibility of C99 to Hydrophobic and Hydrophilic Environments in NMR and MD"; Discussion section, "Finding our monomeric simulations to independently recapitulate the same conclusions as NMR characterizations."
  - **Concern** The comparison between simulation and NMR data is described only in qualitative terms. The text states that the simulations "match well" with the NMR-derived accessibility data, but no quantitative comparison (e.g., correlation coefficients, root-mean-square deviations between predicted and measured protection factors, or statistical tests) is provided. The NMR data referenced are from a previous publication (ref. 31) and were obtained in different lipid environments (SCOR vs. POPC), which the authors acknowledge but do not fully address. The claim of "recapitulating" NMR conclusions is therefore not quantitatively supported in the provided text.
  - **Why it matters** The experimental comparison is the primary external validation of the simulation methodology. Without a quantitative demonstration of agreement, the reader cannot assess whether the simulations are producing physically realistic ensembles. The differences in lipid environment between the NMR experiments and simulations further complicate the comparison and need to be explicitly addressed.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a quantitative comparison between simulation-predicted solvent accessibility (or hydrogen bond probabilities) and the NMR-derived 1−I/I0 values for each residue, including a correlation coefficient or similar statistic. Discuss how the differences in lipid environment (POPC vs. SCOR) might affect the comparison and whether the agreement is expected to hold across different membrane compositions.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** (1) Quantitative convergence evidence for the gREST simulations is not presented in the main text and cannot be evaluated from the supplied material (R1-M1). - **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**  
  *Originality*: The concept that disordered extramembrane domains can modulate TM helix dimerization is a novel and potentially important idea that challenges the prevailing focus on the TM domain itself. This is a genuine conceptual contribution.  
  *Scientific importance*: If the claims are substantiated, the work would have significant implications for understanding APP processing and potentially for other single-pass TM receptors. The importance is high, but contingent on the robustness of the evidence.  
  *Interdisciplinary readership*: The topic bridges membrane biophysics, computational chemistry, and Alzheimer's disease research. The findings would be of interest to a broad readership, but the presentation is heavily simulation-focused and may be challenging for experimentalists without a strong computational background.  
  *Technical soundness*: The use of gREST is appropriate for the problem, but the lack of quantitative convergence and validation data in the main text is a significant weakness. The technical execution cannot be fully assessed from the provided material.  
  *Readability for nonspecialists*: The manuscript is generally well-written and the conceptual narrative is accessible. However, the methods section assumes familiarity with enhanced sampling techniques and clustering algorithms, which may limit accessibility for a general scientific audience. The main text would benefit from a brief, plain-language explanation of the gREST method and why it is necessary for this problem.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The conceptual framework and potential significance are compelling, but the current evidence base, as presented, does not fully establish the causal claim. The authors must provide quantitative convergence data, a more rigorous demonstration of the causal relationship between extramembrane β-strands and TM dimerization, and a quantitative comparison with experimental data. If these are satisfactorily addressed, the manuscript would be a strong candidate for publication.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "formation of extramembrane β-strands controls dimerization of transmembrane helices" is not supported by the evidence presented, which demonstrates correlation but not causation.
- The claim that simulation results "match well" with NMR data is not quantitatively supported in the provided text.
- The claim that the C99 dimer is "stabilized by 15 conformational states" is presented without statistical confidence intervals or a demonstration that this number is robust to the clustering methodology.
- The statement that "extramembrane domains determine the relative rotation of the C99 TMDs" is an assertion of causality that is not directly tested.
- The claim that the observed β-strands "seem a likely explanation for the random coil secondary structure observed" is speculative and not directly tested.
- The generalizability of the findings to "TM protein complexes used to regulate cell signaling" is an extrapolation beyond the specific system studied.
