## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods, and data availability sections.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological soundness, validity of conclusions drawn from the presented simulation data, and clarity of presentation. Not assessed: editorial fit, formatting, or literature completeness beyond what is directly relevant to the claims.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present a full-length model of the TSH receptor (TSHR) generated by combining an AlphaFold2-derived structure of the extracellular domain (LRD-LR) with a previously published homology model of the transmembrane domain (TRIO). A 1000 ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulation in a lipid bilayer with water and ions was performed. The authors report that the leucine-rich domain (LRD) and transmembrane domain (TMD) remain stable, while the linker region (LR, residues 280-410) is highly flexible, forms transient secondary structure elements, and behaves as an intrinsically disordered protein (IDP). Preliminary simulations of the TSHR in complex with TSH show stabilization of LR secondary structure and multiple contacts between LR and TSH. The authors conclude that the LR is an IDP and that its flexibility is important for ligand and antibody binding.
- **Visible evidence base** Full text with figures referenced but not provided, tables 1-3 referenced but not provided, methods section, and data availability statement.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures 1-10, Tables 1-3, and supplementary materials are referenced but not included in the provided text. Simulation parameter files, force field details beyond those stated, and convergence metrics beyond those described are not available. The AlphaFold2 confidence scores for the LR region are mentioned but not shown.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a relevant gap in TSHR structural biology by attempting to model the full-length receptor, including the previously unresolved hinge region. The central claim that the LR behaves as an intrinsically disordered protein is plausible and consistent with prior indirect evidence, but the evidence presented here is limited by the absence of key figures and tables, the reliance on a single 1000 ns simulation, and the preliminary nature of the ligand-bound simulation. The work is technically sound in its use of established MD protocols, but the conclusions regarding the functional significance of LR disorder and TSH-induced stabilization require stronger validation, including replicate simulations, experimental corroboration, and more detailed analysis of the conformational ensemble.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying GPCR structure and dynamics, particularly glycoprotein hormone receptors (TSHR, FSHR, LHR); investigators focused on the molecular basis of autoimmune thyroid disease, especially Graves' disease, where TSHR antibodies are central; and computational biologists interested in the application of AlphaFold2 structures as starting points for MD simulations of large membrane proteins. The finding that the LR is an IDP may also be of interest to the broader intrinsically disordered protein community.
- **Major strengths** 1. Addresses a significant gap: no full-length TSHR structure has been experimentally determined, and the LR has remained structurally uncharacterized. 2. Combines AlphaFold2 predictions with a previously validated TMD model, a reasonable and timely approach. 3. The simulation environment (lipid bilayer, ions, physiological ionic strength) is appropriate for a membrane protein. 4. The observation of LR flexibility and transient secondary structure is consistent with the lack of experimental structure for this region. 5. The comparison with recent LHR cryo-EM structures adds context and strengthens the plausibility of the findings.
- **Major Concerns** 1. The absence of figures and tables prevents independent assessment of the primary data supporting the central claims. 2. A single 1000 ns simulation is insufficient to establish that the LR is an IDP, as conformational sampling may be incomplete. 3. The TSH-bound simulation is described as preliminary, yet it is used to draw conclusions about ligand-induced stabilization and functional significance. 4. The claim that the LR is an IDP relies on indirect indicators (RMSD, Rg, secondary structure dynamics) without quantitative comparison to established IDP benchmarks or experimental validation. 5. The functional implications of LR disorder for antibody binding and signaling are speculative and not directly tested.
- **Minor Comments** 1. The manuscript would benefit from a more detailed description of the AlphaFold2 model quality, including per-residue confidence scores for the LR. 2. The choice of the specific AlphaFold2 model (ranked by confidence or by other criteria) is not justified. 3. The methods for generating the TSH-TSHR complex are described briefly; more detail on the modeling of the TSH beta chain and the alignment procedure would improve reproducibility. 4. The statement that the LR moved away from the TMD in the TSH-bound simulation is interesting but not quantified; a distance or contact analysis over time would strengthen this observation. 5. The discussion of the cysteine bond analysis is somewhat speculative; the authors should clarify whether the simulation included disulfide bonds or only monitored distances between cysteine pairs.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1. Lack of replicate simulations or enhanced sampling methods to support the IDP conclusion. 2. No quantitative assessment of convergence beyond qualitative statements about hydrogen bond saturation. 3. The TSH-bound simulation is presented as preliminary but is used to support a key conclusion; this needs to be clearly labeled as hypothesis-generating. 4. The absence of figures and tables in the provided material makes it impossible to verify the reported RMSD, Rg, and secondary structure data.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The work is original in its attempt to model the full-length TSHR and characterize the LR dynamics, though the concept of LR flexibility has been suggested previously. Scientific importance: The TSHR is a major autoantigen, and understanding its conformational behavior has implications for autoimmune disease mechanisms; however, the current evidence is largely descriptive and preliminary. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges computational biology, structural biology, and endocrinology, but the presentation assumes familiarity with MD simulation terminology. Technical soundness: The methods are generally sound, but the lack of replicates and the preliminary nature of the ligand-bound simulation limit the robustness of the conclusions. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is reasonably clear but would benefit from a more accessible explanation of key concepts such as IDPs and MD simulation limitations.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The central hypothesis is interesting and plausible, but the evidence as presented is insufficient to firmly establish that the LR is an IDP or that TSH stabilizes it. Additional simulations, quantitative analyses, and experimental validation would be needed to strengthen the case.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the LR is an intrinsically disordered protein based on a single 1000 ns MD simulation showing significant flexibility and transient secondary structure elements.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, "Instability of the LR"; Figures 4-6 (not provided)
- **Concern** The conclusion that the LR is an IDP is based on a single simulation trajectory. Intrinsically disordered proteins are characterized by a lack of stable tertiary structure under physiological conditions, but a single 1000 ns simulation may not adequately sample the conformational space, especially for a large, flexible region. The authors do not provide replicate simulations, enhanced sampling, or comparison to known IDP benchmarks. The observed flexibility could be a sampling artifact or could reflect a region that is simply dynamic but not truly disordered.
- **Why it matters** The central claim of the manuscript rests on this conclusion. If the LR is not truly an IDP, the interpretation of its role in ligand and antibody binding changes substantially. Overinterpretation of a single trajectory could mislead the field.
- **Resolution test** Provide at least three independent simulations starting from different initial velocities (or structures) and show that the observed flexibility and secondary structure dynamics are reproducible. Alternatively, use enhanced sampling methods (e.g., replica exchange) or compare the conformational ensemble to experimentally validated IDP markers. Quantitative metrics such as the fraction of time spent in folded conformations or the radius of gyration distribution should be compared to known IDPs and folded proteins.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that TSH binding stabilizes the LR, reducing structural fluctuations and forming multiple contacts with the LR.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, "Simulation of the TSHR in complex with TSH"; Figures 6B and 10 (not provided)
- **Concern** The TSH-bound simulation is described as preliminary, yet it is used to support a key conclusion about ligand-induced stabilization. The methods for building the TSH-TSHR complex are described briefly, and the simulation appears to be a single run. The claim that TSH stabilizes the LR is based on qualitative comparison of DSSP plots and visual inspection of conformations. No quantitative analysis of LR flexibility (e.g., RMSF, Rg distributions) in the bound versus unbound state is provided.
- **Why it matters** The functional significance of the LR and its response to ligand binding is a central theme of the manuscript. If the stabilization effect is not robustly demonstrated, the conclusions about the LR's role in signaling are weakened.
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative comparison of LR dynamics between the apo and TSH-bound simulations, including RMSF, Rg distributions, and secondary structure occupancy over time. If possible, run multiple simulations of the complex to ensure reproducibility. Clearly label the TSH-bound results as preliminary if only one simulation is performed.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Data availability
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript references Figures 1-10 and Tables 1-3 to support the results, but these are not included in the provided material.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, all subsections; Figures 1-10; Tables 1-3
- **Concern** The primary data supporting the claims are not available for review. Without the figures and tables, it is impossible to verify the reported RMSD values, Rg fluctuations, secondary structure assignments, hydrogen bond histories, or the structural overlays shown in the figures. The tables reporting helix geometry changes are also missing.
- **Why it matters** Peer review requires access to the primary evidence. The absence of these materials prevents a full assessment of the technical correctness of the analyses and the validity of the conclusions.
- **Resolution test** Provide the figures and tables in the revised manuscript or as supplementary material. Ensure that all data referenced in the text are available to reviewers and readers.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Technical rigor
- **Claim pointer** The authors conclude that the relative orientation of the LRD with respect to the TMD is variable and that this variability is important for accommodating ligand and antibody binding.
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, "Instability of the LR" and "Receptor orientation"; Figure 8 (not provided)
- **Concern** The analysis of LRD orientation relative to the TMD is based on the angle between the first principal axis of the LRD and the Z axis. This is a single geometric descriptor that may not capture the full range of relative motions. The authors do not provide a detailed analysis of the conformational space sampled by the LRD, nor do they compare the observed variability to known GPCR dynamics. The claim that this variability is functionally important is speculative.
- **Why it matters** The manuscript suggests that LRD mobility is relevant for antibody access, but this is not directly tested. Overstating the functional implications of observed dynamics could mislead readers.
- **Resolution test** Provide a more detailed analysis of LRD-TMD relative motions, such as principal component analysis or a contact map analysis over time. If possible, compare the observed dynamics to those of other GPCRs. Clearly distinguish between observed dynamics and inferred functional significance.

### Minor Comments

- **Concern ID** R1-m1
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Clarity
- **Affected element** Methods section, "Formation of a full-length TSH model"
- **Evidence pointer** Materials and methods, "Formation of a full-length TSH model by combination of the Alphafold2 LRD and LR model with the TRIO TMD model"
- **Issue** The description of the model assembly is brief. The criteria for selecting the final orientation after the rotation scan are described as "having examined visually," which is subjective and not reproducible.
- **Required correction** Provide a more objective criterion for selecting the final orientation, such as minimizing the number of clashes or optimizing a scoring function. Alternatively, state that the choice was arbitrary and test the sensitivity of the results to this choice.

- **Concern ID** R1-m2
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Technical detail
- **Affected element** Methods section, "MD simulation"
- **Evidence pointer** Materials and methods, "MD simulation"
- **Issue** The manuscript states that the simulation used the Charmm36m force field and TIP3P water, but does not specify the temperature and pressure coupling methods, the barostat and thermostat algorithms, or the equilibration protocol details beyond the six-step procedure from Charmm-gui.
- **Required correction** Add details on the thermostat and barostat used, the target temperature and pressure, and the duration of each equilibration step. This is standard practice for MD simulation reports.

- **Concern ID** R1-m3
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Interpretation
- **Affected element** Discussion section, cysteine bond analysis
- **Evidence pointer** Results section, "Analysis of the cysteines and cysteine bonds"; Figure 9 (not provided)
- **Issue** The authors suggest that the proximity of C283-C398 and C284-C408 in the simulation indicates that these disulfide bonds should form, but the simulation did not include disulfide bonds. The absence of these bonds could affect the dynamics of the LR.
- **Required correction** Clarify that the simulation did not include disulfide bonds and discuss the potential impact of this omission on the observed LR dynamics. Consider running a simulation with the predicted disulfide bonds included to test their effect.

- **Concern ID** R1-m4
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Presentation
- **Affected element** Abstract and Discussion
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Discussion
- **Issue** The abstract states that the LR is an intrinsically disordered protein, but this is a strong claim that is not fully supported by the data presented. The discussion also uses this term without qualification.
- **Required correction** Soften the language in the abstract and discussion to reflect that the simulation suggests the LR is "disordered-like" or "highly flexible" rather than definitively classifying it as an IDP, given the limitations of a single simulation.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the LR is an intrinsically disordered protein is not fully supported by a single 1000 ns simulation without replicate runs or comparison to IDP benchmarks.
- The claim that TSH stabilizes the LR is based on a preliminary simulation and lacks quantitative comparison between bound and unbound states.
- The statement that the LR moved away from the TMD in the TSH-bound simulation is not quantified and is based on visual inspection.
- The suggestion that the proximity of cysteine pairs indicates disulfide bond formation is speculative, as the simulation did not include these bonds.
- The functional significance of LRD orientation variability for antibody binding is inferred but not directly tested.
