## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full manuscript, figures, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that amyloid-beta (Abeta) aggregation under diffusion-limited conditions produces fractal self-assemblies, confirmed by confocal microscopy and scanning electron microscopy with energy dispersive x-ray analysis. Molecular docking implicates electrostatic and hydrophobic patches on Abeta oligomer surfaces in mediating fractal assembly. Laser light scattering experiments on sonication-derived oligomers support this, and pH-dependent electrostatic modulation induces a morphological phase transition reflected in fractal dimension changes. The authors suggest applications in drug delivery and biosensing.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods details, or numerical data provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, experimental protocols, microscopy images, docking parameters, scattering data, fractal dimension calculations, statistical analyses, and any control experiment details

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents an interesting hypothesis linking surface patch interactions on Abeta oligomers to fractal self-assembly under diffusion-limited conditions. However, the evidence as summarized is insufficient to establish the central mechanistic claims. Key quantitative data, control details, and methodological specifics are absent, preventing a rigorous evaluation of the conclusions.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in protein aggregation, amyloid biophysics, and soft matter physics would find the fractal assembly concept relevant. Those studying diffusion-limited processes or surface-mediated self-assembly may also be interested. The proposed biomedical applications could attract a broader audience, though these are speculative at this stage.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a relatively underexplored condition (diffusion-limited aggregation) for amyloid-beta, which could offer new insights into aggregation pathways. The combination of imaging, docking, and light scattering provides a multi-method approach. The pH-dependent morphological transition is a potentially valuable observation linking solution conditions to fractal geometry.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that fractal self-assemblies are formed from Abeta rather than salt in the supporting media.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Confocal microscopy and SEM-EDX, location not provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract states that EDX confirmed the fractals are Abeta-derived, but no elemental composition data or comparison with salt controls is presented. Without quantitative evidence distinguishing protein from salt crystals, the claim is not verifiable.  
  - **Why it matters** Salt crystals can form fractal-like structures under drying conditions, and this is a common artifact. If the control is inadequate, the central observation could be misinterpreted.  
  - **Resolution test** Provide EDX spectra for both Abeta fractals and salt-only controls, with elemental maps showing carbon/nitrogen enrichment in the fractals and absence of salt-specific elements.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** Molecular docking implicates electrostatic and hydrophobic patches on the solvent-accessible surface area of Abeta oligomers in mediating fractal self-assembly.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Molecular docking experiments, location not provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract does not specify the docking method, the oligomer model used, or how the identified patches were validated experimentally. Docking predictions alone are not direct evidence of assembly mediation.  
  - **Why it matters** The mechanistic claim is central to the paper. Without experimental perturbation (e.g., mutation or chemical modification of the patches) or correlation with assembly outcomes, the docking result remains speculative.  
  - **Resolution test** Show that altering the identified patches (e.g., via site-directed mutagenesis or pH changes) measurably affects fractal formation or morphology, with quantitative comparisons.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** Laser light scattering experiments on sonication-derived oligomers show self-assembly into fractals when drop casted.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Laser light scattering experiments, location not provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract does not report scattering data, such as intensity profiles, size distributions, or fractal dimension calculations. Drop casting introduces drying artifacts that may not reflect solution-state assembly.  
  - **Why it matters** The claim that oligomers self-assemble into fractals requires evidence that assembly occurs in solution or is not a drying artifact. Without time-resolved or concentration-dependent data, the observation is ambiguous.  
  - **Resolution test** Provide scattering data showing fractal dimension evolution over time or with concentration, and include controls for drying effects (e.g., different drying rates or substrates).
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** pH variation modulates fractal morphologies, leading to a morphological phase transition observed through fractal dimension changes.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Variation in fractal dimension, location not provided  
  - **Concern** The abstract claims a phase transition but provides no quantitative fractal dimension values, pH range tested, or statistical significance. The term "phase transition" implies a sharp change, which is not demonstrated.  
  - **Why it matters** The claim of a morphological phase transition is a strong statement that requires clear evidence of a discontinuity or abrupt change in fractal dimension, not just gradual variation.  
  - **Resolution test** Present fractal dimension as a function of pH with error bars, identify the transition pH, and show reproducibility across multiple experiments.
