## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that hydration water around intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) exhibits more heterogeneous and, on average, more restricted rotational and translational dynamics compared with water around structured proteins of similar size, as measured by megahertz-to-terahertz dielectric spectroscopy and molecular dynamics simulations. They attribute this to excess tightly bound water in the first hydration layer, linked to greater exposure of charged groups, and propose that this correlates with a smaller hydration shell and affects entropy associated with protein-water interactions.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no quantitative data, methodological details, or simulation parameters are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, experimental methods, simulation protocols, quantitative results, statistical analyses, figures, and supplementary information

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting hypothesis regarding IDP-water dynamics, but the evidence base available for review is limited to a summary of findings. The central claims are plausible but cannot be rigorously evaluated without access to the underlying data and methods. The connection between dielectric spectroscopy measurements, simulation results, and the inferred thermodynamic consequences is asserted rather than demonstrated in the abstract. The work may be of interest to the biophysics community, but the current evidence is insufficient to establish the case from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying protein hydration dynamics, intrinsically disordered proteins, and water-mediated interactions in biological systems would be interested. The findings could also appeal to those working on terahertz spectroscopy of biomolecules and molecular dynamics simulations of hydration layers, as the abstract suggests a comparative framework between IDPs and structured proteins.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a relatively underexplored question, namely the origin of bound water dynamics around IDPs, which is relevant to understanding IDP function. The combination of experimental dielectric spectroscopy and molecular dynamics simulations is a potentially powerful approach. The abstract clearly states a comparative design using IDPs with varying charge content and structured proteins of similar size.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that hydration water around IDPs exhibits more heterogeneous and on-average more restricted dynamics compared with structured proteins, and that this arises from excess tightly bound water related to greater exposure to charged groups.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
    **Concern** The abstract provides no quantitative data, such as dielectric relaxation times, simulation-derived diffusion coefficients, or hydration shell sizes, to support the comparative claims. Without these values, the magnitude and statistical significance of the reported differences cannot be assessed.  
    **Why it matters** The central conclusion depends on a robust comparison between IDPs and structured proteins. If the differences are small or within error, the claim of distinct IDP hydration behavior would be weakened.  
    **Resolution test** Provide quantitative results with error estimates and statistical tests for the dynamic and thermodynamic properties described, and specify the number of proteins and simulation replicates used.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
    **Severity** Major  
    **Blocking** Yes  
    **Axis** Methodological transparency  
    **Claim pointer** The abstract states that megahertz-to-terahertz dielectric spectroscopy and molecular dynamics simulations were used, and that entropy contributions were estimated based on these measurements and simulations.  
    **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided  
    **Concern** No details are given on the dielectric spectroscopy setup, frequency range coverage, data analysis procedures, or the force field and water model used in simulations. The method for estimating entropy from dielectric data is not described.

    **Resolution test** Include a methods summary in the abstract or provide access to the full methods section, and specify how entropy was derived from the dielectric and simulation data.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
