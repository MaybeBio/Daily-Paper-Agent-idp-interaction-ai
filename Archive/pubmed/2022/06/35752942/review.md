## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion) without figures, tables, or supporting information
- **Assessment boundary** Mechanistic claims derived from molecular dynamics simulations; no experimental validation data included
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that phosphorylation of p53 at Ser15 enables subsequent phosphorylation at Thr18 by CK1δ through an electrostatically driven induced fit mechanism, where the negatively charged pSer15 is sequestered by a positively charged pocket on CK1δ, positioning Thr18 near ATP for phosphate transfer, and that the resulting complex is a flanking fuzzy complex
- **Visible evidence base** Simulation data from accelerated molecular dynamics (aMD) of free p53 peptides and p53 peptides bound to CK1δ, with and without Ser15 phosphorylation; binding energy calculations (MMPBSA); alanine scanning; conservation analysis
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures, tables, and supporting information referenced but not provided; simulation parameters (force field validation details, aMD boost parameters); raw trajectory data; convergence metrics beyond RMSD; experimental validation data

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a biologically important question regarding the sequential phosphorylation of p53 by CK1δ. The computational approach is appropriate and the authors present a plausible mechanistic model. However, the evidence base is entirely computational, and several key claims regarding the mechanism, complex topology, and functional relevance are inferred from simulations without experimental corroboration. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of validation metrics, more rigorous treatment of force field limitations, and tempering of conclusions where the simulation data are suggestive rather than definitive.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying p53 regulation, kinase substrate specificity, phosphorylation-dependent signaling, and the role of intrinsically disordered regions in protein-protein interactions. The proposed mechanism of electrostatic steering and induced fit in a fuzzy complex may also interest computational biologists studying IDP recognition.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a specific and mechanistically important question. The use of aMD for enhanced sampling is appropriate for disordered systems. The authors consider both free and bound states, and include alanine scanning and conservation analysis to support their conclusions. The proposed model is clearly articulated and testable.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that pSer15 is sequestered in a positively charged pocket of CK1δ and that this interaction constrains Thr18 near ATP for phosphorylation.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Effect of Ser15 phosphorylation on the structure of CK1δ"; Figures 4 and 5 (not provided)
  - **Concern** The central mechanistic claim relies on the identification of a specific binding pose and the proximity of Thr18 to ATP. However, the starting structure for the peptide-CK1δ complex was generated by mutating a p63 peptide from PDB 6ru7 to p53 residues. This introduces potential bias in the initial binding mode. The authors do not demonstrate that the observed interactions are independent of the starting structure, nor do they provide evidence of convergence to a unique or dominant binding mode. Without this, the claim that pSer15 is specifically sequestered by the identified positively charged residues is not robustly established.
  - **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism depends critically on the binding mode and the specific interactions identified. If alternative binding modes are equally populated, the mechanistic conclusion is not supported.
  - **Resolution test** Provide evidence of convergence of the binding mode across independent simulations starting from different initial poses. Show that the identified interactions (e.g., pSer15-Arg178) are consistently observed across replicas and are not artifacts of the initial model. Alternatively, perform simulations with the peptide in a fully extended or random conformation to test whether the same binding mode is reached.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that p53pSer15 binds more strongly to CK1δ than p53, with a ΔG difference of approximately 17 kcal/mol.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Phosphorylated p53 peptide binds more strongly to CK1δ"; Table 1 (not provided)
  - **Concern** The MMPBSA binding free energy values are reported as absolute numbers (ΔG = −96.6 vs −79.7 kcal/mol). These values are known to be highly sensitive to the choice of radii, internal dielectric constant, and entropy contributions, which are not described in the methods. The large magnitude of the difference (17 kcal/mol) is surprising for a single phosphorylation event and may reflect artifacts of the method rather than a true thermodynamic difference. No error estimates or replicate variability are reported.
  - **Why it matters** The claim of stronger binding for the phosphorylated peptide is a key part of the proposed mechanism. If the energy difference is not robust, the mechanistic conclusion is weakened.
  - **Resolution test** Report the standard deviation or standard error of the binding energies across replicate simulations. Provide details on the MMPBSA parameters used (e.g., radii set, internal dielectric, entropy treatment). Consider using an alternative method (e.g., MMGBSA with different radii, or free energy perturbation) to confirm the relative binding affinities.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Claim pointer** The authors propose that the p53pSer15-CK1δ complex is a "flanking fuzzy complex" and that binding occurs via an induced fit mechanism.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Phosphorylated p53 peptide binds more strongly to CK1δ"; Discussion
  - **Concern** The classification of the complex as a "flanking fuzzy complex" is based on the observation that the peptide retains conformational variability after binding. However, the authors do not quantify the degree of disorder or compare it to established criteria for fuzzy complexes. The distinction between induced fit and conformational selection is also not rigorously tested; the authors infer induced fit from the loss of helicity upon phosphorylation, but this could also be consistent with conformational selection if the disordered state is pre-populated.
  - **Why it matters** The proposed binding mechanism and complex topology are central to the manuscript's conceptual contribution. If the classification is not well supported, the novelty of the work is diminished.
  - **Resolution test** Quantify the residual disorder in the bound state (e.g., via secondary structure populations, RMSF, or contact maps) and compare to known fuzzy complexes. Test the induced fit vs. conformational selection distinction by analyzing the free state ensemble for pre-formed binding-competent conformations.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Readability for nonspecialists
  - **Affected element** Introduction
  - **Evidence pointer** Introduction, first paragraph
  - **Issue** The introduction assumes familiarity with IDP concepts and phosphorylation signaling. A brief explanation of why phosphorylation of disordered regions is functionally important would help nonspecialist readers.
  - **Required correction** Add one or two sentences explaining the functional significance of phosphorylation in disordered regions, with a specific example beyond p53.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Affected element** Methods, MD simulations
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Molecular dynamics simulations of free peptides"
  - **Issue** The methods state that implicit solvent MD was used to collapse the extended peptide, followed by explicit solvent MD. The duration of the implicit solvent simulation (0.5 ns) is very short and may not be sufficient to achieve a representative collapsed state. The criteria for choosing this duration are not explained.
  - **Required correction** Justify the 0.5 ns implicit solvent simulation duration or provide evidence that the collapsed state is representative (e.g., comparison of Rg distributions from multiple starting points).
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Affected element** Methods, aMD simulations
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section "Accelerated MD simulations"
  - **Issue** The aMD boost parameters are not reported. The choice of boost energy values can significantly affect sampling and the resulting ensembles.
  - **Required correction** Report the aMD parameters (e.g., threshold energies and boost factors for dihedral and total potential energy) and justify their selection.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Scientific importance
  - **Affected element** Discussion
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion, paragraph 3
  - **Issue** The authors discuss conservation of the positively charged residues across species but do not discuss whether the p53 Ser15-Thr18 motif itself is conserved. This would strengthen the evolutionary argument.
  - **Required correction** Add a brief analysis or comment on the conservation of the p53 sequence motif around Ser15 and Thr18 across species.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (binding mode convergence and starting structure bias), R1-M2 (MMPBSA energy robustness and parameter reporting)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The study offers a novel mechanistic hypothesis for sequential phosphorylation of p53, which is a relevant and under-explored question. However, the conceptual framework (electrostatic steering, fuzzy complexes) is not new, and the specific contribution is incremental. Scientific importance: The question is important for understanding p53 regulation, but the entirely computational nature of the study limits the immediate impact. Interdisciplinary readership: The work will appeal to computational biologists and biochemists studying kinase-substrate interactions, but may be less accessible to a broad biological audience. Technical soundness: The methods are appropriate but several key technical details are missing (aMD parameters, MMPBSA settings, convergence analysis), and the central binding mode claim is not robustly established. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well written but assumes familiarity with IDP and kinase concepts; the abstract is accessible.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The mechanistic hypothesis is interesting and testable, but the current evidence base is insufficient to firmly establish the proposed model. The authors should address the binding mode convergence issue, provide more rigorous energy calculations, and temper conclusions where the data are suggestive rather than definitive.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that p53pSer15 binds more strongly to CK1δ with a ΔG difference of 17 kcal/mol is not robustly supported due to missing MMPBSA parameter details and error estimates.
- The classification of the complex as a "flanking fuzzy complex" is not quantitatively supported.
- The claim that the induced fit mechanism is operative is inferred but not rigorously tested against conformational selection.
- The claim that the identified positively charged residues are the primary determinants of pSer15 recognition is not fully supported without mutagenesis or alternative binding mode analysis.
- The functional relevance of the proposed mechanism for p53 activation in cells is not addressed experimentally and is therefore speculative.
