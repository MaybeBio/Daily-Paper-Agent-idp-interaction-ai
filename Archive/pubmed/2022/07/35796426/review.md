## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, materials and methods, and figure legends as provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific validity, technical soundness, and interpretation of the presented data. No assessment of editorial fit or formatting.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that the intrinsically disordered HVR-1 of HCV E2 acts as an entropic "safety catch" that autoinhibits E1E2 fusion activity on free virions. They argue that host receptor (SR-B1) engagement constrains HVR-1, reducing its conformational entropy and thereby releasing this autoinhibition to permit entry. They further propose that mutations reducing HVR-1 conformational entropy generate hyper-reactive viruses with enhanced entry but reduced stability and increased sensitivity to neutralising antibodies, explaining HCV persistence.
- **Visible evidence base** Experimental data presented in Figures 1–6 and associated supplements, including HCVcc evolution experiments, receptor dependency assays, neutralisation assays, stability measurements, MD simulations, biophysical analyses (CD, nanoDSF), and mathematical modelling.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figure 1—figure supplements 1A–E, Figure 2—figure supplement 1A–B, Figure 3—figure supplements 1–3, Figure 4—figure supplements 1–5, Figure 5—figure supplement 1, Figure 6—figure supplement 1, and all figure panels themselves were not provided. Only figure legends and in-text references were available. This limits verification of data quality, statistical details, and exact numerical values.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a compelling and conceptually novel model for HCV entry regulation, supported by a multi-pronged approach combining viral evolution, reverse genetics, biophysical analysis, MD simulations, and mathematical modelling. The central hypothesis, that HVR-1 acts as an entropic safety catch, is well-articulated and the data are largely consistent with this model. However, the absence of the actual figures and supplements prevents full verification of the quantitative claims and statistical robustness. The mechanistic link between HVR-1 stabilisation and the observed phenotypes, while plausible, is inferred rather than directly demonstrated. The manuscript would be significantly strengthened by direct experimental evidence of the proposed conformational changes and by addressing alternative explanations for the observed phenotypes.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Virologists studying HCV entry and fusion mechanisms, structural biologists interested in intrinsically disordered proteins and their regulatory roles, and vaccinologists working on HCV vaccine development. The concept of an entropic safety catch may also be of interest to the broader cell entry and membrane fusion community, as it proposes a generalisable regulatory mechanism.
- **Major strengths**
  - The central hypothesis is novel and provides a unifying framework for previously disparate observations on HVR-1, SR-B1 dependency, and antibody resistance.
  - The study uses a powerful combination of experimental and computational approaches, including viral evolution under defined selective pressures, reverse genetics, biophysical characterisation, and MD simulations.
  - The mathematical model provides a quantitative framework that integrates the experimental data and generates testable predictions.
  - The finding that hyper-reactive mutants emerge in the absence of antibody selection, but not in its presence, provides strong evolutionary evidence for the functional importance of the safety catch in vivo.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness / Evidence for mechanism): The central claim that HVR-1 stabilisation is the causal mechanism for the hyper-reactive phenotype is not directly demonstrated. The MD simulations show reduced HVR-1 dynamics in the mutants, and the biophysical data show similarity between mutant and ΔHVR-1 E2, but this is correlative. The authors do not provide direct experimental evidence that the conformational entropy of HVR-1 is the specific property that controls E1E2 activity. For example, they do not show that cross-linking or otherwise constraining HVR-1 in the WT protein phenocopies the hyper-reactive state, nor do they show that restoring dynamics to the mutant HVR-1 rescues the WT phenotype. The model is elegant, but the causal chain is incomplete.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes, Axis: Technical soundness / Alternative explanations): The authors attribute the hyper-reactive phenotype to reduced HVR-1 dynamics, but the mutations (I438V, A524T, S449P) are in the E2 ectodomain, not in HVR-1 itself. The MD simulations suggest these mutations stabilise HVR-1 through allosteric effects, but this is a computational prediction. The authors do not experimentally rule out other mechanisms, such as direct effects of the mutations on the stability of the E1E2 heterodimer, on the conformation of the CD81 binding site, or on the membrane fusion machinery in E1. The increased sensitivity to antibodies and reduced thermal stability could be explained by a global destabilisation of the E1E2 complex rather than a specific effect on HVR-1 dynamics.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Technical soundness / Data verification): The manuscript relies heavily on the figures and supplements, which were not provided for review. This makes it impossible to assess the quality of the raw data, the statistical analyses, and the robustness of the quantitative claims. For example, the claim of a "~20-fold increase in sensitivity to mAbs" (Figure 3D) and the "~1000 fold more efficient" SR-B1-mediated CD81 acquisition (Figure 2—figure supplement 1B) are key quantitative results that cannot be verified. The authors should ensure all data, including replicates and statistical tests, are clearly presented.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No, Axis: Technical soundness / Model validation): The mathematical model is a key component of the study, but its validation is not fully described. The model is fit to the experimental data, but it is not clear if it was validated against independent datasets or if it has predictive power beyond the data it was fit to. The authors should provide a more thorough analysis of the model's identifiability and parameter uncertainty.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity / Reproducibility): The Materials and methods section for the MD simulations is detailed, but the exact commands and parameters for the analysis scripts are not provided. The authors state that scripts are available, but a more detailed description of the analysis pipeline would improve reproducibility.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity / Readability): The description of the mathematical model in the Materials and methods is dense and difficult to follow. A schematic diagram of the model states and transitions would greatly improve readability for non-specialists.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Interpretation / Overstatement): In the Discussion, the authors state that the safety catch model "provides mechanistic clarity on the early events of HCV entry." This is an overstatement, as the model primarily describes a regulatory mechanism for E1E2 reactivity, not the detailed molecular events of membrane fusion. The authors should temper this claim.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Data presentation): The authors state that "Mutations that reduce conformational entropy in HVR-1, or genetic deletion of HVR-1, turn off the safety catch to generate hyper-reactive HCV." It would be helpful to see a direct comparison of the phenotypes of the I438V A524T mutant and the ΔHVR-1 virus in a single figure, to more clearly illustrate the quantitative differences and similarities.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality**: High. The concept of an entropic safety catch regulating viral fusion protein activity is novel and not previously described for HCV or other viruses.
  - **Scientific importance**: High. The study addresses a fundamental question in HCV biology (entry and antibody resistance) and proposes a mechanism with broad implications for understanding viral persistence and vaccine design.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: Moderate. The study will be of primary interest to virologists and structural biologists. The biophysical and computational aspects may appeal to a broader readership, but the topic is specialised.
  - **Technical soundness**: Moderate. The multi-pronged approach is impressive, but the lack of direct experimental evidence for the causal role of HVR-1 dynamics, and the inability to verify the data due to missing figures, are significant weaknesses.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: Moderate. The abstract and introduction are accessible, but the results and methods sections are dense and require specialist knowledge.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The central hypothesis is novel and important, and the data are largely consistent with it. However, the manuscript would be significantly strengthened by addressing the concerns raised above, particularly by providing direct experimental evidence for the causal role of HVR-1 dynamics and by making all data available for verification.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that HVR-1 stabilisation is the causal mechanism for the hyper-reactive phenotype is not directly supported by the presented evidence; it is inferred from MD simulations and biophysical similarity to ΔHVR-1.
- The claim that the safety catch mechanism is "likely to be found in other viral fusion machinery" is speculative and not supported by data presented in this manuscript.
- The quantitative claims regarding the magnitude of changes in SR-B1 dependency, entry kinetics, and antibody sensitivity (e.g., "~20-fold", "~1000 fold") cannot be verified without the actual figures and statistical analyses.
- The claim that the model "reconciles numerous reports" is an interpretation that is not fully tested by the model presented.
