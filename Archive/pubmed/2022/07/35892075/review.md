## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a comprehensive and well-structured review that successfully integrates a wide range of experimental and theoretical work on the NPC. The authors make a compelling case for the central role of physical concepts, particularly polymer physics and stochastic transport, in understanding NPC function. The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, though some sections are dense and would benefit from additional explanatory material for non-specialists. The main weakness is the lack of critical depth in some areas, particularly in discussing the limitations and unresolved contradictions of the models presented. The review is a valuable contribution to the field, but it would be strengthened by a more balanced and critical treatment of the evidence.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** This review will be of significant interest to researchers in biophysics, cell biology, and nanoscience. Specifically, it is relevant to: (1) biophysicists studying intrinsically disordered proteins, polymer physics, and transport phenomena; (2) cell biologists investigating nucleocytoplasmic transport mechanisms; (3) nanoscientists designing biomimetic nanopores and synthetic devices for molecular sorting; and (4) computational biologists developing multiscale models of complex biological systems. The review's emphasis on physical principles and model systems makes it particularly valuable for physicists seeking biological applications and for biologists seeking quantitative frameworks.
- **Major strengths** 1. Comprehensive coverage of the field, integrating experimental, theoretical, and computational approaches. 2. Clear articulation of the central physical concepts (polymer physics, phase behavior, stochastic transport) and their application to the NPC. 3. Effective use of simple models to explain complex phenomena, making the material accessible to a broad audience. 4. Good organization, with a logical progression from single-molecule properties to collective behavior and transport dynamics. 5. The discussion of artificial mimics and their role in validating theoretical predictions is particularly strong.
- **Major Concerns** 1. The review is somewhat uncritical in its presentation of the "consensus" view, and does not sufficiently address the significant controversies and unresolved questions in the field. 2. The treatment of the "transport paradox" and its resolution is superficial, and the authors do not adequately discuss the limitations of the proposed solutions. 3. The section on crowding effects is underdeveloped, and the authors do not fully explore the implications of crowding for the selectivity and efficiency of transport. 4. The review would benefit from a more explicit discussion of the limitations of the coarse-grained models and the challenges in translating their predictions to the complex in vivo environment. 5. The authors do not provide a clear roadmap for future research, which would be valuable for a review of this scope.
- **Minor Comments** 1. The abstract is too brief and does not adequately summarize the scope and key conclusions of the review. 2. Some sections are dense and would benefit from additional explanatory text or figures to aid non-specialist readers. 3. The use of terms like "millipede-type motion" is evocative but could be more precisely defined. 4. The authors should consider adding a glossary of key terms (e.g., Flory exponent, Alexander-de Gennes model, potential of mean force) for readers unfamiliar with polymer physics. 5. The discussion of the "futile cycle" is interesting but could be expanded to clarify its implications for the overall energetics of the transport system.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1. The authors do not provide a quantitative comparison of model predictions with experimental data in several key areas (e.g., the scaling of layer height with grafting density, the relationship between transport protein concentration and flux). 2. The discussion of the "anomalous mole fraction effect" in ion channels is presented as an analogy, but the authors do not clearly explain how this concept translates to the NPC system. 3. The authors state that "the full experimental characterization and theoretical understanding of the structure of the FG nups in the NPC remains for the future," but do not specify what specific experimental or theoretical advances are needed to achieve this goal.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** *Originality*: The review is not entirely original in its content, as it synthesizes existing work. However, it offers a novel perspective by framing the NPC as a physical system and emphasizing the role of polymer physics and stochastic transport. This is a valuable contribution. *Scientific importance*: The NPC is a fundamental biological process, and this review provides a comprehensive and up-to-date synthesis of the physical principles governing its function. This is of high importance to the field. *Interdisciplinary readership*: The review is written in a way that should be accessible to a broad audience, including physicists, biologists, and nanoscientists. However, some sections are dense and may be challenging for non-specialists. *Technical soundness*: The technical content is generally sound, but the review is somewhat uncritical and does not fully address the limitations and uncertainties of the models and experiments discussed. *Readability for nonspecialists*: The review is well-written and uses clear language, but the density of information in some sections may be overwhelming for readers without a background in polymer physics or stochastic processes. The authors should consider adding more explanatory material and a glossary.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The review is a valuable contribution to the field, but it would be significantly strengthened by a more critical and balanced treatment of the evidence, a more explicit discussion of the limitations of the models, and a clearer roadmap for future research.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Critical analysis and balance
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript presents a "consensus" view of NPC function, suggesting that the field has largely converged on a unified physical picture.
- **Evidence pointer** Sections 3.4, 4.1, 4.2, and Conclusions.
- **Concern** The review is largely uncritical in its presentation of the field. While it acknowledges some controversies (e.g., the nature of the permeability barrier, the role of cohesiveness vs. entropic exclusion), it does not deeply engage with the evidence for and against different models. For example, the "transport paradox" is mentioned, but the authors do not critically evaluate the various proposed resolutions, nor do they discuss the limitations of the "millipede" model. The review would be more valuable if it provided a more balanced and critical assessment of the strengths and weaknesses of different approaches.
- **Why it matters** A review that presents a single, uncritical narrative can give a misleading impression of the state of the field. A more balanced treatment would help readers understand the uncertainties and open questions, and would be more useful for guiding future research.
- **Resolution test** The authors should revise the relevant sections to explicitly discuss the limitations of the models and the evidence for and against different interpretations. They should also highlight the key unresolved questions and the experimental or theoretical advances needed to address them.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Quantitative rigor
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that simple polymer models "semi-quantitatively" replicate experimental data, and that the scaling of layer height with grafting density follows a specific power law.
- **Evidence pointer** Sections 3.3.2, 3.4.3, and Figures 9, 15, 16, 17.

- **Why it matters** A key strength of the physical approach is its ability to make quantitative predictions. A more detailed comparison of theory and experiment would strengthen the case that the physical models are capturing the essential features of NPC function.
- **Resolution test** The authors should provide a more detailed quantitative comparison of model predictions with experimental data, including error bars and a discussion of the sources of uncertainty. They should also explain the assumptions underlying the models and how these assumptions might affect the predictions.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "the research of the past decade has shown that the behavior of individual FG nups and their assemblies can be well understood in terms of physics of moderately cohesive polymers" is a strong statement that is not fully supported by the evidence presented. While the review provides examples of agreement between models and experiments, it does not systematically address cases where the models fail or where alternative explanations are possible.
- The claim that "minimal physical models have succeeded in semi-quantitatively replicated data from in vitro experimental assays" is vague. The review does not provide a clear definition of "semi-quantitatively" or a detailed comparison of model predictions with experimental data.
- The statement that "the full experimental characterization and theoretical understanding of the structure of the FG nups in the NPC remains for the future" is a truism that does not provide specific guidance on what is needed.
- The discussion of the "anomalous mole fraction effect" in ion channels is presented as an analogy, but the review does not clearly explain how this concept translates to the NPC system, and the analogy may be misleading without further explanation.
