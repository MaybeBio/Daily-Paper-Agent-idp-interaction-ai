- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that "cellular protein-based strategies" and "large-scale chemoproteomics approaches" offer advantages for drugging hard-to-target proteins, implying a comparative benefit over structure-based design.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract asserts the advantages of chemoproteomics without providing any specific examples, quantitative outcomes, or comparative data. It does not mention which chemoproteomic platforms are discussed, what successes have been achieved, or how these approaches overcome the limitations of structure-based design in practice.
- **Why it matters** For a perspective article, the scientific value depends on the strength of the argument, which requires concrete evidence or at least well-chosen illustrative cases. Without this, the claim is an assertion rather than a supported conclusion, and readers cannot evaluate whether the proposed strategy is credible or overhyped.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should include specific case studies (e.g., successful ligand discovery for a previously undruggable protein), data on coverage or hit rates, and a critical comparison with structure-based methods, including limitations such as false positives or lack of structural resolution.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Scope and balance
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that "conformational states of these proteins are shaped in living cells by chemical modifications, protein-protein interactions, oligomerization, and aggregation, offering new ligandable opportunities," implying that these states are broadly exploitable.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not acknowledge the challenges or limitations of targeting conformational states in cells, such as the dynamic and transient nature of these states, the difficulty of achieving selectivity, or the potential for off-target effects. The claim of "new ligandable opportunities" is presented as a general principle without caveats.
- **Why it matters** A balanced perspective is essential for scientific credibility. Overstating the tractability of conformational states could mislead readers, especially those new to the field, and the omission of limitations weakens the manuscript's authority.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should discuss the technical hurdles of chemoproteomics in cellular contexts, such as probe specificity, membrane permeability, and the challenge of mapping ligands to functional outcomes, and should temper the claim with a realistic assessment of current capabilities.
