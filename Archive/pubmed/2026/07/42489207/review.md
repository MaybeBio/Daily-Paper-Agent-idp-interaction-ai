## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as stated in the abstract, without access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that NANOG, a transcription factor critical for pluripotency, forms macroscopic aging gels at high concentrations in a manner dependent on its intrinsically disordered domain. Using molecular dynamics simulations, mass photometry, and cryo-EM, they report discovery of self-limiting micelles with exposed DNA-binding domains, contrasting with unbounded condensates from other intrinsically disordered proteins. They further report that these micelles stabilize DNA entanglements and modulate DNA dynamics, and conjecture a role for NANOG in creating local gel-like environments that restrict genome dynamics and impart mechanical memory to gene regulatory networks.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only. No figures, tables, methods, or supplementary information were provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, experimental protocols, simulation details, statistical analyses, and any supplementary data. Without these, quantitative claims and mechanistic interpretations cannot be independently verified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting and novel biophysical characterization of NANOG, with implications for gene regulation through mechanical rather than purely biochemical means. The combination of experimental and computational approaches is commendable. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to assess the robustness of the key claims, particularly the self-limiting micelle model, the sol-gel transition, and the functional relevance to DNA dynamics. Several claims appear to extend beyond what can be supported by the evidence described at this level of detail.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Biophysicists studying phase separation and condensates of proteins and nucleic acids would find the rheological characterization relevant. Stem cell biologists interested in NANOG function and pluripotency maintenance would be attracted by the proposed mechanical mechanism of gene regulation. Researchers in soft matter physics and polymer dynamics may also be interested in the reported sol-gel transition and DNA entanglement modulation.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses an underexplored aspect of transcription factor behavior, namely macroscopic rheology and aging, which is conceptually distinct from the more commonly studied liquid-liquid phase separation. The use of multiple orthogonal techniques, including simulations, mass photometry, and cryo-EM, is a methodological strength. The proposal that NANOG forms self-limiting micelles, rather than unbounded condensates, offers a potentially novel structural paradigm for disordered proteins.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that NANOG forms self-limiting micelles with exposed DNA-binding domains, as opposed to unbounded condensates.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, methods described as molecular dynamics simulations, mass photometry, and cryo-EM. Location not provided.  
  - **Concern** The abstract states that NANOG forms self-limiting micelles, but no quantitative evidence is presented to support the micellar geometry, the self-limiting size distribution, or the exposure of DNA-binding domains. The distinction from other intrinsically disordered protein condensates is asserted without comparative data.  
  - **Why it matters** The central novelty of the paper rests on this structural claim. If the micelle model is not rigorously established, the subsequent claims about DNA entanglement and modulation lose their mechanistic foundation.  
  - **Resolution test** Provide size distribution data from mass photometry showing a finite, narrow peak, cryo-EM reconstructions or class averages that clearly resolve micellar geometry, and domain-level labeling or structural data demonstrating exposure of DNA-binding domains. Comparative data with a known unbounded condensate-forming protein would strengthen the contrast.

  - **Axis** Technical soundness  
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that NANOG forms macroscopic aging gels at high concentrations dependent on its intrinsically disordered domain.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that NANOG forms self-limiting micelles with exposed DNA-binding domains is unsupported at the abstract level, as no structural or size-distribution data are presented.
- The claim that gel formation and aging depend on the intrinsically disordered domain is unsupported, as no domain-mapping experiments are described.
- The conjecture that NANOG regulates gene expression via local gel-like environments and mechanical memory is speculative and not supported by any direct evidence in the abstract.
- The statement that NANOG modulates DNA dynamics is presented as a finding, but the mechanism and experimental basis are not described in the abstract.
