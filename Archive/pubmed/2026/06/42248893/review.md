## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and figure legends as provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific soundness, methodological rigor, validity of claims relative to presented evidence, and alignment with Nature-style criteria. Editorial or formatting issues are noted only where they affect scientific clarity.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present a machine learning-based enhanced sampling workflow combining REST2, autoencoder collective variables, SWISS, multitask learning, and OPES to characterize metastable conformations of the intrinsically disordered AR-NTD Tau-5 region, elucidate the non-covalent binding mechanism of EPI-002, and discover a novel antagonist K53 with experimentally validated activity in enzalutamide-resistant prostate cancer models.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures 1-7, tables 1-2, methods section, and references to supplementary figures and tables. Supplementary materials were not provided for review.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables (including Supplementary Figs 1-58, Supplementary Tables 1-2), source data files, and the reporting summary were not available. These are essential for verifying convergence analyses, simulation parameters, full Western blot data, and compound characterization.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a technically sophisticated computational workflow with a compelling application to a challenging and clinically relevant target. The integration of multiple enhanced sampling techniques with machine learning is timely and the discovery of K53 with experimental validation is a notable advance. However, several methodological details and validations are incomplete or presented without sufficient evidence, particularly regarding the accuracy of the binding free energy calculations, the justification for the multitask water CV, and the statistical robustness of the biological assays. The claim of a "generalized workflow" is supported by only one additional application to c-Myc, which lacks experimental validation. The manuscript would benefit from clearer presentation of convergence criteria, error analysis, and a more critical discussion of the limitations of the non-covalent model given the known covalent mechanism of EPI-002.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational chemists and biophysicists developing enhanced sampling and machine learning methods for disordered proteins will find the methodological integration of interest. Drug discovery researchers targeting intrinsically disordered proteins, particularly in oncology, will be interested in the practical demonstration of structure-based virtual screening against a dynamic ensemble and the identification of K53. The prostate cancer biology community may also find the characterization of AR-NTD conformational states and the validation of a new antagonist relevant.
- **Major strengths**
  - The workflow is well-structured and integrates state-of-the-art methods (REST2, AE, SWISS, multitask learning, OPES) in a logical sequence.
  - The use of multiple orthogonal experimental validations (SPR, NMR, CETSA, reporter assays) for K53 is commendable and strengthens the translational claims.
  - The identification of nine metastable states and the detailed analysis of water-mediated interactions provide mechanistic insight into a challenging target.
  - The transferability demonstration to c-Myc, while limited, is a valuable addition.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **R1-M1** (Blocking: Yes): The binding free energy calculations for EPI-002 and K53 lack sufficient validation and the reported values are inconsistent with experimental affinities. The manuscript reports a calculated KD of approximately 8 x 10^-7 M for K53, which is more than an order of magnitude tighter than the experimentally measured 2.41 x 10^-5 M. While the authors acknowledge this discrepancy for EPI-002, they do not critically discuss the implications for the accuracy of the computational model or the reliability of the predicted binding mode. The methods section does not provide sufficient detail on the error analysis of the free energy calculations, the convergence criteria used, or the statistical significance of the differences between conformations.
  - **R1-M2** (Blocking: No): The design and validation of the multitask water CV are not fully described. The authors state that the CV integrates water coordination numbers but do not provide a clear justification for the choice of these descriptors, the training data selection, or the validation of the CV's ability to capture the relevant slow degrees of freedom. The statement that the resulting FES "lacked well-defined basins" (Supplementary Figs 27-35) raises questions about the CV's effectiveness and the reliability of the subsequent mechanistic interpretation.
  - **R1-M3** (Blocking: No): The biological validation of K53, while multi-faceted, is presented with limited statistical detail. The number of independent replicates for the MTT assays, reporter assays, and qPCR is stated, but the manuscript lacks a clear description of the statistical tests used, the dispersion of the data, and whether the experiments were performed in a blinded manner. The CETSA data are presented as mean ± SEM of duplicate samples, which is insufficient for a robust conclusion.
  - **R1-M4** (Blocking: No): The claim of a "generalized workflow" is not fully substantiated. The application to c-Myc is purely computational and lacks experimental validation. The authors do not discuss potential limitations or required adjustments when applying the workflow to other IDPs with different sequence properties or interaction mechanisms.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **R1-m1** (Affected element: Abstract and Introduction): The abstract states that ligand recognition is "driven by π–π stacking and structured water-mediated hydrogen bonds." This is a strong causal claim. The evidence presented supports a correlation, but the causal role of these interactions is not directly tested (e.g., through mutation studies). The wording should be softened to "associated with" or "involves."
  - **R1-m2** (Affected element: Results, "Unveiling the binding pockets"): The description of the SWISS simulations is brief. The choice of benzene as a probe, the concentration, and the criteria for identifying a "druggable" pocket should be stated more explicitly.
  - **R1-m3** (Affected element: Results, "Coupling OPES with MLCVs"): The manuscript mentions "positional restraints were imposed to all Cα atoms" during the OPES simulations. This is a significant constraint that could affect the conformational sampling of the protein and the calculated binding free energy. The rationale for this choice and its potential impact on the results should be discussed.
  - **R1-m4** (Affected element: Methods, "Virtual screening"): The description of the virtual screening protocol is incomplete. The specific Glide scoring functions used (SP, XP), the number of conformations per ligand, and the criteria for selecting the final 58 compounds are not fully detailed.
  - **R1-m5** (Affected element: Results, "Assessing transferability"): The statement that the c-Myc workflow used "parameters identical to those employed for Tau-5" is vague. The specific simulation length, number of replicas, and convergence criteria for the c-Myc system should be reported.
  - **R1-m6** (Affected element: Discussion): The discussion of the discrepancy between calculated and experimental binding affinities is brief. A more detailed analysis of the potential sources of error (e.g., force field accuracy, sampling limitations, neglect of covalent bond formation) would strengthen the manuscript.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
  - **Originality**: High. The specific combination of REST2, AE, SWISS, multitask learning, and OPES in a single workflow for IDP drug discovery is novel. The application to AR-NTD and the discovery of K53 are original contributions.
  - **Scientific importance**: High. Targeting intrinsically disordered proteins is a major challenge in drug discovery. A validated computational workflow that can identify druggable conformations and lead compounds has significant potential impact.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership**: Moderate to high. The work bridges computational chemistry, biophysics, and cancer biology. The experimental validation makes it accessible to a broader biological audience, but the methodological depth may limit full comprehension by non-specialists.
  - **Technical soundness**: Moderate. The computational methods are state-of-the-art, but the validation of the free energy calculations is insufficient, and the statistical reporting of the biological assays is incomplete. The lack of supplementary data prevents a full assessment of convergence and robustness.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists**: Moderate. The manuscript is clearly written, but the methods section is dense and assumes significant familiarity with enhanced sampling and machine learning techniques. The main text figures are informative, but the key methodological details are buried in the methods.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core idea and the experimental validation of K53 are promising. However, the manuscript cannot be accepted in its current form. The authors must address the concerns regarding the validation of the free energy calculations, the design of the multitask CV, and the statistical rigor of the biological experiments. The claim of a "generalized workflow" should be tempered or supported with additional evidence.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the workflow "identifies druggable conformations" is supported by the SWISS and docking results, but the functional significance of these conformations is not directly demonstrated.
- The calculated binding free energy for K53 (KD ~ 8 x 10^-7 M) is not consistent with the experimentally measured value (2.41 x 10^-5 M) and is presented without a critical discussion of this discrepancy.
- The statement that the workflow is "readily transferable to other IDPs" is based on a single computational example (c-Myc) without experimental validation.
- The claim that ligand recognition is "driven by" π–π stacking and water-mediated hydrogen bonds is a causal statement not directly tested by the presented experiments.
- The effectiveness of the multitask water CV is questionable given the lack of well-defined basins in the resulting FES, and its contribution to the mechanistic insights is not clearly established.
