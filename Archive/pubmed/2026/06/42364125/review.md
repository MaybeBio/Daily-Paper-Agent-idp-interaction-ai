### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that "Nearly 65% of proteins were either highly acidic or highly basic" and that "almost three-quarters of proteins classified as partially or completely disordered."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract provides no information on the criteria used to define "highly acidic" or "highly basic," nor the prediction tools, thresholds, or statistical methods used to classify disorder. Without this information, these quantitative claims cannot be verified or reproduced.
- **Why it matters** These percentages are central to the paper's argument that charged and disordered proteins dominate shell matrices. If the thresholds or methods are arbitrary or non-standard, the conclusions could be substantially altered.
- **Resolution test** Provide detailed methods for sequence analysis, disorder prediction, and classification criteria. Include validation or benchmarking against known datasets. Show that the results are robust to alternative thresholds or tools.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Support for central model
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that "Quantitative analyses demonstrated strong coupling between intrinsic disorder and regulatory motif enrichment, suggesting that these proteins primarily act as flexible, multivalent scaffolds rather than rigid structural components."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract asserts a "strong coupling" but does not describe the nature of the analysis, the statistical measure used, or the strength of the association. The interpretation that this supports a "multivalent scaffold" model is an inference that requires additional functional or structural evidence.
- **Why it matters** The central model of the paper rests on this coupling. If the statistical relationship is weak or confounded by sequence composition biases, the proposed framework would be undermined.
- **Resolution test** Present the statistical analysis in detail, including effect sizes, confidence intervals, and appropriate controls. Discuss alternative interpretations and provide additional evidence (e.g., experimental or comparative) that supports the scaffold model.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Completeness of evidence
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that "The nacrein protein family, examined as a representative case, showed a conserved carbonic anhydrase catalytic core combined with lineage-specific flexible regions."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided
- **Concern** The nacrein case study is presented as supporting evidence, but no data, alignments, or structural predictions are shown. It is unclear how the "lineage-specific flexible regions" were identified and whether this pattern is generalizable.
- **Why it matters** The case study is intended to illustrate the broader model. Without specific data, it cannot be assessed whether the example genuinely supports the proposed framework or is an isolated observation.
- **Resolution test** Provide the nacrein sequence alignments, disorder predictions, and a clear description of how lineage-specific regions were defined. Show that the pattern holds across multiple lineages and is not an artifact of sequence divergence.

- The statement that "charged macromolecules influence calcium carbonate nucleation" is presented as aligning with existing views, but the abstract does not provide direct evidence linking the identified proteins to nucleation events.
- The quantitative percentages (65% acidic/basic, 75% disordered) are unverifiable without methodological details.
- The nacrein case study conclusions are unsupported in the abstract due to lack of presented data.
