# Fluorine-Thiol Displacement Stapling on the Disordered αB of pKID Domain Increases Its Helicity and Affinity to KIX


## Abstract
  The development of high-affinity ligands specifically targeting intrinsically disordered protein interactions has remained challenging due to the lack of well-defined binding pockets and shallow binding surfaces commonly found at their interfaces. Here, we employed our fluorine-thiol displacement reaction (FTDR) peptide-stapling platform to synthesize a library of peptide-based ligands derived from the αB-helix of the disordered pKID to target its binding partner KIX. Our library revealed that helical formation and affinity to KIX is highly favored when the αB peptide was stapled at sites corresponding to Arg135 and Ser142, further supporting the hypothesis that stabilization of αB significantly influences the overall binding affinity of pKID to KIX. We also found that the highest binding peptide, αB-RSpS, may form secondary contacts at the MLL site on KIX in addition to binding at the primary pKID site. Lastly, no binding to KIX was observed for any αB-stapled peptide that lacked the conserved helix-flanking prolines Pro132 and Pro146. Conserved helix-flanking prolines have previously been shown to modulate the binding affinities of other disordered domains in other proteins including MLL and p53. However, to our knowledge this is the first evidence within αB of pKID.


## The Isolated αB Exhibits Comparable Helicities to αB within Full Length KID/pKID
  While there is structural data on full-length KID/pKID, such data is lacking on αB in isolation.4,13,16,21 Therefore, prior to synthesizing our FTDR-stapled peptide library we determined the helicity of αB in isolation. To this end, an unphosphorylated peptide corresponding to residues 131–145 (Figure 1A) of αB (Ac-αB) was synthesized and the intrinsic helicity was measured using circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy. The CD spectrum revealed that (Ac-αB) is highly disordered with a calculated helicity of 13% (Figure 1B, Table 1), which is comparable to the previous reported value of 10% for αB within the full-length unphosphorylated KID.13 We later on determined the resulting binding affinities for our αB-stapled peptides to KIX using fluorescence anisotropy, however, we wanted to first ensure that the addition of an N-terminal FITC fluorophore and a βAla spacer residue did not perturb the intrinsic helicity previously measured for Ac-αB. When both were included in our peptide (FITC-αB) the intrinsic helicity slightly decreased from 13% to 7% (Figure 1B, Table 1). Lastly, we sought to test the effect of Ser133 phosphorylation on the helicity of FITC-αB. Although phosphorylation of Ser133 within αB has been reported to significantly increase the affinity of pKID towards KIX, it only provides a modest increase in overall helicity (10–15%).13,16,21 When we incorporated pSer133 into the FITC-labeled peptide (FITC-αBpS) the helicity increased to 20% (Figure 1B, Table 1).

  It should be addressed that with respect to the previously reported helical values for αB being measured in the context of full-length KID/pKID, the αB sequence was based on residues 134–144.13 We chose to define αB as residues 131–145 in our studies based on the published NMR solution structure of pKID complexed with KIX (Figure 1A, PDB:1KDX).4,13 In this structure, the N terminus of the αB-helix begins either at Pro132 or Ser133 (Figure 1A).4 Moreover, although Arg131 lies just outside the αB-helix (Figure 1A), we decided to include this residue since it has been proposed to form favorable interactions with pSer133 and aids in the formation of a stable transient structure recognizable by KIX.22 Inclusion of these additional N-terminal residues as well as the removal of the C-terminal Pro146 in our αB peptide likely contribute to why we obtained slightly higher helical values for Ac-αB (13% vs. 10%13), FITC-αBpS (20% vs. 15%13), and the larger net increase in helicity observed from the unphosphorylated (FITC-αB) to the phosphorylated form (FITC-αBpS, ca. 13% vs. ca. 5% in literature13). Overall, these results suggest that our isolated αB peptide does in fact exhibit comparable helicities to αB within full-length KID/pKID.


## Design and Synthesis of the αB-FTDR-Stapled Peptide Library
  Within αB, residues Tyr134, Ile137, Leu138, and Leu141 form a shallow hydrophobic groove with residues located in the α1 and α3 helices of KIX (Figure 1A).4 This shallow groove has been shown to be important as mutations to these residues decreases the affinity of pKID for KIX.7 Moreover, as previously mentioned, Arg131 may allow αB to exist in a more-folded conformation through providing stabilizing interactions with pSer133.22 Therefore, because the staple sites within our peptides require the incorporation of an unnatural amino acid (XL), these residues mentioned above were not chosen as potential staple sites. The remaining solvent-exposed residues were identified as potential staple sites as placement of XL at these positions would ensure our linker would not interfere with the binding interface to KIX (Figure 1A). We synthesized the αB-stapled peptide library using standard Fmoc-based coupling chemistry and positioned our unnatural amino acid, XL, at varying solvent-exposed residues in either an i,i +4 or i,i+7 fashion (Figure 2). The resulting peptides were then FTDR-stapled using either a 1,3 (for amino acid residues i,i+4) or 1,4 (for residues i,i+7) benzendimethanethiol linker following our previously reported procedures.19,23 Unphosphorylated (Ser133) and phosphorylated (pSer133) versions of each αB-stapled peptide were synthesized to observe the effect of the incorporated phosphoserine on the helicity and binding affinity to KIX compared to its unphosphorylated counterpart. We reasoned that incorporation of pSer133 into the resulting αB-stapled peptides would result in higher affinity towards KIX due to the favorable contacts formed between the phosphoryl group and the side chain of Tyr658 of KIX (Figure 1A).4 All peptides were purified by HPLC and the identity and purity were confirmed via LC–MS (see Figures 2–18 and Table 1 in the Supporting Information).


## FTDR Stapling at Positions Corresponding to Arg135 and Ser142 Favors αB-Helical Formation and Results in High-Affinity KIX Binding
  CD spectra were obtained for all synthesized peptides from the aforementioned library to observe how stapling αB at these varying solvent-exposed sites affected its intrinsic helicity (Figure 2). Surprisingly, we obtained significantly varied α-helical values ranging from 8% (αB-PKpS) to 77% (αB-RSpS) (Figure 2, Table 1). When comparing the calculated α-helical values for each pair of αB-stapled peptides (unphosphorylated vs. phosphorylated), we found that incorporation of pSer133 increased the intrinsic helicity for all peptides except αB-PKpS (Figure 2, Table 1). The increase in helicity upon phosphorylation is likely due to the reported formation of favorable interactions between the side chains of Arg131 and pSer133.22 Importantly, the CD results revealed that helical formation of αB appears to be favored when XL was positioned at sites corresponding to Arg135 and Ser142 (as evidenced by αB-RS and αB-RSpS) (Figure 2). Furthermore, αB-RS, which was unphosphorylated, had a measured helicity of 55% which was higher than all other phosphorylated peptides from the library (Table 1). When pSer133 was incorporated into this peptide the helicity further increased to 77% (Table 1). We were curious as to why placement of XL and stapling at these positions was so favorable for peptide folding, given the effect that Arg135 and Ser142 has on the stability of αB has been relatively underexplored. Previously, Arg135 was suggested to be a negative determinant to the helicity of αB, and Ser142 may play a role in transition-state formation with KIX, however, the mechanism for these are not well understood.11,16 Nonetheless, we found that our CD data could be interpreted by comparing the results to an in silico simulation study by Ganguly and Chen that focused on probing the varying helical substates of both αA and αB within full-length KID/pKID.14 Their simulations revealed that the exact helical boundaries of αB extends from different residues between its unphosphorylated and phosphorylated forms. In the unphosphorylated form, the αB-helix extends from Arg131 to Asp144, whereas in the phosphorylated form the helix extends from Tyr134 to Ser142.14 Therefore, we suspect that placement of our staple at sites corresponding to Arg135 and Ser142 essentially induced the folding of the peptide into a conformation that is well aligned with the predicted boundaries of the native phosphorylated αB-helix. This further explains why even the unphosphorylated αB-RS displayed a higher helicity compared to the other phosphorylated peptides as the staple may have rigidly forced this peptide to fold into a ‘phosphorylated-like’ conformation even in the absence of pSer133.

  Next, we tested the binding affinity of the αB-stapled peptides to KIX using fluorescence anisotropy. Both αB-RS and αB-RSpS displayed significantly higher affinities to KIX compared to all other peptides (Figure 3A, B). αB-RS bound to KIX with a corresponding Kd of 7.7 ± 0.2 μM and αB-RSpS displayed a ca. 9.4-fold higher affinity with a Kd of 0.82 ± 0.06 μM (Table 1). We were surprised to observe significantly weaker KIX binding for all the other phosphorylated peptides compared to αB-RS (Figure 3A, B), given that the incorporation of pSer133 would generally lead to stronger binding due to the favorable interactions with Tyr658 in KIX.4 Furthermore, αB-RS bound to KIX with a ca. 3.7-fold higher affinity compared to that of unphosphorylated full-length KID (29 μM21), further supporting our hypothesis that αB-RS exists in a ‘phosphorylated-like’ conformation.21 With the incorporation of pSer133, αB-RSpS displayed similar affinity to KIX as compared to phosphorylated full-length pKID (0.7–0.1 μM).16,21,24


## The Potential Role of the Conserved IDR-Flanking Prolines in KID/pKID
  Crabtree et. al have previously shown that conserved prolines directly flanking helix forming intrinsically disordered regions (IDR) appear to modulate the affinity of the IDR to its cognate binding partner.25 For example, alanine mutations to flanking prolines in the disordered transactivation domain (TAD) of MLL resulted in a 25-fold decrease in binding to KIX, whereas in p53, a 14-fold higher affinity to MDM2 was observed.25 Notably, the disordered pKID also has prolines located directly adjacent to the αB-helix.4 While designing our αB-stapled peptide library, we excluded the C-terminal Pro146 from our peptide sequence since it was not part of the actual αB-helix (Figure 1A).4,14 The N-terminal Pro132, on the other hand, was retained in our peptide sequence and was included as one of the potential staple sites (Figure 2). During our binding experiments, we observed that the two peptides lacking both conserved prolines, αB-PKpS and αB-PNpS, exhibited no discernable change in fluorescence anisotropy upon incubation with KIX at any of the concentrations tested despite possessing pSer133. This made us suspect that the loss of these conserved helix-flanking prolines effectively abolished binding of peptides to KIX. Notably, Crabtree et al. also observed that the helicity of MLL (TAD) increased, albeit only slightly, upon mutation of the flanking prolines.25 To assess the effect on helicity of our αB peptide, we synthesized FITC-αBp-SPro146, which includes the previously excluded C-terminal Pro146. The resulting CD spectrum revealed that inclusion of Pro146 did perturb the intrinsic helicity of αB, reducing the calculated α-helicity from 20% (FITC-αBpS) to 13% (see Figure 1 in the Supporting Information, Table 1). While we were interested in comparing the binding of FITC-αBpS-Pro146 vs. FITC-αBpS to KIX, we did not attempt this experiment due to the fact that isolated αB interacts very weakly with KIX in solution (Table 1) and the binding would not be detectable using our plate reader setup and experimental conditions.11,16 Regardless, these results do further suggest that conserved flanking prolines influence the intrinsic helicity of αB and the affinity to KIX for our αB-stapled peptides.


## The Lead-Stapled Peptide αB-RSpS Forms Secondary Contacts at the MLL Site on KIX
  In addition to the pKID binding site, the KIX domain contains another protein binding surface known as the MLL site.26 The MLL site is situated between the α2- and α3-helices (Figure 4A),27 and although the MLL and pKID sites are located on opposing surfaces there is a high degree of allosteric regulation between the two sites.24,27–31 It has been proposed that allostery between the two sites occurs through dynamic redistributions of KIX microstates as well as re-packing of hydrophobic residues within the core of the KIX whose conformations are highly influenced by bound ligands.27,30 In addition to allosteric regulation, both sites actually share the same ligand binding motif ϕ-x-x-ϕ-ϕ (ϕ = bulky hydrophobic residue, x = any residue).32 Furthermore, the transactivation domain (TAD) of the MLL protein has been shown to bind both the MLL and pKID sites on KIX in vitro, however, pKID has not yet been shown experimentally to bind at any additional sites on KIX.28 Interestingly, Umezawa et. al previously were able to computationally model pKID bound at the MLL site.33 When bound at the MLL site, αB was predicted to completely fold into a helix that was identical in orientation to that of MLL’s TAD and αA was largely unstructured.33 We wondered if this in silico result could explain why we obtained a hill coefficient of 0.78 ± 0.04 (Table 1) for αB-RSpS′ potential negative cooperative binding to KIX in our previous anisotropy experiment.

  Therefore, to test if αB-RSpS could potentially be binding secondarily at the MLL site we incorporated a single alanine mutation at Leu628 of KIX (Figure 4A), which is known to abolish binding to the MLL site on KIX for MLL (TAD).28 Since we hypothesized that αB-RSpS exists in a highly similar conformation as native αB when fully structured, we felt that this mutation was an appropriate starting point to gauge if secondary binding at the MLL site was occurring. Using fluorescence anisotropy, we found that the binding of αB-RSpS to KIXL628A was actually not perturbed and exhibited a comparable Kd to wild-type KIX (Figure 4B, Table 1). From this we thought that αB-RSpS was either interacting with other residues located at the MLL site or that the observed secondary binding was occurring at another location on KIX. Grossman and co-workers have shown that the KID-derived peptide ligand can simultaneously and non-competitively bind to KIX in the presence of MLL.34,35 It is also known that the binary complex of KIX with MLL will have the hydrophobic core allosterically repacked for pKID’s binding.27 These literature precedence suggests the potential presence of an unconventional binding site for KID-derivatized peptides. Notably, Pomerantz and co-workers previously showed through protein-observed fluorine (PrOF) NMR that several ligands appear to bind an unconventional third site located between the α1- and α2-helices on the outer surface of KIX (Figure 4A).36,37 These ligands mainly consisted of small-molecular-weight molecules, however, one molecule, naphthol AS-E phosphate, did possess a phosphate moiety.8,36 Thus, it is possible that our αB-derived stapled peptide can also bind at this cleft location. We then saturated, and effectively blocked, access to the MLL site by pre-incubating wild-type KIX in the presence of four molar equivalent excess of MLL (TAD) prior to incubation with αB-RSpS (see experimental procedures for more information).24 Fluorescence anisotropy revealed that αB-RSpS binding to KIX:MLL decreased with a ca. 2.4-fold lower affinity compared to wild-type KIX (Figure 4B, Table 1). This result confirmed that a small fraction of αB-RSpS may also bind secondarily at the MLL site on wild-type KIX, although the peptide-bound surface may be distant from the Leu628 site. The similar binding affinity observed for αB-RSpS to KIXL628A compared to wild-type KIX also indicates that αB-RSpS may interact with different residues at the MLL site compared to MLL (TAD). The potential secondary binding at the MLL site for αB-RSpS might also explain how we obtained a similar Kd to wild-type KIX as compared to full-length pKID even with exclusion of residues within αA and the interhelical region that also make contacts with KIX.7,21

  In summary, understanding the biophysical underpinnings that influence the binding of intrinsically disordered proteins to their cognate partners is important for the development of high-affinity ligands for these interactions. In this study, we further explored the hypothesis that KIX-bound stabilization of the intrinsically disordered αB within pKID is the major determinant of overall affinity.6,15,16 This hypothesis was explored by utilizing our FTDR chemical stapling approach to synthesize a library of αB-stapled peptides, as mimics of the bound natural αB, to observe their resulting helicities and affinities to KIX. This αB library revealed that both helical formation and affinity to KIX was highly favored when our unnatural amino acid, XL, was placed and stapled at sites corresponding to residues Arg135 and Ser142 for the unphosphorylated and phosphorylated peptide pair, αB-RS and αB-RSpS. We propose that stapling essentially forced these peptides to fold into a conformation similar to that of the predicted native αB-helix in its most folded and competent state to bind KIX.14 We further revealed that conserved flanking prolines directly adjacent to αB may also influence the overall affinity to KIX in addition to stabilization as we almost did not detect any observable binding for αB-stapled peptides which lacked prolines on both terminus.25 Lastly, we determined that our most potent peptide, αB-RSpS, may have a chance to form secondary contacts at the MLL site on KIX, which to our knowledge could be the first evidence that suggests this for αB in vitro. Given αB-RSpS peptide’s exhibition of similar affinity to KIX compared to full-length pKID, we believe that our FTDR stapling approach provides an excellent platform for generating folded peptide fragments mimicking their native protein structures in the bound protein complexes. Further exploration into selective chemical modifications on the αB peptide could allow for not only higher affinity to KIX, but also tunable selectivity to target the pKID site.
