## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** This is a perspective-style abstract with a clear conceptual framework and a useful call for methodological rigour. The central idea that condensate physical state modulates protease function, and that proteolysis can reciprocally shape condensate state, is intellectually appealing and timely. However, the abstract provides no empirical evidence, no specific examples, and no citations, which limits the ability to evaluate the strength of the supporting case. The argument is plausible but currently rests on assertion rather than demonstration. The recommendation to include catalytically inactive controls is sensible and actionable. The piece would interest researchers in phase separation and proteostasis, but the lack of concrete illustration weakens its persuasive force.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying biomolecular condensates and phase separation, particularly those focused on enzymatic reactions within condensed phases. Plant biologists working on proteases and developmental or stress-related proteolysis would also find the framework relevant. The methodological recommendations on controls and phase-resolved measurements would appeal to biophysicists and cell biologists seeking to design experiments in this area.
- **Major strengths** The conceptual framing is clear and parses condensate states into four regimes with distinct kinetic implications. The recognition that condensate composition, not just enzyme concentration, can dictate activity is an important and often underappreciated point. The suggestion that interfaces may serve as reaction compartments adds a useful spatial dimension. The emphasis on catalytically inactive controls to distinguish enzyme-catalysed from condensate-mediated hydrolysis is a practical and valuable caution. The call for falsifiable models is appropriate for a field that risks descriptive stagnation.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "recent evidence suggests that condensates can hydrolyse substrates even without an enzyme."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that recent evidence supports enzyme-independent hydrolysis by condensates, but no citation, experimental system, or data are provided. This is a strong and somewhat surprising claim that challenges conventional assumptions about proteolysis. Without any reference to the specific evidence, the reader cannot assess its reliability, scope, or relevance.  
- **Why it matters** This claim underpins the recommendation for catalytically inactive controls. If the evidence is weak or context-specific, the recommendation may be overgeneralised. Conversely, if the evidence is robust, it should be cited to allow readers to evaluate it. The claim also has implications for interpreting many existing studies that attribute cleavage to resident proteases without such controls.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a citation to the specific study or studies, describe the experimental system, and state the conditions under which enzyme-independent hydrolysis was observed. If the evidence is preliminary, acknowledge its limitations.

- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text, location not provided
