## Review setup

- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript proposes a hypothesis-generating framework for epitranscriptomic drug discovery targeting m6A reader proteins, specifically YTHDF2, through modulation of liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) in synaptic plasticity. It argues that catalytic inhibitors of m6A writers/erasers lack spatial and temporal specificity, and that targeting YTHDF2 condensation dynamics (C-sat) could offer a complementary strategy. The framework includes a Phase Transition Threshold model, four competing mechanistic models, falsifiable predictions, and a staged experimental program.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a well-written, intellectually honest perspective that proposes a novel framework for targeting m6A reader phase separation in synaptic plasticity. The author demonstrates commendable scientific rigor by explicitly acknowledging the framework's vulnerabilities, including the unresolved nucleation-versus-concentration question and the possibility that YTHDF2-mediated decay may not require condensation. The staged experimental program with power calculations and positive/failure criteria is a strength. However, the manuscript's central claims rest on extrapolations from non-neuronal systems, and the key mechanistic distinction (nucleation- vs concentration-driven) is acknowledged as unresolved. The framework is plausible but not yet established. The author's honesty about the incremental nature of the proposed work is refreshing but may limit the manuscript's impact for a broad readership.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in epitranscriptomics, RNA biology, and phase separation will find the framework intellectually stimulating. Drug discovery scientists interested in condensate-modulating compounds may find the pharmacological framing useful. Neuroscientists studying synaptic plasticity and neurodevelopmental/neuropsychiatric disorders (Rett syndrome, Fragile X, addiction) may be interested in the proposed YTHDF2-FMRP competition model. The manuscript is likely to generate discussion and potentially inspire experimental tests, making it suitable for a specialized audience rather than a general one.
- **Major strengths** 1) Intellectual honesty: the author explicitly states the framework's vulnerabilities and failure criteria, which is rare and commendable. 2) Clear articulation of competing models (four alternatives) with testable predictions. 3) Staged experimental program with explicit power calculations, sample sizes, and replication requirements. 4) Recognition of the selectivity problem within the YTHDF family and the limitations of in vitro LLPS assays. 5) The distinction between thermodynamic C-sat and observed C-obs is a useful conceptual contribution.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that "Recent evidence has established YTHDF2 synaptic localization, FMRP condensate formation in neurons, and functional antagonism between the two proteins" and that "FMRP and YTHDF2 are not merely coexisting in the same cellular space—they are functionally antagonistic with respect to transcript fate."
- **Evidence pointer** Section 2.3, references [27,28,29]
- **Concern** The manuscript relies heavily on three recent studies (Flamand and Meyer [27], Niu et al. [28], Koo et al. [29]) to establish the synaptic localization of YTHDF2, FMRP condensate formation in neurons, and functional antagonism. However, these studies are described only briefly, and their methodological quality, experimental rigor, and potential limitations are not critically evaluated. The claim that FMRP "reshapes the YTHDF2 complex" and promotes transport rather than degradation is a strong mechanistic claim that requires careful scrutiny. Without a detailed assessment of these studies, the foundation of the proposed model is not adequately established.
- **Why it matters** The entire framework depends on the validity of these three studies. If any of them are methodologically flawed, overinterpreted, or not reproducible, the proposed model loses its empirical basis. The author should either provide a more detailed critical evaluation or temper the strength of the claims.
- **Resolution test** The author should provide a detailed critical assessment of each of the three studies, including their experimental designs, sample sizes, statistical analyses, and potential confounds. Alternatively, the author should soften the language from "established" to "suggested" or "preliminary evidence."

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Logical coherence
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript proposes a "Phase Transition Threshold model" in which "YTHDF2's condensation concentration decides whether synaptic mRNAs are degraded or maintained in translation-competent, FMRP-associated states," but also acknowledges that "the central unresolved question is whether condensation in living cells is nucleation-driven rather than bulk-concentration-driven."
- **Evidence pointer** Section 3.1, Figure 1
- **Concern** The manuscript's pharmacological premise is that shifting C-sat is a viable drug strategy. However, the author explicitly acknowledges that it is unknown whether condensation is concentration-driven or nucleation-driven. If nucleation-driven, the C-sat framework is not applicable, and the proposed drug discovery strategy would be targeting the wrong parameter. This is a fundamental logical inconsistency: the framework's central therapeutic premise rests on an unresolved mechanistic question. The author acknowledges this but does not resolve it or propose a clear path to resolution before drug discovery efforts begin.
- **Why it matters** The manuscript's title and framing suggest a "framework for epitranscriptomic drug discovery," but the framework's applicability depends on a mechanistic question that is acknowledged as unresolved. This undermines the practical utility of the proposed framework and raises questions about whether the staged experimental program should be prioritized differently.
- **Resolution test** The author should either (a) propose a specific, feasible experiment to distinguish nucleation- from concentration-driven condensation as a prerequisite for drug discovery, or (b) reframe the framework to accommodate both mechanisms, or (c) explicitly state that the framework is contingent on resolving this question and that drug discovery efforts should not proceed until it is resolved.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Generalizability
- **Claim pointer** The manuscript proposes that "hyper-condensation (lowered C-sat)" is relevant to Rett syndrome and "hypo-condensation (elevated C-sat)" is relevant to addiction, but explicitly states that "there is no direct evidence linking Rett syndrome to YTHDF2 hyper-condensation" and that the addiction model is "an untested conjecture with no empirical support."
- **Evidence pointer** Section 3.2, Table 1
- **Concern** The disease relevance sections for Rett syndrome and addiction are presented as hypotheses with no direct evidence. While the author is honest about this, the inclusion of these disease models in a framework for drug discovery may give readers the impression that there is more empirical support than actually exists. The author should more clearly distinguish between evidence-based claims and speculative hypotheses, perhaps by using explicit labels such as "speculative" or "untested."
- **Why it matters** Readers, particularly those in drug discovery, may be misled into thinking that Rett syndrome and addiction are validated indications for YTHDF2 condensate modulators. This could lead to premature investment in drug discovery programs targeting these indications.
- **Resolution test** The author should add explicit labels (e.g., "speculative hypothesis," "untested conjecture") to the disease relevance sections and consider moving them to a clearly marked "Future Directions" section rather than presenting them as part of the framework's rationale.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "FMRP and YTHDF2 are functionally antagonistic with respect to transcript fate" is based on a single study [29] that is not critically evaluated; this claim is not yet established.
- The disease relevance models for Rett syndrome and addiction are explicitly acknowledged as untested conjectures; they should not be presented as part of the framework's rationale without clear labeling.
- The claim that "m6A acts as a multivalent scaffold for reader condensation" is based on in vitro studies [14,15] that may not reflect physiological conditions; this claim is not yet established in neurons.
- The claim that "STC-15 is the first RNA-methyltransferase inhibitor with proven human clinical activity" is based on a single trial [2,3] and may be premature; the author should verify this claim against the broader clinical literature.
- The claim that "no one has raised this question in the literature" regarding nucleation- versus concentration-driven condensation is difficult to verify and may be overstated; the author should soften this claim.
