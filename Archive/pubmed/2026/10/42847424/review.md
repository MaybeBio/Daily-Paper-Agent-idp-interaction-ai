They argue that the same protein ratio can generate different molecular and functional states under different concentration or affinity regimes, and they outline predictions and experimental strategies for testing composition-sensitive behavior.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a conceptual framework that integrates relative protein abundance with binding affinity and absolute concentration to explain how changes in component ratios can alter molecular assembly and cellular function. The authors draw on established biochemical principles and illustrate the framework with several well-chosen biological examples. The writing is clear and the conceptual contribution is potentially useful for interpreting perturbation experiments. However, the manuscript is largely synthetic and descriptive, with no new experimental data or quantitative modeling that would demonstrate the framework's predictive power. The quantitative section is thin, and the predictions, while testable in principle, are not accompanied by concrete experimental designs or worked examples that would allow a reader to implement them. The framework's novelty relative to existing mass-action and stoichiometric models is asserted but not rigorously demonstrated. The manuscript would benefit from a more formal treatment of the quantitative claims and a clearer articulation of what specific, falsifiable predictions distinguish this framework from existing approaches.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Cell biologists and biochemists studying protein-protein interactions, complex assembly, and signaling pathways would find this framework relevant, particularly those working on transcription factor dimerization, apoptosis regulation, receptor assembly, and biomolecular condensates. Systems biologists interested in dose-response relationships and quantitative models of cellular regulation may also find the framework useful as a conceptual organizing principle. The translational implications for cancer biology and therapeutic targeting of protein stability could interest researchers in those areas.

- **Major strengths** The manuscript is clearly written and logically organized. The framework is grounded in established biochemical principles, and the authors are careful to acknowledge its limitations and boundaries. The biological examples are well chosen and illustrate distinct interaction architectures. The emphasis on testable predictions and the explicit statement of conditions under which the framework adds little value is commendable. The authors correctly distinguish between measured abundance and molecular occupancy, and they appropriately caution against interpreting ratios in isolation.

- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The quantitative basis is underdeveloped. The authors present a single equation for a 1:1 interaction but do not derive or discuss its implications in any depth. No worked examples, simulations, or parameter sweeps are provided to illustrate the regimes in which ratio-dependent behavior would be expected to emerge. The claim that the framework generates "testable predictions" is not supported by concrete quantitative predictions that could be distinguished from alternative models.
  - R1-M2: The novelty of the framework relative to existing models is not rigorously established. The authors acknowledge that classical mass-action and fractional-occupancy models already describe how concentration and affinity determine complex formation, and that stoichiometric models constrain assembly. The specific added value of the "ratio rheostat" as an explicit coordinate is asserted but not demonstrated with a comparative analysis or a case where existing models fail.
  - R1-M3: The biological examples are presented descriptively without quantitative analysis. For each example, the authors state that relative abundance matters, but they do not provide quantitative evidence or specific predictions that would allow a reader to test the framework in those systems. The examples serve as illustrations rather than tests.
  - R1-M4: The experimental strategies section is generic and does not provide sufficient detail for implementation. The authors recommend sampling a joint concentration space and measuring assembly states, but they do not specify how to choose concentration ranges, how many conditions to sample, what statistical power would be needed, or how to distinguish ratio-dependent effects from total-abundance effects in practice.

- **Minor Comments**
  - R1-m1: The paint analogy, while accessible, may oversimplify the biological complexity. The authors do acknowledge this limitation, but the analogy is used repeatedly and could be supplemented with a more quantitative illustration.
  - R1-m2: The term "rheostat" is used throughout but never formally defined. A brief operational definition would help readers understand what qualifies as a rheostat versus a simple concentration-dependent effect.
  - R1-m3: The discussion of miRNAs as a mechanism for establishing protein ratios is interesting but somewhat tangential, as the framework does not depend on any specific regulatory mechanism. This section could be shortened or reframed as one example among many.
  - R1-m4: The reference to the VERITAC-2 trial and vepdegestrant is specific and current, but its relevance to the framework is not fully developed. The connection between protein degradation and ratio restoration could be made more explicit.
  - R1-m5: The authors state that "relative composition and total abundance can then be treated as derived coordinates of the experimentally sampled concentration space," but they do not discuss how to handle the statistical challenges of analyzing such derived coordinates, such as collinearity with the original concentration measurements.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3, R1-M4

- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: Moderate. The framework is a synthesis of existing concepts rather than a fundamentally new idea. The emphasis on relative abundance as an explicit experimental coordinate is somewhat novel, but the underlying biochemistry is well established.
  - Scientific importance: Moderate. If validated, the framework could improve interpretation of perturbation experiments and guide therapeutic strategies, but the manuscript does not provide evidence that it changes current understanding or practice.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: Limited. The manuscript is primarily relevant to cell and molecular biologists. The quantitative content is not developed enough to attract systems biologists or physicists.
  - Technical soundness: The qualitative claims are sound and appropriately hedged, but the quantitative treatment is too thin to assess rigorously. The single equation is correct but not sufficient to support the framework's claims.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: Good. The writing is clear and the concepts are explained accessibly, though the quantitative section could be more developed without sacrificing readability.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the framework generates "testable predictions" is not supported by concrete, quantitative predictions that distinguish it from existing models.
- The claim that the framework "complements rather than replaces" existing models is asserted but not demonstrated with a comparative analysis.
- The biological examples are presented as illustrations of the framework but are not analyzed quantitatively, so their support for the framework is limited.
- The statement that "relative composition and total abundance can then be treated as derived coordinates" implies a methodological recommendation that is not developed or validated.
- The relevance of the VERITAC-2 trial to the framework is asserted but not mechanistically connected.
- The claim that "the same protein ratio can therefore generate different molecular and functional states under different concentration or affinity regimes" is a restatement of mass-action principles and is not novel, though the emphasis on it as a framework is.
