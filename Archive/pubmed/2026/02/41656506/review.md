The claim that the review establishes a 'robust framework' is not supported by the evidence presented in the abstract, which is descriptive rather than analytical. The inclusion of computational docking as a complementary approach is appropriate for a review of this type.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that key SAR insights highlight critical contributions of structural motifs, including planar aromatic cores, cationic side chains, and electron-donating or electron-withdrawing groups, that determine inhibitory potency and selectivity.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract asserts specific SAR determinants without presenting any quantitative data, such as potency values, selectivity indices, or comparative activity tables. No specific compounds or their activities are mentioned, and no structure-activity correlations are shown.  
- **Why it matters** A claim about SAR determinants is only meaningful if supported by data that demonstrate a correlation between structural features and biological activity. Without such data, the claim is an assertion rather than an evidence-based conclusion, and the reader cannot evaluate the validity of the proposed framework.  
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript must include SAR tables or figures that show activity data for multiple compounds within each scaffold class, with clear annotation of the structural features being varied. The text should explicitly link each structural motif to a measured effect on potency or selectivity.

- **Axis** Technical soundness

- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided

Representative docking poses with interaction diagrams and residue contacts should be shown for at least one compound per scaffold class.
