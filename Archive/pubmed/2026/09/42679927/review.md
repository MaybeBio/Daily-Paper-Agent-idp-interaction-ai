## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents an ambitious theoretical synthesis that connects epichaperome biology with multiple branches of biophysics and systems science. The conceptual ambition is notable, and the authors are transparent about the speculative nature of the framework. However, the abstract provides no mathematical details, no derivations, no model specifications, and no validation strategy beyond a general statement that testable predictions are generated. As presented, the work is a proposal of a conceptual framework rather than a demonstrated scientific result. The scientific case is not established from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in chaperone biology, proteostasis, stress response, and systems biology may find the conceptual framing of interest as a potential organizing perspective. Biophysicists working on active matter, electrostatics in cellular environments, and transport phenomena may be interested in the proposed integration of formalisms. Clinicians and translational researchers studying cancer, neurodegeneration, and chronic inflammation could find the framework conceptually suggestive for understanding disease mechanisms, though no direct translational claims are made.
- **Major strengths** The authors identify a genuine gap in the epichaperome literature regarding potential physical and organizational roles beyond protein interaction networks. The proposed integration of multiple established biophysical formalisms is intellectually ambitious and could provide a useful conceptual scaffold for future work. The explicit acknowledgment of the speculative nature of the theory and the identification of testable predictions are commendable for scientific honesty. The transcellular extension is a creative hypothesis that could stimulate new experimental directions.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that a mathematical description is developed by integrating Poisson-Boltzmann electrostatics, Nernst-Planck transport theory, active matter physics, percolation theory, graph theory, biomolecular condensate thermodynamics, and porous hydrogel transport models.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Results section; location not provided
- **Concern** No mathematical equations, derivations, model assumptions, or parameter definitions are presented. The abstract lists formalisms but provides no indication of how they are integrated, what variables are used, what approximations are made, or what the resulting mathematical structure looks like.
- **Why it matters** Without any mathematical content, the claim that a mathematical description has been developed cannot be evaluated. The integration of seven distinct formalisms is a nontrivial task that requires careful justification of compatibility, scaling, and approximation. The absence of any technical detail makes the central claim of the paper unverifiable.
- **Resolution test** Provide the full mathematical framework in the manuscript, including governing equations, boundary conditions, parameter definitions, and a clear statement of which formalisms contribute to which components of the model. A derivation or at least a well-specified model formulation is required.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence and validation
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that mathematical analysis predicts the emergence of percolating transport networks, electrostatic coupling domains, synchronized conformational dynamics, and stress-responsive communication pathways when epichaperome connectivity exceeds critical thresholds.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Results section; location not provided
- **Concern** The predictions are stated as outcomes of mathematical analysis, but no results of such analysis are shown. There are no figures, tables, or numerical results demonstrating these predicted phenomena. The critical thresholds are mentioned but not defined or quantified.
- **Why it matters** The predictive content of the theory is its main scientific contribution. Without showing the actual predictions, the reader cannot assess whether the framework produces meaningful or testable outcomes. The claim that thresholds exist is central to the theory but is entirely unsupported in the provided material.
- **Resolution test** Include figures or tables showing the predicted phenomena as functions of model parameters. Define the critical thresholds explicitly and show how they emerge from the mathematics. Provide at least one concrete example of a testable prediction with quantitative specificity.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Falsifiability and testability
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that the framework generates testable predictions regarding membrane-associated epichaperomes, extracellular epichaperome assemblies, electrostatic organization, and intercellular transport behaviors.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Conclusions section; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not specify what these testable predictions are. No experimental design, measurable quantities, or discriminating outcomes are described. The statement that predictions are generated is made without any indication of their content or how they could be distinguished from alternative models.
- **Why it matters** A scientific theory must be falsifiable. The current presentation offers no way to determine what evidence would support or refute the framework. Without explicit predictions, the theory cannot be tested and therefore cannot be evaluated as a scientific contribution.
- **Resolution test** List at least three specific, quantitative predictions with associated experimental approaches. For each prediction, state what result would confirm and what result would refute the framework.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Conceptual coherence
- **Claim pointer** The abstract proposes that epichaperomes function as adaptive organizational scaffolds capable of influencing molecular flux, signaling efficiency, and spatial coordination within cells, and extends this to a transcellular continuum spanning cells, tissues, and organ systems.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Results and Conclusions sections; location not provided
- **Concern** The conceptual leap from intracellular epichaperome assemblies to a transcellular continuum is made without any discussion of mechanistic plausibility. The abstract does not address how intracellular assemblies could be functionally coupled to extracellular ones, what the physical basis of such coupling would be, or what evidence exists for the required extracellular assemblies.
- **Why it matters** The transcellular extension is a major component of the proposed framework. Without mechanistic justification or reference to existing evidence for extracellular epichaperome assemblies, this extension appears speculative in a way that undermines the credibility of the overall framework.
- **Resolution test** Provide a mechanistic argument for how intracellular and extracellular epichaperome assemblies could be functionally coupled. Cite any existing evidence for extracellular epichaperome assemblies or clearly state what new evidence would be required to support this hypothesis.
