## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a compelling and comprehensive model for the multistep recruitment of the ULK1 complex to autophagic membranes, addressing a long-standing gap in the field. The combination of structural predictions, molecular dynamics simulations, biochemical reconstitution, and cell-based assays provides a multi-layered approach to support the proposed pathway. The identification of the ATG13 DHF motif for WIPI3 binding, the membrane-anchoring role of the ATG101 WF finger, and the ULK1 PVP motif for HORMA domain interaction are significant findings. However, the absence of the primary data (figures) prevents a full evaluation of the evidence, and several claims require more rigorous quantitative support.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of autophagy, membrane biology, cell signaling, and protein-lipid interactions. The work provides a mechanistic framework for a central event in autophagy initiation, which is relevant to understanding the pathophysiology of neurodegenerative diseases, cancer, and other conditions where autophagy is dysregulated.
- **Major strengths** 1. Addresses a fundamental, unresolved question in autophagy: how the ULK1 complex is recruited to PI3P-containing membranes. 2. Employs a powerful and complementary combination of computational (AlphaFold, MD simulations) and experimental (biochemical reconstitution, cell-based assays) approaches. 3. Identifies specific, conserved motifs (DHF, WF, PVP) and their functional roles, providing clear targets for future investigation. 4. The stepwise model is logically coherent and integrates multiple known components (WIPI2, WIPI3, ATG13, ATG101, ULK1) into a unified pathway.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Data Integrity and Presentation
    - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims to provide experimental evidence for all key interactions and functional consequences.
    - **Evidence pointer** All figures (Fig. 1-6, S1-S10)
    - **Concern** The manuscript is submitted without the primary data (figures and figure legends). It is impossible to evaluate the quality, controls, reproducibility, and statistical rigor of the experiments described in the Results section. For example, the GUV binding assays (Fig. 2), the reconstituted kinase assays (Fig. 4), and the cell-based autophagy/mitophagy assays (Fig. 4, 6) are central to the conclusions, but their validity cannot be assessed.
    - **Why it matters** A scientific manuscript's conclusions are entirely dependent on the presented data. Without the figures, the review cannot be performed. This is a fundamental requirement for peer review.
    - **Resolution test** The authors must provide the complete manuscript with all figures and figure legends.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Quantitative Support for Key Claims
    - **Claim pointer** The authors state that the ATG13 DHF motif is a "critical region binding to WIPI3" and that the ATG101 WF finger is "essential for synergistic membrane recruitment."
    - **Evidence pointer** Results sections "ATG13 contains a DHF motif that binds to WIPI3" and "ATG101 binds directly to phospholipid membrane through its WF finger and CTH"
    - **Concern** The binding data for the ATG13-WIPI3 interaction (Fig. 1F, G) and the membrane recruitment data (Fig. 2) are described qualitatively or with simple bar graphs. The text mentions "profound reduction" and "quantifiable defect," but without seeing the data, it is unclear if these are statistically significant and biologically meaningful. For the GUV experiments, the concentration-dependent relationship for the WF mutant is mentioned, but the quantitative parameters (e.g., Kd, Hill coefficient) are not provided. The claim of "synergistic" recruitment requires a formal demonstration of synergy (e.g., the effect of WIPI3 + ATG101 WT is greater than the sum of individual effects).
    - **Why it matters** The central model of the paper rests on the strength of these interactions. Without quantitative binding affinities and a demonstration of synergy, the model remains a qualitative hypothesis.
    - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative binding data (e.g., Kd values from isothermal titration calorimetry or surface plasmon resonance) for the ATG13-WIPI3 and ATG101-membrane interactions. For the GUV data, provide a formal analysis of synergy, showing that the combined effect of WIPI3 and the ATG101 WF finger is greater than additive.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Functional Significance of the ULK1-ATG13 HORMA Interaction
    - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the ULK1 PVP motif interaction with the ATG13 HORMA domain is "essential for in vitro ULK1 phosphorylation of ATG16L1 and important for autophagy and mitophagy."
    - **Evidence pointer** Results sections "ULK1 IDR binds directly to the ATG13 HORMA domain" and "The ULK1-IDR interaction with ATG13HD drives proximity of ULK1 KD to the membrane"
    - **Concern** The in vitro kinase assay (Fig. 5F, G) shows a complete loss of phosphorylation with the ULK1 ADA mutant. The modeling (Fig. 5H-J) suggests a reduction in the average distance to the membrane from 19 to 12 nm. While this is a plausible mechanism, the paper does not provide direct evidence that the loss of kinase activity is solely due to the increased distance from the membrane. The ADA mutation could also affect ULK1's intrinsic catalytic activity or its ability to interact with other components of the complex. The modeling is based on a single, specific conformation of the complex and may not represent the full ensemble of states.
    - **Why it matters** The paper proposes a specific spatial mechanism for ULK1 activation. If the ADA mutation has other effects, the conclusion that the sole function of this interaction is to bring the KD closer to the membrane is weakened.
    - **Resolution test** Perform an in vitro kinase assay using a soluble, non-membrane-bound substrate to test if the ADA mutation affects ULK1's intrinsic catalytic activity. Use a different approach to tether the ULK1 KD to the membrane (e.g., a synthetic lipid anchor) to see if this can rescue the activity of the ADA mutant. Provide more extensive conformational sampling in the MD simulations to support the distance reduction claim.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M4
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** No
    - **Axis** Specificity and Redundancy of WIPI Proteins
    - **Claim pointer** The authors state that WIPI3 is a key player in ULK1C recruitment, but also show that WIPI2 can bind ATG13.
    - **Evidence pointer** Results section "ATG13 contains a DHF motif that binds to WIPI3 at a different site than WIPI2" and Discussion
    - **Concern** The paper shows that ATG13 binds to both WIPI2 and WIPI3 via different sites. The reconstitution assay (Fig. 4) requires both WIPI2 and WIPI3 for maximal ULK1 activity. The cellular data (Fig. 4H-L) focuses on the ATG13-WIPI3 interaction. The Discussion mentions that WIPI3 KO has been reported to have no effect on starvation-induced autophagy in some cell types. The paper does not fully address the relative importance and potential redundancy of WIPI2 vs. WIPI3 in different contexts (e.g., starvation vs. mitophagy). The claim that WIPI3 is a "critical driver" may be overstated if WIPI2 can compensate in some conditions.
    - **Why it matters** Understanding the specific roles of WIPI2 and WIPI3 is important for a complete model of ULK1C recruitment and for interpreting the results of WIPI knockout experiments.
    - **Resolution test** Perform a more systematic analysis of the requirement for WIPI2 vs. WIPI3 in the cellular assays. For example, test the effect of WIPI2 or WIPI3 knockdown on ULK1 puncta formation and ATG16L1 phosphorylation in the context of both starvation and mitophagy. The authors should also discuss the potential for redundancy more explicitly.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the ATG13-WIPI3 interaction is "critical" for ULK1C recruitment in all forms of autophagy is not fully supported, as the paper does not rule out redundancy with WIPI2.
- The claim that the sole function of the ULK1 PVP-ATG13 HORMA interaction is to bring the KD closer to the membrane is not directly proven, as other effects of the ADA mutation are not ruled out.
- The claim that the ATG101 WF finger is "essential" for membrane recruitment is supported by the mutant data, but the quantitative contribution relative to the WIPI3-PI3P interaction is not fully defined.
- The claim that the model explains the "more than decade-old observation that ULK1C is stabilized at phagophore initiation sites by PI3P" is a reasonable interpretation but is not directly tested in the paper.
