## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a broad and ambitious narrative review covering the connections between iron and copper homeostasis and a wide range of neurodegenerative diseases. The scope is impressive, and the organization is logical, moving from basic metal metabolism to molecular interactions, downstream pathological consequences, disease-specific implications, and therapeutic strategies. The inclusion of AI applications is a timely and potentially valuable addition. However, the review's primary weakness is its largely descriptive nature. Many sections read as a catalogue of findings rather than a critical synthesis. The authors frequently state that mechanisms are "not fully understood" or "remain unclear" without offering a deeper analysis of competing hypotheses or unresolved questions. The clinical trial discussion is appropriately cautious, but the overall therapeutic outlook could benefit from a more structured evaluation of the evidence. The manuscript would be strengthened by a clearer articulation of the central hypotheses, a more critical evaluation of conflicting evidence, and a more explicit framework for how the field should advance. The absence of figures and tables in the provided materials limits a full assessment of their quality and utility.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of metallobiology, neurochemistry, and neurodegeneration would find this review useful as a broad overview. Clinicians and translational scientists interested in metal-based therapeutic strategies for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and other NDDs would also be a key audience. The sections on AI applications may attract computational biologists and researchers interested in drug discovery. The review serves as a comprehensive entry point for newcomers to the field and a reference for established investigators seeking a summary of the landscape.
- **Major strengths**
    1.  Comprehensive scope covering both iron and copper metabolism, their interactions with key proteins, downstream pathological mechanisms, and therapeutic strategies across multiple NDDs.
    2.  Logical organization that guides the reader from fundamental biochemistry to disease-specific implications and future directions.
    3.  Inclusion of a dedicated section on AI applications, which is a forward-looking and increasingly relevant topic.
    4.  Balanced presentation of therapeutic approaches, including chelators, ionophores, and natural products, with appropriate caution regarding clinical trial results.
    5.  The emphasis on the interconnectedness of iron and copper homeostasis, rather than treating them in isolation, is a conceptual strength.
- **Major Concerns**

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence quality and synthesis
- **Claim pointer** The review claims to provide a "systematic overview" and "comprehensive reference" of the connection between iron and copper homeostasis and NDDs.
- **Evidence pointer** Throughout the manuscript, particularly in sections on disease-specific mechanisms (e.g., Alzheimer's Disease, Parkinson's Disease).
- **Concern** The review is largely descriptive, presenting a sequence of findings from the literature without a critical synthesis. For example, in the Alzheimer's Disease section, the text describes iron accumulation, copper dyshomeostasis, and their interactions with Aβ and tau, but does not critically evaluate the strength of evidence for the amyloid hypothesis versus the metal hypothesis, nor does it discuss contradictory findings in the field. The statement that mechanisms are "not fully understood" is repeated frequently without a deeper exploration of the specific gaps in knowledge or the competing models that could explain the observations.
- **Why it matters** A review's primary value lies in its ability to synthesize a complex field, identify key debates, and provide a critical perspective. A purely descriptive account, while comprehensive, does not advance understanding or guide future research as effectively. The lack of critical analysis makes it difficult for a reader to assess the relative importance of different findings or to identify the most promising avenues for future investigation.
- **Resolution test** The authors should revise the disease-specific sections to include a critical discussion of the evidence. For each major disease, they should explicitly state what is known, what is debated, and what key experiments or clinical trials are needed to resolve the uncertainties. This could involve presenting competing hypotheses and evaluating the evidence for and against each.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Depth of mechanistic insight
- **Claim pointer** The review aims to dissect the "intricate molecular mechanisms" linking metal dyshomeostasis to NDDs.
- **Evidence pointer** Sections on "The Downstream Effect of Dysregulated Iron and Copper Metabolism" and "Interaction of Iron and Copper With Key IDPs in NDDs".
- **Concern** While the review lists many molecular players and pathways, the mechanistic depth is often shallow. For example, the discussion of copper-induced oxidative stress mentions the involvement of CREB and the reduction of BDNF and Bcl-2, but does not delve into the upstream signaling events, the specific reactive species involved, or the crosstalk with other stress pathways. Similarly, the section on organelle interaction networks is brief and does not adequately explain how metal dyshomeostasis specifically disrupts these contacts or how this contributes to neurodegeneration.
- **Why it matters** The stated goal of the review is to understand the "intricate molecular mechanisms." A superficial treatment of these mechanisms fails to meet this goal and limits the review's utility for researchers seeking a deep understanding of the biology. It also makes it harder to identify specific, testable hypotheses for future research.
- **Resolution test** The authors should expand the mechanistic sections to provide more detail on the specific molecular events, signaling cascades, and pathways involved. They should also discuss the evidence for these mechanisms, including any contradictory findings. For example, in the section on organelle interaction networks, they could discuss specific contact sites, the proteins involved, and how metal ions affect their formation or function.

- **Resolution test** The authors should revise the AI section to explicitly connect it to the challenges and opportunities discussed elsewhere in the review. For example, they could discuss how AI-based analysis of neuroimaging data could address the need for biomarkers of brain metal levels, or how AI-driven drug discovery could help develop more selective metal modulators.
