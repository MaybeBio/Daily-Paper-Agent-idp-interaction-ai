## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results and discussion, conclusion, acknowledgements) as provided. Figures, tables, and Supporting Information referenced but not supplied.
- **Assessment boundary** Assessment is limited to the claims and evidence presented in the provided text. Claims referring to figures, tables, equations, and Supporting Information are evaluated based on their textual description only, as these materials were not included in the submission package.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors claim that microbial transglutaminase (mTGase) can install sparse, intramolecular Gln-Lys isopeptide cross-links in the intrinsically disordered protein β-casein (using β-casein-rich sodium caseinate as a surrogate), causing reproducible single-chain collapse into a compact nanoparticle (βSCNP) without inducing secondary structure. They further claim that this collapsed state possesses a defined internal topology comprising a hydrophobic core and flexible loops, that this topology can be probed by Nile red fluorescence and SEC-SAXS, and that the experimental observations are consistent with molecular dynamics simulations implementing the experimentally identified cross-links.
- **Visible evidence base** Textual descriptions of results from SEC-D5, circular dichroism, Nile red fluorescence, SEC-SAXS (including Guinier, P(r), DAMMIF, and form factor analysis), XL-MS, and all-atom MD simulations. Numerical values for Mw, Rη,w, [η]w, Rg, and ν are reported in the text. All figures, tables, equations, and Supporting Information are referenced but not visible.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures (Figures 1-4), all tables (Tables 1, S3, S4, S6, S7), all equations (Equations S9-S13), and the entire Supporting Information (including experimental methods, cross-link validation criteria, and additional data) are not provided. This substantially limits the ability to verify the described results, assess data quality, and evaluate the statistical and methodological rigor of the analyses.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript presents a conceptually interesting and potentially significant approach to generating topologically defined single-chain nanoparticles from an intrinsically disordered protein using enzymatic cross-linking. The combination of experimental characterization (SEC-D5, SEC-SAXS, XL-MS) with MD simulation is a strong methodological strategy. However, the core claims of "topology encoding" and "reproducible cavity architecture" rest heavily on data presented in figures and Supporting Information that are not available for review. The distinction between a compact, collapsed random coil and a topologically defined structure with persistent cavities is subtle, and the evidence as described in the text, while suggestive, is not presented with sufficient detail to be fully convincing. The manuscript would benefit from a clearer articulation of what constitutes "topology" in this context and how the presented data uniquely demonstrate it beyond generic compaction. The work is likely of interest to the SCNP and IDP communities, but the current evidence base is insufficient to fully establish the strongest claims.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in polymer chemistry, particularly those working on single-chain nanoparticles, will be interested in the demonstration of an enzymatic, aqueous route to SCNP formation from a biopolymer. The IDP community will be interested in the concept of using sparse covalent constraints to reshape conformational ensembles and create defined, albeit dynamic, structural features. The use of orthogonal biophysical methods (SEC-SAXS, XL-MS) to characterize a disordered, cross-linked system provides a methodological template of broad interest. The potential for extending this to other IDPs with high Gln/Lys content could attract interest from protein engineering and biomaterials fields.
- **Major strengths** 1) The conceptual framing of enzymatic "constraint installation" as a means to encode topology in a disordered chain is novel and well-articulated. 2) The use of a multi-technique orthogonal approach (SEC-D5, CD, XL-MS, SEC-SAXS, MD) is rigorous and appropriate for the complex system under study. 3) The demonstration that molar mass remains constant while hydrodynamic size decreases is a critical and well-presented control for intramolecular collapse. 4) The inclusion of MD simulations to test the sufficiency of the identified cross-links adds a valuable computational dimension. 5) The use of a guest molecule (Nile red) as a probe for internal cavity structure is a clever and potentially powerful approach.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency for central claim
    - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that the resulting βSCNP possesses a "reproducible cavity architecture" and a "topology combining a stable, compact, hydrophobic core with flexible, disordered loops."
    - **Evidence pointer** Figures 2B-E, Table 1, and associated text in the Results and Discussion section. Location not provided for specific data points within these items.
    - **Concern** The central claim of a defined, reproducible internal topology (core and loops) is a strong structural statement. The evidence described in the text relies on SEC-SAXS data (Kratky plots, P(r) functions, ab initio reconstructions) and NR fluorescence. While the text describes these data as consistent with the proposed topology, the actual figures are not available for review. The interpretation of a bimodal P(r) as evidence for "partially shielded compartments" is plausible but requires careful scrutiny of the data quality and the uniqueness of the fit. Ab initio reconstructions of flexible, disordered systems are notoriously prone to over-interpretation and can represent ensemble averages that do not reflect a single dominant topology. The claim of "reproducible" architecture is particularly strong and requires evidence of reproducibility across multiple independent preparations and measurements, which is not detailed in the text provided.
    - **Why it matters** If the internal topology is not demonstrably reproducible and distinct from a simple, featureless collapsed globule, the central advance of the paper is significantly weakened. The paper's novelty hinges on moving beyond "compaction" to "topology encoding." The current evidence, as described, may not be sufficient to support this distinction without the full SAXS analysis and supporting data.
    - **Resolution test** The authors must provide the actual SEC-SAXS data (scattering curves, Kratky plots, P(r) functions) and the ab initio reconstructions for βNaCn, βSCNP, and βSCNP-NR. They must demonstrate that the differences between the samples are statistically significant and reproducible. They should also provide a more quantitative analysis, such as fitting the data to an ensemble of models (e.g., EOM) to show that the constrained ensemble is significantly more compact and has a different distribution of shapes than the unconstrained ensemble. The reproducibility of the NR fluorescence enhancement and the SAXS-derived parameters across multiple independent experiments should be explicitly stated.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency for cross-link localization and specificity
    - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that XL-MS reveals "reproducible long-range Gln-Lys isopeptide hotspots" and that all 9 identified cross-links are intramolecular.
    - **Evidence pointer** Figure 3B-C, Figures S14-S15, Table S6, and associated text in the Results and Discussion section. Location not provided for specific data points within these items.
    - **Concern** The identification of specific, reproducible cross-links is crucial for the "encoding" aspect of the claim. The text states that 9 unique intramolecular cross-links were identified with a 1% FDR, but the actual list of cross-linked residues, their localization on the protein sequence, and the criteria for reproducibility are not provided. The claim that these are "hotspots" implies a non-random distribution, which needs to be demonstrated statistically. Furthermore, the text mentions a representative cross-link (K44-Q197) but does not provide the MS/MS spectrum or the validation metrics for this specific identification. The distinction between a few specific cross-links and a more heterogeneous mixture is critical. If the cross-linking is not specific, the resulting structure would be a statistical ensemble of many different topologies, not a "reproducible" one.
    - **Why it matters** The specificity and reproducibility of the cross-links are the molecular basis for the claimed "topology encoding." If the cross-links are not specific, the claim of a defined topology is not supported. The paper needs to demonstrate that the same set of cross-links is formed in multiple independent experiments and that this set is sufficient to define the observed compact state.
    - **Resolution test** The authors must provide the full list of 9 cross-linked residue pairs, their sequence context, and the number of times each was observed across replicates. They should provide a statistical analysis showing that the observed cross-links are enriched compared to what would be expected by chance, given the Gln/Lys distribution. The MS/MS spectrum for the representative cross-link (K44-Q197) and others should be shown with annotated fragment ions. The criteria for considering a cross-link as "reproducible" should be explicitly defined.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** No
    - **Axis** Interpretation of MD simulation results
    - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that MD simulations with implemented cross-links support the conclusion that a sparse subset of approximately 3 to 4 long-range isopeptide bonds is sufficient to account for the experimentally observed compact state.
    - **Evidence pointer** Figure 4A-D, Figure S17, Table S7, and associated text in the Results and Discussion section. Location not provided for specific data points within these items.
    - **Concern** The text describes a "qualitative comparison" of MD-derived contraction with experimental g and g' values. The claim that the simulations "support" the experimental findings is vague. The details of the simulation protocol (force field, water model, simulation length, number of replicas, how the cross-links were implemented) are not provided. The text states that the triple-constraint model reaches an Rg of 3.0 nm, which matches the experimental value, but it is unclear if this is a unique solution or if other combinations of cross-links could also produce a similar Rg. The "figure-eight-like" topology is a qualitative description from a simulation trajectory and needs to be supported by quantitative analysis of the ensemble (e.g., contact maps, radius of gyration of sub-domains).
    - **Why it matters** The MD simulations are presented as a key piece of orthogonal evidence. If the simulations are not robust or the analysis is not quantitative, their support for the experimental conclusions is weakened. The claim that 3-4 cross-links are "sufficient" is a strong statement that requires a more systematic analysis of the relationship between the number and position of cross-links and the resulting conformational ensemble.
    - **Resolution test** The authors must provide full details of the simulation setup and analysis. They should show that the simulations are converged and that the results are robust to the choice of force field and simulation length. They should provide a quantitative comparison of the simulated ensembles (e.g., Rg distributions, shape distributions) with the experimental SAXS data, rather than just a qualitative comparison of average values. A systematic study varying the number and position of cross-links would strengthen the claim of sufficiency.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **Concern ID** R1-m1
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Clarity of terminology
    - **Affected element** Introduction, definition of "constraint installation"
    - **Evidence pointer** Introduction section, paragraph 2
    - **Issue** The term "constraint installation" is defined in a somewhat circular manner. The text states that "the exact positions of cross-links are not pre-programmed, the experimentally identified long-range constraint hotspots are reproducible." This clarifies what it is not, but the positive definition is less clear. Is it simply the process of forming cross-links, or does it imply a specific design strategy?
    - **Required correction** Please provide a more direct and positive definition of "constraint installation" as a design concept. For example, "we define constraint installation as the process of introducing a sparse set of covalent linkages at sequence positions that are identified post-hoc as 'hotspots' and are sufficient to bias the conformational ensemble towards a more compact state."
    - **Concern ID** R1-m2
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Data presentation
    - **Affected element** Results and Discussion, SEC-D5 data
    - **Evidence pointer** Figure 1B-D, Table S3
    - **Issue** The text reports a 25% reduction in Rη,w and a 56% reduction in [η]w. While these are presented as indicators of compaction, the text does not discuss the potential influence of the denaturing conditions (high urea concentration) on the accuracy of these measurements, beyond a general statement about reduced contrast. The authors acknowledge the conditions are "cumbersome" but do not provide an error analysis or discuss how the uncertainty in these measurements affects the calculated contraction factors.
    - **Required correction** Please include an estimate of the uncertainty in the reported Rη,w and [η]w values and discuss how this uncertainty propagates to the calculated contraction factors g and g'. This will strengthen the quantitative claims of compaction.
    - **Concern ID** R1-m3
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Generalizability of claims
    - **Affected element** Results and Discussion, final paragraph
    - **Evidence pointer** Results and Discussion section, paragraph 6
    - **Issue** The statement that "other intrinsically disordered proteins with comparable Gln/Lys content may be similarly programmable" is a reasonable hypothesis but is presented as a direct implication of the data. The supporting data for other casein fractions (cNaCn, ακNaCn) are only mentioned in the text and are in the Supporting Information. The generalizability to other IDPs is speculative.
    - **Required correction** Please soften this claim to be more clearly a hypothesis or future direction. For example, "This suggests the potential for other intrinsically disordered proteins with comparable Gln/Lys content to be similarly programmable, a hypothesis that warrants further investigation."
    - **Concern ID** R1-m4
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Methodological detail
    - **Affected element** Results and Discussion, NR fluorescence
    - **Evidence pointer** Figure 2A
    - **Issue** The text states that the strongest NR response is obtained when cross-linking is performed in the presence of NR (βSCNP-NR). This is an interesting observation, but the text does not explain the potential mechanism. Is it due to NR being trapped inside the forming cavity? Or does NR binding alter the cross-linking pattern?
    - **Required correction** Please add a brief discussion of the possible mechanisms for the enhanced NR response in βSCNP-NR, even if speculative. This would strengthen the interpretation of NR as a topology probe.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of a "reproducible cavity architecture" is not fully supported by the evidence described in the text, as the key SAXS data and reproducibility analyses are not visible.
- The claim that the identified cross-links are "reproducible long-range hotspots" is not supported without the full list of cross-links and a statistical analysis of their reproducibility and specificity.
- The claim that the MD simulations "support" the experimental findings is based on a "qualitative comparison" and is not fully supported without detailed simulation and analysis protocols.
- The claim that NR acts as a topology probe demonstrating "partially shielded compartments" is an interpretation that requires the actual SAXS data to be fully evaluated.
- The generalizability claim to other IDPs is a hypothesis, not a direct finding of this study.
- The statement that the contraction factors are "consistent with synthetic SCNPs under theta-like solvent conditions" is a comparison that cannot be evaluated without the referenced data.
