## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, data availability, funding, acknowledgments). Figures and supplementary information referenced but not provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Assessment is limited to the scientific content, methodological rigor, and internal consistency of the provided text. Claims referring to figures, tables, or supplementary data are evaluated based on textual description only.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use large-scale lattice Monte Carlo simulations with Binder cumulant analysis and finite-size scaling to map the full binodal of the prion-like low complexity domain A1-LCD. They identify three regimes along the binodal defined by intersections with the overlap and percolation lines, characterize cluster-size distributions in dilute phases, and argue that common scaling-based estimates of the theta temperature are unreliable for this system.
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of simulation protocols, Binder cumulant methodology, finite-size scaling, and results referencing Figures 1 through 15 and supplementary figures S3, S5, S6, S7, S11. This prevents independent verification of key quantitative claims including the critical temperature, critical volume fraction, theta temperature estimates, ERMSL values, and cluster-size distribution exponents.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses an important methodological gap in the study of phase separation of intrinsically disordered proteins, namely the accurate determination of critical points and the distinction between near-critical and off-critical regimes. The use of Binder cumulants and finite-size scaling in large-scale lattice simulations is appropriate and represents a meaningful advance over common practice. The central finding that the overlap and percolation lines intersect the dilute arm of the binodal, creating three distinct regimes, is conceptually interesting and potentially impactful. However, the manuscript as presented has several significant weaknesses. The absence of all figures and numerical data makes the quantitative claims impossible to verify. The methodological description of the Binder cumulant analysis is incomplete in key aspects, particularly regarding the definition of the order parameter and the handling of finite-size effects. The comparison between the 200-chain and 10,000-chain systems is described but the quantitative differences are not fully contextualized. The claim that scaling-based theta temperature estimates are erroneous is important but the analysis appears to conflate different definitions of the theta point. The discussion of experimental implications is speculative and lacks concrete predictions. Overall, the core idea is sound and the work could be suitable for publication after major revisions that address the concerns below.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying phase separation of intrinsically disordered proteins, particularly those using coarse-grained simulations to map phase diagrams. The methodological framework for accurate critical point determination will be of interest to computational biophysicists. The distinction between near-critical and off-critical regimes has implications for experimental studies of biomolecular condensates, especially those using microscopy or scattering to characterize condensate properties. The cautionary note about theta temperature estimation from scaling analysis is relevant to the broader IDP community.
- **Major strengths** 1. The application of rigorous statistical mechanics tools (Binder cumulants, finite-size scaling) to a biologically relevant polymer model is timely and addresses a real gap in the field. 2. The identification of three distinct regimes along the binodal, defined by intersections with overlap and percolation lines, provides a clear conceptual framework for interpreting simulation and experimental data. 3. The scale of the simulations (10^4 chains, 6x10^11 MC steps) is impressive and likely necessary for accessing near-critical behavior. 4. The demonstration that common scaling-based theta temperature estimates are unreliable for this system is an important cautionary result. 5. The proposed protocol for mapping critical regions is practical and could be adopted by other groups.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence availability
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript reports specific numerical values for the critical temperature, critical volume fraction, theta temperature, ERMSL values, and cluster-size distribution exponents.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figures 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15 and supplementary figures S3, S5, S6, S7, S11. Location not provided for numerical values in text.
  - **Concern** None of the figures or supplementary data are available for inspection. The quantitative claims of the manuscript, including the central result that the critical temperature is approximately 332 K and the theta temperature is approximately 362 K, cannot be verified. The ERMSL values used to compare fitting methods are presented as text but the underlying fits are not shown. The cluster-size distribution exponents are reported but the distributions themselves are not visible.
  - **Why it matters** The entire scientific case rests on these quantitative results. Without access to the figures and data, it is impossible to assess whether the conclusions follow from the evidence. This is a blocking issue.
  - **Resolution test** All figures and supplementary data must be provided for review. The numerical values quoted in the text must be traceable to specific data points in the figures.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodological completeness
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that Binder cumulants are computed using sub-boxes of different lengths, and that the intersection of cumulant curves for different sub-box sizes yields the critical temperature.
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section, subsection "Protocol for accurately mapping the critical regime and estimating critical parameters"; Results section, subsection "Analysis of Binder cumulants to estimate Tc".
  - **Concern** The description of the Binder cumulant analysis is incomplete. The order parameter is stated to be the density difference between phases, but the precise definition of the cumulant (e.g., U4 = 1 - <m^4>/(3<m^2>^2)) is given only in passing. Critically, the manuscript does not describe how the order parameter is computed in a sub-box that may contain an interface, how the sub-box sampling is performed (e.g., whether the sub-box is placed randomly or centered on the interface), or how many independent samples are used for each cumulant estimate. The choice of sub-box sizes and the range of sizes used in the finite-size scaling analysis are not stated. The manuscript also does not discuss potential corrections to scaling or the expected leading finite-size scaling correction for this model.
  - **Why it matters** The Binder cumulant method is the cornerstone of the critical point determination. If the implementation is flawed or the analysis is not properly described, the central result is compromised. The current description is insufficient for a reader to reproduce the analysis or to assess its validity.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a complete description of the Binder cumulant computation, including the order parameter definition, sub-box sampling procedure, number of samples, range of sub-box sizes, and the finite-size scaling analysis. Justify the choice of sub-box sizes and discuss potential corrections to scaling.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Claim substantiation
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that "scaling approaches based on assumptions from two-parameter theories for homopolymers yield erroneous estimates of the theta temperature of an archetypal PLCD" and that the theta temperature estimated from scaling analysis is approximately 269 K, well below the critical temperature of 332 K.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section, subsection "Estimating the theta temperature"; Discussion section.
  - **Concern** The claim that scaling-based theta estimates are erroneous is presented as a central finding, but the analysis appears to conflate different concepts. The theta temperature is defined in the manuscript as the temperature where the two-body interaction coefficient B2 equals zero, and the authors compute this directly. The scaling analysis, on the other hand, identifies the temperature where the chain behaves as a Gaussian chain, which is a different operational definition. The manuscript does not adequately discuss whether these two definitions should coincide for a finite-length chain with specific interactions, nor does it address the possibility that the scaling analysis is sensitive to the range of segment lengths used. The claim that the scaling estimate is "erroneous" is strong and requires a more nuanced discussion of what each method measures and why they differ.
  - **Why it matters** The theta temperature is a fundamental parameter for characterizing polymer solutions. If the manuscript claims that common estimation methods are wrong, this needs to be supported by a careful analysis that rules out alternative explanations, such as finite-size effects, the choice of segment length range, or the specific definition of the theta point.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a more detailed analysis of the scaling behavior, including the range of segment lengths used, the sensitivity of the extracted exponent to this range, and a discussion of why the Gaussian chain criterion does not coincide with the B2 = 0 criterion for this system. Consider whether the discrepancy is specific to A1-LCD or general for associating polymers.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Generalizability
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "the findings reported here are likely to be consistent across different simulation protocols that use similar, single-bead-per-residue models" and that the proposed protocol "provides a prescription for analyzing results from simulations comprising O(10^2) molecules."
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section.
  - **Concern** The claim of generalizability is based on a single comparison to a previous study that showed structural similarities across three simulation engines. This is insufficient to support the strong claim that the findings will hold across different models. The proposed prescription for analyzing O(10^2) molecule simulations relies on fitting to a modified Flory-Huggins model, but the manuscript does not demonstrate that this fitting procedure reliably recovers the true critical point when the simulation cannot access the critical regime. The validation of this prescription appears to be limited to the five A1-LCD variants mentioned, and the details of this validation are not provided.
  - **Why it matters** The practical value of this work depends on whether the proposed methods can be applied by others. If the prescription for small simulations is not properly validated, it could lead to incorrect conclusions when applied by other groups.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a more thorough validation of the modified Flory-Huggins fitting procedure, including tests on systems with known critical points and a discussion of the limitations of this approach. Soften the claim of generalizability or provide additional evidence from different models.
