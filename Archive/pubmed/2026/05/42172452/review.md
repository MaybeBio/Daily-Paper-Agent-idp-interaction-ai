## Review setup

- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that the RNAPII assembly factor Rba50 has a modular organization comprising a structured C-terminal module with assembly-associated activity and an intrinsically disordered N-terminal module acting as a multivalent interaction hub. They report a direct interaction between Npa3 and residues 1-89 of Rba50, functional rescue of specific rba50-3 phenotypes by the C-terminal region, a requirement for determinants outside the C-terminal region for robust function, and a CRM1-dependent leucine-rich nuclear export signal within residues 239-249.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental details, statistical measures, or data displays provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, methods descriptions, statistical analyses, strain and plasmid details, and any supplementary data

## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a plausible and potentially interesting modular model for Rba50 function, with separable C-terminal and N-terminal activities. However, the evidence base available for review is limited to the abstract, which precludes verification of the central claims. The interaction mapping, functional rescue data, and nuclear export signal identification are stated without quantitative or methodological detail. The conclusions are coherent but their robustness cannot be assessed from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying RNA polymerase II assembly and biogenesis, transcription machinery homeostasis, and the roles of intrinsically disordered regions in protein interaction networks. The work may also interest those investigating nucleocytoplasmic trafficking of assembly factors and the functional diversification of conserved eukaryotic proteins.
- **Major strengths** The proposed modular architecture provides a conceptually clear framework for understanding how a single assembly factor can participate in multiple stages of polymerase biogenesis. The identification of a specific Npa3 interaction region and a nuclear export signal offers concrete molecular handles for further investigation. The combination of structural prediction, interaction assays, and functional genetics is appropriate for the stated aims.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract does not provide sufficient detail to evaluate the strength of the interaction data, the specificity of the functional rescue, or the evidence for the nuclear export signal. The claim that the N-terminal module functions as a multivalent interaction hub is not supported by specific interaction partners beyond Npa3. The relationship between the modular activities and the in vivo assembly process remains correlative rather than mechanistic.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract would benefit from stating the number of interaction partners identified for the N-terminal module. The phrase "permissive plasmid-shuffle conditions" is used without explanation of the experimental logic. The relevance of the nuclear export signal to RNAPII assembly, as opposed to general protein localization, is not clarified.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None can be identified from the abstract alone, but the absence of methodological and statistical detail means that all central claims must be treated as unverified.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: the modular view of Rba50 is a reasonable advance but not a conceptual breakthrough. Scientific importance: moderate, as it refines understanding of a specific assembly factor rather than revealing a new principle. Interdisciplinary readership: limited, as the topic is specialized to transcription and protein biogenesis. Technical soundness: cannot be evaluated from the abstract. Readability for nonspecialists: the abstract is clear but assumes familiarity with RNAPII assembly factors and yeast genetic terminology.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract is promising, but the claims require full experimental documentation to be assessed. A supportive posture would be contingent on verification of the interaction data, functional rescue specificity, and nuclear export evidence in the full manuscript.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** "We map a direct interaction between the assembly factor Npa3 and the first N-terminal disordered segment, residues 1-89"
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states a direct interaction but provides no information on the assay used, the affinity or specificity of the interaction, or whether the interaction was validated in the context of the full-length protein versus the isolated segment.
- **Why it matters** The interaction with Npa3 is a central piece of evidence for the proposed modular interaction hub. Without details on the assay and controls, the claim cannot be distinguished from a low-specificity or artefactual binding event.
- **Resolution test** In the full manuscript, the authors should provide the interaction assay data, including appropriate positive and negative controls, quantitative measures if available, and evidence that the interaction occurs in a cellular context relevant to RNAPII assembly.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** "Functional assays indicate that the C-terminal region rescues specific rba50-3 phenotypes and supports proliferative recovery under permissive plasmid-shuffle conditions, whereas robust Rba50 function also requires determinants outside the C-terminal region"
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract reports functional rescue but does not specify which phenotypes were rescued, the magnitude of rescue, the statistical significance, or the nature of the determinants outside the C-terminal region that are required for robust function.
- **Why it matters** The functional data are the primary evidence for the separable activities of the two modules. Without details on the phenotypes, the rescue efficiency, and the identity of the additional determinants, the modular model is not adequately supported.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should include growth curves or plating assays with quantitative comparisons, statistical analysis, and a clear description of the rba50-3 phenotypes and the constructs used. The determinants outside the C-terminal region should be identified or at least mapped to a specific region.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** "We further identify a CRM1-dependent leucine-rich nuclear export signal within residues 239-249 that limits nuclear accumulation of Rba50 at steady state"
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract claims identification of a nuclear export signal but provides no data on the mutagenesis strategy, the effect of CRM1 inhibition or mutation, or the quantitative changes in nuclear accumulation.
- **Why it matters** The nuclear export signal is presented as a distinct functional element, but its relevance to RNAPII assembly is unclear. The claim requires evidence that the signal is functional, CRM1-dependent, and that its disruption affects Rba50 localization in a meaningful way.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should include localization data with and without CRM1 perturbation, mutagenesis of the putative signal, and quantitative measures of nuclear versus cytoplasmic distribution.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- The claim that the N-terminal module functions as a "multivalent interaction hub" is unsupported beyond the single Npa3 interaction.
- The claim that the C-terminal region "provides RNAPII assembly-associated activity" is not substantiated with specific mechanistic evidence.
- The claim that "robust Rba50 function also requires determinants outside the C-terminal region" is vague and does not identify the determinants.
- The functional significance of the nuclear export signal for RNAPII biogenesis is not established.
- All quantitative claims, including rescue efficiency and localization changes, are unverifiable from the abstract.
