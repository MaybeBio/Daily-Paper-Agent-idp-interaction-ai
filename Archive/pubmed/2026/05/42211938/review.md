## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (mini review), including abstract, main text, tables, and figure captions
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, mechanistic coherence, literature support, and internal consistency of the presented framework
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript proposes a unified hierarchical mechanism for protein–polysaccharide hybrid gel formation, spanning molecular interactions (electrostatics, charge patchiness, counterion release, hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic contacts, van der Waals forces, covalent crosslinking), LLPS as a mesoscale bridge, cluster aggregation (DLCA vs RLCA), and percolation into macroscopic networks, with environmental and formulation parameters modulating each stage.
- **Visible evidence base** Four tables (molecular interactions, LLPS control parameters, DLCA vs RLCA characteristics, application requirements), three figure captions (LLPS pathways, fractal network formation, multiscale framework), and in-text citations to prior literature
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures themselves (only captions provided), full reference list with complete bibliographic details, any supplementary materials, and explicit quantitative data supporting the claims in Tables 3 and 4

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This mini review offers a coherent and well-structured conceptual framework for understanding protein–polysaccharide hybrid gel formation. The hierarchical progression from molecular interactions through LLPS to percolated networks is logically presented and provides a useful organizing principle for a complex and fragmented literature. However, the manuscript is largely descriptive and relies heavily on qualitative statements and representative citations without critical evaluation of conflicting evidence or quantitative synthesis. The claimed novelty of a "unified mechanistic perspective" is not fully substantiated, as the framework is presented as an assembly of established concepts rather than a genuinely integrative or predictive model. The absence of figures and incomplete reference details limits full assessment. The manuscript would benefit from clearer articulation of what is genuinely new, more critical engagement with the evidence base, and explicit acknowledgment of the limits of current understanding.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in food science, particularly those working on hydrocolloid gelation, food texture design, and plant-based product development, would find this review useful as a conceptual map of the field. Scientists in soft matter physics, biophysics, and biomaterials engineering interested in phase separation and gelation phenomena may also find the LLPS-focused perspective relevant. The practical framing around applications in drug delivery and tissue engineering could attract readers from pharmaceutical and biomedical engineering. However, the depth of treatment may be insufficient for specialists in any single discipline.

- **Major strengths** The manuscript provides a clear, logical progression from molecular to macroscopic scales, which is genuinely helpful for organizing a complex literature. The explicit connection between LLPS and gelation is timely and relevant given the growing interest in phase separation in soft matter. The tables summarizing molecular interactions, LLPS control parameters, and DLCA vs RLCA characteristics are useful reference tools. The emphasis on pathway-dependent design, rather than purely compositional design, is a valuable perspective. The writing is generally clear and accessible to nonspecialists.

- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Scientific claims vs. evidence
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims to present "a unified mechanistic perspective" and "a predictive, pathway-aware perspective" that advances "beyond prior literature"
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract; Introduction; Section "Toward a predictive framework and future perspectives"
  - **Concern** The central claim of providing a unified and predictive framework is not supported by the content. The manuscript assembles known concepts (electrostatics, LLPS, DLCA/RLCA, percolation) into a sequential narrative but does not provide a genuinely integrative model, quantitative relationships, or testable predictions. The "framework" is presented as a conceptual diagram (Figure 3) but no mathematical formulation, computational implementation, or validation against experimental data is offered. The claim of advancing "beyond prior literature" is asserted without a systematic comparison to existing reviews or identification of specific novel contributions.
  - **Why it matters** The stated novelty and contribution of the review rest on this claim. If the framework is not genuinely predictive or unified, the manuscript reduces to a descriptive summary of known concepts, which may still be useful but does not deliver what is promised. Readers seeking guidance for rational design would not find operational tools or validated relationships.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) provide a concrete example of how the framework generates a prediction that is not obvious from existing knowledge, ideally with quantitative relationships linking molecular parameters to gel properties, or (b) substantially temper the claims to accurately describe the contribution as a conceptual synthesis rather than a predictive framework.

  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Evidence quality and critical evaluation
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "extensive studies" exist on macroscopic properties but molecular mechanisms "remain incompletely understood," and presents the framework as the synthesis of this understanding
  - **Evidence pointer** Throughout; Tables 1–4; in-text citations
  - **Concern** The review is largely uncritical in its use of literature. Tables 1–4 present values and characteristics (e.g., fractal dimensions, mechanical ranges, pore sizes) as established facts without discussing measurement variability, methodological differences, or conflicting results across studies. For example, Table 3 lists specific fractal dimension ranges for DLCA and RLCA without acknowledging that these values depend on system chemistry, concentration, and measurement technique. Table 4 presents mechanical ranges across different parameters (storage modulus, Young's modulus, compressive modulus, shear modulus) in a single column, which is potentially misleading without clear parameter specification for each entry. The manuscript does not identify controversies, unresolved questions, or alternative interpretations in the literature.
  - **Why it matters** A review that synthesizes literature should critically evaluate the quality and consistency of the evidence. Presenting representative values without context or caveats can mislead readers, particularly nonspecialists, into treating approximate or system-specific values as universal constants. This undermines the reliability of the framework as a reference.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should add explicit caveats about the variability and context-dependence of reported values, discuss at least one area of active debate or conflicting evidence in the field, and clarify in Table 4 which mechanical parameter corresponds to each entry.

  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Completeness and balance
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims to cover "molecular interactions, mesoscale phase behavior, and macroscopic network formation" and discusses "environmental and formulation factors" as key modulators
  - **Evidence pointer** Sections "Molecular interactions governing early complexation"; "Liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS) as a mechanistic bridge"; "From droplets to percolated gel networks"; "Environmental and formulation controls over gelation"
  - **Concern** The treatment of environmental and formulation factors is uneven. pH and ionic strength receive substantial attention, but temperature, shear, and protein–polysaccharide ratio are discussed only briefly and largely qualitatively. The manuscript does not address kinetic arrest, glassy dynamics, or non-equilibrium effects that are known to be important in biopolymer gelation. The role of polysaccharide type (e.g., charged vs neutral, rigid vs flexible) is not systematically discussed, despite being a primary determinant of interaction mechanism. The section on applications (Table 4) is superficial, with no discussion of how specific formulation parameters translate to specific application outcomes.
  - **Why it matters** A "unified" perspective should provide balanced coverage of the key variables that control gel formation. The current treatment is weighted toward electrostatic interactions and LLPS, with other factors treated as secondary. This limits the practical utility of the framework for researchers working with diverse polysaccharide types or processing conditions.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should expand the discussion of temperature, shear, and polysaccharide structural diversity, and provide a more systematic treatment of how each environmental factor affects each stage of the assembly hierarchy.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of providing a "unified mechanistic perspective" and "predictive, pathway-aware perspective" is not supported by the content, which is descriptive and conceptual rather than quantitative or predictive
- The assertion that the framework advances "beyond prior literature" is not substantiated by a systematic comparison with existing reviews
- Specific values in Tables 3 and 4 (fractal dimensions, mechanical ranges, pore sizes) are presented without adequate context regarding measurement conditions, variability, or parameter type
- The claim that "environmental and formulation factors strongly influence each stage of assembly" is asserted but not systematically demonstrated across all factors and stages
- Statements about the role of charge patchiness in directing binding geometry are presented as established without discussion of the evidence base or limitations
- The distinction between LLPS droplets, aggregated clusters, and percolated networks is presented as a clear hierarchy, but the manuscript does not address cases where these categories may overlap or be difficult to distinguish experimentally
