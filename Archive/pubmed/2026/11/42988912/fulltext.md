RNA-dependent RNA polymerase 6 (RDR6) and suppressor of gene silencing 3 (SGS3) form small interfering RNA (siRNA) bodies to synthesize double-stranded RNA, triggering RNA interference. Like viral RDRs, RDR6 not only has polymerase action but also has terminal nucleotidyltransferase activity that tails single-stranded RNA templates, causing their degradation. How the dual activities are controlled is unknown. Here we discovered that Arabidopsis Tousled (TSL) predominantly phosphorylates Ser65 and Ser68, located in the intrinsically disordered region of SGS3, to promote RNA interference. SGS3 phosphomimics trigger the formation of liquid-like siRNA bodies but have lower RNA-binding affinity, thereby enhancing RDR6 polymerase activity to synthesize double-stranded RNA for downstream processing by Dicer-like 2/4. Conversely, hypo-phosphorylated SGS3 forms larger and solid-like aggregates that hold RNA templates with stronger affinity, thereby prompting RDR6 terminal nucleotidyltransferase activity. Moreover, viral infection drives SGS3 hypo-phosphorylation and aggregation, suppressing viral siRNA production and host defence. Collectively, stoichiometric phosphorylation of SGS3 switches two distinct activities of RDR6 to tune RNA silencing and host anti-viral immunity.
