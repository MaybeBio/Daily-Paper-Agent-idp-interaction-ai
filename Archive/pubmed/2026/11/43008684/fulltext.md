HYPOTHESIS: The partitioning of biomolecules in aqueous two-phase systems (ATPS), a liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) process that biomimics cellular compartmentalization, is governed by quantifiable protein biophysical properties. Therefore, the partitioning behavior of uncharacterized biomolecular variants can be predicted mechanistically rather than optimized empirically. EXPERIMENTS: Using SARS-CoV-2 spike protein variants (wild type, Beta, Omicron, and the Omicron receptor-binding domain) as a model system, we combined process-intensified LLPS in an evaporating sessile droplets, tie-line-resolved phase diagram analysis, microfluidic picoliter-droplet partitioning assays, and polymer-engineered ATPS in which polyethylene glycol (PEG) was substituted with the more hydrophobic PEG-PPG-PEG triblock copolymer. AlphaFold3-based structural analysis, Kyte-Doolittle hydrophobicity and isoelectric point calculations were integrated with multivariate linear regression to link partition coefficients to protein properties. A Collander-type inter-system correlation was established to test transferability across ATPS formulations. FINDINGS: All spike protein variants preferentially partitioned into the dextran-rich phase, with enrichment amplified by longer tie-lines and further enhanced by the more hydrophobic PEG-PPG-PEG system, confirming hydrophobic interactions as the dominant partitioning mechanism. The Omicron variant consistently exhibited the weakest dextran-phase enrichment. Partition coefficients correlated strongly with hydrophobicity and isoelectric point, and obeyed a Collander-type relationship across polymer chemistries, enabling prediction of variant partitioning from a single reference measurement. Integrated upstream of an enzyme-linked oligonucleotide assay (ELONA), the optimized ATPS pre-concentration module enhanced detection sensitivity by 11.7-fold. This work establishes a generalizable, property-driven framework for the rational design of ATPS bioseparations and rapid adaptation to emerging pathogens.
