Glioblastoma (GBM) is one of the most lethal human malignancies, with a five-year survival rate of approximately 5% and limited effective therapeutic options beyond standard-of-care regimens, underscoring an urgent need for novel therapeutic agents and druggable targets. Here, we report that PM534, a marine sponge-derived synthetic compound, potently suppresses GBM malignancy in orthotopic patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models and in vitro, with IC(50) value of 1.86 nM in patient-derived GBM#P3 cells. Target identification via activity-based protein profiling (ABPP) revealed P23, a co-chaperone of HSP90, as the direct molecular target of PM534. Subsequent LC-MS/MS analysis, molecular dynamics simulation, and site-directed mutagenesis collectively demonstrated that PM534 covalently bond P23 at Cys58 and Cys75. P23 demonstrated significant upregulation in human GBM tissues and orthotopic PDX models, correlating with tumor progression. Mechanistic studies exhibited that P23 interacted with splicing factor proline- and glutamine-rich protein (SFPQ) to regulate liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS). Covalent engagement of P23 by PM534 disrupted the interaction of SFPQ with the HSP90/P23 complex, thereby triggering proteasomal degradation of SFPQ and consequently abrogating SFPQ-driven LLPS and oncogenic condensate formation. These findings establish PM534 as a promising drug candidate for clinical GBM therapy and highlight P23 as a therapeutic target in GBM.
