Biomolecular condensates, formed via phase separation, play vital roles in cellular organization, yet their nucleation and growth dynamics remain poorly understood, particularly under controlled physiological conditions. Here, we employ a microfluidic platform to precisely manipulate the nucleation and growth of PGL-3 protein condensates at fluid-solid interfaces. We demonstrate that ionic strength and co-phase-separating proteins (SEPA-1) robustly tune the nucleation rate without altering the long-term growth scaling  approximately tn. Single-condensate tracking further resolves a dynamic scaling crossover from rapid isolated growth with n  approximately  1/2 to diffusion-limited collective growth with n  approximately  1/3, consistent with simulations of overlapping protein-depletion fields. Furthermore, we show that this classical scaling breaks down during viscoelastic phase separation, where elastic-dominated condensates exhibit a significantly reduced exponent. Our work establishes molecular modulation and interfacial mechanics as key regulators of condensate dynamics, revealing physical principles that govern the size and material state of biological condensates.
