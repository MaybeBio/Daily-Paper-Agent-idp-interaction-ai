## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full manuscript, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report the rational design of a 65-mer RNA, U1'+TERRA, combining a stem-loop-GGU motif and a G-quadruplex structure to target distinct domains of FUS. They claim strong binding affinity, effective inhibition of FUS aggregation in vitro, and enhanced biological stability via 2'-O-methyl modification. They further claim that the modified RNA suppresses FUS aggregation at low concentration and propose structure-guided RNA engineering as a therapeutic strategy for ALS and related neurodegenerative diseases.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental details, quantitative data, or methodological descriptions are provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, experimental protocols, statistical analyses, raw data, and any supplementary information

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a conceptually interesting approach to FUS aggregation inhibition through multivalent RNA design. The rationale of combining two distinct RNA structural motifs to engage multiple FUS domains is scientifically sound and potentially valuable. However, the abstract provides insufficient quantitative evidence to evaluate the strength of the claims. Key parameters such as binding affinities, aggregation inhibition efficiencies, nuclease resistance levels, and the specificity of the observed effects are not reported. The claim of low-concentration efficacy lacks comparative context. The work may be of interest to the RNA therapeutics and neurodegeneration communities, but the current evidence base is too limited to establish the case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in RNA biology, nucleic acid therapeutics, and neurodegenerative disease mechanisms would find this work relevant. Those studying FUS pathology in ALS, RNA phase separation, and structure-guided nucleic acid design would be the primary audience. The multivalent RNA approach could also interest groups working on other RNA-binding proteins implicated in disease.
- **Major strengths** The design rationale is clear and mechanistically grounded, linking specific RNA structural motifs to distinct FUS domains. The introduction of 2'-O-methyl modifications for nuclease resistance is a practical and clinically relevant consideration. The concept of multivalent RNA constructs as aggregation inhibitors is novel and potentially generalizable.
- **Major Concerns** None identified from the supplied material, as the abstract does not provide sufficient data to formulate specific technical concerns. The absence of quantitative results is itself a limitation that prevents rigorous assessment.
- **Minor Comments** None identified from the supplied material, as the abstract is too brief to contain minor technical details.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The abstract does not report any quantitative data. Without binding affinity values, aggregation inhibition percentages, nuclease degradation half-lives, or dose-response relationships, the core claims cannot be evaluated. The specificity of U1'+TERRA for FUS over other RNA-binding proteins is not addressed. The relationship between the two motifs and their individual contributions to the overall effect is not quantified.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality is moderate to high, as the multivalent RNA design concept is not widely explored for FUS. Scientific importance is potentially significant given the unmet need for ALS therapeutics, but the abstract does not demonstrate translational relevance beyond in vitro data. Interdisciplinary readership is plausible, spanning chemistry, biology, and clinical neuroscience. Technical soundness cannot be assessed from the abstract alone. Readability for nonspecialists is adequate, though some domain knowledge of RNA structure and FUS biology is assumed.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The concept is promising, but the abstract lacks the quantitative and methodological detail necessary to support the claims. A full manuscript with comprehensive data would be required for a supportive assessment.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that U1'+TERRA exhibited strong binding affinity is unsupported, as no affinity values or comparison benchmarks are provided.
- The claim that U1'+TERRA effectively inhibited FUS aggregation in vitro is unsupported, as no quantitative aggregation data are provided.
- The claim that (U1'+TERRA)-2'-OMe demonstrated resistance to nuclease degradation is unsupported, as no degradation kinetics or half-life data are provided.
- The claim that (U1'+TERRA)-2'-OMe suppressed FUS aggregation at low concentration is unsupported, as no concentration range or dose-response data are provided.
- The claim that the two motifs each interact with distinct FUS domains is presented as known, but the abstract does not provide evidence that the combined construct engages both domains simultaneously.
- The generalizability of the approach to other RNA-binding proteins is speculative and not supported by data.
