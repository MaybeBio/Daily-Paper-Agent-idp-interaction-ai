## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results and discussion, conclusion, and supplementary material references.
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological rigor, internal consistency, and support of claims by the presented evidence. No assessment of formatting, grammar, or journal fit.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The E46K mutation in α-synuclein acts as an electrostatic switch that remodels intramolecular NTD–CTD interactions, shifting the interaction site from the N-terminus to the mutation region. This increases hydrophobic solvent exposure of key LLPS-promoting motifs, enhances intermolecular interactions, and promotes phase separation and condensate solidification, as supported by multiscale simulations and biochemical experiments.
- **Visible evidence base** All-atom REMD simulations of WT and E46K monomers; coarse-grained HPS phase-coexistence simulations; interaction analyses (electrostatic, polar, hydrophobic, H-bonding); turbidity assays; fluorescence imaging; FRAP experiments; comparison with cryo-EM fibril structures (PDB 6A6B, 6L4S).
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Simulation parameters and force field details are referenced to Supporting Information, which was not provided. Experimental protocols for turbidity, imaging, and FRAP are not fully described in the main text. Statistical details and replicate numbers for experiments are not provided. The definition of "LLPS-promoting motifs" and "aggregation-promoting motifs" is referenced to prior work and Supporting Information, not fully restated.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a comprehensive multiscale computational and experimental study addressing a significant open question: how the E46K mutation enhances α-synuclein phase separation. The central hypothesis, that E46K acts as an electrostatic switch to remodel NTD–CTD interactions, is mechanistically plausible and well-aligned with existing literature. The combination of all-atom REMD, coarse-grained phase-coexistence simulations, and biochemical validation is a strength. However, several concerns limit the current confidence in the conclusions. The causal link between monomer-level conformational changes and phase-separation behavior is inferred rather than directly demonstrated. The experimental validation is correlative and lacks quantitative rigor. The relative contributions of electrostatic versus hydrophobic interactions are asserted but not systematically deconvoluted. The claim that NTD–CTD electrostatic crosstalk is the "key modulator" while NAC–NAC interactions are "auxiliary" requires stronger evidence, particularly given the established role of NAC in α-syn aggregation. The manuscript would benefit from clearer articulation of how the simulation findings translate to the experimental observations and from additional controls or perturbation experiments to test the proposed mechanism directly.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying phase separation of intrinsically disordered proteins, particularly those focused on neurodegenerative disease mechanisms. The work provides a mechanistic framework for how a single disease-associated mutation can alter phase behavior, which is relevant to understanding α-synuclein pathology and potentially other IDP systems. Computational biophysicists interested in multiscale simulation approaches for IDPs and their phase behavior would also find the methodology of interest.
- **Major strengths** 1) The study addresses a clinically relevant and mechanistically unresolved question. 2) The multiscale simulation strategy, combining all-atom REMD with coarse-grained phase-coexistence simulations, is appropriate and powerful. 3) The integration of computational predictions with experimental validation (turbidity, imaging, FRAP) strengthens the translational relevance. 4) The comparison with cryo-EM fibril structures provides an important link between monomeric properties and fibrillar states. 5) The proposed mechanism, involving an electrostatic switch and altered hydrophobic exposure, is specific and testable.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Causal inference
  - **Claim pointer** "The E46K mutation acts as an electrostatic switch to remodel interactions between the oppositely charged NTD and CTD... Such reorganization increases the hydrophobic solvent exposure of key LLPS-promoting motifs, potentially facilitating intermolecular interactions that initiate phase separation."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results sections "The E46K Mutation Alters the Local β-Structure Propensity..." and "The E46K Mutation Remodels the Intramolecular Interaction Network"; Figures 2, 3, 4
  - **Concern** The manuscript establishes correlations between monomer-level conformational changes (increased compaction, altered β-propensity, remodeled intramolecular contacts) and enhanced phase separation in coarse-grained simulations. However, the causal chain from monomer conformational remodeling to enhanced LLPS is not directly tested. The claim that increased hydrophobic solvent exposure of β1 and β6 "potentially facilitates" intermolecular interactions is speculative. No simulation or experiment directly demonstrates that the specific conformational changes observed in the monomer are necessary or sufficient for the enhanced phase separation. The phase-coexistence simulations use a coarse-grained model that may not faithfully capture the all-atom conformational details, creating a gap between the two simulation scales.
  - **Why it matters** The central mechanistic claim of the paper rests on this causal link. Without direct evidence that the remodeled monomer conformations drive enhanced LLPS, the proposed mechanism remains a hypothesis. The field has moved toward testing such mechanisms through perturbation experiments (e.g., mutations that specifically disrupt the proposed interactions) or through simulations that directly connect monomer properties to phase behavior.
  - **Resolution test** Provide additional simulations or experiments that perturb the proposed mechanism. For example, mutate residues in the β6 motif or the CTD to disrupt the specific interactions identified, and test whether this abolishes the E46K-enhanced phase separation. Alternatively, perform simulations where the monomer conformational ensemble is constrained to match WT or E46K distributions and assess the impact on phase behavior. Directly measure the intermolecular contacts predicted to be enhanced in the E46K mutant using experimental approaches (e.g., cross-linking mass spectrometry) or in the coarse-grained simulations.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** "We found that, upon phase separation, the E46K mutation strengthens interchain electrostatic interactions between the mutation-site region within the NTD and the CTD, along with hydrophobic interactions mediated by the LLPS-promoting motif in the NAC tail, thus promoting α-syn LLPS and condensate solidification."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "CTD-Driven Electrostatic Interactions and β6-Mediated NAC–NAC Interactions Synergistically Govern α-syn Phase Separation"; Figures 5, S21–S23
  - **Concern** The relative contributions of electrostatic versus hydrophobic interactions to the enhanced phase separation are asserted based on contact number analyses. The claim that electrostatic interactions are the "primary driver" and hydrophobic interactions are "auxiliary" is not supported by a systematic deconvolution. The contact analysis shows that charged and hydrophobic residues both contribute substantially to increased interchain contacts. No simulations or experiments systematically vary the strength of electrostatic interactions (e.g., by salt concentration) or hydrophobic interactions (e.g., by mutation) to determine their relative importance. The statement that electrostatic interactions are primary appears to be an interpretation rather than a demonstrated conclusion.
  - **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism has two components: electrostatic switching and hydrophobic exposure. If the relative importance of these components is not established, the mechanistic framework is incomplete. This distinction matters for therapeutic targeting, as different interventions would be needed to disrupt electrostatic versus hydrophobic interactions.
  - **Resolution test** Perform simulations at varying salt concentrations to modulate electrostatic interactions and assess the impact on phase separation. Alternatively, design mutations that specifically disrupt the electrostatic interactions between the Mut-region and CTD (e.g., charge-neutralizing mutations) or the hydrophobic interactions involving β6, and test their effects on phase separation in both simulations and experiments. Quantify the free-energy contributions of each interaction type to phase separation.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Experimental rigor
  - **Claim pointer** "Turbidity measurements showed that WT α-syn displayed an increase in turbidity at PEG concentrations above 14%, whereas E46K α-syn exhibited a similar increase at PEG concentrations above 10%... fluorescence imaging revealed that WT and E46K α-syn formed large numbers of well-defined droplets... FRAP experiments showed that E46K α-syn condensates exhibited a markedly prolonged post-bleaching recovery time, almost an order of magnitude longer than that of WT α-syn condensates."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Biochemical Experiments Corroborate the Critical Role of the Negatively Charged C-Terminal Region in α-syn Phase Separation"; Figure 6
  - **Concern** The experimental data are presented qualitatively. No statistical analysis is reported (e.g., error bars, replicates, significance testing). The turbidity threshold concentrations are stated without error estimates. The FRAP recovery times are described as "almost an order of magnitude longer" without quantitative values or statistical comparison. The imaging data are shown as representative images without quantification of droplet size, number, or distribution. The experimental protocols are not described in sufficient detail to assess reproducibility.
  - **Why it matters** The experimental validation is a key component of the manuscript's claim to provide a "complete and detailed mechanistic framework." Without quantitative rigor, the experimental support is weakened. The field of phase separation research has established standards for quantitative characterization of condensates, and the current presentation falls short of these standards.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative data with appropriate statistical analysis. Report the number of independent replicates, error bars, and significance tests for turbidity and FRAP measurements. Quantify droplet properties (size, number, density) from imaging data. Provide detailed experimental protocols, including protein concentrations, buffer conditions, and imaging parameters.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1) The causal link between monomer conformational remodeling and enhanced phase separation is not directly demonstrated (R1-M1). 2) The relative contributions of electrostatic versus hydrophobic interactions are not systematically deconvoluted (R1-M2). 3) Experimental validation lacks quantitative rigor and statistical analysis (R1-M3).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** **Originality:** The work is original in its specific mechanistic proposal for E46K, but builds on established concepts of IDP phase separation and charge effects. The "electrostatic switch" concept is a useful framing. **Scientific importance:** The question of how disease mutations alter phase behavior is important for understanding neurodegeneration. The work has the potential to inform therapeutic strategies. **Interdisciplinary readership:** The topic bridges biophysics, computational biology, and neurobiology, and would appeal to a broad readership. **Technical soundness:** The multiscale simulation approach is appropriate, but the causal inference gap and lack of experimental rigor weaken the technical foundation. **Readability for nonspecialists:** The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, though some sections assume familiarity with phase separation concepts and simulation methods.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses an important question with a sophisticated approach, and the proposed mechanism is plausible. However, the causal link between monomer-level changes and phase behavior must be strengthened, the relative contributions of interaction types must be clarified, and the experimental validation must be made quantitative. These are substantial but addressable concerns.
