### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims to present "a comprehensive methodological framework for studying biomolecular condensates in plants, spanning in silico prediction, in vitro reconstitution, molecular dynamics simulations, live-cell and super-resolution imaging, material property measurements, membrane-associated condensates, and synthetic condensate engineering."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract lists seven methodological areas but provides no indication of the depth, specificity, or quality of coverage for any of them. A "comprehensive framework" is asserted, but there is no evidence that the treatment is balanced, that all areas receive adequate detail, or that the authors have sufficient expertise across all listed techniques. The abstract does not mention how the framework was developed, whether it is based on systematic literature review, community consensus, or expert opinion, and whether any areas are covered in more depth than others.
- **Why it matters** The central value proposition of a methodological review is the quality and utility of the guidance provided. Without evidence of depth and balance, a reader cannot judge whether the framework is genuinely comprehensive or merely a superficial listing of topics. The claim of comprehensiveness is the primary basis for the manuscript's contribution, and it is currently unsupported.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should demonstrate, for each of the seven areas, specific protocols, decision trees, troubleshooting guidance, or comparative evaluations of available methods. The authors should state the criteria for inclusion and the process by which the framework was assembled. If some areas are covered in less depth, this should be acknowledged and justified.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Claim substantiation
- **Claim pointer** The abstract states that "plant cells present a unique set of biological and technical challenges, including rigid cell walls, turgor pressure, plastid autofluorescence, complex endomembrane organization, and acute environmental responsiveness. These distinctive features impede the direct transfer of existing methodologies."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract lists five plant-specific challenges but provides no examples, data, or literature references demonstrating that these challenges actually impede method transfer in practice. While the listed challenges are plausible, the abstract does not show how each challenge specifically affects a given technique, nor does it provide any comparative evidence that plant systems are more difficult than other non-animal systems. The claim that these features "impede the direct transfer" is presented as fact rather than as a hypothesis requiring support.
- **Why it matters** The entire rationale for the manuscript rests on the premise that plant condensate research faces unique obstacles that existing methods do not address. If this premise is not substantiated, the need for a plant-specific framework is weakened. The abstract should provide at least one concrete example of a method that fails or requires substantial modification in plants, with a mechanistic explanation.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should provide specific examples, with citations, of how each listed challenge affects at least one methodological step. For instance, the authors could show how cell wall rigidity complicates optogenetic perturbation, or how plastid autofluorescence interferes with specific fluorescence imaging approaches. Each challenge should be linked to a concrete methodological consequence.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Outcome clarity
- **Claim pointer** The abstract claims the review "aims to establish a foundation for rigorous, reproducible, and conceptually coherent research in condensate biology of plants and beyond, with emerging implications for crop genetic improvement and synthetic biology applications."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states aspirational outcomes but provides no indication of how the framework will achieve these goals. The terms "rigorous," "reproducible," and "conceptually coherent" are undefined in this context. The claimed implications for crop improvement and synthetic biology are stated without any explanation of the mechanistic or practical link between condensate methodology and these applications. The abstract does not specify what constitutes success for the framework or how a reader would know whether the field has become more rigorous or reproducible after adopting it.
- **Why it matters** A community comment should provide actionable guidance that changes practice. If the outcomes are purely aspirational, the manuscript risks being a statement of good intentions rather than a practical resource. The abstract should articulate specific, testable recommendations or standards that would operationalize the stated goals.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should include concrete recommendations such as minimum reporting standards for condensate experiments in plants, specific validation criteria for identifying condensates, or standardized protocols for material property measurements. The link to crop improvement and synthetic biology should be explained with at least one illustrative example of how condensate manipulation could be applied.

- **Concern ID** R1-m3
- **Severity** Minor
- **Axis** Terminology precision
- **Affected element** Use of "phase separation"
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Issue** The abstract uses "phase separation" and "biomolecular condensates" as if they are interchangeable, but the relationship between the two is debated in the field. The abstract does not clarify whether the framework covers all condensates or only those formed through phase separation.
- **Required correction** The authors should clarify their operational definition of condensates and whether the framework applies to condensates formed through mechanisms other than phase separation, such as multivalent interactions without a clear phase transition.
