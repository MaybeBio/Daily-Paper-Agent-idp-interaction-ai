## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodology, and claims as presented in the provided manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use coarse-grained polymer simulations with explicit hydrodynamics to investigate how sequence composition, patterning, and hydrodynamic interactions affect the conformational dimensions and dynamics of intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs), with emphasis on sequence "patchiness" and implications for interpreting FRET, SAXS, and FCS experiments.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text including abstract, introduction, results (Sections 2.1.1–2.2), discussion, and methods (Section 4). Figures 1–7 and Tables 1 are referenced but not provided. Supplementary Information is referenced but not provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures 1–7, Table 1, and all Supplementary Information (S1–S3, Figures S1–S2) are not available. Simulation parameter details, validation data, and quantitative comparisons to experimental results are therefore not assessable.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a timely and important question in IDP biophysics: how sequence features beyond simple composition influence polymer dimensions and dynamics. The authors employ a minimal coarse-grained model with explicit hydrodynamic interactions, which is a useful and relatively underexplored approach in this context. The conceptual framing, particularly the distinction between global conformational variables (Rg, Rh) and sequence-sensitive observables (Re, dynamics), is valuable. However, the manuscript as presented has several significant limitations. The absence of figures and supplementary material prevents verification of the central quantitative claims. The mapping of heterogeneous sequences onto effective homopolymer models, while interesting, is presented with insufficient theoretical justification and quantitative criteria. The treatment of hydrodynamics, a claimed novelty, is not benchmarked against known results or experimental data in the visible text. The discussion of experimental implications, while thoughtful, remains largely qualitative and speculative. The manuscript would benefit from clearer articulation of the novelty relative to existing coarse-grained IDP studies, and from a more rigorous presentation of the conditions under which the proposed mappings hold.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying IDP conformational ensembles, particularly those interpreting FRET, SAXS, and FCS experiments; computational biophysicists developing coarse-grained models for IDPs; and investigators of nuclear pore complex biology, specifically FG nucleoporin structure and dynamics. The results could inform experimental design and data interpretation, and provide a framework for mesoscopic modeling of IDPs.
- **Major strengths** 1. The systematic exploration of sequence composition, patterning, and hydrodynamics within a unified coarse-grained framework is valuable. 2. The distinction between robust global observables (Rg, Rh) and sequence-sensitive ones (Re, dynamics) provides a useful conceptual advance. 3. The inclusion of explicit hydrodynamic interactions, often neglected in IDP simulations, is a strength. 4. The connection to experimental techniques (FRET, SAXS, FCS) and the discussion of potential artifacts (e.g., Sanchez–Haran model limitations) are relevant and timely. 5. The writing is generally clear and accessible.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes): The central quantitative claims cannot be verified without figures and supplementary data. The manuscript repeatedly refers to specific numerical results, trends, and comparisons (e.g., "Figure 1a shows...", "as shown in Figure 4b") that are not visible. This makes it impossible to assess the validity of the conclusions.
  - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes): The claimed mapping of heterogeneous sequences onto effective homopolymer models is insufficiently justified. The text states that "highly heterogeneous sequences of IDPs can be well mapped onto averaged minimal polymer models" but does not provide quantitative criteria for what constitutes "well mapped," the range of validity of this mapping, or the error bars associated with it. The theoretical basis for this mapping, beyond empirical observation, is not articulated.
  - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No): The treatment of hydrodynamics, while a stated focus, is not adequately benchmarked. The authors claim that hydrodynamic interactions "substantially modify polymer properties" but do not provide comparisons to simulations without hydrodynamics, to analytical predictions (e.g., Zimm theory), or to experimental diffusion data. The specific contribution of hydrodynamics to the observed results is therefore unclear.
  - **R1-M4** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No): The discussion of experimental implications, while interesting, is largely qualitative. The authors suggest that their results "might explain" certain experimental observations but do not provide quantitative predictions that could be tested against existing or future experiments. The link between the coarse-grained model parameters and specific experimental conditions (e.g., denaturant concentration) is not established.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity, Affected Element: Section 2.1.1, Evidence Pointer: location not provided): The definition of the "cohesiveness parameter ϵ" and its relationship to the Flory parameter χ is stated but not derived or discussed in detail. A brief explanation of this connection would improve accessibility.
  - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Completeness, Affected Element: Section 2.1.4, Evidence Pointer: location not provided): The choice of the "four letter" model (HP+−) is motivated, but the specific parameter values (e.g., interaction strengths, charges) are not given in the main text. This information is presumably in the methods or supplementary, but its absence in the results section makes the simulations difficult to interpret.
  - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Precision, Affected Element: Section 2.2, Evidence Pointer: location not provided): The statement that "the reconfiguration time is a non-monotonic function of the chain dimensions" for the (HPP)33H sequence is intriguing but the physical mechanism is only briefly described. A more detailed explanation of the bistability and its dependence on sequence would strengthen the argument.
  - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Readability, Affected Element: Section 3, Evidence Pointer: location not provided): The discussion section is somewhat repetitive of the results. It could be streamlined to focus on the broader implications and future directions.
  - **R1-m5** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical, Affected Element: Section 4, Evidence Pointer: location not provided): The methods section describes the simulation algorithm but does not provide details on the choice of time step, system size, or equilibration protocol. These details are important for reproducibility.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1. Missing figures and supplementary data (R1-M1). 2. Lack of quantitative criteria for the homopolymer mapping (R1-M2). 3. Insufficient benchmarking of hydrodynamic effects (R1-M3). 4. Absence of quantitative experimental predictions (R1-M4).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The work is moderately original. The application of coarse-grained models with explicit hydrodynamics to IDP sequence-patterning questions is not entirely new, but the systematic focus on "patchiness" and the distinction between different observables provides a fresh angle. However, the conceptual framework overlaps significantly with prior work on IDP sequence-ensemble relationships.
  - Scientific importance: The question of how sequence encodes IDP conformation and dynamics is of high importance in the field. The potential implications for interpreting FRET and SAXS data are significant. However, the importance is somewhat diminished by the lack of quantitative, testable predictions.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The topic is relevant to biophysics, computational biology, and cell biology (particularly NPC research). The writing is accessible to nonspecialists, though some familiarity with polymer physics is assumed.
  - Technical soundness: The methodology appears sound in principle, but the lack of visible data and benchmarking prevents a full assessment. The treatment of hydrodynamics, while a strength, needs more rigorous validation.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and the logical flow is clear. However, the heavy reliance on undefined parameters and the absence of figures make it difficult for a nonspecialist to follow the quantitative arguments.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses a relevant and timely question, and the conceptual framework is promising. However, the current version does not establish the central claims due to missing data and insufficient quantitative justification. The authors should provide the missing figures and supplementary material, benchmark the hydrodynamic treatment, and articulate the conditions and limitations of the homopolymer mapping with quantitative criteria.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that "highly heterogeneous sequences of IDPs can be well mapped onto averaged minimal polymer models for the purpose of prediction of the IDPs dimensions and dynamic relaxation times" is not supported by visible quantitative evidence.
- The claim that hydrodynamic interactions "substantially modify polymer properties" is not supported by comparisons to simulations without hydrodynamics or to analytical/experimental benchmarks.
- The claim that the (HPP)33H sequence exhibits bistability between compact and swollen conformations is not supported by visible data (Figure 7c is referenced but not provided).
- The claim that the results "might explain" experimental observations of non-monotonic reconfiguration times is speculative and not quantitatively linked to specific experimental systems.
- The comparison to atomistic simulations (e.g., Das and Pappu) is mentioned but the quantitative agreement is not shown in the visible text.
