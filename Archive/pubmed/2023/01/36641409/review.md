## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting observation regarding the decoupling of dynamical arrest from liquid-liquid phase separation in protein solutions under screened electrostatic conditions, supported by a combination of experiments and theory. However, the evidence base is too limited to evaluate the robustness of the claims. The key assertion of arrest line independence from attraction range and strength is stated without quantitative support, and the theoretical comparison is described only qualitatively. The work may be of interest to soft matter and biophysics communities, but the current abstract does not establish the case convincingly.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in soft condensed matter physics, protein biophysics, and colloid science would be interested, as the interplay of phase separation and dynamical arrest is central to understanding gelation and glass formation in protein and colloidal systems. The potential universality of arrest line behavior across interaction parameters could inform theoretical models and practical applications in food and pharmaceutical formulation.

The combination of multiple experimental techniques (cloud-point, microscopy, centrifugation, light scattering) with a theoretical framework (NE-SCGLE) is methodologically ambitious. The observation that arrest lines may be insensitive to attraction range, while binodals shift, is a potentially novel and testable claim.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "the dynamical arrest line seems to be independent of ionic strength" and later that "the same phenomenology can also be observed when the electrostatic repulsions are largely screened, and the range and strength of the attractions are varied."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states that arrest lines overlap across different ionic strengths and, by extension, across different attraction ranges and strengths, but no quantitative data (e.g., phase diagram coordinates, error bars, or statistical comparisons) are presented to support this claim. The statement "seems to be independent" is tentative, yet the conclusion is presented as a key finding.  
- **Why it matters** The central novelty of the work hinges on the robustness of this independence. Without quantitative evidence, the claim could be an artifact of limited sampling or experimental uncertainty, which would undermine the theoretical interpretation.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a figure or table showing arrest line positions for multiple attraction ranges and strengths, with error bars and a statistical test (e.g., overlap of confidence intervals) demonstrating independence. Specify the range of parameters tested and the criteria for "independence."

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Theoretical validation  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "the theoretical predictions find a good qualitative agreement with experiments when the range of the attraction is not too small compared with the size of the protein."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided  
- **Concern** The agreement is described only as "qualitative," and the condition "not too small" is not quantified. No details are given on how the model parameters (e.g., attraction range, strength) were mapped to experimental conditions, nor how the comparison was performed (e.g., which observables were compared, what deviations were tolerated).  
- **Why it matters** A qualitative match with a vague parameter regime does not establish that the theory captures the essential physics. Without a clear metric for agreement and a defined parameter space, the theoretical support for the experimental observation remains weak.  
- **Resolution test** Present a quantitative comparison (e.g., overlay of predicted and measured arrest lines and binodals) with defined error metrics. Specify the range of attraction ranges tested in the model and the threshold for "good agreement" (e.g., within experimental error bars).

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
