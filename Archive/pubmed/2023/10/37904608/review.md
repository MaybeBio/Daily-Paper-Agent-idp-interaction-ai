## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, technical soundness, database utility, and FAIR principles as described in the manuscript
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors describe the 2024 update of the Protein Ensemble Database (PED), reporting a substantial increase in entries (461 entries, 538 ensembles), inclusion of ensembles generated without experimental data via machine learning methods, new data types (EPR, CD), a redesigned deposition service, improved user interface, new cross-referencing options, and integration with the 3D-Beacons network, all aimed at improving FAIRness.
- **Visible evidence base** Full manuscript text including abstract, introduction, methods descriptions, and data availability statement. No figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures (Figure 1, Figure 2), tables, supplementary data, and the actual database contents referenced in the text. No quantitative validation metrics for the new deposition pipeline or the ML-generated ensembles were provided.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript describes a significant update to a valuable community resource for intrinsically disordered protein structural ensembles. The reported growth in database content and the inclusion of ML-generated ensembles without experimental data represent notable developments. The new deposition service and FAIR improvements are commendable. However, the manuscript lacks quantitative assessment of the new features, provides insufficient detail on the quality control of ML-generated ensembles, and does not present user feedback or usage statistics that would substantiate claims of improved usability. The technical descriptions are adequate but would benefit from more specific implementation details and validation metrics.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the intrinsically disordered protein field, including structural biologists, computational biophysicists, and bioinformaticians. Also relevant to developers of machine learning methods for protein structure prediction and those working on FAIR data infrastructure for biomolecular data. The database serves as a community benchmark resource, so method developers and experimentalists studying IDP conformational behavior would find this update relevant.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript addresses a clear community need by expanding a primary deposition database for IDP ensembles. The inclusion of ML-generated ensembles without experimental data is forward-looking and reflects current methodological trends. The new deposition service with automated validation and ORCID integration represents a practical improvement. The integration with 3D-Beacons enhances interoperability. The description of the NMR ensemble identification pipeline from BMRB is methodologically interesting.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The manuscript claims the database has grown to 461 entries and 538 ensembles, but provides no breakdown of how many entries fall into each category (experimental, ML-generated, NMR-derived from PDB). This makes it difficult to assess the relative contribution of each source and the overall quality distribution.
  - R1-M2: The inclusion of 61 ensembles generated without experimental data raises significant quality control questions. The authors state that idpGAN ensembles were not reweighted against experimental data, but do not describe any validation of these ensembles' physical plausibility or agreement with known IDP properties. The claim that these are suitable for community use requires stronger justification.
  - R1-M3: The NMR ensemble identification pipeline from BMRB is described, but the criteria for inclusion (publication availability, disorder prediction consistency, minimum 10 consecutive disordered residues, at least 10 structures) are presented without any assessment of how these criteria affect the quality or representativeness of the resulting ensembles. No comparison with existing NMR ensemble quality metrics is provided.
  - R1-M4: The manuscript claims the deposition service reduces time between deposition and publication, but provides no data on actual processing times, user experience feedback, or comparison with the previous system. This claim is unsupported.
  - R1-M5: The description of the "novel deposition service" lacks technical detail on how the automated validation pipeline works, what specific checks are performed, and how errors are reported to users. The mention of DSSP and MolProbity is useful but insufficient to assess the robustness of the validation.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states "461 entries and 538 ensembles" but does not clarify the relationship between entries and ensembles. Some entries may contain multiple ensembles. This should be clarified.
  - R1-m2: The introduction mentions "fuzzy complexes" but does not define this term for nonspecialist readers. A brief definition would improve accessibility.
  - R1-m3: The description of the IDPConformerGenerator ensembles states they were "assessed in the original publications" but does not summarize what those assessments found. A brief summary would help readers evaluate the quality of these entries.
  - R1-m4: The controlled vocabulary (CV) is mentioned as being expanded, but no details are provided on what new terms were added or how the ontology is structured. This is relevant for users who need to search the database effectively.
  - R1-m5: The manuscript mentions "integration with the 3D-Beacons network" but does not explain what this integration means practically for users. A brief description of how data flows between PED and 3D-Beacons would be helpful.
  - R1-m6: The "Data availability" section is minimal and only states that data are openly available at the PED website. Given the emphasis on FAIR principles, a more detailed description of data formats, licensing, and access mechanisms would be appropriate.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 
  - R1-M2 (quality control of ML-generated ensembles) and R1-M3 (NMR ensemble quality assessment) are the most critical technical issues. Without addressing these, the scientific validity of a substantial portion of the database content cannot be established.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: Moderate. The update itself is incremental, but the inclusion of ML-generated ensembles without experimental data is a novel step for a community deposition database. The NMR ensemble identification pipeline from BMRB is also a novel approach.
  - Scientific importance: High for the IDP community specifically, but the broader impact is limited to structural biology and bioinformatics. The database serves as a critical community resource, and its expansion has direct utility for method development and benchmarking.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: Limited. The manuscript is primarily relevant to structural biologists, computational chemists, and bioinformaticians working on disordered proteins. The technical content would not appeal to a broad interdisciplinary audience.
  - Technical soundness: The described methods are generally sound, but the lack of validation metrics for ML-generated ensembles and the absence of quality assessment for NMR-derived ensembles weaken the technical case. The deposition pipeline description lacks sufficient detail to assess its robustness.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is reasonably well-written but assumes familiarity with IDP ensemble determination methods. Some terms (e.g., "fuzzy complexes," "reweighting") are used without definition. The structure is logical and the progression from database growth to new features to future work is clear.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The database update is valuable and the manuscript describes meaningful improvements. However, the quality control issues for ML-generated ensembles and the lack of validation data for the NMR-derived ensembles must be addressed before the scientific case is fully established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the deposition service "significantly reduces the time between ensemble deposition and publication" is unsupported by any data.
- The claim that the database is "the primary resource for depositing structural ensembles of intrinsically disordered proteins" is asserted without comparative analysis of other potential resources.
- The statement that ML-generated ensembles without experimental data are suitable for community use is not supported by validation evidence.
- The claim that the database "will keep growing in size" is speculative and not supported by data on deposition trends.
- The assertion that the new features represent improvements in FAIRness is plausible but not quantitatively demonstrated.
