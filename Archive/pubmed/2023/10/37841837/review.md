## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, figure legends as embedded in text)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, experimental evidence, modeling approach, and internal consistency as presented in the provided manuscript text. No external literature verification or replication of experiments performed.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that nucleolar and pericentromeric heterochromatin (PCH) condensates co-assemble during Drosophila embryogenesis through a hierarchy of affinities mediated by amphiphilic proteins, with the DEAD-box RNA helicase Pitchoune identified as a key molecular linker. They support this with live imaging, genetic perturbations, and coarse-grained simulations.
- **Visible evidence base** Live and fixed imaging data (Figures 1-3, 5-6; Extended Figures 1-4), genetic manipulations (Su(var)3-9 mutants, HP1a RNAi, Pitchoune RNAi), coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulations (Figure 4; Extended Figure 5), and quantitative image analyses. Figure panels are referenced but individual data points, statistical details, and full image sets are not fully visible in the provided text.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full figure images and supplementary movies; exact numerical values for all quantitative measurements beyond means and p-values; details on replicate numbers for imaging experiments; validation of antibody and probe specificities; complete simulation parameter sweeps and sensitivity analyses; raw data files.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a fundamental and timely question in nuclear biology: how two distinct biomolecular condensates, the nucleolus and pericentromeric heterochromatin, achieve their conserved spatial association. The combination of high-resolution live imaging in a developing organism, genetic perturbations, and physical modeling is ambitious and largely successful. The identification of Pitchoune as a candidate amphiphilic linker is novel and potentially significant. However, several technical concerns and gaps in evidence need to be addressed before the central claims are fully established. The causal role of Pitchoune, the quantitative rigor of the imaging analyses, and the direct relevance of the minimal model to the in vivo complexity require strengthening.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Cell biologists studying nuclear organization, phase separation, and biomolecular condensates; chromatin biologists interested in heterochromatin structure and function; developmental biologists studying early embryogenesis; biophysicists working on theoretical models of phase behavior. The work provides a new conceptual framework for how distinct condensates interact and identifies a specific protein candidate with potential relevance to human disease states involving nucleolar and heterochromatin dysfunction.
- **Major strengths** 1) The use of high-resolution live imaging in Drosophila embryos to capture the dynamic, de novo assembly of PCH-nucleolar associations is a significant technical achievement. 2) The reciprocal genetic perturbations (removing heterochromatin components vs. removing nucleoli) provide strong evidence for interdependence. 3) The integration of a minimal physical model with in vivo observations is elegant and generates testable predictions. 4) The identification of Pitchoune as a specific amphiphilic protein with a demonstrated role in PCH-nucleolar association is a novel and important finding. 5) The writing is clear and the logical flow from observation to model to validation is compelling.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Causal evidence
  - **Claim pointer** "Pitchoune is required for PCH-nucleolar associations" and "Pitchoune acts as a molecular linker"
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 6; Extended Figure 6
  - **Concern** The causal role of Pitchoune is supported by a single RNAi condition in eye-antennal discs. The maternal knockdown resulted in no egg laying, precluding analysis in the embryonic system where the primary observations were made. The eyeless-GAL4 driven knockdown shows a severe developmental phenotype in the disc, raising the possibility that the observed reduction in PCH-nucleolar association is an indirect consequence of general tissue disruption rather than a specific loss of the linker function. Furthermore, the quantification of the H3K9me3-nucleolar edge overlap is presented as a single mean and p-value without clear information on the number of biological replicates, number of nuclei analyzed, or the distribution of the data.
  - **Why it matters** The central claim of the paper rests on Pitchoune being a specific molecular link. If the only in vivo perturbation is confounded by pleiotropic developmental defects, the specificity of the proposed mechanism is not established. The quantitative basis for the 50% reduction claim is essential for assessing its robustness.
  - **Resolution test** Provide additional details on the number of independent biological replicates and nuclei analyzed for the Pitchoune knockdown experiments. Ideally, perform a rescue experiment by expressing an RNAi-resistant version of Pitchoune. Alternatively, use a different, inducible or tissue-specific driver that allows analysis at a stage before gross morphological defects appear. Show that other nucleolar or heterochromatin proteins are not globally mislocalized upon Pitchoune knockdown.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Modeling validity
  - **Claim pointer** "Simulating this affinity hierarchy recapitulated the canonical PCH-nucleolar organization observed in +rDNA animals in vivo"
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 4; Extended Figure 5; Methods section "Modeling"
  - **Concern** The coarse-grained model is a minimal representation with several assumptions that are not fully justified or tested. The model treats PCH as a single long polymer and Fibrillarin and protein X as single beads, ignoring the multi-component, multi-phase nature of the nucleolus and the complexity of heterochromatin. The key parameter, the affinity hierarchy, is imposed based on the desired outcome. The model's predictive power is limited by the lack of sensitivity analysis. For instance, how robust is the "surrounded" configuration to variations in the interaction strengths, concentrations, or polymer length? The model is used to predict the phenotype upon Pitchoune depletion, but this is a qualitative prediction. The manuscript would be strengthened by a more systematic exploration of the parameter space and a clearer statement of the model's limitations.
  - **Why it matters** The model is presented as a key piece of evidence supporting the proposed mechanism. If the model is not robust or its assumptions are not well-grounded, the mechanistic insight it provides is weakened. A model that is only tuned to reproduce the observed phenotype has limited explanatory power.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a phase diagram or a systematic parameter sweep showing how the final configuration (extended vs. surrounded vs. mixed) depends on the relative interaction strengths (e.g., ϵXX vs. ϵFF, ϵXH vs. ϵFX) and concentrations. Discuss the sensitivity of the results to the choice of polymer length and persistence length. Show that the model can make a non-trivial, testable prediction beyond the conditions it was designed to reproduce.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Quantitative imaging rigor
  - **Claim pointer** "PCH and nucleolar proteins are initially diffuse, then both condensates undergo independent nucleation, growth, and fusions, and display cycle-specific spatiotemporal differences"
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 1; Extended Figure 1
  - **Concern** The description of the dynamic process is largely qualitative. While the representative images and movies are likely compelling, the quantitative analyses (e.g., fraction of nucleolar edge occupied by HP1a in Figure 1B) are not described in sufficient detail. The methods mention manual selection of nuclei and specific time windows, but the criteria for selection, the number of embryos/nuclei analyzed, and the statistical treatment are not fully clear. The claim of "cycle-specific spatiotemporal differences" would be strengthened by a more rigorous quantitative comparison across cycles.
  - **Why it matters** The paper's first major finding is the dynamic nature of the co-assembly. To be convincing, this needs to be supported by robust quantitative data that can be evaluated by the reader. Qualitative descriptions of dynamic processes are often subject to observer bias.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a detailed description of the image analysis pipeline, including the number of embryos and nuclei analyzed per condition and time point. Include error bars and statistical tests for the measurements in Figure 1B and Extended Figure 1. Consider tracking individual PCH foci or nucleoli over time to quantify their movement and coalescence.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 1) The lack of a robust, specific in vivo perturbation of Pitchoune function that is not confounded by developmental arrest or gross tissue disruption (R1-M1). 2) The absence of a systematic sensitivity analysis for the coarse-grained model, which is crucial for establishing that the proposed affinity hierarchy is a necessary and sufficient condition for the observed organization (R1-M2). 3) The incomplete quantitative description of the imaging data, particularly regarding the number of replicates and the statistical methods used for the dynamic measurements (R1-M3).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** **Originality:** High. The concept of amphiphilic proteins mediating interactions between distinct condensates is a novel and potentially influential idea. The study moves beyond describing individual condensates to understanding their inter-relationships. **Scientific importance:** High. The nucleolus-heterochromatin association is a conserved and functionally significant feature of nuclear architecture. Understanding its mechanistic basis has implications for development, aging, and disease. **Interdisciplinary readership:** Good. The work bridges cell biology, developmental biology, and biophysics. The modeling will appeal to physicists, while the imaging and genetics will appeal to biologists. **Technical soundness:** The imaging and genetic experiments appear largely sound, but the quantitative rigor and the causal evidence for Pitchoune need strengthening. The modeling is a useful tool but requires more rigorous validation. **Readability for nonspecialists:** The abstract and introduction are accessible. The results section is clear, but the modeling section may be challenging for non-physicists. The authors make a good effort to explain the concepts in plain language.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The core observations are novel and the conceptual framework is compelling. However, the causal role of Pitchoune and the robustness of the model need to be more firmly established. The manuscript would be significantly strengthened by addressing the concerns related to the specificity of the in vivo perturbation and the quantitative rigor of the analyses.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that Pitchoune is "required" for PCH-nucleolar associations is not fully supported by the evidence, as the only effective perturbation (maternal RNAi) prevents egg laying, and the tissue-specific knockdown shows severe developmental defects that could indirectly affect nuclear organization.
- The claim that the model "recapitulated" the in vivo phenotype is only qualitative. The model's ability to predict the specific quantitative aspects of the organization (e.g., the degree of PCH envelopment) is not demonstrated.
- The speculation that NPBs in mammalian oocytes are formed by a similar mechanism involving DDX-rich neocondensates is unsupported by any data presented in this manuscript.
- The statement that "disrupting affinity hierarchies between interacting condensates can liberate molecules to form neocondensates or other aberrant structures that could contribute to cellular disease phenotypes" is a reasonable hypothesis but is presented as a conclusion without direct experimental evidence linking the observed neocondensates to disease.
