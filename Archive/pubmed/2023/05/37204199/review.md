## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no full-text data, figures, or methods were available for evaluation
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that three types of graphene quantum dots (GQDs) prevent stress granule (SG) formation, promote SG disassembly, directly interact with the protein fused in sarcoma (FUS), inhibit and reverse FUS liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS), prevent abnormal phase transition, inhibit amyloid aggregation, and disaggregate preformed FUS fibrils. They further claim that edge-site differences among GQDs determine binding affinity to FUS monomers and fibrils, accounting for differential activity, and propose GQDs as potential therapeutic modulators of protein LLPS.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no experimental sections, figures, tables, or supplementary materials were provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, methods, quantitative data, statistical analyses, control experiments, characterization data for GQDs, and any in vivo or cellular validation beyond the abstract

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting and timely finding on the modulation of protein LLPS and SG dynamics by GQDs, with relevance to neurodegeneration. However, the claims are broad and mechanistic, and the abstract alone provides insufficient evidence to assess technical rigor, reproducibility, or the strength of the causal relationships asserted. The work may be of interest to the nanomedicine and phase separation communities, but the current evidence base does not establish the case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in protein phase separation, RNA-binding protein biology, neurodegeneration, and nanomaterial-based therapeutics would be interested. The potential to modulate SG dynamics and FUS aggregation with GQDs could appeal to those seeking small-molecule or nanomaterial interventions for ALS and related disorders, as well as to materials scientists exploring edge-chemistry-dependent biological activity.
- **Major strengths** The abstract addresses a timely and important problem, namely the dysregulation of LLPS in neurodegeneration. The proposed mechanism, linking GQD edge chemistry to differential binding and activity, is conceptually interesting and could provide design principles. The scope of activities reported, from SG modulation to fibril disaggregation, suggests a comprehensive experimental effort.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that GQDs "exhibit potent activity in preventing SG formation and promoting SG disassembly" and "directly interact with FUS."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract provides no quantitative data, experimental conditions, or controls to support the claimed potency of GQDs in modulating SG dynamics. No information is given on cell types, GQD concentrations, exposure times, or how SG formation and disassembly were measured.  
- **Why it matters** Without quantitative and controlled data, the claimed potency cannot be evaluated, and the biological relevance of the observed effects remains unclear.  
- **Resolution test** Provide dose-response curves, time-course data, and appropriate controls (e.g., untreated cells, non-GQD nanoparticles) in the full manuscript, with statistical analysis.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Mechanistic support  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "GQDs with different edge-site exhibit distinct binding affinity to FUS monomers and fibrils, which accounts for their distinct activities."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The mechanistic link between edge-site chemistry, binding affinity, and functional activity is asserted but not demonstrated. No binding data, structural analysis, or correlation between affinity and activity is presented.  
- **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism is central to the paper's novelty and potential design principles. Without direct evidence, the claim remains speculative.  
- **Resolution test** Include binding assays (e.g., ITC, SPR, or NMR), structural characterization of GQD edge sites, and a clear correlation between binding affinity and functional outcomes.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Claim scope  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that GQDs "prevent abnormal phase transition" and "disaggregate preformed FUS fibrils."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract does not specify the nature of the "abnormal phase transition" or provide evidence for fibril disaggregation, such as kinetic assays, microscopy, or toxicity data. The distinction between preventing aggregation and actively disaggregating mature fibrils is significant and requires distinct experimental support.  
- **Why it matters** These are strong claims with therapeutic implications. If disaggregation is not rigorously demonstrated, the conclusions may overstate the potential of GQDs.  
- **Resolution test** Provide thioflavin T assays, electron microscopy, or atomic force microscopy data showing fibril disassembly over time, along with controls for nonspecific effects.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Reproducibility and specificity  
- **Claim pointer** The authors imply that GQDs are "effective modulators of protein LLPS" with therapeutic potential.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** No information is provided on the specificity of GQDs for FUS versus other LLPS-prone proteins, nor on potential off-target effects, cytotoxicity, or in vivo behavior. The therapeutic claim is therefore unsupported.  
- **Why it matters** Specificity and safety are critical for any proposed therapeutic application. Without these data, the translational relevance cannot be assessed.  
- **Resolution test** Include experiments testing GQDs against other LLPS proteins, cell viability assays, and, if possible, preliminary in vivo data or a clear discussion of limitations.
