## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, and supplementary figure/table references as described in text)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific content, methodological validity, claims versus evidence, and presentation quality as presented in the supplied text. No external data or code were accessed.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present the Analytical Flory Random Coil (AFRC), a sequence-based analytical reference model for unfolded and disordered proteins. The model is parameterized from all-atom Flory Random Coil (FRC) Monte Carlo simulations, requires only amino acid sequence as input, and provides probability distributions for global and local conformational properties. The authors validate the AFRC against FRC simulations, compare it with existing polymer models, demonstrate its utility in identifying sequence-specific interactions in all-atom simulations, and apply it to a curated set of 145 experimental SAXS-derived radii of gyration.
- **Visible evidence base** Methods descriptions for FRC and excluded volume simulations; results sections describing parameterization, heteropolymer generalization, comparisons with existing models, all-atom simulation comparisons, and SAXS data analysis; references to supplementary figures (Fig. S1, S2, S4, S5, S6), tables (Table S1, S4), and external repositories (GitHub, PyPI, ReadTheDocs).
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures and tables (S1–S6, Table S1, Table S4) were not provided. The full list of 145 SAXS data points and their associated references (Table S4) was not accessible. Code implementation details and the Google Colab notebook were not reviewed. Specific numerical values for correlation coefficients, RMSEs, and parameter fits were only partially reported in the text.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a conceptually useful and practically accessible reference model for unfolded and disordered proteins. The AFRC addresses a genuine need in the field by providing a parameter-free (from the user perspective) analytical model that requires only sequence as input. The methodological approach is sound, and the validation against FRC simulations is convincing based on the reported correlation coefficients and RMSEs. The application to all-atom simulations and experimental SAXS data demonstrates practical utility. However, several technical details require clarification, and some claims regarding the model's performance and the interpretation of experimental comparisons need to be more carefully qualified. The manuscript is well-written and the figures are described clearly, but the lack of access to supplementary materials limits full verification of the results.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying intrinsically disordered proteins and unfolded states, particularly those using small-angle X-ray scattering, Förster resonance energy transfer, or molecular simulations. The model provides a simple, sequence-based reference for normalizing and interpreting experimental or computational data. The open-source implementation and Google Colab notebook make it accessible to a broad audience, including non-specialists in polymer physics. The work is also relevant to the broader biophysics and computational biology communities interested in sequence-ensemble relationships.
- **Major strengths** 1) The AFRC fills a practical gap by providing a parameter-free reference model that is easy to use. 2) The parameterization from explicit FRC simulations is rigorous and well-documented. 3) The validation against a large set of heteropolymeric sequences (320 sequences) is thorough. 4) The comparison with existing polymer models is informative and appropriately framed. 5) The application to experimental SAXS data with a curated dataset of 145 proteins is a valuable contribution. 6) The open-source implementation and user-friendly interface (Google Colab) enhance accessibility and reproducibility.
- **Major Concerns** 
  - R1-M1: The claim that the AFRC is "parameter-free" for the user is somewhat overstated. While the user does not need to input parameters, the model itself is parameterized, and the choice of FRC simulation parameters (e.g., allowed phi/psi angles, bond lengths) may influence results. The manuscript should more clearly distinguish between user-level and model-level parameters.
  - R1-M2: The comparison with experimental SAXS data, while useful, is presented in a way that could be misinterpreted. The authors state that the AFRC "outperforms" several established coarse-grained models, but this comparison is based on a single metric (correlation coefficient and RMSE) and may not account for differences in data quality, experimental conditions, or the specific models used. The claim should be qualified.
  - R1-M3: The empirical upper and lower bounds for biologically accessible radii of gyration (0.8RgAFRC to 1.45RgAFRC) are derived from a limited dataset and may not be universally applicable. The authors acknowledge this is not a hard threshold, but the presentation could be more cautious.
  - R1-M4: The manuscript does not provide a clear discussion of the limitations of the AFRC, particularly regarding sequences with strong secondary structure propensities or those that deviate significantly from ideal chain behavior. The model is explicitly for ideal chains, but the practical implications of this limitation for users should be discussed.
- **Minor Comments** 
  - R1-m1: The abstract states that the AFRC provides "direct access to probability distributions of global and local conformational order parameters." The term "order parameters" is vague and could be clarified (e.g., end-to-end distance, radius of gyration, contact probabilities).
  - R1-m2: The methods section describes FRC simulations but does not provide sufficient detail on the simulation protocol (e.g., temperature, number of replicas, convergence criteria). This information is likely in the supplementary materials but should be summarized in the main text.
  - R1-m3: The comparison with the WLC model uses a persistence length of 3.0 Å, which is relatively short. The authors should justify this choice or note that other values could be used.
  - R1-m4: The discussion of the SAXS data would benefit from a more explicit statement about the experimental conditions (e.g., presence of denaturant, pH, ionic strength) and how these might affect the comparison with the AFRC.
  - R1-m5: The manuscript mentions "dangling end" effects in self-avoiding chains (Fig. 1E) but does not explain the physical origin of this effect. A brief explanation would improve readability for non-specialists.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** 
  - R1-M2 (comparison with coarse-grained models) needs to be more carefully qualified or the comparison should be expanded to include more models and metrics.
  - R1-M4 (limitations of the AFRC) needs to be addressed with a dedicated discussion section.
  - The manuscript should provide more details on the FRC simulation protocol and the parameterization procedure in the main text or clearly reference the supplementary materials.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - Originality: The AFRC is a novel contribution in that it provides a sequence-based, parameter-free reference model, which is distinct from existing models that require user-defined parameters. The approach of parameterizing from explicit FRC simulations is original and well-executed.
  - Scientific importance: The model addresses a practical need in the field of disordered proteins and has the potential to be widely adopted. The demonstration of its utility in interpreting experimental data is important.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work is relevant to biophysicists, computational biologists, and experimentalists studying protein conformation. The open-source implementation and user-friendly interface enhance its accessibility.
  - Technical soundness: The methodological approach is sound, and the validation is thorough. However, some claims (e.g., outperforming coarse-grained models) are not fully supported by the evidence presented.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, but some sections (e.g., the derivation of the Lhuillier approximation) may be challenging for non-specialists. The use of figures and the Google Colab notebook helps.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a useful and well-validated model, but the concerns regarding the comparison with coarse-grained models and the discussion of limitations need to be addressed before the case is fully established.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the AFRC "outperforms" several established coarse-grained models is not fully supported by the evidence presented, as the comparison is based on a limited set of metrics and models.
- The empirical upper and lower bounds for biologically accessible radii of gyration (0.8RgAFRC to 1.45RgAFRC) are derived from a limited dataset and may not be universally applicable.
- The statement that the AFRC is "parameter-free" for the user is somewhat overstated, as the model itself is parameterized.
- The claim that the AFRC "faithfully reproduces" homo- and hetero-polymeric dimensions is supported by the reported correlation coefficients, but the specific numerical values for all comparisons are not fully reported in the main text.
- The manuscript states that the AFRC "provides direct access to probability distributions of global and local conformational order parameters," but the term "order parameters" is not clearly defined.
