## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including Significance, Results, Discussion, and Materials and Methods sections. Figures and supplementary materials referenced but not provided.
- **Assessment boundary** Assessment is limited to the scientific claims, methodology, and evidence presented in the provided text. Figures, tables, and supplementary information are referenced but were not available for direct inspection.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose that proximity to a critical point governs the surface tension of biomolecular condensates. Using coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulations of a sticker-spacer polymer model, they demonstrate that surface tension follows universal power-law scaling near criticality, that the critical regime extends over a physiologically relevant temperature range, and that polymer sequence effects on surface tension are mediated primarily through shifts in the critical temperature. They further show that a universal ratio relating surface tension to correlation length (via interface width) holds in their simulations, enabling surface tension prediction from a single interface width measurement.
- **Visible evidence base** Text descriptions of simulation results, including fitting of surface tension to Eq. 1, extraction of Tc and γ0, comparison of γ0 across block lengths, and validation of Eq. 5 against direct simulation. References to Fig. 2B, Fig. 3A-D, Fig. 4A-B, and SI Appendix figures are made but the figures themselves were not provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** All figures, tables, and supplementary information. Specific numerical values for surface tensions, critical temperatures, fit parameters, and error bars are not available. The SI Appendix content, including Fig. S2 and Fig. S7, is referenced but not provided. Simulation parameter details beyond those in the text are not fully specified.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a conceptually appealing and potentially impactful application of critical phenomena to biomolecular condensate physics. The central idea, that universal scaling laws near criticality can predict macroscopic properties like surface tension from minimal thermodynamic inputs, is well-motivated and timely. The simulation methodology is appropriate for the model system, and the authors have taken care to address finite-size effects and capillary wave contributions. However, the strength of the conclusions cannot be fully evaluated without access to the figures and numerical data. The claim that the critical regime extends over the full physiological range is based on a specific model and a limited set of block lengths; the generality of this finding requires more discussion. The demonstration of the universal ratio R- is a key result, but the validation appears to rely on a single comparison (Fig. 4A) and the robustness of this agreement across all systems studied is not clear from the text alone. The discussion of in vivo applicability is reasonable but speculative. Overall, the manuscript is well-written and the logic is clear, but the evidence base as presented is incomplete for a definitive assessment.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of biomolecular condensates, phase separation, soft matter physics, and biophysics. The work provides a framework for connecting microscopic sequence features to macroscopic material properties, which is a central goal in the condensate field. The use of universal critical scaling offers a potentially general predictive tool that could be of interest to both experimentalists and theorists. The connection between interface width and surface tension may also be of interest to those studying condensate dynamics and morphology.
- **Major strengths** 1. The conceptual framework is elegant and timely, applying well-established critical phenomena to a pressing problem in condensate biology. 2. The use of a minimal sticker-spacer model allows for systematic variation of sequence features (block length) and isolates the key physics. 3. The authors directly address the question of the size of the critical regime, which is crucial for biological relevance. 4. The demonstration that surface tension can be predicted from interface width via a universal ratio is practically useful and experimentally testable. 5. The manuscript is clearly written and the logical flow from model to results to implications is easy to follow.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence completeness
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "for all the systems studied the surface tension accurately obeys the pure power-law behavior in Eq. 1 until τ≈0.2 or even beyond" and that "the critical regime can be large enough to encompass the full physiological range (~25% of Tc)."
  - **Evidence pointer** Fig. 3A-D, location not provided
  - **Concern** The central quantitative claim regarding the extent of the critical regime is presented without the actual data. The figure is not available for inspection, and no numerical values for the goodness of fit, the range of τ over which the power law holds for each system, or the uncertainty in the extracted Tc and γ0 are provided in the text. Without this information, it is impossible to assess whether the claim of a large critical regime is robust or an artifact of the specific fitting procedure or model parameters.
  - **Why it matters** The biological relevance of the entire framework rests on the assertion that the critical regime extends over a physiologically relevant temperature range. If the power-law scaling only holds very close to Tc, the predictive utility of the approach for real condensates would be severely limited. The current evidence is insufficient to support this key claim.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the actual data for the scaled surface tension versus reduced temperature for all systems studied, including the fits and residuals. Quantify the range of τ over which the power law holds for each system, and report the uncertainties in the fitted parameters. Show that the conclusion is robust to the choice of fitting range and to the inclusion or exclusion of data points near Tc.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Claim support
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that "the full temperature-dependent surface tension can be recovered from Tc and one measurement of the interface width" and that "the critical amplitude γ0 for surface tension calculated using Eq. 5 agrees well with the simulation results."
  - **Evidence pointer** Fig. 4A-B, location not provided
  - **Concern** The validation of the universal ratio R- and the resulting predictive relationship (Eq. 5) is a central result of the paper. However, the text only states that the agreement is "good" without providing quantitative measures of the agreement. It is unclear how many systems were tested, what the typical error between the predicted and simulated γ0 is, and whether the agreement is uniformly good across all block lengths or degrades for certain parameter regimes. The comparison between γ0 from Eq. 5 and from direct simulation is only shown in one figure, and the robustness of this comparison is not discussed.
  - **Why it matters** The claim that surface tension can be predicted from a single interface width measurement is a strong and practically important statement. If the agreement is not quantitatively demonstrated, the utility of this relationship for experimental applications is not established.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a quantitative comparison between γ0 predicted from Eq. 5 and γ0 obtained from direct simulation for all systems studied. Report the percentage error for each system and the overall mean and standard deviation. Show the data in a parity plot or equivalent. Discuss any systematic deviations and their potential causes.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Generality
  - **Claim pointer** The authors conclude that "in these model systems proximity to criticality is the key determinant of surface tension" and anticipate that "thermodynamic relations will apply to a broad range of biomolecular condensates."
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section, location not provided
  - **Concern** The model system is highly idealized, with a specific interaction scheme (sticker-spacer with saturating heterotypic bonds) and a limited range of block lengths (ℓ=2 to 12). The authors do not discuss how variations in other sequence features, such as sticker strength, sticker density, or the presence of nonspecific interactions, might affect the size of the critical regime or the validity of the universal ratio. The claim of broad applicability to real condensates, which are compositionally complex and out of equilibrium, is a significant extrapolation from this idealized model.
  - **Why it matters** The generality of the conclusions is a key selling point of the paper. If the findings are specific to the chosen model or parameter range, their relevance to real biological systems would be diminished. The authors do address some of these points in the Discussion, but the arguments are largely qualitative and speculative.
  - **Resolution test** Discuss the expected dependence of the critical regime size and the universal ratio on model parameters, such as interaction strength, sticker density, and chain length. If possible, show preliminary data for a wider range of parameters or for a model with additional interaction types. Acknowledge the limitations of the idealized model more explicitly and outline what would be needed to test the predictions in more realistic systems.

However, the manuscript cannot be accepted in its current form because the key quantitative claims are not supported by the visible evidence. ## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the critical regime extends over the full physiological range is not supported by the visible evidence, as the relevant figure and numerical data are not provided.
- The claim that the universal ratio R- holds for the model systems is not quantitatively supported, as the comparison between predicted and simulated γ0 is only described qualitatively.
- The claim that the findings will apply to a broad range of biomolecular condensates, including those in living cells, is an extrapolation from an idealized model and is not directly supported by the presented evidence.
- The statement that "the low concentration of the dense phase (<10% volume fraction) means that this overlap has essentially no effect on equilibrium properties" is an assertion that is not directly tested or supported by data in the provided text.
