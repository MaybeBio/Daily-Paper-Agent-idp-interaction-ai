## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence as presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report a combined in silico and physicochemical study of the interaction between the myelin proteolipid plasmolipin (PLLP) and ganglioside GM1 in lipid raft environments. They claim that GM1 induces structuring of PLLP extracellular loops, that this interaction propagates a conformational signal across the membrane involving a cholesterol molecule between transmembrane domains, and that this signal is transmitted to the intracellular domain, supporting a role in signal transduction. They further frame the work as an example of an "epigenetic dimension of protein structure" and propose therapeutic relevance for PLLP-associated diseases.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; methods described as molecular dynamics simulations and microtensiometry on ganglioside monolayers; no quantitative results, figures, or statistical details provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, simulation parameters, experimental protocols, raw data, statistical analyses, and any supplementary information

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents an ambitious and potentially interesting hypothesis regarding ganglioside-mediated conformational coupling in PLLP, but the evidence base visible in the abstract is insufficient to evaluate the validity of the central claims. The mechanistic narrative, particularly the "conformational wave" and cholesterol-mediated signal propagation, is stated without quantitative support or methodological detail. The work may be of interest to membrane biophysicists and neurobiologists, but the current abstract does not establish the case.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying myelin biology, lipid raft organization, membrane protein-lipid interactions, and ganglioside function. Those interested in the molecular basis of neurological disorders, type 2 diabetes, and cancer metastasis may also find the proposed PLLP-ganglioside interaction relevant, though the abstract does not provide disease-specific mechanistic links.
- **Major strengths** The study combines computational and experimental approaches, which is a strength if properly integrated. The focus on ganglioside-induced protein structuring and long-range conformational coupling is a timely and mechanistically interesting topic. The potential pathophysiological relevance of PLLP adds translational significance.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract lacks quantitative results, simulation details, and experimental validation of the proposed conformational wave. The claim of signal propagation across the membrane is not supported by any visible evidence. The "epigenetic dimension" framing is not defined or justified. The therapeutic implications are speculative without disease-specific data.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract would benefit from clearer definitions of key terms, such as "conformational wave" and "epigenetic dimension of protein structure." The relationship between monolayer microtensiometry and the proposed full-membrane conformational coupling is not explained. The abstract does not state the number of simulations, replicates, or statistical measures.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** No quantitative data on interaction affinities, structural changes, or simulation convergence are provided. The link between monolayer experiments and the claimed transmembrane signal is not established. The role of cholesterol is asserted but not demonstrated.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The concept of ganglioside-mediated conformational coupling is not entirely novel, but the specific application to PLLP may be original. Scientific importance: Potentially high if the claims are validated, but the abstract does not provide sufficient evidence to assess impact. Interdisciplinary readership: The topic bridges biophysics, neurobiology, and lipid biology, which could attract broad interest. Technical soundness: Cannot be assessed from the abstract; no methodological details or results are visible. Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is reasonably clear but uses jargon without definition, which may limit accessibility.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract presents a plausible hypothesis but does not provide the data needed to support the central mechanistic claims. A revised version with quantitative results and clearer methodological context would be required for a supportive posture.

- **Severity** Major

- **Claim pointer** The authors use microtensiometry on ganglioside monolayers to measure PLLP-ganglioside interactions and infer full-membrane conformational effects.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Methods section; location not provided

- **Resolution test** Clarify how monolayer measurements inform the bilayer model, or provide complementary bilayer-based experiments, such as vesicle assays or electrophysiology, to support the conformational coupling claim.
