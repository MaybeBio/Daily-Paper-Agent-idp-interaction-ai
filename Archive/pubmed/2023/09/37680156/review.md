## Reviewer

- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a reasonable premise for a review article, namely that MDS applications in leishmaniasis research are under-documented compared to other diseases. However, the abstract provides insufficient methodological detail and no quantitative or qualitative evidence to support the central claims. The stated findings, such as the limited number of vaccine studies and the absence of PCA and FEL analyses, are presented without supporting data or context. The recommendation to adopt PCA and FEL is plausible but not justified within the abstract. The scientific contribution cannot be adequately assessed from the supplied material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational biologists, structural biologists, and medicinal chemists working on neglected tropical diseases, particularly those applying molecular dynamics to drug target characterization, vaccine epitope design, or protein interaction studies. The review could serve as a reference point for researchers seeking to apply MDS in leishmaniasis and for methodologists interested in identifying gaps in simulation practice.
- **Major strengths** The topic is timely and relevant, as MDS is increasingly used in infectious disease research. The abstract identifies a specific gap in the literature, namely the limited application of MDS to vaccine development for leishmaniasis. The recommendation regarding PCA and FEL is specific and actionable.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract lacks any description of the search strategy, inclusion criteria, or number of studies reviewed. The claim that only a limited number of studies focused on vaccine development is not quantified. The statement that PCA and FEL were not carried out in most studies is presented without a denominator or examples. The rationale for why PCA and FEL are "globally accepted utilities" is asserted rather than argued. The abstract does not state the time frame of the literature search, which limits reproducibility and currency.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract uses the term "MDS" without defining it at first use, though the full manuscript likely does so. The phrase "appears to be used in all possible ways" is vague and could be replaced with a more precise statement. The final sentence is a recommendation but is not tied to any specific evidence from the reviewed studies.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The absence of methodological transparency in the abstract, including search dates, databases, and study selection criteria, prevents verification of the review's scope and completeness. The lack of quantitative support for the two main findings (limited vaccine studies, absence of PCA and FEL) means the central claims are not established from the provided evidence.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The premise is moderately original, as a focused review of MDS in leishmaniasis is not common, but the abstract does not demonstrate a novel framework or insight. Scientific importance: The topic is of importance to a niche community, but the abstract does not articulate broader implications for drug discovery or vaccine development beyond a general recommendation. Interdisciplinary readership: The abstract is accessible to computational and structural biologists but may not attract a wider audience without clearer statements of translational relevance. Technical soundness: Cannot be assessed from the abstract alone, as no methods or data are presented. Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is readable but uses field-specific terms such as PCA and FEL without explanation, which may limit accessibility.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract alone does not provide sufficient methodological or quantitative detail to support the review's conclusions. A revised abstract with explicit search parameters, study counts, and evidence for the PCA and FEL gap would be required for a supportive posture.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Methodological transparency
- **Claim pointer** The review "systematically collected articles" illustrating MDS implementation in drug discovery, vaccine development, and structural studies related to leishmaniasis.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract claims a systematic collection of articles but provides no details on the search strategy, databases used, inclusion and exclusion criteria, or the time period covered. Without this information, the claim of systematicity cannot be verified.
- **Why it matters** A review's value depends on the reproducibility and completeness of its literature search. If the search was not systematic or was limited in scope, the conclusions may be biased or incomplete, undermining the review's utility as a reference.
- **Resolution test** Provide in the abstract or manuscript a clear description of the search methodology, including databases, search terms, date range, and the number of articles screened and included.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence support for findings
- **Claim pointer** "Only a limited number of studies focused on the development of vaccines against Leishmaniasis through MDS" and "PCA and FEL studies were not carried out in most of the studies."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** These two findings are stated without any quantitative support. The abstract does not report the total number of studies reviewed, the number of vaccine-related studies, or the proportion of studies that omitted PCA and FEL analyses.
- **Why it matters** Without numbers or examples, the reader cannot assess the magnitude of the identified gaps or the strength of the conclusions. The claims may be accurate, but they are not evidenced in the abstract.
- **Resolution test** Include specific counts or percentages in the abstract, such as "of X studies reviewed, Y focused on vaccine design" and "Z percent of studies did not perform PCA or FEL analysis."

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** No
- **Axis** Justification of recommendation
- **Claim pointer** "PCA and FEL were globally accepted utilities to understand the conformational changes and hence it is recommended that this analysis should be taken up in similar approaches in the future."
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract, location not provided
- **Concern** The recommendation to adopt PCA and FEL is presented as a conclusion, but the abstract does not explain why these methods are superior or how their absence has limited the reviewed studies. The claim that they are "globally accepted" is an assertion without citation or argument.
- **Why it matters** A recommendation without justification is not persuasive. If the authors believe PCA and FEL are essential, they should explain what insights these methods provide that were missed in the reviewed studies.
- **Resolution test** Add a sentence in the abstract or manuscript that explains the specific value of PCA and FEL in the context of leishmaniasis targets, with examples from the reviewed literature or general method principles.
