## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract, as supplied
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that aqueous mixtures of oppositely charged homochiral polypeptides (poly-L-lysine and poly-L-glutamic acid) exhibit diverse morphologies, including partially coalescing and aggregated droplets, bulk precipitates, and a previously unreported re-entrant liquid-liquid phase separation at high polypeptide concentration and ionic strength. They combine experimental observations with all-atom molecular dynamics simulations to propose that the relative composition of beta-sheet-rich secondary structure correlates with macroscale morphology, and they argue this reveals a balance of interactions important for controlling coacervation morphology.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only. No figures, tables, methods, or simulation details are provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, experimental methods, characterization data (e.g., microscopy, spectroscopy), simulation parameters and validation, quantitative analysis linking secondary structure to morphology, and any statistical or reproducibility information.

## Reviewer

However, the evidence base available for review is limited to the abstract, and the central claim of a concentration-dependent secondary structure composition correlating with morphology is stated without quantitative support. The re-entrant phase separation is described as previously unreported, which is a strong claim that requires robust experimental validation. The work may be of interest to researchers in biomolecular assembly and biomaterials, but the case is not established from the supplied material.

The combination of experimental and simulation approaches is appropriate for probing molecular-level mechanisms. The reported diversity of morphologies suggests a rich phase behavior that could have broad implications.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Evidence strength  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "a concentration dependence of beta-sheet-rich secondary structure, whose relative composition correlates with the observed macroscale morphologies of the mixtures."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract states a correlation between secondary structure composition and morphology but provides no quantitative data, such as beta-sheet fraction measurements (e.g., from circular dichroism or infrared spectroscopy) or a statistical analysis of the correlation. The simulation results are described only as "revealing" a concentration dependence, without details on how the simulations were analyzed or how they relate to experimental conditions.  
- **Why it matters** The central mechanistic claim of the paper rests on this correlation. Without quantitative evidence, the reader cannot evaluate whether the relationship is robust, causal, or merely coincidental.  
- **Resolution test** Provide quantitative measurements of beta-sheet content across a range of concentrations and ionic strengths, with corresponding morphological characterization, and a statistical test of the correlation. Simulation results should include error estimates and a clear mapping to experimental observables.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Claim novelty  
- **Claim pointer** The authors report "a previously unreported re-entrant liquid-liquid phase separation at high polypeptide concentration and ionic strength."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** The claim of a previously unreported phenomenon is strong and requires a thorough literature search and clear phase diagrams showing the re-entrant behavior. The abstract does not provide any phase boundary data, nor does it indicate how the re-entrant region was identified (e.g., turbidity, microscopy, rheology).  
- **Why it matters** If the re-entrant phase separation is not robustly demonstrated, the novelty claim is unsupported, and the broader significance of the work is diminished.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a complete phase diagram with clear boundaries for the re-entrant region, supported by multiple independent experimental techniques, and a discussion of prior literature that confirms the absence of this observation.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Technical soundness  
- **Claim pointer** The authors state that "combining our experimental results with all-atom molecular dynamics simulations of folded polypeptide complexes" supports the proposed mechanism.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
- **Concern** No details are given on the simulation methodology, including force field, system size, simulation time, equilibration, or how the simulations were validated against experimental data. The phrase "folded polypeptide complexes" is ambiguous and does not clarify whether the simulations represent the actual solution conditions or idealized states.  
- **Why it matters** The simulation results are used to support the mechanistic interpretation. Without methodological transparency, the reliability of the simulations cannot be assessed, and the conclusions drawn from them are not verifiable.  
- **Resolution test** Include full simulation methods, convergence checks, and a comparison of simulated secondary structure propensities with experimental measurements across the same conditions.
