## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, methods, results, discussion, conclusions) without supplementary figures or tables
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, experimental design, data interpretation, and theoretical modeling as presented in the provided text
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors investigate how polyethylene glycol (PEG) crowders of varying molecular weights modulate the conformations and RNA binding of the intrinsically disordered N-terminal tail (NTD) of the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein using single-molecule FRET. They report that high-molecular-weight PEG compacts the disordered tail, low-molecular-weight PEG expands it, and binding affinity for (rU)40 shows a nonmonotonic dependence on PEG molecular weight. They interpret these effects through a balance of excluded volume screening and weak protein–crowder interactions, supported by a polymer model and van't Hoff analysis. They further show that crowding reduces the binding affinity difference between Wuhan and Omicron variants.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures referenced (Figures 2, 3, 4, 5, 6), supplementary figures and tables cited but not provided (Figures S1–S13, Tables S3–S11), methods section
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary Information (detailed model derivations, fitting procedures, all supplementary figures and tables), raw FRET distributions, experimental protocols for temperature-dependent measurements, mutation details for variants, RNA binding titration data

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a systematic single-molecule FRET study of how PEG crowders of different molecular weights affect the conformations and RNA binding of an intrinsically disordered tail. The experimental design is thoughtful, spanning a wide range of crowder sizes and concentrations, and the authors appropriately apply polymer theory to interpret their observations. The finding that low-molecular-weight PEG expands the disordered tail while high-molecular-weight PEG compacts it, and the nonmonotonic effect on RNA binding affinity, are potentially interesting contributions to the crowding field. However, the manuscript as provided has several limitations that prevent full evaluation: the key theoretical model is only summarized in the main text, the supplementary data supporting central claims are not available for assessment, and some interpretations appear to extend beyond what the presented evidence can support. The temperature-dependent analysis is a strength, but the error bars on derived thermodynamic quantities are large, and the conclusions regarding entropic versus enthalpic contributions are correspondingly tentative. The comparison between variants is intriguing but based on a single crowder condition. Overall, the work is likely of interest to the biophysics and intrinsically disordered protein communities, but the strength of the conclusions depends critically on the supplementary material that was not provided.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying intrinsically disordered proteins, macromolecular crowding, and the physical chemistry of cellular environments would find this work relevant. The study addresses a long-standing question about how crowders of different sizes affect disordered protein conformations and provides experimental data that can test polymer theory predictions. The SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid connection adds relevance for virologists interested in the biophysical context of viral replication. The methodological approach combining single-molecule FRET with polymer models and van't Hoff analysis will interest biophysicists developing quantitative frameworks for crowding effects.
- **Major strengths** The systematic variation of PEG molecular weight from 50 to 35,000 Da is a well-designed approach that allows discrimination between different crowding regimes. The use of single-molecule FRET provides distribution information that ensemble methods cannot access, and the nsFCS control experiments appropriately rule out folding or specific interactions with the folded domain. The application of the Schellman weak binding model to extract interaction parameters from the data is theoretically grounded. The temperature-dependent measurements represent a genuine effort to separate enthalpic and entropic contributions, which is rarely attempted in crowding studies. The comparison between Wuhan and Omicron variants under crowded conditions is a creative and biologically relevant extension.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that their data can be explained by two opposing contributions: favorable protein–crowder interactions and screening of excluded volume interactions, and that the extracted interaction parameter ε follows the Schellman weak binding model.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 3C, Table S5, Equation 1, "location not provided" for model derivation
  - **Concern** The central theoretical interpretation rests entirely on the model summarized in Equation 1 and the fitting results in Table S5, neither of which can be evaluated from the provided text. The model derivation is relegated to the Supporting Information, and the main text provides only a brief verbal description. Without seeing the assumptions, approximations, and fitting procedures, it is impossible to assess whether the model is appropriately applied, whether the extracted ε values are reliable, or whether alternative models could equally explain the data. The claim that the model "can be summarized as" Equation 1 is insufficient for a rigorous evaluation of the central interpretive framework.
  - **Why it matters** The theoretical modeling is not peripheral but central to the manuscript's main conclusions about the balance between excluded volume and weak interactions. If the model is misapplied or the fitting is not robust, the entire interpretive framework collapses. The authors need to present the model derivation and fitting details in a way that allows independent assessment.
  - **Resolution test** Provide the full model derivation, fitting procedures, and goodness-of-fit metrics in the main text or a clearly accessible supplement. Show that the model uniquely fits the data and that alternative models (e.g., purely entropic depletion without interaction terms) are explicitly ruled out by statistical comparison.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that crowding reduces the binding affinity difference between Wuhan and Omicron variants from a factor of 2.7 to nearly identical values (0.7 ± 0.1) in the presence of 15% w/v PEG 600.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 6, Figure S12, Table S11, "location not provided" for titration data
  - **Concern** This is a striking and biologically significant claim, but the evidence presented is minimal. The main text states the association constants for both variants in the presence and absence of PEG 600, but no titration curves, binding isotherms, or statistical details are shown. The error on the ratio in the presence of PEG 600 (0.7 ± 0.1) is substantial, and it is unclear whether the difference from 1.0 is statistically significant. Moreover, only a single crowder condition is tested, so the claim that crowding "normalizes" variant differences is based on one data point. The biological interpretation that this reflects context-dependent function is speculative without additional crowder concentrations or conditions.
  - **Why it matters** The variant comparison is one of the most novel and potentially impactful findings, with implications for understanding how the same protein might behave differently in different cellular environments. If this claim is not robustly supported, the manuscript loses a significant portion of its broader relevance.
  - **Resolution test** Show full binding titration curves for both variants in the presence and absence of PEG 600, with replicates and appropriate statistical tests demonstrating that the affinity difference is indeed abolished. Ideally, test at least one additional crowder concentration or molecular weight to show the effect is not coincidental.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Interpretive rigor
  - **Claim pointer** The authors state that the temperature-dependent behavior of protein conformations in the presence of different PEGs indicates that the specific temperature response is due to excluded-volume screening effects, while Δε increases with temperature for all three crowding agents.
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 5G,H, Figures S6–S8, "location not provided" for temperature-dependent FRET data
  - **Concern** The interpretation that the temperature response is dominated by excluded-volume effects while Δε captures interaction contributions is presented with confidence, but the error bars on the derived quantities appear large (e.g., ΔΔHexp = −5 ± 5 kcal/mol for PEG 600). The authors acknowledge that exact quantification would require sequence variants, but the qualitative conclusion that excluded-volume screening drives the temperature response is not directly tested. The separation of entropic and enthalpic contributions via van't Hoff analysis assumes that the binding mechanism does not change over the temperature range, which is not explicitly validated.
  - **Why it matters** The manuscript's contribution to understanding the physical origins of crowding effects depends on correctly attributing observed changes to entropic versus enthalpic mechanisms. If the attribution is not robust, the conclusions about the balance of forces are weakened.
  - **Resolution test** Provide confidence intervals for all derived thermodynamic quantities and demonstrate that the qualitative conclusions are robust to fitting procedures. Consider testing whether the van't Hoff assumption of constant enthalpy holds by examining residuals or using an alternative analysis method.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Generalizability
  - **Claim pointer** The authors generalize their findings to suggest that the observed balance of excluded volume and interaction effects provides insights into how disordered regions behave in cellular environments, and that the nucleocapsid protein may experience different crowding conditions in the cytoplasm versus the virion.
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section, "Implications for SARS-CoV-2"
  - **Concern** The extrapolation from PEG solutions to cellular environments is made with limited caveats. PEG is a specific polymer with particular chemical properties, and the authors themselves note that PEG is not inert. The claim that the protein "may experience different crowding when it is in the cytoplasm of the infected cell compared to when it is inside the virion" is speculative, as the actual cytoplasmic environment contains a complex mixture of macromolecules with diverse properties. The rough estimate of virion volume fraction (1–2%) is acknowledged as approximate, but the functional implications drawn from this difference are not well constrained.
  - **Why it matters** The broader significance of the work for understanding disordered proteins in cells depends on the relevance of the PEG model system. Overstating the connection to cellular conditions could mislead readers about the applicability of these findings.
  - **Resolution test** Add explicit caveats about the limitations of PEG as a crowder model and the assumptions involved in extrapolating to cellular conditions. If possible, include preliminary data with a different crowder or a more complex crowder mixture to test the generality of the findings.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The central theoretical model (Equation 1) and its application cannot be evaluated without the Supporting Information. The variant comparison claim (R1-M2) requires full binding data and statistical analysis. The temperature-dependent analysis needs explicit validation of the van't Hoff assumption and reporting of confidence intervals. The manuscript relies heavily on supplementary material for essential details, which is acceptable for a journal like JPC B but makes evaluation of the main claims difficult from the provided text alone.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The work addresses a question that has been explored in previous studies (including the authors' own prior work), but the specific focus on a disordered tail attached to a folded domain and the systematic variation of PEG molecular weight provides a degree of novelty. The finding of nonmonotonic binding affinity as a function of crowder size is interesting. Scientific importance: The results contribute to understanding crowding effects on disordered proteins, which is an active area of research. However, the significance is incremental rather than transformative, as the qualitative framework of excluded volume versus interaction effects is already established. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges polymer physics, biophysics, and virology, which could attract readers from multiple fields. Technical soundness: The experimental approach is sound in principle, but the inability to evaluate the model fitting and the large uncertainties in thermodynamic quantities temper confidence. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, though the theoretical sections assume familiarity with polymer physics concepts. Overall, the work is solid but would not meet the bar for Nature-level novelty or broad significance; it is more appropriate for a specialized biophysics journal.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The experimental design and data collection appear thoughtful, and the findings are potentially valuable to the crowding and disordered protein communities. However, the central theoretical interpretation and the variant comparison claim cannot be fully evaluated from the provided material. If the Supporting Information adequately addresses the concerns raised, particularly regarding model fitting and statistical rigor, the manuscript could make a solid contribution to JPC B.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the data can be explained by two opposing contributions (favorable interactions and excluded volume screening) is not fully supported without access to the model derivation and fitting details.
- The claim that crowding reduces the binding affinity difference between Wuhan and Omicron variants to nearly identical values is based on limited data and requires full titration curves and statistical analysis.
- The interpretation that the temperature response is due to excluded-volume screening effects is not directly tested and relies on assumptions about the van't Hoff analysis.
- The extrapolation from PEG solutions to cellular environments and the implications for SARS-CoV-2 biology are speculative and not directly supported by the data.
- The statement that the "correlation molecular weight" (Pc = 900 ± 200 Da) represents the point where end-group interactions become negligible is an empirical fit parameter without clear physical validation.
