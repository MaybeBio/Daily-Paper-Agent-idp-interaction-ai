## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a plausible mechanistic hypothesis linking IDR charge properties to oligomer stability and droplet fluidity. However, the evidence base is limited to a summary with no quantitative data, no statistical reporting, and no methodological detail. The central claim that biochemically subtle differences in oligomer stability have functional consequences for droplet quality is intriguing but cannot be evaluated from the supplied material. The work may be of interest to the phase separation and nucleolus communities, but the case is not established from the abstract alone.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers studying liquid-liquid phase separation, nucleolar organization, intrinsically disordered proteins, and post-translational regulation of membraneless organelles. The proposed link between phosphorylation-driven charge modulation and droplet material properties would appeal to those investigating how biochemical modifications tune phase behavior in vivo.
- **Major strengths** The hypothesis is mechanistically coherent, connecting IDR charge, oligomer stability, and macroscopic droplet properties. The inclusion of phosphorylation as a regulatory input adds biological relevance. The acknowledgment that oligomer stability differences are only observed under stringent conditions is an honest framing of the subtlety of the effect.
- **Major Concerns**  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1  
  - **Severity** Major  
  - **Blocking** Yes  
  - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency  
  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that acidic residues within the IDRs attenuate oligomer stability and that this effect is augmented by phosphorylation.  
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided  
  - **Concern** No quantitative data are presented to support the claimed differences in oligomer stability. The abstract states that differences were observed only under stringent conditions, but no magnitude, reproducibility, or statistical significance is reported.  
  - **Why it matters** Without quantitative evidence, the reader cannot assess whether the reported effect is robust or within experimental noise. The claim that a subtle biochemical difference has functional consequences requires clear demonstration of effect size and consistency.  
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative measurements of oligomer stability (e.g., dissociation constants, thermal or chemical denaturation profiles) with replicates and statistical analysis, showing significant differences between wild-type and mutant or phosphorylated variants under defined conditions.  
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2  
  - **Severity** Major

  - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that decreased oligomer stability leads to increased fluidity of liquid droplets formed by NPM1.

  - **Concern** The abstract asserts a causal relationship between oligomer stability and droplet fluidity, but no experimental data are shown to support this link.
