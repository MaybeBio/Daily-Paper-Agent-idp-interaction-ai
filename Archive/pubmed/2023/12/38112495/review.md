## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (abstract, introduction, results, discussion, conclusion, methods) without supplementary figures, tables, or movies
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific claims, methodological soundness, and evidence consistency as presented in the main text; supplementary materials referenced but not independently verifiable
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors present a GPU-accelerated version of their HyRes coarse-grained protein model (HyRes-GPU) with an atomistic backbone representation, and demonstrate its utility for simulating phase separation of intrinsically disordered proteins. They report that HyRes-GPU reproduces experimental Rg values for a panel of IDPs, recapitulates mutational effects on GY-23 phase behavior, predicts increased β-structure in condensates, and captures the correlation between helicity and condensate stability for TDP-43 CR. Mechanistic analyses suggest that side chain hydrophobicity and backbone availability, rather than helicity itself, govern phase separation propensity.
- **Visible evidence base** Main text figures referenced (Figures 1–7), tables referenced (Tables S1–S5 in supplementary), equations referenced (Flory-Huggins fitting via FIREBALL), simulation protocols described in Methods
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Supplementary figures (S1–S16), tables (S1–S5), and movies (S1–S5) are referenced but not provided; detailed parameter values for the recalibrated backbone LJ terms are not given in the main text; convergence criteria for phase equilibrium simulations are described qualitatively

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a genuine and timely need in the field of biomolecular condensate simulation: the development of computationally tractable models that retain sufficient chemical detail to capture backbone-mediated interactions and secondary structure propensities. The HyRes-GPU model represents a logical and valuable extension of the authors' prior HyRes work, and the application to GY-23 and TDP-43 CR provides biologically relevant test cases. The central claims are largely supported by the evidence presented, though several technical details and validations require clarification. The mechanistic insight regarding the role of helicity versus side chain chemistry in TDP-43 phase separation is interesting and potentially important, but the evidence for this conclusion relies on indirect manipulations (artificial dihedral restraints) that warrant careful scrutiny. Overall, the work is technically sound in its core methodology and the conclusions are generally consistent with the presented data, but some concerns about parameter transferability and the interpretation of the helicity correlation remain.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of computational biophysics, intrinsically disordered proteins, and biomolecular condensates would find this work of direct interest. The development of a CG model that can capture secondary structure coupling to phase separation addresses a recognized limitation of existing coarse-grained approaches. The specific findings on GY-23 and TDP-43 may also interest experimental groups studying these systems, particularly those focused on the molecular determinants of phase separation and the role of aromatic residues. The methodological advance (GPU acceleration enabling microsecond-scale simulations of multi-chain systems) will be of practical value to the simulation community.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript addresses a well-defined and important problem: the need for CG models that can capture backbone-mediated interactions in IDP phase separation. The GPU implementation is a practical and significant advance, enabling simulations that would otherwise be computationally prohibitive. The validation against experimental Rg data for a diverse set of IDPs is appropriate and convincing. The application to GY-23, including the recapitulation of single-point mutation effects, provides a strong test of the model's sensitivity. The TDP-43 study is biologically relevant and the mechanistic analysis goes beyond simple correlation to propose a specific physical explanation. The writing is clear and the logical flow from model development to validation to application is effective.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Technical soundness
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that HyRes-GPU "semi-quantitatively capture[s] the residue helical propensity and overall chain dimension of monomeric IDPs" is supported by comparison to experimental Rg and NMR-derived helicity, but the recalibration of backbone LJ parameters (removal of SASA term) is described only briefly.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "GPU acceleration of the HyRes protein model"; Figure 1C–D; Figure 2A–B; Table S1
  - **Concern** The recalibration of backbone LJ parameters to compensate for removal of the SASA term is a significant modification to the energy function. The validation is performed on only two model peptides ((AAQAA)3 and KID) for the recalibration, and the transferability of these parameters to diverse IDP sequences is not fully established. The authors report a Pearson correlation of r = 0.66 for Rg across 26 IDPs, which is reasonable but notably lower than some Rg-optimized models. The helicity correlation coefficients vary widely (r > 0.5 in 5 of 10 cases, but < 0.1 in others), and the authors acknowledge that low-helicity cases are problematic. The claim of "semi-quantitative" accuracy is appropriate, but the limitations of the model for predicting sequence-specific helicity in low-propensity regions should be more explicitly discussed.
  - **Why it matters** The central value proposition of HyRes-GPU is its ability to capture backbone-mediated interactions and secondary structure propensities. If the recalibrated parameters introduce sequence-dependent biases, the model's utility for studying the coupling between secondary structure and phase separation would be compromised. The validation set is relatively small and the correlation coefficients are modest, so the robustness of the model across diverse IDP sequences remains uncertain.
  - **Resolution test** Provide additional validation of the recalibrated backbone parameters on a larger and more diverse set of IDPs with experimentally characterized helicity profiles. Report the performance of HyRes-GPU on IDPs with known β-structure propensities. Quantitatively compare the backbone conformational distributions from HyRes-GPU to atomistic simulations for a representative set of sequences.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Evidence quality
  - **Claim pointer** The claim that "HyRes simulations also successfully predict increased β-structure formation in the condensate, consistent with available experimental CD data" is based on analysis of GY-23 simulations, but the quantitative comparison to CD data is not presented in the main text.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "Molecular basis of GY-23 phase separation"; Figure 5C–F; Figure S8–S9
  - **Concern** The increase in β-structure in the condensed phase is reported as a qualitative observation (Figure 5C), and the comparison to experimental CD data is stated but not quantified. The authors report that "almost 35% of the peptides are involved in β-bridges" and that "over half of these are intermolecular ones," but the relationship between these simulation-derived quantities and the CD signal is not established. The CD data referenced (from a previous study of HBP-1) is from a longer construct, and the direct comparability to GY-23 simulations is unclear.
  - **Why it matters** The coupling between β-structure formation and phase separation is a central claim of the manuscript. Without a quantitative link between the simulation observables and the experimental CD measurements, the strength of this claim is limited. The interpretation of β-bridges as contributors to condensate stabilization is plausible but requires more direct evidence.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a quantitative comparison between simulated β-structure content (e.g., fraction of residues in β-sheet or β-bridge conformations) and experimental CD-derived estimates for GY-23 under comparable conditions. If direct comparison is not feasible, clearly state the limitations of the comparison and provide additional simulation-based evidence for the role of β-structures in condensate stabilization (e.g., energetic analysis or mutation studies).
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Interpretation
  - **Claim pointer** The conclusion that "the balance between backbone and sidechain-mediated interactions, but not helicity itself, actually determines phase separation propensity" is based on the TDP-43 CR analysis, including artificial mutants with imposed dihedral restraints.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "How residual helicity modulates TDP-43 CR saturation concentration"; Figure 7B–C; Figure S13–S16
  - **Concern** The conclusion that helicity itself does not directly promote phase separation is derived from the observation that artificially increasing helicity (WT+ΔHel) increases Csat, while the G335A mutation (which increases helicity and hydrophobicity) decreases Csat. However, the artificial dihedral restraints used to create WT+ΔHel may have unintended effects on the conformational ensemble beyond simply increasing helicity. The authors do not fully address whether the restraints alter backbone dynamics or side chain packing in ways that could independently affect phase behavior. Additionally, the conclusion that "helicity itself" is not important is somewhat at odds with the observation that G335A and G338A, which increase helicity, do reduce Csat. The authors attribute this to the hydrophobic side chain, but the relative contributions of helicity and side chain chemistry are not cleanly separated.
  - **Why it matters** The mechanistic conclusion is a key contribution of the manuscript and has implications for how the field thinks about the role of secondary structure in phase separation. If the conclusion is overstated or the interpretation is not fully supported, it could mislead future research directions.
  - **Resolution test** Perform additional control simulations to assess the effects of the dihedral restraints on the conformational ensemble (e.g., compare the distribution of backbone dihedrals, side chain contacts, and overall chain dimensions between WT+ΔHel and G335A). Consider designing mutations that alter helicity without changing side chain chemistry (e.g., using D-amino acids or other backbone modifications) to more cleanly separate the effects of helicity from side chain interactions.

  - **Issue** The description of the phase equilibrium simulation protocol states that "multiple copies of constant volume and temperature (NVT) simulations were then performed at every 5 K from 260 K to 320 K for 2 μs," but the number of independent simulations per temperature is not specified for GY-23.   - **Evidence pointer** Figure 1B, location not provided

  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Completeness
  - **Affected element** Results section "Phase separation of TDP-43: correlation between helicity and condensate stability"
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 6B, location not provided
  - **Issue** The comparison between simulated and experimental Csat values is presented as a correlation (r = 0.77), but the experimental values are from TDP-43 CTD constructs while the simulations use the CR domain alone. The potential impact of this construct difference on the comparison is not discussed.
  - **Required correction** Add a brief discussion of the potential effects of using the CR domain versus the full CTD on the Csat comparison, and note any limitations this imposes on the interpretation.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** The recalibration of backbone parameters is validated on a limited set of peptides; the quantitative link between simulated β-structure and experimental CD data is not established; the artificial dihedral restraint approach for separating helicity effects from side chain effects requires additional validation; the number of replicate simulations for GY-23 phase equilibrium is not clearly stated.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
  - **Originality** The work is original in its specific contribution: a GPU-accelerated CG model with explicit backbone representation applied to phase separation. The concept of hybrid-resolution models is not new, but the application to condensate simulations with attention to secondary structure coupling is a meaningful advance.
  - **Scientific importance** The problem addressed is important: understanding the molecular determinants of IDP phase separation, particularly the role of backbone interactions and secondary structure. The findings on GY-23 and TDP-43 provide new insights, though the mechanistic conclusions for TDP-43 require further support.
  - **Interdisciplinary readership** The work will appeal to computational biophysicists, soft matter physicists, and experimental biologists studying condensates. The methodological advance is of broad interest, but the specific findings are more specialized.
  - **Technical soundness** The core methodology is sound, and the GPU implementation is a practical advance. However, the validation of the recalibrated parameters is limited, and some quantitative comparisons (e.g., β-structure vs. CD) are not fully established.
  - **Readability for nonspecialists** The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, with clear explanations of the motivation and findings. Some technical details (e.g., FIREBALL fitting, DBSCAN clustering) may be challenging for nonspecialists but are adequately explained.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript presents a valuable methodological advance and interesting biological findings, but the strength of the central claims regarding β-structure coupling and the role of helicity in TDP-43 phase separation depends on additional validation and clarification. The authors should address the concerns regarding parameter transferability, quantitative comparison to experimental data, and the interpretation of the artificial restraint experiments.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that HyRes-GPU "semi-quantitatively" captures residue helical propensity is supported for some IDPs but not others; the correlation coefficients are highly variable and the model's performance for low-helicity sequences is weak.
- The claim that increased β-structure formation in the condensate is "consistent with available experimental CD data" is not quantitatively supported in the main text; the comparison is qualitative and the experimental data is from a different construct.
- The conclusion that "the balance between backbone and sidechain-mediated interactions, but not helicity itself, actually determines phase separation propensity" is not fully established, as the artificial restraint experiments may introduce confounding effects.
- The claim of "several hundred-fold" speed-up for GPU versus CPU is not fully documented with hardware specifications.
- The transferability of the recalibrated backbone parameters to diverse IDP sequences is not established beyond the limited validation set.
