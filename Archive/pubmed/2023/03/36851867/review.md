## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This is a well-written and informative review that covers a timely and important topic in structural biology. The authors successfully articulate the unique capabilities of native MS for resolving different types of protein heterogeneity, and they provide a clear and logical narrative supported by relevant examples. The manuscript is accessible to a broad audience and effectively positions native MS within the wider structural biology toolkit. However, the review is largely descriptive and would benefit from a more critical and comparative analysis of the discussed methods. The lack of figures and references in the provided material prevents a full assessment of the evidence base.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists, biochemists, and analytical chemists interested in protein structure, dynamics, and interactions. Researchers in the pharmaceutical and biotechnology industries, particularly those involved in biopharmaceutical characterization and quality control, would also find this review useful. The manuscript is relevant to anyone seeking to understand the capabilities and limitations of native MS for studying complex, heterogeneous protein systems.
- **Major strengths**
    - The review is clearly structured around different types of heterogeneity (compositional, conformational, PTM-based), which provides a logical and effective framework.
    - The authors successfully highlight the unique advantages of native MS, such as the ability to analyze intact complexes, resolve mixtures, and measure stoichiometry, which are often challenging for other methods.
    - The manuscript is well-written and accessible to a non-specialist audience, with technical concepts explained clearly.
    - The inclusion of recent applications, such as the analysis of membrane protein complexes in near-native environments and the use of charge detection mass spectrometry, demonstrates the forward-looking nature of the field.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **R1-M1** (Major, Yes): The review is largely descriptive and lacks a critical, comparative analysis of the methods it discusses. For example, the section on "BENEFITS AND CHALLENGES OF NATIVE MS" mentions limitations but does not systematically compare the performance of native MS against other structural biology techniques (e.g., cryo-EM, NMR, crosslinking mass spectrometry) for the same biological questions. A more critical evaluation of when native MS is the preferred method versus when it is complementary or inferior would significantly strengthen the review's value.
    - **R1-M2** (Major, Yes): The manuscript makes a strong claim that native MS can resolve heterogeneity that "a single analytical method struggles to accomplish." While this is likely true, the evidence presented is anecdotal, consisting of selected examples. The review would be more persuasive if it included a more systematic assessment of the scope and limitations of native MS, such as the size limits for high-resolution analysis, the challenges in mass assignment for very large or heterogeneous systems, and the potential for gas-phase artifacts to mislead structural interpretations.
    - **R1-M3** (Major, Yes): The section on "QUANTIFYING THE CONFORMATIONAL HETEROGENEITY OF 'STRUCTURELESS' PROTEINS" is a key part of the review, but the discussion of how native MS and ion mobility (IM) data are interpreted is somewhat superficial. The claim that CCS values can be compared to calculated values from structural models is presented without a critical discussion of the accuracy and limitations of such comparisons, which are known to be non-trivial. A more detailed explanation of the methodology and its caveats is needed.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **R1-m1** (Minor, Axis: Clarity and Precision): The term "native" is used throughout, but the authors could more explicitly define its operational meaning in the context of mass spectrometry, acknowledging the debate and nuances surrounding the term.
    - **R1-m2** (Minor, Axis: Completeness): The review mentions the use of native MS for screening enzyme inhibitors and ligands but does not discuss the quantitative aspects of binding affinity measurements (Kd) in detail. A brief discussion of the accuracy and limitations of affinity measurements by native MS would be a valuable addition.
    - **R1-m3** (Minor, Axis: Readability): The figure descriptions are referenced in the text, but without seeing the figures, it is difficult to assess if they are effective. The authors should ensure that the figures are self-explanatory and clearly illustrate the key concepts discussed in the text.
    - **R1-m4** (Minor, Axis: Completeness): The "OUTLOOK" section is forward-looking but could be more specific about the key technical and methodological developments that are needed to realize the vision of "native proteomics." For example, what are the main bottlenecks in sensitivity, throughput, and data analysis that need to be overcome?
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
    - The lack of a critical comparison with other structural biology methods (R1-M1).
    - The lack of a systematic discussion of the limitations and potential artifacts of native MS (R1-M2).
    - The superficial treatment of the methodology for interpreting conformational data from IM-MS (R1-M3).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
    - **Originality**: The review is not entirely original in its topic, as other reviews on native MS exist. However, its specific focus on "structural heterogeneity" as a unifying theme provides a somewhat fresh perspective.
    - **Scientific importance**: The topic is of high scientific importance, as understanding protein heterogeneity is a major challenge in structural biology and drug discovery.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The review is likely to appeal to a broad readership across structural biology, biochemistry, and analytical chemistry.
    - **Technical soundness**: The technical content appears sound, but the lack of a critical and comparative analysis limits the depth of the technical assessment.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The manuscript is well-written and accessible, making it suitable for a broad audience.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The review covers an important topic and is well-written, but it needs to be more critical and analytical to be a truly valuable contribution. The authors should address the major concerns by adding a more comparative and critical discussion of the methods and their limitations.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that native MS can resolve heterogeneity that "a single analytical method struggles to accomplish" is a general statement that is not systematically supported by the evidence presented.
- The claim that "strong experimental evidence" supports the preservation of native-like states in the gas phase is supported by cited examples, but the review does not discuss the known exceptions and limitations of this assumption.
- The specific details of the examples discussed (e.g., stoichiometries, CCS values, binding affinities) cannot be verified without the full reference list and figures.
