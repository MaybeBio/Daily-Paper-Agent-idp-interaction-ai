# Intrinsically disordered proteins SAID1/2 condensate on SERRATE for dual inhibition of miRNA biogenesis in Arabidopsis


## Significance
  Thousands of protein-coding genes have been annotated but not assigned with any function or biological process in Arabidopsis. Two of these genes, SAID1/2, encode intrinsically disordered proteins, and are hypothetic dentin sialophosphoprotein-like proteins. SAID1/2 turn out to be in the regulatory hub of SE (SERRATE), which are the master regulators of RNA metabolism in eukaryotes. SAID1/2 act to hijack pri-miRNAs from SE and inhibit microprocessor activity, whereas promoting phosphorylation and degradation of SE protein, leading to repression of production for a majority of miRNAs. These biochemical events likely take place in SAID1/2-SE condensates in vivo. Thus, this study not only deciphered the functions of poorly understood gene family, but also uncovered a new regulatory layer of miRNA biogenesis in plants.


## Results

### SAID1/2 Are Bona Fide Partners of SE protein.
  We previously performed Immunoprecipitation and mass spectrometry (IP-MS/MS) analysis of SE complexes and identified several partners that regulate SE functions and homeostasis (17, 31–33). Among the rest of the candidates of SE interactors is DSPPL9/SAID1 (At5g52530), which is annotated as a homolog of human DSPP with unknown function (SI Appendix, Fig. S1A). This protein was also recovered in an independent IP-MS/MS of SE complexes by others (18), validating the plausibility of SAID1 as a genuine partner of SE. Phylogenetic analysis suggested SAID1 has a homolog, SAID2 (At5g13590), although they only share 15.6% identity and 23.6% similarity (SI Appendix, Fig. S1 B and C). To validate the interaction of SE and SAID1/2, we performed bimolecular fluorescence complementation (BiFC) assay. Cotransfection of either nYFP-SAID1 or nYFP-SAID2 with cYFP-SE, but not with cYFP, displayed clear condensates in nuclei, reminiscent of the nuclear punctate foci formed by the positive control of nYFP-DCL1 and cYFP-SE (Fig. 1A). Next, we validated the interaction of SE and SAID1/2 through the yeast-two-hybrid (Y2H) assay (Fig. 1B). Of note, cotransfection of SE-BD with SAID1-AD could readily result in the growth of heathy colonies on the quadruple-deficient medium, and so could the combination of SE-BD/SAID2-AD but with fewer colonies (Fig. 1B). This result implied that SAID2 might have low binding affinity with SE or might be not stable in vivo. Further Y2H assays with a serial of truncated variants revealed that SAID1 interacts with the C-terminal region (469-720aa) of SE covering zinc finger domain and C-terminal unstructured tail (SI Appendix, Fig. S1 D and E). Interestingly, this region is also the site for SE interaction with DCL1 and PRP4KA among other partners (17, 32). On the other hand, SE interacts with the middle part (377-580aa) of SAID1 (SI Appendix, Fig. S1 F and G). Of note, fine mapping assays delineated the domain (377-580aa) of SAID1 being crucial for its interaction with SE (SI Appendix, Fig. S1 F and G). We also determined the C-terminal part (663-1169aa) of SAID2 being crucial for its interaction with SE (SI Appendix, Fig. S1H). SAID1 and SAID2 did not form a heterodimer in vivo in Y2H assays (SI Appendix, Fig. S1I). Moreover, supply of SAID2 did not impact the interaction of SAID1 and SE in BiFC assays, suggesting that they would not compete for binding with SE in vivo (SI Appendix, Fig. S1J).


### Interdependent and Independent Regulatory Roles of SAID1/2 and SE in Gene Expression.
  To investigate the functional relevance of SAID1/2-SE interaction, we screened two T-DNA homozygote mutants for each gene (SI Appendix, Fig. S2A). The said1-1 and said1-2 lines did not show obvious morphological change, while said2-1 and said2-2 displayed mild down-curved leaves and slightly reduced stature phenotype (Fig. 1C and SI Appendix, Fig. S2 B and C). Lack of developmental defect in said1-1 and said1-2 might result from the fact that T-DNA insertion sites are at the last exon of the gene, and the lines might still encode truncated but partially functional protein. Alternatively, there might be the functional redundancy between SAID1 and its homolog SAID2. Indeed, the seedlings of the said1-1; said2-1 double mutant showed pleiotropic phenotype such as down-curved leaves, small stature, and their mature plants exhibited dwarfism and mild clustered siliques. Moreover, the developmental defects were more obvious in a long-day growth condition (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 B and C).

  We then generated CRISPR/Cas9 knockout lines for SAID1 and/or SAID2 (34), with the guide RNAs targeting the first and third exons of SAID1 and SAID2, respectively (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 D and E). Like T-DNA insertion mutants, the single CRISPR/Cas9 knockout lines displayed normal morphology or subtle growth defect (Fig. 1 D and E and SI Appendix, Fig. S2 F and G). Intriguingly, most of the double knock-out lines showed severer pleiotropic phenotypes than said1-1; said2-1, namely down-curved leaves, dwarfisms, often bushy, and clustered siliques of short internode and altered phyllotaxis (Fig. 1 D and E). We selected two representative lines (SAID1; SAID2-Cas9 #3 and #13) (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 H–K) and crossed them with Col-0 to clean the background for further functional analysis. Of note, computational analysis ranked 4 and 3 top possible off-target candidates for CRISPR-Cas9 knockout lines; however, this concern was cleared as Sanger sequencing did not recover any polymorphisms in the potential off-target sites (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 L and M).

  We then conducted RNA-seq of SAID1; SAID2-Cas9 #3 (thereafter renamed as said1-3; said2-3) plants with se-2 and chr2-1 references (SI Appendix, Fig. S3A). Bioinformatic analysis recovered 5,520 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in said1-3; said2-3 vs Col-0. By contrast, only 1,801 DEGs were detected in se-2 (Fig. 2A and Dataset S1C). This number was much lower than that in se-2 observed in our earlier RNA-seq (31), likely due to different growing conditions, with further suggestion that the numbers of DEGs recovered in this RNA-seq might be underestimated.

  Among 5,520 DEGs, 2,737 and 2,783 genes were either up-regulated or down-regulated in said1-3; said2-3 vs Col-0, respectively (Fig. 2 A–C). Notably, 936 of 5,520 DEGs in said1-3; said2-3 were shared with se-2. Among the shared DEGs, they exhibited more concordance than difference between se-2 and said1-3; said2-3 mutants. For instance, 272 genes, representing ~34% of 807 up-regulated ones in se-2, were accordantly up-regulated in said1-3; said2-3, whereas only 103 genes, representing 13% of 807 up-regulated genes in se-2, were down-regulated in said1-3; said2-3 (Fig. 2B). On the other hand, 425 of 994 down-regulated in se-2 (~43%) were repressed in said1-3; said2-3, and only up to 14% of se-2-repressed genes were up-regulated in the double mutant (Fig. 2C). These results suggested that SAID1/2 and SE might have shared targets. Although more than 50% of DEGs in se-2 overlapped with those of said1-3; said2-3, a majority of DEGs in said1-3; said2-3 were not observed in se-2 (Fig. 2 A–C). In addition, several SE-dependent alternative splicing genes did not show obvious defect of pre-mRNA splicing in said1-3; said2-3 (SI Appendix, Fig. S3B). These results suggested that SAID1/2 and SE might also have inter-independent regulatory roles in gene expression in vivo.

  The DEGs shared between said1-3; said2-3 and se-2 were mostly enriched in the pathways of photosynthesis, metabolism of carbon and starch, and stress responses (Fig. 2D). This pattern is in lines with the low biomass production of both mutants (Fig. 1 C–E). Gene ontology (GO) analysis of the genes specifically down-regulated in said1-3; said2-3 pointed out that the most enrichment of DEGs were related to metabolic and cellular processes and response to stimulus, followed by regulation of rhythmic and other biological processes, whereas DEGs in se-2 are related to phyllotaxis development and organ polarity among others (Fig. 2 E and F). In lines with the results is that SAID1 is predicted to interact with the oscillator Time for Coffee that regulates circadian rhythm, whereas SAID2 potentially interacts with cold acclimation regulator Cold Shock Domain-Containing Protein 3 in plants. Unexpectedly, the significantly overrepresented genes in said1-3; said2-3 were engaged in immune responses, systemic acquired resistance, and plant responses to various hormones or environmental stresses (Fig. 2G). The fact that the defense- and other stress-responsive mRNAs accumulate despite growth in a normal physiological condition with simultaneous repression of mRNAs required for general biological processes in said1-3; said2-3 vs Col-0 implied that wild-type SAID1/2, reminiscent of DHH1/DDX6-like RNA helicases (35), suppress stress-responsive mRNAs under nonstress conditions to maintain growth/defense balance in plants. The DEG pattern also explained the dwarfism phenotype characteristic of the double mutants.


### SAID1/2 Reduce Accumulation of a Majority of miRNAs In Vivo.
  We next conducted small RNA-seq (sRNA-seq) to evaluate miRNA profiles in said1 and/or said2 mutants (SI Appendix, Fig. S4A). Whereas most of annotated miRNA species were down-regulated in se-2, small proportions of miRNAs were differentially accumulated in said1-1 (Fig. 2H and SI Appendix, Fig. S4B). Of note, ~80 miRNAs were up-regulated and ~30 miRNAs down-regulated in said2-1, and the relatively increased differentially accumulated number of miRNAs was consistent with the mild morphological change in said2-1 relative to said1-1 (Fig. 1C and SI Appendix, Figs. S2 B and C and S4C). Importantly, the said1-3; said2-3 line showed ~110 up-regulated miRNAs, with less than 50 of down-regulated miRNAs (Fig. 2I). An independent line (said1-13; said2-13) also showed a similar pattern, but to a lesser extent (SI Appendix, Fig. S4D). Majority of the deregulated miRNAs in said1-3; said2-3 shared common characteristics of 21-nt miRNA length with U at the 5′ nucleotides, with great diversities in the precursor sequences. Moreover, the up-regulated miRNAs typically belong to SE-controlled canonical species (Fig. 2J), indicating negative role of SAID1/2 to regulate miRNA accumulation. We next conducted sRNA blot or stem-loop RT-qPCR assays to further validate the roles of SAID1/2 on miRNA accumulation. We selected 21 miRNAs that covered canonical and noncanonical species, which also contained the ones that displayed different expression patterns in sRNA-seq (Dataset S1D and Fig. 2 K and L). We found that most of the tested canonical miRNAs accumulated more in the said1-3; said2-3 mutant vs Col-0 (Fig. 2 K and L). For three newborn miRNAs elevated in sRNA-seq (miR781, miR827, and miR864), they also displayed elevated expression in said1-3; said2-3 vs Col-0. For the miRNAs that showed comparable (miR158, miR408, and miR824), or reduced accumulation (miR169, miR858, and miR863) in the sRNA-seq, the stem-loop qPCR assays again showed consistent trends in said1-3; said2-3 vs Col-0. Taken together, we concluded that the two proteins SAID1/2 generally repress miRNA accumulation in vivo. The fact that some miRNAs are reduced in said1-3; said2-3 implies that SAID proteins might have some additional regulatory roles in addition to impacting SE function.

  We also assessed the expression of canonical miRNA targets in the mutants by RT-qPCR assays. Indeed, selected miRNA targets displayed significantly reduced expression in said1-3; said2-3, but only mildly in the single mutants said1-1 and said2-1 (Fig. 2M and SI Appendix, Fig. S4 E and F). These results were also consistent with increased accumulation of miRNAs in the double mutants. Of note, the expression of certain miRNA targets in said1-3; said2-3 was reduced (e.g., those of miR156 and miR159), suggestive of elevated miRNA accumulation (Fig. 2M), despite the observation that there was no or subtle difference in the expression of related miRNAs in the sRNA-seq (or sRNA blot) analysis (Fig. 2 I and K). This contrast might be caused by low sensitivity of RNA blot assays or barcode bias in the sRNA-seq (36, 37). We also observed that numerous experimental-validated miRNA targets displayed increased or relatively stable expression, indicating that these miRNA targets might be subjected to additional layers of regulation in vivo. Importantly, the overall expression pattern of miRNA targets in said1-3; said2-3 very much resembles that in chr2-1 (17), further suggesting that SAID1/2, like CHR2, are negative regulators in the miRNA pathway in vivo.


### SAID1/2 Regulate miRNA Biogenesis at Transcriptional and Posttranscriptional Levels.
  We next examined mRNA levels of the known components in miRNA pathway in the mutant. Most of the components, including DCL1, HYL1, and SE as well as miRNA degrading enzymes SDNs (small RNA degrading nucleases), displayed similar transcript expression in said1-3; said2-3 compared with Col-0 with the difference within 1.5-fold (SI Appendix, Fig. S3C). This result suggested that the increased miRNA accumulation in said1-3; said2-3 was not caused by the deregulation of the known genes in miRNA pathway, with a further suggestion that SAID1/2 might directly impact miRNA biogenesis. Of note, the transcript of AGO1 was accumulated in the mutant (SI Appendix, Fig. S3C), likely due to feedback regulation for increased need of RISC to house the accumulated miRNAs in the mutant.

  Then, we assessed the steady-state level of pri-miRNA debris. While the recovered reads of pri-miRNA debris were very low in RNA-seq and did not allow us to conduct vigorous analysis of differential expression (SI Appendix, Fig. S4G), we conducted RT-qPCR assays to measure the accumulation of 22 pri-miRNAs that corresponded to the miRNAs tested in the sRNA blot and/or stem-loop RT-qPCR assays. We observed that debris levels of 15 pri-miRNAs that corresponded to the elevated miRNAs except for pri-miR168b and pri-miR781a were significantly reduced in said1-3; said2-3 compared with Col-0 (SI Appendix, Fig. S4H). By contrast, for the unchanged or repressed miRNAs, the corresponding pri-miRNA debris (i.e., pri-miR158a, pri-miR408, pri-miR824, pri-miR169a, and pri-miR858a) did not display significant changes in said1-3; said2-3 vs Col-0 (SI Appendix, Fig. S4H). The general inverse accumulation between pri-miRNA debris and miRNAs suggested possible efficient processing of pri-miRNAs in said1-3; said2-3 compared with Col-0. Alternatively, the reduction of pri-miRNA debris levels might also result from reduced transcription and/or faster decay of the transcripts in the mutants. To distinguish these possibilities, we selected MIR159 and MIR164 loci for further analysis. The levels of miR159 and miR164 were increased in said1-3; said2-3 vs Col-0 (Fig. 2 K and L), whereas histochemical and western blot assays with the GUS reporters for MIR159 and MIR164 loci showed that the transcription of the reporters might be marginally increased in the said1-3; said2-3 background to their sibling controls (Fig. S4 I and J). These results implied that SAID1/2 might regulate miRNA biogenesis at both transcriptional and posttranscriptional levels. Whereas this study focused on the posttranscriptional role of SAID1/2, how exactly SAID1/2 regulate transcriptional regulation will be studied elsewhere and not be discussed further here.


### SAID1/2 Promote Degradation and Phosphorylation of SE.
  SAID1 and SAID2 did not seem to alter transcript levels of the known factors that regulate miRNA biogenesis and turnover (SI Appendix, Fig. S3C). However, western blot assays indicated that protein accumulation of SE, AGO1, and possibly HYL1 were all increased in said1-3; said2-3 compared with that of WT and the single said1 and said2 mutants (Fig. 3A). Since SAID1/2 are IDPs, we hypothesized that they might bind SE to regulate its stability. To test this, we performed a cell-free protein decay assay using lysates extracted from three-week-old seedlings. When cycloheximide was applied to block protein translation, SE had a half-life of approximately 20 min and was barely detectable afterward in WT lysates. By contrast, SE protein was relatively more stable and clearly detected even in 60 min in the extracts from said1-3; said2-3 (Fig. 3B). In either case, the proteasome inhibitor MG-132 could inhibit SE decay, and SE protein levels were relatively stable and detectable in the extended incubation periods (Fig. 3C). The results suggest that increased stability might account for the elevated SE accumulation in said1-3; said2-3, with further suggestion that SAID1/2 are negative regulators of SE accumulation.

  We recently found that PRP4KA phosphorylates SE and triggers its degradation (32). We hypothesized that the phosphorylation status of SE might be altered in said1-3; said2-3. Indeed, phosphor-tag gel blot clearly showed the faster migration of SE protein from said1-1; said2-1 and said1-3; said2-3 relative to that of WT (Fig. 3D and SI Appendix, Fig. S5A). SE protein from the single said1-1 and said2-1 mutants also appeared to migrate faster but to less extent compared with that of said1-3; said2-3 (Fig. 3D). These results indicated that SE is hypo-phosphorylated in the mutants vs Col-0, with a suggestion that SE is stabilized in a hypophosphorylation form in the double mutants. We next surmised that SAID1/2 might directly interact with PRP4KA and/or PAG1 to regulate phosphorylation and degradation of SE. However, these possibilities were disapproved by Y2H assays (SI Appendix, Fig. S6 A and B). We also performed Y3H assays to assess whether SAID1/2 promote the interaction of PRP4KA with SE, which might in turn facilitate the phosphorylation. Unexpectedly, addition of either SAID1 or SAID2 to the combination of PRP4KA and SE resulted in slower growth of yeast colonies vs the combination of PRP4KA/SE alone (SI Appendix, Fig. S6C). This result superficially suggested that SAID1/2 might block PRP4KA/SE interaction. However, further western blot assays showed that SE accumulation was largely reduced once SAID1/2 were expressed (SI Appendix, Fig. S6D). This result indicated that SAID1/2 could trigger SE degradation in yeast similarly to that in plants.

  SAID1/2 stimulation of SE degradation in both Arabidopsis and yeast promoted us to hypothesize that the two IDPs might alter SE conformation in vivo, facilitating PRP4KA enzymatic activity on SE. To test this, we purified recombinant full-length SAID1/2 and their truncated variants SAID11-300aa and SAID21-400aa that lost interaction with SE. An in vitro kinase assay showed that addition of high-dosage of wild-type SAID1 (~270 nM) marginally impacted SE phosphorylation signals relative to SAID11-300aa (Fig. 3E and SI Appendix, Fig. S5B). To our surprise, PRP4KA could also phosphorylate SAID1 but not its truncated mutant SAID11-300aa in vitro. This result suggested that two substrates might compete for limited amount of the kinase and ATP in the reaction system. To minimize the potential competition, we added a low dosage (up to 110 nM) of SAID1 or SAID11-300aa to the kinase assay system. Remarkably, SAID1 clearly boosted the phosphorylation signal of SE compared with SAID11-300aa (Fig. 3F and SI Appendix, Fig. S5C). This result suggested that once mingled together, SE and SAID1 both become the substrates of PRP4KA and mutually promote the phosphorylation of each other in vitro. PRP4KA appeared to phosphorylate SAID2 in vitro as well. In this scenario, SAID2 clearly and progressively boosted SE phosphorylation signal whereas the SAID21-400aa did not (Fig. 3 G and H and SI Appendix, Fig. S5 D and E). Altogether, both in vivo and in vitro assays showed that SAID1/2 could enhance PRP4KA activity on SE and promote its degradation in vivo.


### The Assembly of SE-Scaffolded Macrocomplexes Is Not Altered in said1; said2.
  Homeostasis of SE accumulation is critical for its proper functions in different macromolecular complexes as excessive SE often causes assemblies of intermediate complexes and subsequential protein destruction as observed in prp4k3 (amiR-PRP4KA;B;C) and pag1 mutants (31, 32). To test if this was the case in the said1-3; said2-3 mutant, we performed size exclusion chromatography (SEC) assays. We observed that the distribution patterns of SE fractions were comparable between Col-0 and said1-3; said2-3 with most of the SE fractions eluted at the 8 to 10 mL volume, equivalent to the range of 680 to 250 kDa (Fig. 3I). This scenario was quite different from the broader ranges of SE elution in either pag1 or prp4k3 mutants (31, 32). The contrasted results indicated that the accumulated SE protein in said1-3; said2-3 does not seem to interfere with its assembly in macro-ribonucleoprotein complexes. To further test it, we coexpressed cYFP-HYL1 and nYFP-DCL1 in the protoplasts prepared from Col-0 and said1-3; said2-3. The YFP signal, indicative of interaction of the HYL1 and DCL1, was even stronger in said1-3; said2-3 vs Col-0 (Fig. 3J). The results validated that increased accumulation of SE did not disrupt the assembly of microprocessor complexes in the said1-3; said2-3 mutant, likely because the accumulation of several tested factors in miRNA biogenesis and RISC effector were concertedly increased in the mutants (Fig. 3A).


### SAID1/2 Bind to pri-miRNAs.
  We further investigated whether SAID1 and SAID2 impacted pri-miRNA processing. Electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA) showed that purified SAID1/2 did not bind to homogenous single-stranded (ssRNA) that comprised of 44nt poly(A)s (Fig. 4 A and B). To our surprise, SAID1/2 both could bind to hairpin structured pri-miRNAs with dissociation constants (Kds) of 1.950 ± 0.317 nM, and = 0.996 ± 0.179 nM, respectively (Fig. 4 C–E and SI Appendix, Fig. S7 A–C). Since the truncated variants of SAID1/2 (SAID11-300 and SAID21-400) barely associated with pri-miRNAs, or bound pri-miRNAs with much reduced affinity (Fig. 4F and SI Appendix, Fig. S7 D and E), then potential RNA-binding domains are likely located in the middle and C-terminal parts of the proteins. Importantly, we conducted in vivo RIP assays and observed that SAID1/2 immunoprecipitates (IP) were clearly enriched with selected pri-miRNAs relative to a control IP (Fig. 4G). Thus, both in vitro and in vivo assays showed that SAID1/2 are dsRNA-binding proteins.

  The Kd(s) of SAID1 and SAID2 association with pri-miRNAs are at a similar scale of that of HYL1 (Kd = 0.7 nM) but much less than that of SE (17), indicating that SAID1/2 have binding affinity with pri-miRNAs comparable to that of HYL1, but retain significantly higher affinity with pri-miRNAs than SE, with a further suggestion that SAID1/2 might sequester pri-miRNAs from SE protein. To test this, we preincubated SE with pri-miRNA followed by addition of various concentrations of SAID1/2, or their truncated variants (Fig. 4H). EMSA results showed that addition of the truncated variants did not alter the migration pattern of SE/pri-miRNA complex, whereas the electrophoretic mobility of pri-miRNA-protein complexes either recapitulated that of pri-miRNA-SAID1/2 or became slower than that of SE-pri-miRNAs (Fig. 4H). These results indicated that the addition of full-length SAID1/2 proteins indeed sequestered pri-miRNAs from SE protein. Similar results were also obtained when the order of protein incubation with RNA was reversed (SI Appendix, Fig. S7F). Taken together, the results further validated that SAID1/2 retained higher binding affinity with pri-miRNAs than SE and could readily sequester/occupy pri-miRNAs from SE to prevent the downstream processing.


### SAID1/2 Inhibit pri-miRNA Processing.
  Given the fact that SAID1/2 can sequester pri-miRNAs from SE and the fact that MIR loci are often derepressed while the steady-state levels of pri-miRNAs are reduced in said1-3; said2-3, miRNA production might be more efficient in the mutant vs WT. To address the direct impact of SAID1/2 on pri-miRNA processing, we performed in vitro microprocessor reconstitution assays (17). First, we preincubated either SAID1 and SAID2, or their truncated variants, with pri-miR166g and pri-miR166f for 15 min before addition of SE and the core microprocessor components DCL1/HYL1 (Fig. 5 A and B). We observed that supply of truncated SAID1/2 exerted minor or literally no effect on pri-miRNA processing (Fig. 5 C–F). By contrast, preincubation of either SAID1 or SAID2 with pri-miRNAs greatly decreased the pri-miRNA processing efficiency, and this inhibition was exerted in a dose-dependent manner in vitro (Fig. 5 C–F and SI Appendix, Fig. S8 A–D). We also preincubated SE with pri-miRNAs before addition of SAID1/2 or their truncated variants as the prevailing model is that SE binds to pri-miRNAs cotranscriptionally (38, 39). Again, SAID1/2, but not the SE-interaction compromised mutants, still inhibited microprocessor activity. Even more, the inhibition might be even more severe compared with the situation when SAID1 and SAID 2 is preincubated with pri-miRNA earlier than SE (SI Appendix, Fig. S8 E and F). One possibility is that preincubation of SE with pri-miRNAs might alter the conformation of the ribonucleoprotein complex and facilitate its recruitment of SAID1/SAID2 for inhibition. This scenario might imply that SE recruits SAID1/2 that in turn sequester pri-miRNAs from SE to inhibit pri-miRNA processing in vivo.


### SAID1/2 Dysfunction Does Not Alter LLPS Capacity of SE and HYL1.
  To further investigate how SAID1/2 regulate SE function in vivo, we performed a series of confocal experiments (Fig. 6 A–E and SI Appendix, Fig. S10–S13). SE is an IDP and has been reported to show a feature of LLPS when expressed under a constitutive promoter (15). In our hands, SE-tagged mCherry could indeed form numerous condensates or foci in nucleus when transiently expressed under the Super promoter (Fig. 6E). However, SE-GFP expressed in the native promoter in the transgenic lines appeared to be distributed homogenously in nucleus. A few tiny nuclear sparkles might be observed from time to time, but the sizes were typically smaller than the ones when overexpressed (Fig. 6 A, C, and E). When the small particles were bleached by laser, the GFP fluorescence could be recovered within 20s, suggestive of LLPS feature (Fig. 6C). Since the majority of SE-GFP was evenly dispersed in nucleoplasm (Fig. 6 A and C), we used HYL1-GFP as a proxy of D-bodies. Nucleus-localized HYL1-GFP displayed 1 to 2 bright foci in the root tip of stable transgenic plants. While most cells in WT showed one HYL1-GFP speckle in their nuclei, increased numbers of cells in said1-3; said2-3 showed one or two brighter HYL1-GFP foci in the nuclei, indicative of efficient D body assembly in the mutant (Fig. 6B and SI Appendix, Fig. S9).

  We also assessed effects of SAID1/2 mutation on the assembling of D bodies and LLPS of SE-GFP, no significant differences were observed between WT and said1-3; said2-3 mutants (Fig. 6C). Phase separation capacity of HYL1-GFP was also not affected in said1-3; said2-3 mutants (SI Appendix, Fig. S10), indicating that microprocessor assembling and LLPS of SE was not interfered and was maintained normal in said1; said2 mutants vs Col-0.


### SAID1/2 Display LLPS Feature and Regulate SE Function in the Condensates In Vivo.
  SAID1/2 are also predicted to be IDPs with several intrinsic disordered regions identified (SI Appendix, Fig. S11 A–F). We also examined whether SAID1/2 could form nuclear condensates. When expressed transiently in protoplast, full-length proteins of SAID1/2 were detected in the nucleus and clearly assembled in the nuclear condensates (Fig. 6E and SI Appendix, Figs. S11 G–L and S12), reminiscent of SE foci in the overexpression lines (15). We generated stable transgenic plants expressing native promoter-driven YFP-SAID1 and GFP-SAID2. Both SAID1 and SAID2 expressed ubiquitously at the root, hypocotyl, cotyledon, true leaves and shoot apical meristem among other tissues (SI Appendix, Fig. S13 A and C), indicating their ubiquitous functions in regulating plant development and adaption. YFP-SAID1, GFP-SAID1, and GFP-SAID2 were localized in the nucleus, and they also assembled into nuclear condensates (Fig. 6 D and E and SI Appendix, Fig. S13). Importantly, they showed phase separation patterns, as evidenced by fast recovery in FRAP assays of GFP-SAID1 and GFP-SAID2 (SI Appendix, Fig. S12). Next, we tested whether the IDRs were crucial for their capacity to form nuclear condensates by examining expression pattern of truncated SAID1/2 with depleted IDRs. Compared with full-length SAID1, fluorescent signals of the truncated SAID1 proteins (SAID1∆5-55aa, SAID1∆377-407aa, SAID1∆377-580aa, and SAID1∆515-577aa) was decreased, additionally, the number and size of nuclear condensates were also decreased, but not completely disrupted (SI Appendix, Fig. S11 G–I). As for SAID2, depletion of amino acid 656 to 727 of SAID2, but not amino acid 33 to 77 resulted in reduced protein level, less number and smaller size of nuclear condensates (SI Appendix, Fig. S11 J–L). The results showed that SAID1/2 are nucleus-localized proteins and assembled in the nuclear condensates, and their stability and assembling in nuclear condensates rely on their IDRs.

  Next, we investigated SAID1/2 expression pattern in the se mutant background. When expressed in protoplast, dimmer signal of SAID1/2 was detected in se-1 vs Col-0. Meanwhile, no obvious nuclear condensates of SAID1/2 were detected in the se-1 protoplast compared with that of Col-0. Introduction of mCherry-SE∆IDR1 into se-1 did not restore the nuclear condensation of SAID1/2. By contrast, mCherry-SE could recover the nuclear condensation of GFP-tagged SAID1/2 (Fig. 6E). Finally, we examined expression patterns of native SAID1/2 in Col-0 and se mutants. Again, much faint signal of eYFP-SAID1 or eGFP-SAID2 was detected in se-1 and se-2 mutants, whereas strong signal and nuclear condensates of eYFP-SAID1 or eGFP-SAID2 were detected in Col-0 background (Fig. 6D and SI Appendix, Fig. S13E). By contrast, the nuclear condensation patterns of SAID1/2 were minimally altered in hyl1-2 vs Col-0 (Fig. 6D and SI Appendix, Fig. S13E). These results indicated that SAID1/2 nucleate on SE to form condensates, which in turn promote SE degradation or deprive pri-miRNAs from SE to inhibit pri-miRNA processing in vivo.


## Discussion
  Here, we unexpectedly identified SAID1/2 as novel partners of SE and reported their inhibitory roles in production for a majority of miRNAs. Several pieces of evidence supported our notion: First, SAID1/2 promote SE phosphorylation in vivo and in vitro, triggering its destruction in plants (Fig. 3 and SI Appendix, Fig. S5); Second, SAID1/2 possess very strong binding affinity to pri-miRNAs and can sequester the substrates from SE protein, which only has moderate binding affinity to pri-miRNAs (Fig. 4 and SI Appendix, Fig. S7). Finally, SAID1/2 could directly inhibit pri-miRNA processing by microprocessor (Fig. 5 and SI Appendix, Fig. S8). Given that the SAID1/2 condensate on SE in nucleus (Fig. 6 D and E and SI Appendix, Fig. S13E), we envision that the two proteins could hijack pri-miRNAs from SE to prevent the downstream microprocessor activity while the proteins could also stimulate phosphorylation of SE via PRP4KA, leading to degradation of SE, so that miRNA production is further stringently confined in plants (Fig. 6F). Thus, SAID1/2 represent a unique mode of action of controlling SE function that takes places at the levels of posttranscriptional processing and posttranslational modification. This mode of action is different from that of CHR2 that can sequester pri-miRNA and remodel the RNA secondary structure to inhibit miRNA production (17). SAID1/2 are also distinct from a recently reported IDP, constitutive alterations in the small RNA pathway 9 (CARP9) that promotes RNA silencing by facilitating shuttling of mature miRNA from HYL1 to AGO1-mediated RISC (40).


### Mechanisms for Dual Inhibitions of SAID1/2 on miRNA Production.
  The dual inhibitions on SE might result from the unique biochemical features of SAID1/2; and might also be independent from each other. SAID1/2 proteins are IDPs, and so is SE. Once attached to SE, SAID1 or SAID2 might change the conformation or configuration of SE, exposing its serine or threonine residues to the kinase PRP4KA, and facilitating the catalytic activity (Fig. 3 G–J and SI Appendix, Figs. S5 and S6). This proposal can be supported by our AlphaFold2 modeling in vitro (41). For in vivo, SAID1/2-mediated promotion of SE phosphorylation likely involves LLPS as the SAID1/2-SE nuclear condensates might serve as a platform to recruit PRP4KA for enzymatic activity (Fig. 6 A–E and SI Appendix, Figs. S11–S13). On the other hand, SAID1/2 inhibitory effect on microprocessor activity is likely through its high affinity of pri-miRNAs. The observation that two proteins do not bind to ssRNA excludes the possibility of the nonspecific package of RNA (Fig. 4 B–H and SI Appendix, Fig. S7). Thus, the association of SAID1/2 with pri-miRNAs likely involves the recognition of RNA secondary structure. Of note, the two proteins do not appear to have classic RNA binding or recognizing motif (RBM or RRM). Deep-learning predicts that SAID1/2 possess spaghetti-like three-dimensional structures centered on small alpha-helix domains. It is possible that upon supply with pri-miRNAs, the IDPs might reshape their structures, which in turn enable the accommodation of pri-miRNAs with high affinity. This scenario is reminiscent of the discovery of FVE (flowering locus VE), the protein which harbors WD (Trp-Asp) domains and would otherwise be thought to be only involved in protein–protein interaction, does not have canonical RBM or RRM, but can also bind to double-stranded RNA with strong affinity (42).

  It did not escape from our attention that SAID11-300 retains certain binding capacity of pri-miRNAs but does not interact with SE (Fig. 4F and SI Appendix, Fig. S7 D and E). Whereas SAID1 represses pri-miRNA processing significantly, truncated SAID11-300 does not (Fig. 5 and SI Appendix, Fig. S8). The results imply that SAID1 inhibition of pri-miRNA processing entails its physical interaction with SE. Taken together, IDPs SAID1/2 are recruited to SE scaffolded ribonucleoprotein complexes, stripping pri-miRNAs from SE, and inhibiting pri-miRNA processing, while the proteins could also promote PRP4KA-mediated phosphorylation of SE to elicit its degradation (Fig. 6F).


### Physiological Significance for SAID1/2 Repression of SE Function.
  miRNAs are crucial regulators of gene expression in eukaryotes and their accumulation must be tightly controlled. SAID1/2-mediated double inhibition of SE function might provide a gateway to quickly change miRNA accumulation, which could further reprogram transcriptome for better adaption to physiological changes. Indeed, the accumulation of numerous miRNAs is restricted into certain niches, whereas the expression of many others needs to be reprogramd in response to environmental stresses and stimuli (3, 4). Thus, proper adjustment of protein accumulation of microprocessor components including SE, a master regulatory cohort for RNA metabolism, might be an efficient checkpoint. Under these scenarios, accumulated SAID1/2 could promote SE phosphorylation via PRP4KA and facilitate its degradation, leading to reduced miRNA levels. Conversely, lowered expression of SAID1/2 would promote SE accumulation, and resultant miRNA upregulation. Furthermore, the accumulation of pri-miRNAs and miRNAs are often asynchronized. In numerous cases, expression of pri-miRNAs increases but miRNAs are down-regulated, and vice versa (43, 44). As a strong RNA binding protein, SAID1/2 could serve as an insulator to separate pri-miRNA accumulation from miRNA production. For instance, SAID1/2 could simply sequester excessive pri-miRNAs from SE, preventing the downstream processing, thus miRNA homeostasis is maintained. Otherwise, lack of SAID1/2 would allow prompt handover of pri-miRNAs from SE to DCL1/HYL1 for processing. Indeed, the expression of SAID1/2 displays ubiquitous expression patterns but shows various fluctuation in response to plant hormone treatments and light stimuli and circadian oscillation. Their expression is also deregulated under different biotic (i.e., Pseudomonas syringae) and abiotic stresses such as cold, salt, osmatic, genotoxic and UV-B, and repressed by heat stress. The dynamic response of SAID1/2 expression to both developmental and stresses infers their surveillance roles on expression homeostasis of SE and supply chains of pri-miRNAs. Thus, the dual inhibitory roles of SAID1/2 on SE serve as a prompt regulatory mechanism to fine-tune miRNA accumulation in responses to physiological and environmental variations.


### Interdependent and Independent Functions of SAID1/2 and SE.
  Whereas SAID1/2 have clear cross talk with SE in miRNA pathway, they might each have their own regulatory regimes. This surmise can be highlighted by the fact that a majority of said1/2-deregulated genes are not overlapped with those in se-2, despite more than half of se-2-regulated genes fall into the territory controlled by said1-3; said2-3 (Fig. 2 A–C). These scenarios might be accredited to biochemical features of the IDP proteins. Typically, IDPs partner with different scaffold proteins and perform various functions accordingly. It is highly likely that SAID1/2 might like SE contribute to the assembly of numerous ribonucleoprotein complexes, participating in different regulatory processes of gene expression. Intriguingly, the genetic pathways responding to multiple hormones (i.e., salicylic acid) and autoimmunity have been up-regulated (Fig. 2 D–G), whereas general biological processes (such as photosynthesis, metabolite accumulation, cell wall biogenesis) are repressed in said1; said2, indicating that SAID1/2 proteins might control the balances of plant growth and defense in normal conditions. Loss-of-function mutants of said1; said2 display short internodes, more branches, and early flowering, but without clear defect in overall seed yield. These ideal agronomic traits likely result from sophisticated and mutual regulations of miRNA with phytohormones (45), immunity (46), and environmental stimuli (3). These features make SAID1/2 ideal targets for genetic engineering of crops for better agronomic improvement. To a broader level, whether DSPP in metazoans act as plant SAID1/2 to regulate RNA silencing in addition to their classic roles in dentin hardening, remains to be explored.
