### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Statistical rigor and dataset validity  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "the average residual helicity is 17% and increases to 60% upon target binding" and that "helix contents of residual and target-bound structures do not correlate, however the relative location of helix elements in both states shows a strong overlap."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; dataset description and statistical analysis not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract provides no information on how the 65 peptides were selected, how helix content was measured, what experimental methods were used, or how statistical significance was assessed. The claim of "no correlation" requires a quantitative correlation coefficient and confidence interval. The claim of "strong overlap" requires a defined metric and statistical test.  
- **Why it matters** Without these details, the reader cannot evaluate whether the dataset is representative, whether the measurements are reliable, or whether the reported patterns are statistically robust. The central descriptive claims of the study rest on this analysis.  
- **Resolution test** Provide full dataset description, measurement methods, correlation coefficients with confidence intervals, and statistical tests for the overlap analysis.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Experimental validation and generalizability  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that "substitution of leucine motifs to other hydrophobic residues (valine or isoleucine) leads to reduction of residual helicity" and that "from the three hydrophobic residues only leucine can efficiently stabilize residual helical structure."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; experimental details for the two model peptides not provided  
- **Concern** The abstract reports experiments on only two model peptides. The claim that "only leucine can efficiently stabilize residual helical structure" is a strong generalization from a very limited sample. No information is provided on the sequence context of the model peptides, the extent of helicity reduction, or whether the effects are statistically significant.  
- **Why it matters** The central mechanistic conclusion of the study depends on the generality of the leucine effect. Two peptides are insufficient to establish that leucine is uniquely capable of stabilizing residual helicity across diverse sequence contexts.  
- **Resolution test** Provide full experimental details for the model peptides, including sequences, methods for measuring helicity, quantitative results with error bars, and ideally additional validation in more sequence contexts.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** Yes  
- **Axis** Causal inference  
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that leucine residues and leucine motifs "are the major contributors to helix stabilization and target-binding" and that the high occurrence of leucine at binding interfaces "can be explained by its unique ability to stabilize helical elements."  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no causal analysis or mechanistic experiments provided  
- **Concern** The abstract presents correlative evidence (enrichment of leucine in HBMs) and limited mutational data, but the claim of causation is not supported. The assertion that leucine's helix-stabilizing ability explains its prevalence at binding interfaces is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated conclusion. Alternative explanations, such as hydrophobic burial at interfaces or evolutionary constraints, are not addressed.  
- **Why it matters** The abstract overstates the strength of the causal link between leucine's biophysical properties and its evolutionary prevalence. This overreach could mislead readers into accepting a mechanism that is not fully tested.  
- **Resolution test** Provide additional experiments or analyses that directly test the causal role of helix stabilization in driving leucine prevalence, or temper the language to reflect the hypothesis-generating nature of the claim.

- **Concern ID** R1-M4  
- **Severity** Major  
- **Blocking** No  
- **Axis** Completeness of evidence  
- **Claim pointer** The abstract implies a comprehensive analysis of HBMs, but the scope of the dataset and the breadth of the conclusions are unclear.  
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no information on dataset coverage or limitations  
- **Concern** The abstract does not state whether the 65 peptides cover a diverse range of protein families, binding partners, or structural contexts. The generalizability of the findings to all HBMs is assumed but not demonstrated.  
- **Why it matters** If the dataset is biased toward certain types of HBMs, the conclusions may not be broadly applicable. The abstract should acknowledge limitations in dataset composition.  
- **Resolution test** Provide a description of dataset diversity and any potential biases, and discuss how these might affect the generality of the conclusions.
