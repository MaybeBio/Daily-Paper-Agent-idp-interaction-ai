### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that fibrillation of IDPs is accelerated in D2O compared to H2O, exhibiting faster kinetics.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that fibrillation is accelerated in D2O but provides no quantitative kinetic parameters, no measures of variability, no statistical comparisons, and no indication of the number of replicates. Without these data, the claim of acceleration cannot be evaluated for robustness or significance.
- **Why it matters** The central conclusion of the paper rests on this comparative kinetic claim. If the effect size is small or the variability is high, the conclusion may not be supported. The contrast with insulin is also presented without quantitative support.
- **Resolution test** Provide kinetic rate constants or lag times with standard deviations and statistical tests for each protein in both solvents, with clear reporting of replicate numbers.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that IDPs did not show significant structural changes in D2O that could influence accelerated fibrillation.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text referencing electrospray ionization ion mobility mass spectrometry, small-angle X-ray scattering, and molecular dynamics simulations; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract asserts the absence of significant structural changes but provides no data, no comparison metrics, and no statistical thresholds. The sensitivity of the techniques used to detect subtle structural differences is not discussed. An absence of detected change is not equivalent to an absence of change.
- **Why it matters** This claim is used to argue that the accelerated fibrillation is not due to monomeric structural changes, which is a key part of the mechanistic reasoning. If structural changes were present but undetected, the proposed mechanism would be incomplete.
- **Resolution test** Show representative ion mobility arrival time distributions, SAXS profiles with fits, and simulation-derived structural ensembles for each protein in H2O and D2O, with quantitative similarity metrics and stated detection limits.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Mechanistic support
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that increased hydrogen bonding of D2O and enhanced hydrophobic interactions stabilize beta-sheet structured fibrils in D2O, resulting in enhanced and faster fibrillation.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract text referencing umbrella sampling of protein protofibrils; location not provided
- **Concern** The mechanistic conclusion is based on umbrella sampling simulations, but the abstract provides no details on the system setup, force field, sampling convergence, or free energy differences. The connection between simulated stabilization of protofibrils and the observed kinetic acceleration in bulk solution is not established. No experimental validation of the proposed mechanism is presented.
- **Why it matters** The proposed mechanism is the explanatory framework for the observed phenomenon. Without evidence that the simulated stabilization translates to the experimental system, the mechanism remains speculative.
- **Resolution test** Provide free energy profiles from umbrella sampling with error estimates, details of simulation convergence, and ideally experimental validation such as binding assays or thermodynamic measurements that support the proposed stabilization.
