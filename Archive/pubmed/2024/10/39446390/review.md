## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript addresses a timely and important question regarding the applicability of AlphaFold-Multimer to IDR-mediated interactions. The authors have assembled multiple carefully constructed datasets, including a blind set, and provide a systematic evaluation across different binding modes. The introduction of the minD metric derived from the Distogram head is a novel contribution with potential practical utility. However, the manuscript has several significant weaknesses. The claim that AlphaFold-Multimer "accurately captures dynamics" is not fully supported by the evidence presented, as the correlation analyses between predicted scores and experimental dynamics metrics are indirect and the magnitude of predicted heterogeneity is acknowledged to be insufficient. The validation of the minD metric relies on datasets where the binding partner is known, and the generalizability to discovery settings is not established. The analysis of NOE violations, while appropriate, reveals that AlphaFold-Multimer structures have substantially higher RMS violations than experimental models, which tempers the overall success narrative. The manuscript would benefit from clearer reporting of effect sizes, confidence intervals, and direct comparisons to alternative approaches.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Computational structural biologists studying protein-protein interactions, researchers developing or applying AI-based structure prediction methods, experimentalists studying IDR-mediated interactions who may use these predictions to guide their work, and bioinformaticians interested in binding-site prediction from predicted structures. The practical guidelines for using AlphaFold-Multimer on IDR systems and the minD metric for binding-site identification would be of particular interest to these communities.
- **Major strengths** The authors have assembled multiple well-constructed datasets, including a blind set that addresses the training data leakage concern. The systematic evaluation across different binding modes (homogeneous, fuzzy, peptide-mediated) is comprehensive. The introduction of the minD metric from the Distogram head is a novel and potentially useful contribution. The NOE-based validation approach is appropriate for assessing predictions against experimental data that does not rely on a single static structure. The practical guidelines for score thresholds provide actionable information for users of AlphaFold-Multimer.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Claim-evidence alignment
  - **Claim pointer** The title and abstract claim that AlphaFold-Multimer "accurately captures interactions and dynamics" of IDRs. The Discussion states that "AlphaFold-Multimer scores are clearly able to separate predictions for true complexes from predictions for decoys" and that "AlphaFold-Multimer has an intrinsic knowledge of more dynamic parts of IDR-containing complexes."
  - **Evidence pointer** Results sections "AlphaFold-Multimer Intrinsic Scores Allow Separating Decoy and Inaccurate Structures from High-Quality Ones" and "AlphaFold-Multimer Structures Are Mostly Consistent with Nuclear Overhauser Effect (NOE)-Derived Distances and Capture Binding Diversity"
  - **Concern** The claim of "accurately captures dynamics" is not fully supported. The authors report that correlations between predicted scores (ripTM, PAE) and experimental dynamics metrics (RMSF, DV) are high for intrachain DV (0.84) but "much lower" for interchain DV and "low" for RMSF. The magnitude of fluctuations in predictions is acknowledged to be "generally much smaller" than in experimental models. The NOE analysis shows median RMS violations of approximately 1.5 Å for AlphaFold-Multimer structures versus 0.2-0.3 Å for experimental models, indicating substantial inaccuracies in predicted interatomic distances. While the authors argue that AlphaFold-Multimer identifies which regions are dynamic, the quantitative accuracy of the predicted dynamics is not established. The claim of "accurate" capture of dynamics requires either more direct validation or more tempered language.
  - **Why it matters** The title and abstract make a strong claim about capturing dynamics that goes beyond what the evidence demonstrates. If the predictions systematically underestimate the magnitude of conformational heterogeneity, then the practical utility for understanding IDR function is limited. Overstating the accuracy of dynamics prediction could mislead users who rely on these predictions for biological interpretation.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should either (a) provide quantitative evidence that the predicted dynamics magnitudes are accurate, not just that relative trends are captured, or (b) revise the claims to state that AlphaFold-Multimer identifies which regions are dynamic (qualitative) rather than accurately capturing the magnitude of dynamics. Direct comparison of predicted and experimental order parameters or ensemble widths would strengthen the case.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Validation sufficiency
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that the minD metric identifies binding sites within full-length proteins with high accuracy (AUROC = 0.94 and 0.92 for Rg datasets) and that this could be used to identify interaction sites and reduce computational costs in interaction discovery.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "AlphaFold-Multimer Is Capable of Identifying the Binding IDR in a Full-Length Protein" and Discussion section
  - **Concern** The validation of minD for binding-site identification is performed only on complexes where the binding partner is known and the interaction is known to occur. The authors do not test whether minD can distinguish true binding sites from false positives in a discovery setting where the receptor is paired with proteins that do not interact. While the authors create a "balanced negative set" for one analysis, the primary binding-site identification results appear to use only true interaction pairs. The claim that minD could "reduce computational costs" in interaction discovery requires demonstration that minD does not produce excessive false positives when scanning many potential partners.
  - **Why it matters** The practical utility of minD for interaction discovery depends on its specificity in a screening context. If minD identifies many false-positive binding sites in non-interacting proteins, the proposed advantage of reducing computational costs would be negated by the need to validate many false candidates.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should report the performance of minD in a true discovery setting, where each receptor is paired with many non-interacting full-length proteins, and report precision-recall or false discovery rates at the protein level, not just the residue level. The existing negative set analysis (Fig. S11) should be more prominently featured and its results reported in the main text.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Methodological completeness
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript states that "AlphaFold-Multimer generates correct complex structures for over 76% of the samples present in each of the datasets" and that the quality varies substantially across datasets.
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "AlphaFold-Multimer Predicts Different IDR-Containing Complexes with High Success Rate but Varying Quality"
  - **Concern** The manuscript does not report the distribution of prediction quality in sufficient detail. While the success rate of 76% is stated, the breakdown by quality class (acceptable, medium, high) is only partially reported. The authors note that high-quality predictions are about 25% for Rg/Rg+ID sets but only 3% for the Fuzzy set, but the full distribution across all quality classes and datasets is not provided in the text. Additionally, the criteria for classifying a prediction as "correct" versus "incorrect" are not fully specified in the main text.
  - **Why it matters** The practical utility of AlphaFold-Multimer for IDR complexes depends on the distribution of prediction quality, not just the success rate. Users need to know what fraction of predictions are likely to be high-quality versus merely acceptable. The lack of detailed reporting makes it difficult to assess the practical implications of the findings.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should provide a complete breakdown of prediction quality classes for each dataset, either in the main text or in a clearly referenced supplementary table. The definition of "correct" predictions should be explicitly stated.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Comparative benchmarking
  - **Claim pointer** The manuscript implies that AlphaFold-Multimer's performance on IDR complexes is comparable to or better than alternative approaches, and that the identified limitations (e.g., underestimation of dynamics) are inherent to the method.
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section
  - **Concern** The manuscript does not compare AlphaFold-Multimer's performance to alternative methods for predicting IDR-mediated interactions or IDR dynamics. Given that the authors acknowledge limitations in capturing the magnitude of dynamics, it would be important to know whether alternative approaches (e.g., molecular dynamics simulations, specialized IDR docking methods, or other AI-based predictors) perform better or worse. Without such comparisons, it is difficult to contextualize the significance of the findings.
  - **Why it matters** The claim that AlphaFold-Multimer is useful for IDR interactions is strengthened by demonstrating that it performs comparably to or better than existing methods. Conversely, if alternative methods capture dynamics more accurately, the practical recommendations would differ.
  - **Resolution test** The authors should include a comparison to at least one alternative method for predicting IDR-mediated complex structures or dynamics, or explicitly state that no such comparison is available and discuss the implications of this limitation.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity of reporting
  - **Affected element** Dataset construction
  - **Evidence pointer** Materials and Methods section "Curating a Blind Dataset Based on Radius of Gyration (Rg)"
  - **Issue** The description of the Rg-based dataset construction is somewhat technical and the rationale for specific thresholds (e.g., Rg/N < 0.26) is not fully explained in the main text. The relationship between the Rg-based selection and the biological properties of IDRs could be clarified.
  - **Required correction** Provide a brief explanation of why Rg/N is an appropriate classifier for identifying IDR-containing complexes and what the threshold of 0.26 represents biologically.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Statistical reporting
  - **Affected element** Correlation analyses
  - **Evidence pointer** Results section "AlphaFold-Multimer Structures Are Mostly Consistent with Nuclear Overhauser Effect (NOE)-Derived Distances and Capture Binding Diversity"
  - **Issue** The manuscript reports Spearman's correlation coefficients for DV comparisons but does not provide confidence intervals or p-values for these correlations. The sample sizes for these analyses are also not clearly stated.
  - **Required correction** Report confidence intervals and p-values for the reported correlations, and state the number of complexes/residues included in each analysis.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Reproducibility
  - **Affected element** Code availability
  - **Evidence pointer** Data, Materials, and Software Availability section
  - **Issue** The code for calculating minD is available on GitHub, but the manuscript does not specify the version of the code used for the analyses or provide a DOI for archival purposes.
  - **Required correction** Provide a versioned release of the code with a DOI (e.g., via Zenodo) to ensure reproducibility.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Terminology
  - **Affected element** Use of "dynamics"
  - **Evidence pointer** Throughout the manuscript
  - **Issue** The term "dynamics" is used to describe both experimental measures (RMSF, DV from NMR ensembles) and predicted scores (ripTM, PAE). These are conceptually different quantities, and the manuscript would benefit from clearer distinction between experimental dynamics and predicted confidence scores.
  - **Required correction** Use more precise terminology, such as "experimental conformational variability" for RMSF/DV and "predicted confidence" or "predicted variability" for ripTM/PAE, to avoid conflating these distinct concepts.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (claim of accurate dynamics capture not fully supported), R1-M2 (minD validation not demonstrated in discovery setting), R1-M3 (incomplete reporting of prediction quality distributions)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The work is original in its systematic evaluation of AlphaFold-Multimer across a spectrum of IDR binding modes and the introduction of the minD metric. The use of a blind dataset is a methodological strength. Scientific importance: The question of whether AI-based structure prediction can handle IDR-mediated interactions is of high importance given the prevalence of IDRs in cellular processes and the increasing reliance on AlphaFold-Multimer for interaction prediction. The findings have practical implications for users of this tool. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges computational biology, structural biology, and biophysics, and would be of interest to researchers in these fields. The practical guidelines for using AlphaFold-Multimer would also be relevant to experimental biologists studying IDR-mediated interactions. Technical soundness: The dataset construction is careful and the validation approaches are appropriate. However, the manuscript has gaps in reporting (effect sizes, confidence intervals, full quality distributions) and the validation of the minD metric in a discovery context is incomplete. The NOE analysis is appropriate but reveals substantial quantitative inaccuracies in predicted structures. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, though some sections (particularly the Materials and Methods) are technical. The significance statement provides a clear summary for a broad audience. Overall, the work addresses an important question with a thoughtful approach, but the strength of the claims exceeds what the evidence currently supports.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses an important question and the dataset construction is a significant contribution. However, the claims about accurately capturing dynamics need to be tempered or supported with additional quantitative evidence. The validation of minD in a discovery setting should be strengthened. The reporting of prediction quality distributions and statistical details should be improved. If these concerns are addressed, the manuscript would make a valuable contribution to the field.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that AlphaFold-Multimer "accurately captures dynamics" of IDRs is not fully supported by the evidence presented. The correlations between predicted scores and experimental dynamics are moderate to low for some measures, and the magnitude of predicted heterogeneity is acknowledged to be insufficient.
- The claim that minD can "reduce computational costs" in interaction discovery is not directly tested. The validation is performed on known interaction pairs, and the performance in a true discovery setting with many non-interacting candidates is not established.
- The statement that "AlphaFold-Multimer is capable of producing correct complex structures for the majority of samples in Rg and Rg+ID sets when using the full-length protein" is reported without the specific success rates, making it difficult to assess the practical utility.
- The claim that "AlphaFold-Multimer scores are clearly able to separate predictions for true complexes from predictions for decoys" is based on AUROC values, but the specific AUROC values for the combined score across all datasets are not fully reported in the text.
- The assertion that "a Combined score of 0.62 sets apart a large fraction of the high accuracy predictions from those with lower quality" is presented without reporting the sensitivity and specificity at this threshold.
- The statement that "AlphaFold-Multimer has an intrinsic knowledge of more dynamic parts of IDR-containing complexes" is an interpretation based on correlations with experimental measures, but the causal or mechanistic basis for this claim is not established.
