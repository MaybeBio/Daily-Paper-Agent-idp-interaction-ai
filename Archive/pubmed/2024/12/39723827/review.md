## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text, including abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods, and data availability statement. No figures or supplementary materials were provided for review.
- **Assessment boundary** The review is limited to the scientific claims, methodology, and conclusions as presented in the text. Experimental validation of computational predictions is assessed based on the described methods and results. No assessment of the actual figures, tables, or supplementary data is possible without the full manuscript package.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The manuscript claims that the N642H oncogenic mutation in the STAT5B SH2 domain enhances the stability of the parallel dimer state by altering the hydrogen bonding network within the phosphotyrosine (pY) binding pocket, specifically by strengthening pY-R618 interactions. The authors further claim that the mutation rigidifies the SH2 domain in the apo state, reducing conformational entropy and thereby lowering the entropic cost of parallel dimer formation. A parallel dimer model was constructed by homology to STAT6, and the predictions were partially validated by hydrogen-deuterium exchange mass spectrometry.
- **Visible evidence base** MD simulation trajectories (nearly 100 μs cumulative sampling) of STAT5B and STAT5BN642H in monomeric, antiparallel dimer, and parallel dimer forms; homology model of the STAT5B parallel dimer based on STAT6; TRESI-HDMS experiments on STAT5B and STAT5BN642H with and without a phosphopeptide; PCA-based conformational landscape of STAT SH2 domains from crystal structures.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figures, tables, and supplementary materials were not provided. This includes all structural figures, simulation analysis plots, HDMS data, and the supporting tables (Table 1, S1, S2). The absence of these materials limits the ability to independently assess the quality of the data, the statistical significance of the reported differences, and the validity of the conformational analysis.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents a comprehensive computational study of the STAT5B N642H mutation, a clinically relevant oncogenic driver. The work addresses a significant gap in the literature, as no structure of the STAT5B parallel dimer is currently available. The authors employ a multi-pronged computational approach, including homology modeling, extensive MD simulations, and conformational landscape analysis, to propose a mechanistic model for how N642H enhances STAT5B activity. The inclusion of TRESI-HDMS experiments to validate the computational predictions is a notable strength. However, the manuscript has several weaknesses that need to be addressed. The most significant concern is the reliance on a homology model for the central claim regarding the parallel dimer, which introduces uncertainty that is not fully discussed. The interpretation of the HDMS data, particularly the claim that the mutation and pY binding have "similar" effects, requires more careful quantitative analysis. The discussion of the entropic mechanism, while plausible, is speculative and not directly supported by the data presented. The manuscript would be strengthened by a more critical assessment of the limitations of the computational approach and a more nuanced interpretation of the experimental validation.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in the fields of cancer biology, signal transduction, and structural biology would be interested. Specifically, those studying STAT protein function, JAK-STAT pathway dysregulation in leukemia, and the molecular mechanisms of oncogenic mutations. The work is also relevant to computational biologists interested in the application of MD simulations to understand mutation effects on protein dynamics and to drug discovery scientists targeting STAT5B for therapeutic intervention.
- **Major strengths**
    1.  The manuscript addresses a clinically important and unresolved question: the molecular mechanism of the most frequent oncogenic STAT5B mutation.
    2.  The computational approach is extensive, with nearly 100 μs of cumulative MD simulation time across multiple oligomeric states.
    3.  The use of TRESI-HDMS to experimentally validate the computational predictions of rigidification is a strong point, providing orthogonal evidence.
    4.  The construction of a conformational landscape of STAT SH2 domains is a useful analytical tool for contextualizing the simulation results.
    5.  The manuscript is generally well-written and logically structured, with a clear narrative.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **R1-M1** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes): The central claim regarding the parallel dimer structure and the effect of N642H on pY binding is based on a homology model, not an experimentally determined structure. The manuscript does not adequately address the potential limitations and uncertainties associated with this model. The validity of the specific hydrogen bonding interactions and the conformational states identified in the parallel dimer simulations depends critically on the accuracy of the model. The authors should provide a more thorough validation of the model, discuss its limitations, and consider whether the key conclusions are robust to the choice of template or modeling approach.
    - **R1-M2** (Severity: Major, Blocking: Yes): The interpretation of the TRESI-HDMS data is not sufficiently rigorous. The authors state that the data "support" the computational predictions, but the analysis presented in the text is qualitative. The claim that the N642H mutation and pY binding have a "similar" effect on rigidification requires a quantitative comparison of the HDMS data, including statistical analysis of the differences in deuterium uptake. Without this, the strength of the experimental validation is unclear. The authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the HDMS data, including the specific peptides that show significant changes and the magnitude of those changes.
    - **R1-M3** (Severity: Major, Blocking: No): The proposed entropic mechanism for increased dimer affinity is plausible but speculative. The authors suggest that rigidification of the apo state reduces the entropic cost of binding, but they do not provide direct evidence for this. The calculation of conformational entropy from MD simulations is challenging and was not performed. The authors should either provide a more direct assessment of the entropic contributions or temper their conclusions about the mechanism.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **R1-m1** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity, Affected Element: Introduction, Evidence Pointer: Section "INTRODUCTION", location not provided): The introduction could be more concise. The description of STAT protein function and the JAK-STAT pathway is somewhat lengthy and could be streamlined to focus more directly on the specific questions addressed in this study.
    - **R1-m2** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Technical Soundness, Affected Element: Methods, Evidence Pointer: Section "MD simulation methods", location not provided): The methods section would benefit from additional details on the force field parameters for the phosphorylated tyrosine residue and the histidine protonation state. The choice of the neutral histidine state for N642H is justified, but the specific tautomer used should be stated.
    - **R1-m3** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Reproducibility, Affected Element: Methods, Evidence Pointer: Section "Analysis of simulation trajectories", location not provided): The description of the PCA analysis is clear, but the choice of 55 aligned residues is a potential limitation. The authors should discuss whether the results are sensitive to the specific set of residues used for the analysis.
    - **R1-m4** (Severity: Minor, Axis: Clarity, Affected Element: Discussion, Evidence Pointer: Section "DISCUSSION", location not provided): The discussion of the potential therapeutic implications is somewhat brief. The authors could expand on how the structural information presented could be used to design specific inhibitors, which would increase the impact of the work.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established**
    - The validity of the homology model of the parallel dimer needs to be more rigorously established (R1-M1).
    - The quantitative analysis of the HDMS data needs to be presented to support the claim of experimental validation (R1-M2).
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
    - **Originality**: The work is original in its focus on the STAT5B parallel dimer, which has not been previously characterized. The combination of MD simulations with HDMS to study this specific mutation is a novel approach.
    - **Scientific importance**: The N642H mutation is a clinically relevant oncogenic driver, and understanding its mechanism is of high importance. The findings could have implications for the development of targeted therapies.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The work is likely to appeal to a specialized audience of structural biologists, computational biologists, and cancer researchers. The level of detail in the methods and analysis may limit its accessibility to a broader readership.
    - **Technical soundness**: The computational methods are state-of-the-art and the simulation setup is generally sound. However, the reliance on a homology model and the qualitative interpretation of the HDMS data are significant weaknesses that need to be addressed.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists**: The manuscript is well-written but assumes a high level of familiarity with MD simulations, protein structure, and STAT biology. The abstract is accessible, but the main text is likely challenging for nonspecialists.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The manuscript addresses an important question and presents a plausible mechanism, but the central claims are not fully established from the provided evidence. The authors must address the concerns regarding the homology model and the quantitative analysis of the HDMS data before the case is convincing.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the N642H mutation "enhances the pY-binding interaction" in the parallel dimer is based on simulations of a homology model and is not directly supported by experimental binding affinity measurements.
- The claim that the N642H mutation and pY binding have a "similar" impact on SH2 domain rigidification is based on a qualitative comparison of HDMS data and requires quantitative analysis.
- The proposed entropic mechanism for increased dimer affinity is speculative and not directly supported by the data.
- The statement that the "mutation's mechanism of action" is revealed is an overstatement; the work provides a plausible model, but the mechanism is not definitively established.
- The claim that the findings "could help pave the way for novel therapeutic strategies" is a reasonable speculation but is not supported by any direct evidence of therapeutic efficacy.
