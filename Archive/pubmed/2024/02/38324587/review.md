## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript text (Abstract, Introduction, Materials and methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion, Supporting information list)
- **Assessment boundary** Scientific soundness, methodological rigor, internal consistency, and support of claims by presented evidence. No assessment of code functionality, reproducibility of GitHub materials, or peer review history.
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors use multi-scale MD simulations (conventional all-atom and Martini3.0 coarse-grained) and umbrella sampling PMF calculations to argue that ASPP2 and iASPP bind p53 through an ensemble of binding modes, reconciling crystallographic and NMR data. They claim single crystal structures fail to capture verified interactions, ensemble-average contacts match NMR data, ensemble-average binding free energies match experimental KDs, and p53 IDRs modulate binding kinetics and mode selection.
- **Visible evidence base** Figures 1–6 (described in text), S1–S6 Figs (described), S1 Table (described), Methods section, Results section, Discussion section
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Figure files not provided; exact numerical values for PMF curves, contact frequencies, and statistical tests not provided; simulation convergence metrics not shown; force field parameter details for Zn2+ coordination incomplete; cluster analysis details for representative structure selection not described; backmapping validation not shown.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The manuscript addresses a genuine and important discrepancy in the structural biology of ASPP-p53 interactions. The ensemble binding framework is conceptually appealing and the multi-scale simulation strategy is appropriate. However, the evidence as presented has several gaps that prevent full confidence in the conclusions. The qualitative agreement with NMR data is encouraging but the quantitative claims regarding binding free energies require closer scrutiny given the known limitations of the methods employed. The IDR-related findings are interesting but the mechanistic interpretation is not fully supported by the data shown. The manuscript would benefit from additional validation steps and clearer presentation of numerical results.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Structural biologists studying protein-protein interactions, particularly those interested in the limitations and complementarity of crystallography versus NMR. Computational biochemists developing and applying ensemble-based approaches to reconcile experimental structural data. Cancer biologists studying p53 regulation, as ASPP2 and iASPP are important modulators of p53-dependent apoptosis. The methodological framework could also interest researchers studying intrinsically disordered regions in protein interactions.
- **Major strengths** The manuscript tackles a well-defined and significant controversy in the field. The multi-scale approach combining coarse-grained sampling with all-atom refinement is methodologically sound and appropriate for the question. The use of multiple independent lines of evidence (contact analysis, binding free energies, kinetic analysis) strengthens the argument. The authors are appropriately cautious in some interpretations, particularly regarding the encounter complex framework. The work has clear potential therapeutic relevance given the importance of these interactions in cancer.
- **Major Concerns**
  - **Concern ID** R1-M1
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Quantitative claim support
  - **Claim pointer** "the ensemble-average binding free energies better match experimental KD values compared to single crystallography-determined binding mode"
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 4, S4 Fig, Results section "Ensemble-average binding free energy agrees with experimental affinities"
  - **Concern** The claim that ensemble-average binding free energies match experimental KDs is central to the paper's thesis, but the presented evidence is insufficient. The ΔG values for single crystal structures are reported as −12.73 ± 0.12 kcal/mol (ASPP2) and −7.57 ± 0.11 kcal/mol (iASPP), which the authors note contradicts the comparable experimental KDs. However, the ΔG values for the ensemble-based poses are not explicitly reported in the text. The statistical comparison (t-test) is mentioned but the actual p-values, sample sizes, and the distribution of ΔG values across poses are not shown. Furthermore, the conversion from KD to ΔG (ΔG = RT ln KD) for the experimental values is not presented, making it difficult to assess the magnitude of the claimed agreement. The PMF calculations are based on a single reaction coordinate (center-of-mass distance), which may not capture the full binding free energy landscape, and no convergence analysis for the umbrella sampling windows is shown.
  - **Why it matters** The quantitative agreement with experimental affinities is one of the two primary pillars supporting the ensemble binding framework. If this claim is not rigorously established, the manuscript's central thesis is weakened. The current presentation does not allow the reader to verify the statistical significance of the claimed agreement or to assess whether the ensemble approach genuinely outperforms the single-structure approach.
  - **Resolution test** Provide explicit ΔG values for all calculated poses (crystal-based and ensemble-based) with uncertainties. Show the experimental ΔG values derived from KDs. Present the full statistical comparison including effect sizes and confidence intervals. Include convergence analysis for umbrella sampling (e.g., overlap between windows, histogram distributions). Consider using multiple reaction coordinates or a more rigorous free energy method (e.g., well-tempered metadynamics) to validate the PMF results.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M2
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** Yes
  - **Axis** Methodological validation
  - **Claim pointer** "the binding mode based solely on a single crystal structure does not enable iASPP's RT loop to engage with p53's C-terminal linker—a verified interaction"
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 2, Results section "MD simulations starting from crystallography binding modes fail to capture the experiment-verified inter-protein interactions"
  - **Concern** The conclusion that crystal structure-based simulations fail to capture the RT loop-linker interaction is based on simulations starting from two specific crystal structures (1YCS and 6RZ3). However, the simulation time (3 × 1 μs) may be insufficient to observe conformational rearrangements that could bring the linker to the RT loop, especially if there is a significant free energy barrier. The authors do not show whether the linker samples alternative conformations during the simulation or whether it remains trapped in a local minimum. Additionally, the use of the p53P-DBD construct (with N-terminal Pro-domain) for the initial simulations, but the p53P-DBD-L construct (with additional C-terminal linker) for later simulations, creates a potential inconsistency. The authors should demonstrate that the failure to observe the interaction is not a sampling artifact.
  - **Why it matters** This negative result is used to motivate the need for an ensemble approach. If the failure is due to insufficient sampling rather than the inherent limitations of the single-structure model, the argument for the ensemble framework is weakened. The distinction between "the crystal structure cannot support this interaction" and "our simulations from the crystal structure did not observe this interaction" is critical.
  - **Resolution test** Show time evolution of the minimum distance between the RT loop and the C-terminal linker for all simulations. Perform additional simulations with enhanced sampling (e.g., replica exchange, metadynamics) to verify that the linker cannot reach the RT loop from the crystal structure starting point. Alternatively, run simulations starting from a model where the linker is pre-positioned near the RT loop to test whether the interaction is stable. Clarify which construct was used for which simulation and justify the choice.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M3
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Interpretation of IDR effects
  - **Claim pointer** "the ensemble binding complexes are sensitive to p53's intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs), attesting to experimental observations that these IDRs contribute to biological functions"
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 5, Results section "p53's IDRs accelerate the binding rates and further differentiate p53's bindings toward ASPP2 and iASPP"
  - **Concern** The claim that IDRs "differentiate" binding patterns between ASPP2 and iASPP is based on the sketch-map projections and contact analysis. However, the sketch-map dimensionality reduction method is sensitive to the choice of parameters (e.g., sigmoid functions, weights), and the authors acknowledge that the default values were used. The interpretation of "more concentrated and distinct binding patterns" is qualitative and not supported by quantitative metrics. Additionally, the claim that IDRs accelerate binding through a "fly-casting" mechanism is inferred from the reduced first-contact time, but the authors do not directly measure the capture radius or the conformational sampling of the IDRs. The control experiment with rigidified IDRs is valuable, but the authors do not show whether the rigidified IDRs also alter the binding mode distribution, which would be important for separating kinetic from thermodynamic effects.
  - **Why it matters** The IDR findings are presented as a significant contribution of the work, with implications for understanding how disordered regions modulate protein interactions. If the differentiation claim is not robustly supported, the biological significance of the ensemble framework is diminished. The fly-casting interpretation, while plausible, requires more direct evidence.
  - **Resolution test** Provide quantitative metrics for the sketch-map projections (e.g., silhouette scores, clustering validity indices) to support the claim of distinct binding patterns. Show the contact maps for the rigidified IDR control to determine whether the binding mode distribution is also affected. Consider measuring the radius of gyration of the IDRs over time to directly assess the fly-casting mechanism. If possible, compare the simulated encounter complex structures with experimentally determined encounter complexes for related systems.
  - **Concern ID** R1-M4
  - **Severity** Major
  - **Blocking** No
  - **Axis** Force field and model limitations
  - **Claim pointer** "We showed that the ensemble-average inter-protein contacting residues and NMR-detected interfacial residues qualitatively overlap on ASPP proteins"
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 3, Results section "Ensemble-average interprotein contacts agree with solution NMR revealed PPI interfaces"
  - **Concern** The qualitative overlap between simulated contacts and NMR chemical shift perturbations is encouraging, but the comparison is not quantitative. The RMSE values (0.291 and 0.248 for iASPP and ASPP2, respectively) are reported, but there is no benchmark or threshold to assess whether these values represent good agreement. The authors do not compare against a null model (e.g., random contacts) or against alternative methods. Additionally, the Martini3.0 coarse-grained model has known limitations in representing side-chain packing and electrostatic interactions, which could affect the contact analysis. The backmapping procedure from coarse-grained to all-atom representations may introduce artifacts, and the authors do not validate the backmapped structures.
  - **Why it matters** The agreement with NMR data is the second primary pillar supporting the ensemble framework. If the comparison is not rigorous, the validation is incomplete. The limitations of the coarse-grained model could systematically bias the contact analysis, and the authors should demonstrate that their conclusions are robust to these limitations.
  - **Resolution test** Provide a quantitative comparison metric with a defined threshold for agreement (e.g., Matthews correlation coefficient, F1 score). Compare the simulated contacts against a null model (e.g., random protein surfaces) to demonstrate specificity. Validate the backmapping by comparing all-atom properties (e.g., RMSD to the original crystal structure, Ramachandran plots) before and after backmapping. Consider running a subset of simulations with a different force field (e.g., CHARMM36m) to test robustness.
- **Minor Comments**
  - **Concern ID** R1-m1
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Clarity and completeness
  - **Affected element** Methods section, "Conventional molecular dynamics (MD) simulations"
  - **Evidence pointer** Methods section, paragraph 1
  - **Issue** The description of the Zn2+ coordination restraint is vague. The authors state that "harmonic constraints" were introduced between Zn2+ and coordinating atoms with a force constant of 300 kcal/mol/Å2, but do not specify the equilibrium distances or whether the restraints were applied to all coordinating atoms equally. The choice of force constant is not justified.
  - **Required correction** Provide the equilibrium distances for the Zn2+-coordinating atom restraints, specify which atoms were restrained, and justify the force constant choice. If possible, cite precedent for this approach in similar simulations.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m2
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Statistical reporting
  - **Affected element** Results section, "Ensemble-average binding free energy agrees with experimental affinities"
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 4D, Results section
  - **Concern** The t-test results are mentioned but the specific p-values, test type (one-tailed vs. two-tailed, paired vs. unpaired), and sample sizes are not reported. The comparison between crystal structure-based ΔG and Martini-based ΔG for iASPP is stated to be significant (p = 0.0008), but the direction of the difference and the effect size are not given.
  - **Required correction** Report the full statistical details for all t-tests, including p-values, confidence intervals, effect sizes, and sample sizes. Consider using a more robust statistical test if the data are not normally distributed.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m3
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Terminology consistency
  - **Affected element** Throughout
  - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Results, Discussion
  - **Issue** The terms "binding mode," "binding pose," and "binding configuration" are used interchangeably throughout the manuscript. This creates ambiguity, particularly when discussing the ensemble framework.
  - **Required correction** Define these terms clearly at first use and use them consistently throughout. For example, "binding mode" could refer to the overall topological arrangement, while "binding pose" could refer to a specific conformation within a mode.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m4
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Figure quality
  - **Affected element** Figure 3
  - **Evidence pointer** Figure 3A, 3B
  - **Issue** The description of Figure 3A mentions "red '*'-marked residues" but the figure itself is not available for review. The authors should ensure that the figure is legible and that the markers are clearly visible. The qualitative overlap between CG and NMR data is described in the text but the figure should allow readers to visually verify this.
  - **Required correction** Ensure that the figure is high-resolution and that all markers are clearly visible. Consider adding a quantitative comparison panel (e.g., scatter plot of CG contact frequency vs. NMR chemical shift) to complement the qualitative representation.
  - **Concern ID** R1-m5
  - **Severity** Minor
  - **Axis** Discussion completeness
  - **Affected element** Discussion section, paragraph 3
  - **Evidence pointer** Discussion section
  - **Issue** The discussion of the "multi-funnel protein-protein binding free energy landscape model" is brief and not fully developed. The authors mention that the 1YCS mode exhibits stronger affinity than the Martini CG samples and the 6RZ3 mode, but do not discuss the implications of this observation in detail.
  - **Required correction** Expand the discussion of the multi-funnel model, including a more detailed explanation of how the different binding modes relate to the free energy landscape and how crystal packing might shift the equilibrium. Consider including a schematic figure to illustrate the proposed landscape.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1 (quantitative binding free energy comparison), R1-M2 (sampling sufficiency for negative result)
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The ensemble binding framework is a valuable conceptual contribution, though similar ideas have been proposed for other systems. The application to the ASPP-p53 controversy is novel. Scientific importance: The work addresses a significant discrepancy in an important cancer-related interaction. If rigorously established, the framework could influence how structural biologists interpret conflicting crystallographic and NMR data. Interdisciplinary readership: The work bridges structural biology, computational biophysics, and cancer biology, and should appeal to readers across these fields. Technical soundness: The multi-scale approach is appropriate, but the quantitative claims require stronger statistical support and validation. The lack of convergence analysis and the qualitative nature of some comparisons are concerns. Readability for nonspecialists: The manuscript is generally well-written and accessible, though some methodological details could be clarified. The abstract effectively communicates the main findings.
- **Recommendation posture** Supportive if technical concerns are resolved. The conceptual framework is valuable and the work addresses an important question. However, the quantitative claims regarding binding free energies and the negative result from crystal structure-based simulations need to be more rigorously supported. The authors should provide additional validation, clearer statistical reporting, and more detailed methodological descriptions.
