The focus on intrinsically disordered sequences addresses an under-targeted class of PPIs, which is a timely and important problem.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that N-methylation in appropriate locations results in faster target binding and higher affinity, as shown by fluorescence anisotropy, relaxation-based NMR experiments, and computational analysis.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract states that N-methylation leads to faster binding and higher affinity, but no quantitative data, such as binding constants, kinetic rates, or error estimates, are provided. The techniques listed are named but not described in terms of experimental design, controls, or reproducibility.
- **Why it matters** Without numerical results and statistical context, the magnitude and significance of the affinity enhancement cannot be evaluated. The claim of "faster" binding requires kinetic data that are not visible in the abstract.
- **Resolution test** Provide binding affinities and kinetic parameters for methylated versus unmethylated peptides, including replicates and statistical analysis, in the full manuscript.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Mechanistic support
- **Claim pointer** The authors claim that higher affinities occur as a consequence of an increase in the energy of the unbound state and a reduction in the entropic contribution to the binding and activation energies.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The thermodynamic interpretation is presented as a definitive conclusion, but the abstract does not show how these energetic and entropic terms were derived. The connection between N-methylation, conformational restriction, and the proposed energy changes is not substantiated with data.
- **Why it matters** The mechanistic claim is central to the paper's novelty. If the thermodynamic decomposition is not rigorously supported, the proposed rationale for affinity enhancement remains speculative.
- **Resolution test** Include a detailed thermodynamic analysis, such as van't Hoff or isothermal titration calorimetry data, and computational free energy calculations that explicitly separate enthalpic and entropic contributions.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Generalizability
- **Claim pointer** The authors suggest that backbone N-methylation may represent a useful modification within the peptidomimetic toolbox to probe β-strand mediated coupled binding and folding interactions and to develop short peptides that inhibit these less targeted PPIs.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract only describes the SUMO-SIM interaction. The claim of general utility for other β-strand mediated PPIs is an extrapolation that is not supported by data from additional systems.
- **Why it matters** The broader significance of the work depends on whether the approach is transferable. Without evidence from at least one other interaction, the generalizability claim is premature.
- **Resolution test** Provide data from a second, distinct β-strand mediated PPI system to demonstrate that the observed effects are not specific to SUMO-SIM.
