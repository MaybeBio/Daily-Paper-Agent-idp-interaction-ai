Protein–protein interactions (PPIs) are increasingly recognised as therapeutically actionable targets, yet the discovery of small-molecule PPI inhibitors remains challenging because PPI interfaces are often large, shallow, dynamic and structurally heterogeneous. Machine learning (ML) approaches have shown promise for PPI inhibitor prediction, but most models rely on ligand-centred descriptors and may therefore capture scaffold similarity rather than compatibility with a specific protein–protein interface. In this study, we evaluated a structure-aware ML framework for the prediction of small-molecule PPI inhibitors by comparing ligand-only Extended Connectivity Fingerprints (ECFPs) with Protein–Ligand Extended Connectivity (PLEC) fingerprints. ECFP-based models showed high sensitivity on the external labelled prediction set, while PLEC-based models behaved more conservatively, recovering fewer positives but showing stronger rejection of ChEMBL-derived negatives. Family-specific ECFP models further demonstrated that PPI inhibitors could often be assigned to the correct PPI family, particularly when additional negative examples were included during training. Leave-one-family-out analyses revealed that extrapolation to unseen PPI families was strongly family-dependent: ECFP descriptors provided broader generalisation across several families, whereas PLEC descriptors improved recovery in selected cases. Overall, these results indicate that ligand-only and structure-aware descriptors provide complementary information for PPI inhibitor prediction.
